# Nuclear Weapons play-test checklist

What the next play-test of the Nuclear Weapons panel should look at: the journal entry `je_nuclear_program` and the Military panel's Nuclear tab, which compose the same types. In-game checks don't gate the merge (CLAUDE.md, "In-game checks"). The console harness is `events/te_debug_nuclear_events.txt` and `events/te_debug_deterrence_events.txt` (`event te_debug_deterrence.3` option d opens a private crisis between two other countries).

## The GUI audit's rework (2026-10-27, #1208)

The panel was reworked from the 2026-10-27 GUI and localization audit (`docs/audits/gui_audit/nuclear.md`, NUC-1 to NUC-21). Nothing here rests on an unverified engine fact in script, so there is no self-check: every item is display, and each fails visibly (a blank cell, a raw key, an empty table, a misplaced child). Where an item uses an accessor or a shape new to this panel, it says so and names the fallback.

### Overview (NUC-21, NUC-4)

1. **Banking's cells.** The overview is a grid of boxes two to a row, each with its icon in an inset box down the left and three lines beside it, the same as the Banking overview's. The cells are `te_banking_ov_cell` itself (`banking_dashboard_widget.gui`), reused for the first time outside Banking. If a cell draws without its background or icon box, define a `te_nuclear_ov_cell` copy as the Seabed and Demographics panels did.
2. **The rows.** In a crisis, a Nuclear Crisis cell alone at the top (its stage, "Deadline: N wk", red from two weeks out). Then Program (state, "Next in N mo" or "Not progressing") and Warheads (the count, "World est. N"). While armed, Doctrine (a short name, "Change in N mo" or "Can change now") and Readiness (its name, "→ X" while moving, else the deterrent's weekly upkeep), then Launch Authority (short name, when it can change) and Credibility (Doubted, Steady or Trusted, "N of 100"). The taboo bar's row last.
3. **Captions are concepts.** Hovering Program, Warheads, Doctrine, Readiness, Launch Authority and Credibility opens the concept; hovering the rest of the cell, its breakdown.
4. **Nothing ends in "...".** At 16 points "Launch Authority" is the longest caption (budgeted 151 of 154). Check every short name ("No First Use", "Compellence", "On Warning") and "Deadline: 10 wk".
5. **Credibility's icon** (icon batch 7) is the warhead under a round brass seal stamped with clasped hands. At 44 px the seal reads as brass, not as the red wax seal of a frozen program.
6. **SelectLocalization with JournalEntry data.** The figures that pick between two keys ("Change in N mo" / "Can change now"; the deadline in red or not; readiness moving or its upkeep) read `JournalEntry.GetCountry.MakeScope.ScriptValue` inside the chosen key. Demographics proves the shape with `State`; if a figure is blank here, put the number in the calling key and choose only the words.

### Posture (NUC-3, NUC-4, NUC-5, NUC-6, NUC-1)

7. **One row per axis.** Doctrine, Readiness and Launch Authority each read "Label … value [Change]". The label is a concept and hovers as one (the textbox is not alwaystransparent); the value is underlined and its hover gives the modifier lines in force and when it can next change.
8. **Change opens the list beneath.** Each Change button reads Hide once open and opens that axis's choices directly under its row, a line above them saying when it can change (or that a stand-down holds readiness at Routine). The three open independently.
9. **The option in force is tinted** in the doctrine and authority lists, and the readiness level ordered (not the one in force while moving) in the readiness table, the same tint as Covert's Funding Levels. Its button is the greyed one.
10. **The readiness table.** Four rows under a head: the level (underlined, its hover the level's modifier lines), the weekly upkeep at that level, the survivability a crisis would count there, and Order. The figures match the old tooltips' (`nd_upkeep_weekly_at_readiness_N`, `nd_effective_survivability_at_readiness_N`).
11. **Display modifiers that nothing adds.** Every readiness level's and launch authority's hover prints `GetStaticModifier('nd_readiness_terms_N')` / `('nd_authority_terms_N')`: static modifiers never added to any country, carrying `script_only` display types (`nuclear_deterrence_display_types.txt`). Check that the lines render with their names and colours, the boolean lines (Nothing Can Be Launched…, An Incident Can End in a Launch…) as a line without a number, and that `GetCustom` → `GetStaticModifier` in the axis rows' hovers renders the same lines. `GetStaticModifier` reads a definition, so a modifier nobody holds should print as the UN's and Grand Monuments' previews do; if the lines are blank, write the figures as prose under the existing modifiers' `GetDesc`, which already render.
12. **Choice rows are widgets now.** Every choice row (doctrine, authority, the crisis moves, Lift / Dismantle / Halt) is `nd_choice_row`, a fixed 480 x 32 widget with the name and the button in its blocks. Check no row draws an empty name or a button over the name.

### Crisis (NUC-7 to NUC-10, NUC-2)

13. **The headline.** "Against France · we made the threat · over a war, in public" (as the target: "the threat is against us"). The line is printed at our own scope with the opponent through a temporary scope saved in a nested one (`nd_crisis_opponent_sgui`), the shape World Crises' old rows used. If the opponent's name is blank, print the line inside the opponent's scope and read our words through `ROOT.GetCountry.GetCustom`.
14. **The grid.** Stage (its word, "Week N of 52"), Deadline ("N weeks", then we decide / then they decide), Danger and Pressure (a word over "N of 100"; Pressure reads Firm, Wavering, Yielding or Breaking at 50 / 70 / 85), then Follow-through and Next pressure, shorter. The danger and pressure hovers keep their breakdowns.
15. **Our Moves** sit right under the grid. Each move's only hover is its button's: the conditions, then what it does. The names are not underlined.
16. **Outcomes.** Three rows under a head: They concede / We concede, We back down / They back down, Both stand down, each with what happens and the credibility change. Compare the figures with the outcome notice when the crisis ends: issuer +10 / +15 (public) on a concession, -5 / -15 on backing down, a stand-down +5 or -5 (a public demand traded away); target -5, +10, +5.

### Lists (NUC-14)

17. **The datamodels.** Nuclear Powers and World Crises are tables over `GetGlobalVariable('nd_lists_store').GetList(...)` (proven for Cultural Hegemony's history chart), each item read with `Scope.GetState` (proven by Demographics) and the country with `State.GetOwner`. `State.GetOwner` off a datamodel item is new to the mod (Grand Monuments uses it on a state panel; covert reads `GetState.GetCountry` off a `Var`). If the flags or names are blank, try `State.GetCountry`.
18. **Nuclear Powers.** Up to ten rows, largest first, each a flag (name on hover, a click opens the country), the name, the world's estimate and the public doctrine's icon (its name on hover). Our row is tinted. It appears once the world's monthly step has run after the first warhead (`nd_powers_listed_sgui`).
19. **World Crises.** One row a crisis, most dangerous first: the issuer's flag → the target's (the target through `Var('nd_wc_target_cap').GetState.GetOwner`), the stage, the danger. A crisis of ours is tinted. The row's hover (an `ExecuteTooltip` rooted at `State.GetOwner` from a datamodel item's tooltip) names both and gives the dispute, the clock, talks, a guarantor and a private warning.
20. **`tiny_flag` fits** its 40 x 28 box in a 30-high row.

### Forces, taboo, reputation (NUC-11 to NUC-18)

21. **Warheads held** sits under Arsenal Size, "N (est. M)", with " · treaty: N", " · UN ban: N" or " · dismantling" when they apply.
22. **Chance of a halt** reads "not delegated" under Central Authorization, the figure under the other three.
23. **Incident exposure's hover** ends with four factor lines ("×4.0  Readiness, against Routine", doctrine, crew strain, command reliability) and, in a crisis, the danger's word.
24. **One taboo row**, "62 → 68 · Strong", no green or red on the direction; the overview's bar hover is two sentences.
25. **Credibility** is a concept: the effect lines that name it ("Our credibility falls by 15") underline the word and hover its concept, in events as in the panel.
26. **The Burden of the Bomb** hover prints the modifier's lines, UN Authority Target among them.

### Type sizes (NUC-20)

27. **17 / 20 / 16.** Rows, values, buttons and notes at 17 (EBGaramond), subheadings at 20, the overview at 16 (the tooltip face), all through `te_nuclear_fontsize_*` (`nuclear_deterrence_widget.gui`). Check that the larger rows fit their cells without "..." (Survivability's 94-wide value, the At Home tables, the steppers) and that the taboo history's subsection header reads at 17.

## The crisis rework, phase 1 (2026-10-28)

What `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-nuclear-crisis-rework-design.md` phase 1 changed (survivors, launch authority, the chance of a halt, war exposure, the launch under attack, stakes, co-belligerents). The numbers were tuned offline (`scripts/analysis/nuclear_incident_rates.py`, `test_nuclear_incident_risk.TestSpecBands`); the game has to show the mechanisms work.

28. **First-strike survivors.** An armed country with a rival shows "First-strike survivors: N of M" in Forces; the hover names the rival and its three figures are numbers, not blanks. An armed country with no armed rival, war enemy, crisis opponent or hostile armed country shows no row. Fallback if the rival's name is blank: drop it from the hover header and body.
29. **Pressure breakdown.** In a crisis between two armed powers, the pressure hover lists "Whether the threatened side could disarm the threatening side first" when the issuer is small, and a crisis over a play lists "What the threatening side demands"; the danger hover lists the vulnerable side's forces when survivability is under 50. The listed parts add up to the total.
30. **Authority hovers.** Delegation and Launch on Warning list their launch credit (25%, 60%); those two and Automatic Retaliation list "Answer Not Discounted for the Capital"; Central Authorization lists neither. The safeguards hover lists −5% launch credit per level.
31. **The launch under attack.** In an Acute crisis with an armed rival at peace, force a warning (`event te_debug_deterrence.2`, or wait): "Give the launch order" is available under every doctrine but No First Use, and greyed with the context line outside a war or an Acute crisis. Ordered at peace, it opens a peacetime exchange, as an unordered launch does.
32. **Co-belligerent cover.** An unarmed country fighting beside an armed one is warned or struck by their common enemy: the armed ally gets "A Promise Called In" with the co-belligerent text, once; walking away costs 5 credibility and the ally gets "We Stand Alone".
33. **The AI's posture.** Over an observer run, AI safeguards spread over 0–3 (not stuck at 2–3), and AI powers that can't make their force survivable move to Delegation, Launch on Warning or Automatic Retaliation, and back to Central once the threat passes. `nuclear_observer_report.py` reports Acute crises, use per Acute crisis, near misses and launches nobody meant; spec §2.1's targets are about 6–10% use per Acute crisis.
34. **War exposure.** At war with an armed enemy, the incident exposure hover lists "Our war with an armed enemy adds misreadings of its own", and the exposure band rises.
35. **An order at peace is a first use.** After the government's launch under attack in an Acute crisis at peace, the taboo falls and the UN records a first use, as for a wartime strike.

## The crisis rework, phase 2 (2026-10-28)

36. **The Suez concession.** A war leader that yields in a war with an issuer its side holds goals against loses those goals in the war's peace screen and 15 war support; a target holding none, or a co-belligerent that is not its side's leader, loses only the war support. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_suez_goals` a day after the concession.
37. **A peacetime strike as a dispute.** After a nuclear strike outside a war, the struck country (or its guarantor) can open a crisis over it, also when the strike ended a crisis the struck country had opened; the World Crises hover names "A nuclear strike" as the dispute; conceding pays reparations (the treasury moves) and the striker takes the Custody Inspections modifier. Honor after a peacetime strike on a protégé opens the crisis. A country that answered a first strike gives the first striker no dispute.
38. **Disavow our strikes.** The Posture section shows the toggle while armed; a deliberate first strike with it on shows the claim in the victim's "A City Erased" (a second save, or the observer log's `type=claim; truth=false`).
39. **The victim's answer.** An AI victim of a single warhead from a friendly launcher, not under Automatic Retaliation, accepts the claim more often than not (`type=claim_belief`); accepting sends the launcher "Reparations Demanded", once a strike, and its Pay option shows the sum.
40. **The truth.** Six to eighteen months after a claim (three to nine with a covert network in the launcher), the victim gets "It Was an Accident" or "It Was No Accident"; a lie exposed sends the launcher "Our Lie Is Exposed" with its costs in the option.

## The crisis rework, phase 3: the exchange (2026-10-28)

41. **The Strike section.** Armed and at war with another country, Strike appears below Nuclear Crisis and names the enemy; the stepper icons go through every country you could strike and wrap around; the mission rows tint the one chosen; the warheads stepper stops at the stockpile (and 25) and at 1; Order opens "Launch Order", whose Stand down fires nothing and whose Launch is greyed with the failing line after, say, Recessing. At peace with nobody to strike, or with nobody in reach (phase 4a), the section is gone within a week (it follows the weekly refresh, so a war just declared can also take up to a week to show it). The target's name is not blank (fallback: print it from the target's own scope, as the crisis headline does).
42. **A Cities salvo.** Five warheads at Cities land on five different states (or as many as the enemy has); "A Nuclear Salvo from …" and "Our Salvo Strikes …" give the warheads that landed; the taboo falls and the UN record grows by about √5 single strikes, not five; the stockpile falls by five; each notification and the world's "…devastating …" name a state a warhead hit. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_salvo_count` (written at the game's first salvo of two or more, whoever orders it) and `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_salvo_targets` (at the first Cities salvo, which only a player orders).
43. **A Forces salvo.** Against an armed enemy with low survivability, its stockpile falls by about K × the warheads landed (the victim's "A Nuclear Salvo" gives the number destroyed), the struck states lose a fortieth of their people, and a hardened enemy loses far fewer.
44. **Launch on Warning fires first.** A Forces salvo on an enemy under Launch on Warning draws up to three answering warheads on the attacker's states before the victim's own "A Nuclear Salvo" choices (the observer's `type=fire_first`); the attacker still pays a first use's taboo and UN record, and its pledges break as for any first use.
45. **Decapitation.** A Leadership salvo of 7 or more on a small Central Authorization power's capital cuts it off often (observer `type=decapitation; result=yes`): its "A Nuclear Salvo from …" offers "Our command is cut off" (and accepting a claim, if the salvo carried one), with the answers greyed and absorbing hidden; the attacker's "Our Salvo Strikes …" says the government has gone silent; the strike actions, the crisis execute and guarantor options, the launch under attack and the launch authority choices are greyed "Our command is not cut off"; two to four weeks later "Command Restored" asks how to answer, if the war is still on and no claim was accepted. A Delegation or Launch on Warning power, or a country without warheads, is never cut off.
46. **The AI's salvos.** Over an observer run with wars between armed powers, `nuclear_observer_report.py` shows salvos by mission: Forces from Warfighting, Compellence and Flexible First Use powers, Leadership against small armed Central Authorization powers, and single strikes otherwise; no AI fires its last third.

## The crisis rework, phase 4a: range (2026-10-28)

47. **Reach before ICBMs.** An armed country without ICBMs shows a Reach row in Forces: a count of countries, its hover listing them (its neighbors by land and sea, their strategic regions' owners). A distant country at peace is not in it: a warning or ultimatum against it previews "A bluff: our weapons can't reach them" (reason 8, pressure −25), and the AI issues no public threat against it. With ICBMs the row is gone and everyone is in reach. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_reach` (the first weekly rebuild of an armed country without ICBMs that has a strategically adjacent country).
48. **War-goal reach.** At war with a distant country over a war goal, the strike actions work (war-goal adjacency puts it in reach); against a distant co-belligerent of the enemy with no goal between you, they are greyed "Our weapons can reach them". Only reachable states can be picked. With missile submarines and no ICBMs, only coastal states of a distant enemy are pickable, and a Leadership salvo at an inland capital is greyed "Our weapons can reach their capital".
49. **Nuclear Basing.** Propose it from an armed country to a friend: the guest's Reach row gains the host's neighbors a day after the treaty starts, and its hover marks the host. The host's guarantor line in a crisis counts the guest at full weight. Break the treaty: a day later the reach is gone.
50. **Warning time.** A rival basing weapons in a country next to you: your First-strike survivors against that rival fall (10 survivability points' worth) at the next monthly refresh of the panel; crisis figures move within a week. Ending the article puts them back on the same timing.
51. **An accident abroad.** Give a guest High Alert and strain until an accident fires: about one bomber or silo accident in three happens in the host, whose "An Accident on Our Soil" offers ordering the weapons out (the treaty ends, all of it), a protest (−30 relations) or silence (−3 legitimacy). The guest's report names the host's state and has no "Classify the whole affair".
52. **An unordered launch out of reach.** A commander's or Automatic Retaliation's launch at a country out of reach is recalled (the observer's `action=out_of_reach`): no strike, no exchange opened.
53. **Ordering the weapons out while the treaty binds.** As the host, take "Their weapons leave our soil" within the treaty's first five years: the whole treaty ends (if it doesn't, script `withdraw` is refused inside the binding period, and the option needs the engine's break instead), and no penalty for breaking a treaty lands (no infamy, no relations loss beyond the event's own), as the tooltip says: script `withdraw` applies none unless it passes `apply_break_penalties = yes` (engine docs, default no).
54. **A war declared mid-week.** A bomber-era country whose only path to a distant enemy is the new war's goal can answer that enemy's strike on the war's first day: our own strategic adjacency is read live, not from the weekly list.

## The crisis rework, phase 4b: four new disputes (2026-10-28)

55. **A claims crisis on a shared region.** Threaten a rival that claims one of your incorporated states in a region you share with a third country. The preview reads "Over: their claims on our states". After the rival concedes, the claim is gone from the whole region, the third country's part included. The opening actions' dispute requirement is then red for that rival (the 24-month cooldown hides it from the action itself, so read the requirement's line). Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_concession_8`.
56. **A covert concession, then a relaunch.** Catch a rival's operation against you, then threaten. Once it concedes, the operation is gone from your Known Foreign Operations. Then switch to the rival (`play <tag>`), or watch as an observer: the operation is gone from its list, its hostile actions against you are greyed out with "We agreed in a nuclear crisis…", and an operation you hadn't caught keeps running. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_concession_9`.
57. **Missile defense switched back on.** As the yielder of a deployment crisis, switch a military base next to the issuer back to Missile Defense. Within a week it is back at none, and a "Missile Defense Switched Off" notice names the issuer. A base elsewhere keeps its choice. Then go to war with the issuer: the bar ends, and the base keeps Missile Defense.
58. **A deployment concession inside the binding period.** Concede while a Nuclear Basing or guarantee treaty with a neighbor of the issuer is less than five years old. The treaty ends whole, with no break penalty (see item 53). The issuer's First-strike survivors against you recover at the next refresh, and a new basing or guarantee article with any neighbor of the issuer is refused with "A concession in a nuclear crisis bars…". Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_concession_7`.
59. **A battlestation site torn down.** Concede an orbital crisis with one site half built, a second site in another state, and a third queued but not yet built. The two built sites go with their progress, finished levels stay, and placing a new site is refused for ten years ("We agreed in a nuclear crisis to build no Orbital Battlestation…"). Note whether the queued site completes: if it does, the freeze doesn't stop construction already queued. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_concession_10`.
60. **The AI's new threats.** Over an observer run, count crises by dispute (`type=crisis_tick` logs `dispute=`). Expect deployment, claims and covert crises only between rivals, orbital ones only from a great power at a hostile target, and none of them from an AI whose doctrine makes the threat a bluff (Existential, No First Use). If they crowd out the war, play and guarantee crises, the lever is their licences in `nd_ai_would_warn`, not the actions' evaluation chance, which every dispute shares. Also check that an AI guarantor whose guarantee shares a treaty with an alliance never concedes a deployment crisis.

## The crisis rework, phase 4c: trades (2026-10-29)

61. **A base for a base.** Base weapons in a country next to a rival that bases its own next to you (Nuclear Basing or a guarantee), and open a deployment crisis. Our Moves shows "Offer a trade" and the Outcomes table a Trade row (neither shows in a war or play crisis). An AI rival accepts only once pressure on it is 40 or more (raise it with High Alert, an exercise or a public ultimatum), when your side gives at least as much (a treaty counts 10, a missile-defense state 4) and when its own half ends none of its alliances. Below that line it refuses, and the crisis goes on: that is the rule, not a bug. Accepted, both treaties end whole, and each side's new basing article next to the other is refused for ten years. The outcome notice reads "A Bargain Struck" with +5 credibility for each, and neither side carries a Nuclear Climb-Down. The log's `type=trade` line gives `balance=` 0 for one treaty each: if it reads −4 or 4 with treaties on both sides, `every_scope_treaty` counted nothing in the script value, so count the treaties in the trigger instead. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_trade_7`.
62. **A claim for a claim, and the other two.** The same with mutual claims (8), mutual caught operations (9) and two battlestation sites (10). Each side's claims on the other's incorporated states go, each side's caught operations end, and both sites go with their progress. Read `TE_SELF_CHECK nd_trade_8`, `_9` and `_10`.
63. **A stale offer.** Be the side that answers. Against an AI issuer whose deployment crisis mirrors yours, bring its deadline forward with `event te_debug_deterrence.4` (option a). Repeat after each deadline until it offers a trade (it takes "Offer a trade" at a fraction of its deadlines), or switch to its tag and pick it yourself. Before answering, leave your own basing treaty. The answer's Accept now says the trade no longer matches. Taking it ends nothing, and the crisis goes on with pressure resumed.
64. **The AI's trades.** Over an observer run, `action=crisis_offer_trade` comes only from an issuer at its deadline, and only when the other side would accept by the AI's own rule. `crisis_end; outcome=11` follows only when the target's pressure was 40 or more (an AI target), or the issuer's danger was and its crisis was at least 8 weeks old (an AI issuer). Check that no AI accepts a trade that ends a treaty holding its alliance.
65. **No trade frees a bar.** Concede a deployment crisis to one rival, then open one with another whose deployment mirrors yours: "Offer a trade" is greyed by the matching line, which names the ten-year bar, and the AI's deadline never offers one.
