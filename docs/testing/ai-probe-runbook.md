# AI telemetry probe: runbook

A temporary probe (2026-11-15) that logs what every AI country has, does and decides, so that AI changes can be
sized on evidence and then measured. The owner's order of importance for the AI: economic growth, technological
advancement, legal modernization, military modernization, expansion. The probe is generated by
`scripts/generators/gen_ai_probe.py`, whose docstring lists the files and every kind of line;
`scripts/analysis/ai_probe_report.py` reads the lines back, and `scripts/analysis/ai_probe_compare.py` compares
runs ("Reading a run", below).

It is inert in a played game: every hook tests one global variable and stops. It arms itself at the first monthly
pulse of a game with no human player, and `event te_debug_ai_probe.1` arms it by hand (`.2` disarms it and keeps it
from arming itself again in that game).

**A run is judged first by the largest economies.** The owner (2026-11-16): "I am most interested in ensuring the
big economies grow - they are typically the competitors to the player. A game where only the big economies grow is
better than one where only small economies grow. But by country count, most economies are small. So just a warning
about measuring success by median country or anything like that." In the first long run half of all AI snapshots
were unrecognized powers, so a median over countries is a median of tiny countries. The scorecard gives every
table for the 20 largest AI economies of each decade first, then for all AI countries weighted by GDP, and the
typical country last; its headline compares the 20 largest with the rest.

## Before a run

1. `python3 scripts/generators/gen_ai_probe.py` and commit what it writes: the probe indexes laws, technologies,
   strategies, institutions, building groups and play types as they were when it last ran, and one added since
   goes unread. Regenerating after a law or technology was added re-chunks the positional lines (L, T), so a log
   of the earlier build then needs its own key: `--key` takes the `ai_probe_key.json` of the build that ran.
2. Deploy the build (`docs/guides/git_workflow.md` § Play-testing unmerged work). The probe's lines go to
   `debug.log`, which rotates at 512 KB through six generations, so a long run must copy the log as it is written:
   the QA harness's unattended run does (`fixture_debug.log` in its run folder).

## An unattended run from 1836 (the usual one)

Needs the owner's grant, of the launch and of the hours (`.claude/skills/game-qa/SKILL.md`).

```bash
python3 -m game_qa doctor
python3 -m game_qa fixture --until 1836.3.20 --permission "<the owner's words>"      # two minutes: the short check
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_report.py check <backup root>/runs/<snapshot id>/fixture_debug.log
python3 -m game_qa fixture --until 1930 --dry-run                                     # the harness's estimate
python3 -m game_qa fixture --until 1930 --permission "<the owner's grant of the run>" --max-hours 9   # in the background
```

The run plays under the game rules the owner last applied in a lobby (the dry run lists them). The run's reply
names its run folder; the log copy is there whether or not the run reached its date.

**The harness's estimate is low.** The first long run (2026-11-16) took 6 h 46 min from 1836 to 1925, 0.75 s a
game day on average, where the harness estimated 4.85 h. At that rate a run takes about 4.9 hours to 1900, 7.6 to
1936 and 8.7 to 1950, and later years are probably slower than the average. Give `--max-hours` room.

**The short check comes first, always.** Nobody had printed most of the probe's lines before it was written, and a
`debug_log` line the engine cannot render is dropped whole. `check` says whether the probe armed (if not, the
unattended game counted a human player: arm from the console instead, or force the arming test for the run),
which of the data-function canaries printed, and whether any snapshot line never printed. Take a failing accessor
out of `DATA_FUNCTIONS`, or split a line that is too long, regenerate, deploy and check again before the long run.
A short run ends before any country's yearly snapshot is due unless it reaches the month a serial gives it, so
expect a twelfth of the countries a month: a run to 20 March shows two months' worth.

For a version-2 log `check` also reads each version-2 addition, none of which had printed when it was written
(2026-11-16). What to look for, and what a failure means:

| `check` line | Good | If not |
|---|---|---|
| world (W) lines | one at the arming month | a version-2 log's `dn` and `dn_states` are 0 in every line, and always were: they counted with `every_country`, which does not reach a decentralized nation (run 3, 2026-11-16; version 3 reads them another way, below) |
| law ends by how they ended | `passed` equal to the law_pass lines; `maximum setbacks` 3 | the pass mark does not reach the end hook (hook order), or `max_law_enactment_setbacks` does not read as a value |
| laws set outside an enactment | above 0 over a year (the law-consistency sweep, new countries) | the `on_law_activated` hook does not run with the law as root |
| plays by type | named types, a rank and a serial on each | `is_diplomatic_play_type` in `te_aip_play_type_index` does not evaluate in the play's scope (type 0), or the globals do not carry the sides (rank or serial 0) |
| institutions: level above investment_max | 0 of the held institutions | `institution:<x> = { add = investment_max }` does not read the maximum (every one reads below its level: the check fails) |
| units: the R line's shares add to 1 | nearly all armies | a unit type missing from the line, or the fraction measures something other than the army |
| technology flags held | about half of the 24 or more, even in 1836 | a flag misnamed (the generator stops on a key no file defines, so this would be the read) |
| purchase recomputed matches cm_target | 90% or more | a term of `te_construction_market_ai_purchase_target` the snapshot still lacks, or misread (`cm_level_max`, `bubble`). `cm_admin`, a country short of bureaucracy with a Government Administration queued, is no longer a term: the purchase through the engine's pause was removed on 2026-11-16, and a log from a build that still had it differs on the rows of a paused country with `cm_admin` 1 |
| Construction Sector: levels on a named method add to its levels | nearly every sector | `has_active_production_method` misses a method the sector runs (a method the generator's catalog lacks: rerun it) |
| sector staffing (cs_staffed over levels) | between 0 and 1, and above 0 where a sector is not all under half staffed (else the check fails) | `add = { value = level multiply = occupancy }` does not read occupancy as a value from a country's `every_scope_building` |
| construction good, price over base | spread around 1.3 to 1.5, as `cm_price` was; never exactly 1 everywhere (else the check fails) | the `this.market.mg:construction` reads return nothing |
| cs_buy, cs_sell above 0 | in most snapshots | `market_goods_buy_orders` or `market_goods_sell_orders` does not read as a value (the price does not depend on them) |
| Construction Sites under half staffed | some, in a market game | the sites' own staffing (`site_low`, `site_low80`); their levels are `bg_te_construction_market_bg` in G3 |

For a version-3 log `check` also reads version 3's additions. None had printed in a game when they were written
(2026-11-16), so `check` reports each until one does; none of them fails the verdict.

| `check` line | Good | If not |
|---|---|---|
| W: `dn_states` equals `states - c_states` | in every line, `dn` above 0 (a new game on 2026-11-05 began with 158 decentralized nations) | `dn` 0 everywhere: `every_decentralized_country` reaches nothing in a script value, and the land left to colonize is `states - c_states`; the two disagree: `every_state` from a country scope or `num_states` on a decentralized nation reads something else |
| reports in the country's own month | every report in its month (serial modulo 12, 0 = January), the month after for a February one, the month before where a pulse on the 1st ran before the global pulse | elsewhere, or a country-year with two reports: the calendar schedule (`te_aip_country_tick`, `te_aip_due`, `te_aip_month_index`) does not work as written |
| banking cycle running in N snapshots | 0 in 1836, rising once countries research the stock exchange and grow an Urban Center to level 5 | 0 past about 1860 with the banking rule on: `te_banking_cycle_running` does not read in the probe |
| `bank_phase` is the band of `bank_cycle` | in all | the phase test (`banking_cycle_is_*`) or the variable reads something else |

Two lines read every log, whatever its version:

- **X construction readings left out of the report's construction figures**: the snapshots whose interface figures
  show private construction points used with no private construction goods booked (*unpaid*), or no construction at
  all while the country is building (*idle*: not paused, private levels queued, a pool of a million or more). They
  are what the engine held that week, not a read the probe got wrong (below), and they are not the year. In runs 1
  and 2 there were 235 and 149 unpaid, 18 and 13 idle; among the 20 largest of their year 49 and 39 together.
- **spacing**: the months between a country's reports and the country-years holding two. Versions 1 and 2 hold
  some in every log (280 in run 1); version 3 should hold none.

It also prints, for unpaused snapshots, how often `wasted` reads yes against how often `con_unused` is above 0: the
flag is not a measure of unused points (below).

`check` on the full log of a seven-hour run takes about 15 seconds.

## A run from a save (the late game)

An unattended run from 1836 reaches about 1925 overnight. For the late game, load a late save in debug mode, type
`event te_debug_ai_probe.1` and then `observe`, and leave it running; a human-played game does not arm the probe by
itself. Nothing copies the log in this case, so run a follower beside it (the harness's `game_qa.logtail` shows
how), or keep the run under the six generations the game keeps (about 3 MB: a year or two of the world's
snapshots).

## Reading a run

```bash
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_report.py report <fixture_debug.log> --md <where>.md   # the scorecard, by decade
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_report.py parse  <fixture_debug.log> --out <folder>    # snapshots.jsonl, events.jsonl
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_report.py report <folder> --md <where>.md              # the same, from the parse
```

**Parse each run alone.** Serials and month counts start again in each run, so `parse` or `report` given two runs'
logs in one call mixes their countries; both now warn when the month count goes back. A version-1 or -2 log reports
with the version-3 key, its later versions' tables saying "not logged" or left out, as long as the build that ran it
indexed the same laws, technologies and building groups; when in doubt, give `--key` the `ai_probe_key.json` of the
build that ran.

**Comparing runs: `scripts/analysis/ai_probe_compare.py`.** Each run is `NAME=LOG:KEY`, its log (or a parsed
folder) and the key of the build that ran it, so two builds that index their positional lines differently are each
read right:

```bash
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_compare.py side    base=<run A log>:<key A> main=<run B log>:<key B> --until 1912 --md <where>.md
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_compare.py paired  base=<run A log>:<key A> main=<run B log>:<key B> --until 1912 --md <where>.md
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_compare.py cadence --until-k 168 base=<log>:<key> main=<log>:<key> test=<log>:<key>
python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_compare.py series  --out <folder> base=<log>:<key> main=<log>:<key>
```

- `side` sets two runs period by period, every cell A → B: the 20 largest economies of each year first (and each
  of them by tag), the rest, single-year world totals, then every section through the report's three lenses, and
  the typical country's own tables last.
- `paired` is the fairest reading of two single runs: the same tag, among the 20 largest of either run in the
  decade, only in the years both runs have it under the same economic-system law, with no banking crash in either,
  and GDP within 1.5 times; B over A of the sums, by decade, with how many tag-years each rule left out. A crash
  year is read from the probe's banking fields in a version-3 log, and inferred before that (pool income under 60%
  of the country's own median under that law, for a country whose median is 5% of GDP or more).
- `cadence` gives law starts per idle month by agenda for several runs cut at one month count (168 is January
  1850 in a run from 1836), for every AI country and for the 20 largest at the cut by each country's last report.
- `series` writes `yearly.json` (each run, lens and year: the 20 largest and the world) and `countries.json` (each
  economy ever among the 20 largest, Prussia's line carried on as Germany's) for charts, through the last year every
  run completed.

Every subcommand counts as the report does: AI countries that are not decentralized nations, each once a calendar
year, and construction figures without the readings that are not the year. One run of each build is one sample of
a game whose AI is not deterministic: read a difference only where it is large, holds in every period, or survives
`paired`.

### How the rows are spaced

A country reports once a year, a twelfth of the world in each month: its month is its serial (`id`) modulo 12.
A row's `k` is the probe's month count (1 is the month it armed in; 168 is January 1850 in a run from 1836) and `y`
its year; the K and KDATE lines give each month's date. Each row's counters (`n_*`) are flows since the country's
last report.

- **Version 3** (2026-11-16) reports each country once a calendar year, in its month (0 = January): at its first
  monthly pulse in that month or after it. Country pulses come every 30 days, not once a calendar month
  (`scripting_best_practices.md`, `on_monthly_pulse_country` Is a 30-Day Timer), so a February report moves to
  March when the pulse misses February, and a report on the 1st can carry the month count of the month before.
  Reports are 11 to 13 months apart.
- **Versions 1 and 2** reported at every twelfth pulse, 360 days apart, so a country's report drifted five days
  earlier a year and every six years or so fell twice in one calendar year, in January and December: in run 1, 3,237
  of the 19,798 gaps between a country's reports were 11 months, and 280 country-years held two reports (China in
  1900 in runs 1 and 2). The brief that found it blamed the list shortening; it was the pulse.
- **`report` and `ai_probe_compare.py` count a country once a calendar year** (`once_a_year`): a second report in a
  year is folded into the later one, the earlier's counters added to it, so no flow is lost and no year's sum
  counts a country twice. A version-3 log folds nothing; `check` says when one does.

### Fields to read with care

- **The X construction figures are one week's.** A snapshot's interface figures (construction used, unused, by
  queue, and the construction goods billed) are what the engine held in the week of the snapshot. Two kinds of week
  are not the year: private points used with no private construction goods booked (*unpaid*: Great Britain's 1893
  to 1895 in run 1, 1,464, 1,089 and 455 points a week, in years its counters show building at its usual rate) and
  no construction at all on a country that is building (*idle*). Neither is a read the probe got wrong: every X
  figure is printed in one effect with nothing between the reads, and the S lines printed in the same instant agree
  (in 95 to 99% of the unpaid readings the snapshot's income equals its fixed income, so the budget held no
  investment-pool transfer either; in 28 of the 31 idle readings of runs 1 and 2 `queue_days` reads the engine's
  "never", 2147483647). What puts the engine there that week is not known. The report leaves both out of its
  construction figures (`x_construction`, table 1g) and `check` counts them.
- **`wasted` is not unused construction points.** `has_wasted_construction` held on 11,750 of run 2's 16,119
  unpaused snapshots while `con_unused` was above 0 on 108, and on none of the 1,056 paused ones, 35 of which had
  unused points (PR #1874's analysis). For points bought and not used, read `con_unused`; nothing in the report
  reads `wasted`.
- **The banking cycle** (version 3, line C2): `bank_phase` is 0 with no cycle running (the rule off, or the cycle
  not started: it starts with a stock exchange and an Urban Center of level 5), else 1 to 7, panic, downturn,
  stagnation, stable, expansion, boom, frenzy (`bank_phases` in the key), the bands of `finance_cycle_value`
  (`bank_cycle`: under 10, 25, 40, 60, 75, 88, then frenzy); `bank_momentum` is `finance_cycle_momentum`;
  `bank_crashed` is 1 when the country crashed in the 365 days before the snapshot (`banking_crashed_last_year`,
  set by every crash, origin or contagion); `n_bank_down` (N2) counts the country's monthly pulses in a panic or
  downturn since its last report. Together they say whether a year held a crisis, which `paired` and table 1h read.
- **The world line** (W): version 3 counts the decentralized nations and their states with
  `every_decentralized_country` (version 2's `every_country` never reached them, so its `dn` and `dn_states` are 0),
  and adds `states` (every state in the world) and `c_states` (the states of every other country), so
  `states - c_states` reads the same land a second way.

Version 3 adds a few fields to two lines and none of its own; in game, one month-index script value a country a
pulse and, once a year, one pass over every state for the W line.

- **The interest rate and the AI construction pass** (version 4, #1877, line C2): `rate` is the rate the country pays
  (`te_rate_paid_pts`, points a year) and `rate_base` the world reference rate it is read against
  (`te_mon_era_base_now`); `cm_reserve` is the reserve the rate asks the purchase to keep and `cm_credit` the debt
  it lets the purchase run, each a share of its limit (te_construction_market_ai_values.txt, THE RATE); `cm_pass` is
  the levels the AI construction pass has queued for the country since the game began (te_ai_build_pass_effects.txt).
  `purchase_target` reads `cm_reserve` and `cm_credit`, so the recomputed purchase still matches `cm_target`; a log
  without them is from a build before the rate, whose rule they leave unchanged.

The scorecard starts with a headline (the 20 largest economies' growth against the rest's, and their share of AI
GDP) and GDP growth by rank band, then a table for each of the five priorities (growth has seven: outcomes, the means
of growth, staffing, the Construction Sector, the construction purchase, solvency, and where the money goes), each
through the three lenses
above. Version 2 adds: the Construction Sector itself (1f: its staffing against half and against the 80% below
which vanilla's AI expands no building, its subsidies, its levels by construction method, the construction good's
price over base and its sell orders over its buy orders, and the Construction Sites' staffing apart from the
sector's); specific technologies held (2a); law starts per idle month by political agenda (3a), the
computation of the 2026-11-16 baseline analysis, which a change to the AI's law cadence is read by; how enactments
ended (3b); old laws kept while the technology for the next is held (3c); institutions against their maximum (3d);
the army by unit type (4a); plays by type with both sides' ranks (5a); and the decentralized nations and the states
they hold, which is the land left to colonize (5b). Defaults are counted as episodes too (a default within 12
months of the last ending continues it), since the engine moves a country in and out of default month to month.
`snapshots.jsonl` holds every field for anything the scorecard does not show: one record per country and year, the
indexed fields decoded with `scripts/analysis/ai_probe_key.json`, which also names the tax and wage levels
(`level_names`, 0 = very low), how an enactment ended (`law_end_how`), each R-line unit type and the banking
phases (`bank_phases`). Version 3 adds construction points a week (1g: used, by queue, unused, goods per private
point, and the readings left out) and the banking cycle (1h: the share of each lens's GDP in a panic or downturn,
with a crash in the year before, or with either in the year at all, the 20 largest's first), and the 5b table
gains the second reading of the land.

What the probe cannot see, and where to look instead: which building the AI queued and why (the save's
construction queues, `scripts/analysis/econ_save_series.py`), production methods in use (`save_world_report.py
buildings`), pops and wages. The run's last save is kept as the harness's dated fixture.

## What the first long run taught (1836 to 1925, 2026-11-16)

- **It took 6 h 46 min**, 0.75 s a game day, against the harness's estimate of 4.85 h (above).
- **`on_law_enactment_fail` fires only when an enactment's setbacks reach the country's maximum** (vanilla's comment
  on the hook: "fails by accumulating too many setbacks"; the maximum is 3, `00_code_static_modifiers.txt`). No
  enactment of the 2,938 that ended had more than 2, so the hook never fired: the 534 that ended unpassed were
  stopped before a third setback, 346 of them with a chance to pass under 5%. Version 2 logs how each ended.
- **Events before a country's first monthly pulse, and every play, carried serial 0** (546 of 12,687). Version 2
  gives a country its serial at its first event.
- **The counters run below the event counts for a reason**: a counter is reported at the country's next snapshot,
  so a country's last months and a dead country's are lost, and plays by decentralized nations (81 of 394) are
  never counted at all. `n_war` counts the plays that became wars, not plays.
- **`low_mfg` counts the Construction Sector**, which the mod puts under bg_heavy_industry, so a small country's
  idle sectors read as idle factories. Version 2 logs `low_mfg_nc` beside it.
- **In the 20 largest economies, the manufacturing levels under half staffed were almost exactly the Construction
  Sector's levels** (Great Britain 1836: 80 against 82; France 55 against 57; the United States 42 against 41),
  while the construction good sold at 1.3 to 1.5 times its base price and private investors, who do 75-89% of a big
  economy's construction, got that many fewer points for their money. That the sectors themselves sat under half
  staffed was an inference: version 1 had no field for the sector's own staffing. Vanilla's AI expands no building
  under 80% staffed unless it hired the week before (`00_ai.txt`, `PRODUCTION_BUILDING_LOW_EMPLOYMENT_THRESHOLD`), so
  version 2 logs the sector's staffing against both thresholds (table 1f).

## What a run costs

Version 1 wrote 32 lines a country a year (26 snapshot lines and 6 data-function lines), a twelfth of the world
each month: 646,000 of the first run's 660,960 probe lines, the rest 12,687 events and two lines a month. Version 2
writes 36 a country a year (the O2, J1, R1 and H1 lines) and one world line a year; the events are the same lines
with more fields. On the first run's countries that is about 742,000 probe lines, 12% more, and about 14% more
bytes (some 8 KB a country a year, 165 MB for 1836 to 1925, in the run folder on the backup drive). In game: a few
hundred script-value reads a country a year, eleven more passes over its buildings for the Construction Sector and
its sites, and one global-variable test on each hooked on-action, `on_law_activated` now among them; nothing a
player would notice, and nothing at all in a played game beyond that test.

## Removing it

Delete the four generated files, the generator, the report, `ai_probe_compare.py`, the key, `test_ai_probe.py`,
`test_ai_probe_compare.py`, `test_fixtures/ai_probe/` and this runbook, and take its rows out of `docs/auto_generated_files.md`, `scripts/nightly_audit_select.py`,
`docs/guides/python_tools.md` and `docs/systems/mod_systems/ai.md`. The variables it leaves in a save (`te_aip_*`)
are inert.
