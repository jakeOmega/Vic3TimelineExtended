# AI economy play-test checklist

What the next play-test should look at in the AI's budgets and production-method choices. In-game checks do not gate a
merge (CLAUDE.md, "In-game checks: self-checks and the play-test list"): a PR lands on the offline gate and adds what
only the game can show here. Each PR adds its own section, newest last, and a fixed item is struck through with the
commit that fixed it.

## How to run

- **The run.** The late game's subsidy bill needs the late technologies, so use the 4× fast run from
  `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-12-demographics-fast-mode-revenue.md` §4: a new 1836 game, observed, `event
  te_debug_demog.1` option h on day 1, plain-text yearly autosaves (debug mode), to about calendar 1905. A normal-speed
  observer run reaches the same buildings between 1990 and 2030.
- **Reading a save.** Each record under `building_manager` carries `building=`, `production_methods={ … }`,
  `subsidized=yes` (the chosen flag only), `balance_subsidies=` and, while it retools, a `pm_retooling` timed modifier.
  Compare a building's `production_methods` between two yearly saves (the record id is stable) to count switches. The
  baseline below came from scratch scripts that did exactly that on Fast A's 1911 and 1912 saves
  (`/mnt/d/vic3te-data/vic3te-demog-fast4x-2026-10-10/`).

## The AI doesn't subsidize coal and oil power plants (#933)

`building_power_plant = should_remove` (the coal and oil plant; nuclear, fusion and renewable plants are their own
buildings, which no strategy subsidizes) in every vanilla administrative strategy, by INJECT
(`common/ai_strategies/te_ai_subsidy_strategies.txt`; `test_ai_subsidy_strategies.py` keeps the list
complete). A strategy's value overrides the default's. The default strategy's own INJECT (`edited_default_strategy.txt`)
is inert: an INJECT can't change a key vanilla's `subsidies` block already holds, though it adds a new one
(`scripting_best_practices.md`). AI countries running no administrative strategy keep the default's `must_have`.

**How it was read.** A once-a-game self-check, removed once it had answered, logged the subsidized share of AI power plants
and railways (the `must_have` control) by the country's administrative strategy, in the owner's European Union game loaded
at 2034.9.13 and run to 2035.1.1 (2026-10-16):

| Round | Strategies with `should_remove` | Power plants subsidized | Railways subsidized |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The three with no vanilla `subsidies` block | Those three: 1% of 54. The other four, reached only by the default's INJECT: 99% of 256 | 98% |
| 2 | All seven | The three: 1% of 54. The other four (a second `subsidies` block): 19% of 257 | 98% |

No load error named a strategy file in either round. The 19% of the second group still subsidized after 3½ months was
slow stripping: 3 of 313 AI plants were subsidized in 2036.5.20, and none from 2040 on (read 2026-10-18, below).

## Unemployment as a share of the workforce (log triage 2026-10-17)

The engine's `state_unemployment_rate` reads the idle workforce over all the state's people, not over the workforce: in
Mecklenburg-Strelitz in January 1836 it read 0.048 where the pops held 0.195 of their workforce with no workplace
(Demographics' `unemployment_rate_read`, `docs/testing/demographics-playtest.md` § Log triage 2026-10-17). Owner's call:
fix it. Every mod reader outside the Demographics census now reads `te_state_unemployment_share`: the engine's figure,
read live, over the workforce's share of the state's people, which a yearly walk of each state's pops counts
(`common/script_values/te_unemployment_values.txt`; § Unemployment as a Share of the Workforce in `mod_systems/pops_and_migration.md`).
Their thresholds now mean a share of the workforce, so each bites at about a quarter of the unemployment it did. The
Settlement Authority's was raised from 5% to 10% as well (owner, 2026-10-17). The Demographics census stores the same
conversion at its walk (`te_dg_unemp`), which its youth bulge, event wave, pension AI and panel read (owner, 2026-10-17:
one source). Its own count beside it (`te_dg_unemp_walk`) went with its self-check, `unemployment_rate_read`, once both checks read OK (#1046).

| Reader | Threshold | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Settlement Authority `ai_value` (`common/buildings/07_zz_te_resettlement.txt`) | any of the AI's states above 10% (5% until the owner raised it, 2026-10-17) | +300 on an open frontier |
| Greenest Grass Campaign `ai_weight` (`common/decrees/extra_decrees.txt`) | 10% or more | × 0 |
| Recruitment Drive `ai_weight` (the same file) | above 10% | +50 |
| `migration_attraction_script_value` (`common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt`), the mass-migration destination's order | above 10% | × 0 |
| Demographics, under the census: the youth bulge (full at 20%), the event wave's Youth Bulge, the pension AI (10% and 5%) and the panel's Youth row, through `te_dg_unemp` | their own | the census's stored figure, as before; only its source moved from the walk's count to the conversion |

## Construction Cost Scaling: the slack part (#1034 follow-up)

The scaling is now the GDP part (+1,000% at £100 again) times the share of it that applies, which depends on slack, peasants plus unemployed over the workforce: all of it at 3% or less, none at 15% or more, moving a third of the remaining way each year. #1037's +100% cap is gone.

The slack is read over the whole owner country. The economy growth sim's counterfactual (`docs/systems/economy_growth_sim.md`, ministries at +1% a level) expects for the owner's playthrough:
- the EU as a whole at 3.5% slack in 2045, with no labour shortage;
- China at 8%.

France's core, inside a country whose colonies and annexed regions hold most of the slack, lies between two bounds:
- short of labour from 2004, about 31% by 2045, if nobody moves into its new jobs;
- 1.6% slack, if the brake followed the core alone.

Look for both failure modes, especially in a rich core with a poor periphery, and for the price swinging.

A new game has no old scaling, so it starts from 0.

What was done about it (the same PR). Raising the job attractiveness could not help: the young sites sit on the hiring line, not under it, so the limit is the weekly hiring rate and not a lack of takers. A standing site in the capital carrying the whole purchase would take ten weeks to fill and is short of laborers in about a third of countries. The owner pointed to what the Free Market Construction mod does, which this copy had dropped on 2025-09-02: cut the site's jobs to its first week's hires. That is now in (the section above), with two changes from the other mod that these saves call for. A site cut while it holds more than its first hires, as every site in a save like this one does, is not counted until a weekly pass finds the surplus gone: the engine lets those laborers go (owner, 2026-11-13), but ten of them on one job for even a few days would deliver ten times the site's share. Three more things the saves and the review turned up were closed in the same PR (owner, 2026-11-14; #1783):

- **A country whose only site moved built nothing for a week or two each time**, and 223 of the 269 countries on 1 April had exactly one site. One site now always stands in the capital and stands by while others work (the section above). What is left is up to a week after the last other site goes with its construction, since the capital's site comes off standby only at a weekly pass.
- **A crewed site that arrived with a conquered state kept its throughput** until its new owner's next weekly pass. `on_state_owner_change` now takes it out of the count at once.
- **A level-1 site kept through a switch back to the market** held a sector's staff on ten jobs. Every kept site is now made new.

And one reading from the same saves that looked like a fault and is not. A staffed Russian site's throughput read 125.7 where its multiplier gives 115.9, 8% more. Across the three saves the difference is exactly zero for the countries whose purchase and sites did not change (Singapore in all three, Japan in two), and for the others it has either sign and changes from save to save (Russia +8% then +37%; China −9%, −14%, then +10%): the figure a save holds for a building's throughput and the multiplier of its modifier are from different moments. Nothing multiplies a site's throughput beyond the modifier; other modifiers add about −0.01 to it (Singapore: 22,773.14 where the multiplier gives 22,773.16). No throughput type is registered for the site or its group, and the sizing measures none.

## The AI's construction purchase fits its budget (2026-11-15)

In a market game script sets what an AI government buys of construction each week. Its floor was 10 points for every country, which was several times the whole income of most AI countries, so they borrowed to the engine's pause within months. The floor is now a twentieth of fixed income, never more than 10 points, paid from reserves (nothing with none) or, up to a tenth of it, from the surplus, and at least a fifth of fixed income while the country is short of bureaucracy; the purchase is no longer rounded to whole points. A government with no surplus, no reserves and no such shortage buys nothing, and its queued level waits (owner, 2026-11-16). A paused country buys nothing, whatever its bureaucracy: a purchase through the pause for a queued Government Administration was tried and removed on 2026-11-16 (*Verdict*, below). Checked offline: the script against a model and a week-by-week budget for the long run (`test_construction_market_ai_purchase.py`). In the game, one observer run on the build with that purchase (`main` at 81b947368, 1836 to October 1913) has been read for the pause alone; the rows below are still to be read.

**How to read it.** With the AI telemetry probe (PR #1848) in the build, an observer run's `debug.log` carries a row for each AI country once a year, a twelfth of the world each month: `python3 scripts/analysis/ai_probe_report.py parse <log> --out <dir>` writes them to `snapshots.jsonl`. The fields used below: `cm_public` (the purchase the pulse wrote, points a week), `cm_target` (the formula read on the day of the row), `cm_price` (the smoothed price it divides by), `income`, `fixed_income`, `net_fixed`, `expenses`, `fixed_expenses`, `gold_scaled` and `debt_scaled` (reserves and debt as shares of their limits), `paused`, `default`, `bankrupt`, `queue_gov`, `bur`, `tax_level`, `gov_wage`, the year's counts `n_built_gov`, `n_built_mil`, `n_built_infra` and `n_default`, and per country `con_used_gov`, `con_goods_gov` and `con_used_priv` (government construction points used and their cost that week, and private points used).

**Two things about the probe decide how far the rows below can be read.** The rows are written for the probe as it will be, and each names what to use where a run's log lacks a field.
- *Decimals.* In the first runs `cm_public` printed with no decimals and `cm_target` with one, so a purchase of 0.08 logged as 0. Since #1856 the probe prints both with three. In a log from before it, read the purchase off the bill instead: (`expenses` − `fixed_expenses`) ÷ `cm_price` is the construction billed that week in points at the smoothed price. It counts private construction too, which the investment pool repays (`income` − `fixed_income`), and it reads high while the price paid is above the smoothed one.
- *The construction-used fields.* `con_used_gov`, `con_goods_gov` and `con_used_priv` were printed for two countries a run in the first runs (Great Britain and the United States in one, the United States and the Ottoman Empire in the other: that build printed them for the highest ranks only). `main` prints them with every country's row since #1848's last commit.

The baseline is the build before: the probe's two short runs of February and March 1836 (`/mnt/d/vic3_qa_backups/runs/20261116T060341Z` and `20261116T061004Z`), a long run on `main` at 3a0e13409, and the owner's saves as the save study read them. The rule itself is arithmetic on values the probe logs for every country, and the rows below read it from those.

### Verdict: BROKEN, and the purchase through the pause removed (2026-11-16)

`TE_SELF_CHECK … construction_admin_while_paused` asked whether the engine builds a Government Administration, for an AI country short of bureaucracy, on construction bought while it reports construction paused. The row here expected `wasted_weeks` near `weeks` for points left unspent, and had no case for what happened: the points went to the private queue and the pool paid for them. In no paused row of the game did the government queue use construction (`con_used_gov`, `con_goods_gov`), and in one of the 76 rows of a country buying through the pause the treasury did pay: Uruguay in 1909, 2,370 in the week, with its investment pool at 0. ## The AI electrification pass (#1877, 2026-11-28)

Monthly, an AI country with Electrical Generation queues a batch of coal and oil plant levels in the one of its states shortest of Electricity, one state a month (what they buy plus what their urban centers would take on electric street lights, less what is made there), at most 5 levels a batch, while it has under a year of government construction queued; and switches a state's urban centers to electric street lights once a batch is built there or its spare power covers half the lights' need. Details: `docs/systems/mod_systems/ai.md` § AI Electrification Pass.
