# Grand Monuments: audit of the buried sites, gaps and proposals

**Date:** 2026-10-16, asked for by the owner ("audit our current list of 35 buried archaeological sites. Add Pompeii as a
'discoverable' site already being excavated, review the existing list for gaps, and propose additional plausible fictional
sites to add"). Pompeii is built (row 87, rework spec `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-16-grand-monument-archaeology-rework-design.md`
§ 5); everything else here is a proposal for the owner to pick from.

Source of the list: issue #939 (14 candidates in its body) and its comment (the Gemini design: 30 historical sites, 8
speculative breakthroughs S1–S8, 6 fantasy easter eggs E1–E6). The MVP built the 30, Olympia, Delphi and Sidon from the body,
and S3 (Villa of the Papyri) and S4 (Tomb of Alexander) as plain sites. The owner skipped the easter eggs and their game
rule (2026-10-14).

Thirty-five sites (thirty-six with Pompeii) across nine regions, but the eastern Mediterranean and Mesopotamia hold twenty of
them, and Empiricism alone opens seven, four of them around the Aegean. There is nothing at all in sub-Saharan Africa, Iran, Central Asia and Russia, Southeast Asia, Japan and
Korea, North America, Mexico beyond Palenque, Iberia or central Europe.

## Checks on the existing rows

- **Placement.** Three sites sit in a stand-in state because the map has none closer: Machu Picchu in Ica (Cusco is the
  real region), Delphi in Attica (Phocis), Sipán in Cajamarca (Lambayeque). Vindolanda is in Yorkshire,
  the map having no Northumberland.
- **Vectors.** Mohenjo-daro is incidental in the table, but it was found by a survey officer (Banerji) following brick
  robbers, which reads as either. Deir el-Bahari is right as incidental (the Abd el-Rassul family). Fine as they are.
- **Technologies.** Göbekli Tepe at E-commerce (1995) and Caral at Digital Telecommunications (1994) are very late for sites
  a survey could identify; they are faithful to the dates, so they fit only long campaigns. No change proposed.
- **Pieces.** Eleven sites have no named part for a trophy: Winged Victory, Antikythera, Oseberg, Nefertiti, Lascaux, Nag
  Hammadi, Dead Sea Scrolls, Ötzi, Caral, Villa of the Papyri, Tomb of Alexander. The statue and stele forms carry off whole,
  so most of these need none; Villa of the Papyri ("the Herculaneum scrolls") and the Tomb of Alexander ("the golden
  coffin") could have one.
- **Pompeii** is the most famous excavation in the world and was being dug in 1836, so it was the clearest omission. It is
  now row 87, seeded as an Antiquarian dig already under way by the owner of Campania.

## A second opinion: Gemini's prioritisation

The owner pasted a ranking from Gemini on 2026-10-16 (not the owner's view, and not authoritative). It ranks 38 sites in
four tiers against three tests: regions with no dig, eras with no dig, and a mix of portable hoards and sites that stay where
they are. It recommends a 50-site roster: the original 30, a "Core 12" and eight more.

### What it gets wrong about this mod

- Its baseline is "the original 30". The game has 36: Olympia, Delphi, Sidon, the Villa of the Papyri, the Tomb of Alexander
  and Pompeii besides. The Tomb of Alexander, in its Tier 2, is already built (row 86).
- It calls the list front-loaded into 1850–1930. That is half right: seven sites open from 1974 to 1995 (the Terracotta Army,
  Vergina, Marquis Yi, Sipán, Ötzi, Caral, Göbekli Tepe). The real gap is after 2000, where there is nothing but the two
  speculative finds, in a game that runs past 2100.

### Where it is right: Angkor Wat stands

Angkor Wat already stands. The base game places it in Cambodia as a unique monument
(`building_angkor_wat`), and the landmark rules keep base-game monuments out of the Grand Monuments
(`2026-10-12-grand-monuments-landmarks-design.md`). Angkor Wat was never lost either: it was a working Buddhist monastery
and a pilgrimage site through 1836. What was overgrown and later cleared was the city around it: Angkor Thom, the Bayon,
Ta Prohm and Banteay Srei, cleared and rebuilt by the École française d'Extrême-Orient from 1908. That is the Machu Picchu
case (standing ruins under forest, known to the people living nearby), which the list already treats as a buried site, so
the dig is Angkor Thom and the Bayon, standing beside the base game's Angkor Wat as Beijing's Temple of Heaven stands beside
the Forbidden City. Mahendraparvata, the city on Phnom Kulen found by LiDAR in 2012, would be Cambodia's second dig, after
2000.

### Facts to check before building from it

- Pazyryk (Rudenko, 1929 and 1947–49) and the Ice Maiden (1993, the Ukok plateau) are different sites.
- The first Olmec head (1862) came from Tres Zapotes, not San Lorenzo.
- Derinkuyu has about eight levels, not eighteen, and the people living above it always knew of parts of it.
- Olduvai Gorge's finds are fossils and stone tools. They suit a Grand Monument poorly (nothing stands there to be raised or
  carried off), and fit the deferred breakthroughs better.

### Where the two lists agree

Both rank highly Yinxu (Anyang), the Dunhuang Library Cave, Ife or Igbo-Ukwu, Mapungubwe, Mesa Verde, Pazyryk, and the
speculative Inner Mausoleum of Qin, Tomb of Genghis Khan and Paititi. Both list Nok, Skara Brae and the Library of Ivan the
Terrible lower down. Agreement between two independent passes is the strongest signal here.

### What each adds

- **From Gemini, worth taking:** Taxila (India beyond the Indus), Tillya Tepe (Afghanistan, 1978, hidden through the wars
  and shown again in 2003), Mahendraparvata (a 2012 LiDAR find, which fills the gap after 2000), the Olmec heads of La Venta,
  Monte Albán's Tomb 7, the Varna necropolis, the Belitung wreck (a find at sea) and Lake Mungo (Oceania, which has
  nothing). Two of its ideas are mechanics rather than sites: a chance find driven by what is built in the state (tin mines
  turning up Nok, as the issue's design wanted for every incidental site), and a looting disaster where a country without
  heritage law loses a site to private diggers (the Spiro Mounds, 1933–35).
- **From this audit, missing from Gemini's:** Susa and the Code of Hammurabi (Iran), Hattusa, Templo Mayor, Altamira (it has
  nothing in Iberia), Akrotiri, Heracleion, and the speculative tombs of Cleopatra and Imhotep and the army of Cambyses.
