- Victoria 3 1.15.2: the mod is updated for the 1.15.2 hotfix, and a game saved under 1.3.3 carries on. The hotfix makes recovery also save part of the casualties as Demoralized soldiers and halves the base game's medical sources, and the mod's sources follow: Medevac Helicopters gives +40% recovery (was +80%), Bioenhanced Soldiers +25% (was +50%), and Humanitarian Regulations, Impregnable Fortress and Food Standardization V +5% (was +10%). Official Nationality gives the primary culture the hotfix's acceptance of 100 (was 50). A regime change now imposes the mod's six economy law groups (Intellectual Property, Monetary Policy, Right to Information, Financial Regulation, Genetic Rights, and Antitrust & Market Structure) at a progressiveness difference of 25, as it does the base game's economic system and trade policy. Single-Party State's choice of the one legal party offers the Green, Populist and Technocratic parties through a new event, "The Sole Legal Party"; the base game's event had no option for them.
- Nuclear strikes: a strategic strike that lands now destroys part of the state's urban buildings as well as its people: industry, power plants, ports, railways and universities among them. Each loses the state's metropolitan share of its levels, from 10% in a state of market towns to 80% in a metropolis, or half outside Full Demographics, and the struck government takes what stands of each building that lost levels into state ownership. Farms, mines, military buildings, financial districts and company headquarters are spared. A loose warhead destroys between a tenth and all of that share, and a Forces salvo or a tactical strike destroys none. A Leadership salvo puts its first warhead and every second one on the capital and the rest on other states, those with military buildings first, then the capital's region.
- Nuclear command: the chance that a salvo cuts off a command under Central Authorization rises faster. There is still no chance until more warheads land than its Command Redundancy, but it is three in four once twice as many land, and every warhead of a Leadership salvo counts, wherever it lands. Redundancy now also grows with survivability (1 for each 20), each staffed level of a Nuclear Command Center on a colony (+2, at most +6) and command reliability (+1 for each 10 above 50, −1 for each 10 below, so sabotage wears it down).
- Nuclear crises: a country that backs down in a crisis over a war drops its own war goals against the other side, including goals to free or take that side's subjects, whether or not it leads its side; its allies keep theirs. Before, only a war leader's yield counted, and it could take a goal against another country and leave the ones against you. The AI now weighs the goals it would give up before it backs down. When a crisis ends with your forces above the readiness you had ordered when it opened, the outcome notice offers to stand them back down, and six months at High Alert with no crisis under way brings a reminder.
- United Nations: an AI mandate holder's strike and the Standing UN Force's strike remove every barred war goal against the protected country. One could survive behind an older same-kind goal against someone else, and the AI holder could then be condemned and suspended for ten years. A same-kind goal against another country that the Standing UN Force's strike takes with them gets its infamy surcharge back.
- Culture merges: each entry's description ends with a line for each optional culture, saying whether it has joined or switched, or else the first condition it still fails, with the census figures. The year conditions say how many years have passed. An optional culture that lives better than the core cultures is no longer kept out; only living too far below the best-off one holds it back.
- AI: once a year an AI country at peace clears its conscription centers that hold battalions three or more eras behind its technology and builds them again with current units. Your conscripts are never touched. A formed India no longer annexes Persia, Afghanistan or Oman without a war through "The Holdout": the event now targets only an Insignificant or Unrecognized Power, Muslim-ruled, with its capital in one of India's core states, such as Hyderabad. An AI invited into a Diplomatic Framework bloc counts its relations with the inviter, as intended.
- Banking and power blocs: Development Lending pays its members; the bloc's average GDP per head had read 0, so every member counted as above it. An uprising that brings in a Command Economy keeps its State-Owned Banking instead of getting its old banking law back. A Monetary Union leader no longer pays the Lender of Last Resort backstop for an adopter that a treaty guarantee or a receivership already covers, and the Banking Help gives all five Monetary Union tiers. Monetary Policy's International Arrangements can be folded. AI blocs of the mod's three identities draw their statues from the pieces of the nearest base-game identity.
- Performance: Internal Resettlement counts each source state's people once a month instead of four or five times per Settlement Authority. In a 1977 United States game with 41 Authorities, that took 17 to 22 seconds off the monthly tick that runs it.
- Fixes: with several warheads on one state, the strike popups and the census no longer count more dead than it had people. When the mod's own kills, nuclear strikes among them, take a third of a state's people within a year, its census starts again from the people left instead of showing runaway birth and death rates. A state with a great dam (Hoover, Aswan High, Itaipu or Three Gorges) can build Hydro Plants without a river, so the Hoover Dam's ten extra levels count in Nevada. A buried site dug up while a Grand Monuments opportunity is on offer or open no longer counts toward it. The City in Smog picture's hover gives its pollution floor as an impact of 60%, the Covert Warfare concept names every source that opens the system, and "The Overspill"'s picture loses a figure cut off at the waist.
- Translations: German, Spanish and Russian cover every line of this version.
