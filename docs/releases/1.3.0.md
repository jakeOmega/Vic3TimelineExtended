- Victoria 3 1.15: the mod is updated for 1.15.0. Start a new game: 1.15 loads no save from 1.14, with or without the mod. Journal entries end through 1.15's outcomes, and every outcome has a conclusion text. The base game's new and renamed Russian states, Tula and Kostroma among them, carry the mod's state content (tourism, Local Climate, seabed basins, capital names). Its new laws, such as Labor Suffrage, Okhrana, the Third Section and Caesaropapism, take the mod's effects from the law each most resembles, and its Communal Administration replaces the mod's law of that name.
- Copper and lead: the base game's copper and Copper Mine replace the mod's copper mine. Copper is named Conductive Metals and lead Base Metals. Motors, electronics, telecoms, robots, aircraft, power grids and launch vehicles take Conductive Metals, while glass, porcelain and fission shielding stay on Base Metals. Most of the mod's thirteen mines make several goods, split by how their mineral is really used. Conductive Metals has a row in the Strategic Reserve and a prestige good, High-Conductivity Copper.
- Space: Full Colonies is now the Space Race rule's default for a new game. Under it, countries build a Space Command and warships that fight in space, blockade, seize or destroy colonies, interdict a rival's colonies in peace, and blockade Earth orbit, which stops every launch from Earth. The Moon is colonization's first stage: its six sites open after the Moon Landing, need no launch window or transit, and take the place of the Moon Base. Colonies gain autonomy and can be abandoned or break away as free colonies, modules can be taken down, Terraforming grows a site's room over decades, and the UN can take up a Convention on the Settlement of Outer Space. A milestone lost with its program can be won again.
- Nuclear weapons: a crisis now weighs how much of each side's force would survive a first strike, its launch authority and the risk of an accident. Before Intercontinental Ballistic Missiles, your weapons reach only as far as your bombers fly, and Nuclear Basing stations them in a host country. In war, a Strike section orders salvos with counterforce and leadership missions, and a launcher can claim a strike was unauthorized. Deployments, claims, covert operations and orbital weapons can each start a crisis, and a trade can settle one.
- Technology and laws: era 12's most speculative technologies move to a new era 13, and the tree past the base game now holds 191 technologies. New ones include Advanced Fission, Vactrains, AI Tutors, Von Neumann Machines (self-replicating construction and miners), Aneutronic Fusion, Terraforming, and Geoengineering with an Aerosol Injection policy that masks warming. New laws: Liquid Democracy, a voting law, with a Continuous Vote once Neural Lace is researched; two law groups, Frontier AI Control and Automation Policy; and the Bodily Autonomy laws, with a twenty-year Morphological Freedom debate that Clade Citizenship can settle after Self-Directed Evolution.
- Interface: the Timeline Extended window has a Full Screen mode that lays each tab out in columns, and it now holds the mod's tabs from the Budget, Market, Population, Society, Diplomacy and Military panels too, with new Seabed and Power Grids tabs. Every system has a Help page behind its "?". The Map Room is a page of world maps: turmoil, GDP per capita, wealth, climate comfort, the census's figures and one map for each of eight systems, most of which also draw theirs in their journal entry. National Info, the base game's Census Data window renamed, shows another country's panels. History charts open full size, and production methods have painted icons.
- Climate: every state region has a Local Climate record (its hottest days, humidity, coldest month, winter sun, water and malaria), shown on a state tile. A harsh climate cuts migration pull; technology eases it, and warming moves it. Under the Global Warming rule, a World Climate entry in every country's journal carries the warming's effects once the world has warmed 0.1 °C.
- Diplomacy: a power bloc leader can spend a mandate on a ten-year summit initiative instead of a principle. 22 port cities, from New York and Singapore to Lagos, can be released as city-states. Treaty articles build Involvement, as the base game's do. The UN vote event comes once, and left unanswered it casts no vote. The AI weighs whether its army can reach a place before it intervenes for colonial rebels, sends peacekeepers or proposes a union.
- Decolonization: an AI that holds colonies never condemns colonialism, the AI backs it more often, and the pressure on an empire is a quarter weaker. The Colonial Empire tab stays open once you have researched Decolonization, the entry can start again after it ends, and arms for a war of independence scale with the backers' economies.
- Also: Public Housing, a government building from Urban Engineering; InGen and RejoovenEsense, two late biotechnology companies; Verizon and BlackBerry arrive with Cellular Networks; power grids settle at the grid's price; ship modules are sized to their hull and late-war accuracy matters again; a covert operation's priority takes that many operation slots; Strong Privacy Rights comes with Cybersecurity, so the Digital Rights debate can be won in time; Largest Cities names real towns; institutions take names by country and government (the Bank of England, the Federal Reserve from 1913, a Ministry of Defense); and the mod's countries show formal names under Country Names on Map.
- German and Spanish: both translations cover every line of this version.
- Fixes: adding Cede Colony to a treaty or war goal crashed the game; the country panel's modifier list slowed late games (it moves to the Nation tab's All Modifiers); shipyard focus methods drove civilian output below zero; covert tech theft could steal research without its prerequisites; and more from log triage.
