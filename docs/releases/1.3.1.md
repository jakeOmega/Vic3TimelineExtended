- Culture merges (new, off by default): closely related cultures can merge into one national culture over a few decades, such as North and South German into German, Yankee and Dixie into American, or Occitan into French. Turn on the Culture Merges game rule when you set up a game. Each of the 32 merges has a journal entry under Domestic Affairs listing what it needs: a technology set by how close the cultures are (Pan-nationalism, Mass Media or Television), your sphere holding most of each culture's homeland and accepting it fully, standards of living close together, and for most merges a condition from history, such as Germany formed for German or slavery settled for American. Once a merge starts, a share of its cultures' pops converts each year, and when the merged culture holds 60% of a culture's homeland, every pop of that culture switches. A game saved under 1.3.0 keeps the rule off; turned on there, only the eleven merges into a culture that already exists can start.
- Game rules switched off mid-game: a system's journal entry now closes within a few days of its rule being switched off (from the screen that changes country), and its bonuses, costs and events go with it. Until now an entry already open kept running. A legislated tax code gives way to the base-game taxation law closest to it. What you finished stays: space milestones, monuments already built, a religion already founded. Culture merges and a probe on its way to another star pause instead. Turned back on, the Banking Cycle, Monuments, Colonial Empire and space race entries return within a month or two.
- Map Room: ten new maps: City Rank, Ownership, Company HQs (regional headquarters striped gold), Armed Forces, Fighting-Age Men, War Dead, Population Growth, Health Care, Family Planning and Power Grids. A map of figures now runs its colors from the world's fifth-lowest state to its fifth-highest, measured when you pick it and each month, so it shows how states differ now; a map with a meaningful middle keeps it, such as Fertility at replacement.
- War and treaties: the Seize Strait war goal takes only the shore of one of twelve straits (among them the Panama, Suez and Kiel canals, Gibraltar, the Bosporus and Malacca) for at least a quarter of the infamy of conquering the whole state, and holding a shore lets you charge tolls or close the strait. Enforce Privatization and Enforce Emissions Reduction are now the Privatization and Emissions Reduction articles, so their war goals no longer read "Enforce Enforce", and an enforce war goal's tooltip names the country the article binds. The AI demands an enforceable article only where it gains something, and weighs Privatization, Forced Disarmament, Free Port Concession and Emissions Reduction by the target's GDP rank.
- Transportation inputs: on mines and plantations, Partial Bulk Transport sits between Road Carts and Rail Transportation again. Rail Transportation takes the base game's 5 Transportation a level (it took 1), Partial Bulk Transport 2.5 and Extensive Rail Transport 10. Iron-Frame Buildings and the late automation methods of chemical plants, explosives factories, power plants and the electronics, semiconductor and robotics industries take 10 Transportation, and Drone Delivery Systems 20; a conversion slip had left most of them at 1.
- Investment pool: its weekly modifiers now apply before your banking cycle starts, so Neocolonial Dependency takes its share of the pool's income from the start, and a UN Development Fund grant goes into the investment pool rather than the treasury.
- Demographics and pensions: under Full, the workforce follows a new pension age or women's rights law within a few years, because the census now leads the game's own figure, which moves only as people are born. When a law or technology changes what the census sets, the state now updates a day later; that update had never run. Linked to Common Longevity is grayed out until longevity care begins, since until then it gives the same age as Linked to Average, and both linked settings' tooltips say how they play out once treatment spreads. The Power Grids tooltips are rewritten to be easier to read.
- Nuclear weapons: a program finishes at most three warheads a week, and an AI that war or a bigger rival pushes past its target stockpile stops funding at twice the target (at most 50 warheads).
- Covert Warfare: the journal entry opens only once you gain a covert operation slot beyond your rank's, from Mainframe Computers, a later technology or the Ministry of Intelligence and Security law. It could open in 1836 for a country whose rank had changed.
- Base-game content: the Ukrainian and Byelorussian Cultural Renaissances close once their culture has lost every homeland. Caesaropapism counts as State Religion where the base game left it out, in the Urban Center's clergy methods and the school laws, so it allows Public Schools. The Old Rules has a different picture, and its priest's sermon shows only in countries with a Christian state religion; elsewhere a matchmaker takes his place.
- Wording: "dear" in the sense of "expensive" is gone from the text, so Dear Money Politics is now Tight Money Politics.
- German and Spanish: both translations cover every line of this version.
- Fixes: an annexation that merged the parts of a state region could destroy the Grand Monument standing there (Jantar Mantar, Hampi and Great Zimbabwe among them), and a monument's rebuild and orphan passes waited up to a month; an open nuclear crisis event flooded the error log; two uprisings in one tick could leave the second's rebels without their share of the warheads; two of the mod's text keys displaced base-game strings, one of them a character's last name; the construction panel's minus button logged errors every frame early in a new game; and more from log triage.
