- Power grids: Grid Transfer Stations (from Electrical Capacitors) join neighbouring states into a grid that shares Electricity every month. HVDC Undersea Interconnectors and a Grid Interconnection Agreement treaty article join grids across seas and borders, and later technologies cut transmission losses. The AI builds them too.
- Transport: Bulk Transportation is retired. Transportation now covers all movement inside a state and is only available where it is made, so a busy state needs its own railways or highways; Merchant Marine is shipping between markets. The AI no longer subsidises railways and ports. Railways, ports and power plants pay construction upkeep by the method they run, and older mines pay none until they modernise.
- Wars of independence: a colony is now judged by the UN's 1960 test (overseas, a distinct people, held down), so settled land and land empires no longer count. Below 40 stability a colony can declare independence: let it go, negotiate the handover, or fight a war that is hard to win and settles nothing. Colonial Holdings lists every territory and colonial subject with a Release button.
- Space colonies: the Space Race rule has a new, experimental Simple Colonies setting (Races Only stays the default). Colony fleets take months or years to arrive, colonies are settled from Earth and built up with modules, and a colony can be ceded by treaty. In every setting you can choose your next colony's world, and the Space Race tab stays open with Space Powers and Orbit sections.
- Grand Monuments: monuments are built as construction sites and finished with a Dedicate button. Grandeur is measured against world wealth, so a monument is as grand as it was costly in its day, and it keeps growing for a century. Local effects, prestige and cultural pull taper more slowly, there is no upkeep, and four new monument policies each favour one thing. 51 great sites of the past, from the Pyramids to Petra, stand To History in 1836 and carry their state's heritage tourism.
- Politics: First Past the Post, a new amendment to the voting laws, favours the two largest parties and replaces the base game's fixed American two-party rule. Courts under Constitutional Review or Judicial Supremacy can strike down laws you force through, and you can comply, defy or pack them; seven court amendments and landmark cases come with it. New inheritance events, and reform movements now oppose Primogeniture & Entail.
- Wealth Concentration also follows health, school, IP and slavery laws and the return on capital, and wars, crashes, hyperinflation and land reform knock it down. Great fortunes add bubble pressure to the banking cycle; dispersed wealth calms it.
- Money and budget: a budget deficit adds at most 1.5 points of inflation pressure (3 at war), so bankrupt microstates no longer sit near 50%, and policy rates can reach 60. Contagion scares reach you at most once every two years. Renewable Investment costs 0.5% of GDP instead of forcing plant subsidies. The Light tax code lets you cut any levied tax to zero, and the Budget Debate's full budget costs a share of income rather than reserves. Penalties that took authority now count as authority costs, so they still bite when authority runs low.
- Spanish: a full machine translation joins German, and both are complete.
- Also: a motion to expel a permanent member of the UN Security Council weighs its veto record; the USA's name follows its government and territory; Deep-Sea Mines get a Support Vessels group; Settlement Authority frontiers scale with world population; and notices and events name the countries and states involved.
- Fixes: UN proposal cooldowns lasted 0 days, and so did a restored anti-satellite disruption; a regent began A New Reign; a civil war's winner didn't take over the loser's monuments; carbon capture offered two "none" options; and more from log triage.
