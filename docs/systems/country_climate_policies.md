# Country climate policy and accounting

The Top Emitters table ranks countries, including members of a shared market.
Annual national snapshots sum the same state formula as market totals. Each
country capital contributes its snapshot once to world cumulative emissions and
its own cumulative total. Buildings count where they stand, regardless of
investment ownership. Negative years subtract; market merges and leadership
changes do not move a country's total. Country accounting begins next January
on an old save; old market cumulative variables remain untouched but are not
used as national history.

Carbon Tax, Renewable Investment and Emission Standards now modify only the
adopter. The industrial multiplier reads the state owner's country directly,
so a member's ministry, principle and conventions also matter. Legacy shared
modifiers remain as freely revocable national choices. Market joins and monthly
maintenance never overwrite those choices. Treaty article 109 binds only its
source, including a market member; its forced seven and authority waiver are
unchanged.

Fossil-Fuel Tariffs adds 0.10 to the four native coal/oil import/export tariff
rate modifiers. All four need explicit type registrations, even though the
engine lists their patterns. The journal marker costs 100 authority, has an AI
will with a repeal band, and is shown for market members through their leader.
Adoption/repeal does not write native levels; legislated customs can coexist.
Monthly maintenance only removes the former leader's modifier after loss of
customs authority or with climate disabled. The market-merge hook also clears
it; national choices never synchronize to the leader.

Registration evidence: the 2026-10-02 unregistered grain probe reported unknown
modifier types. The deployed grain/iron registration file matches the repository
and predates the latest modded-game debug/error logs, modified 2026-10-04
06:25:37 UTC. Those logs contain no unknown modifier types, while showing
production tax-code execution. The engine catalog also lists the coal/oil rate
patterns, but vanilla and this mod had no explicit definitions for them. This
rules out the old load rejection as evidence that rates are unsupported.
Actual collection at native levels, under customs unions and treaty exemptions
still requires the P09c runtime probe; clean loading alone cannot establish it.

Tariffs cannot distinguish synthetic coal/oil from mined fossil fuels, and
import barriers can protect domestic extraction. They carry no automatic
emissions reduction: actual production methods, staffing and heating determine
the result.

Further policy candidates (not implemented): methane-abatement standards for
mines and rigs; household heat-pump retrofit support; clean-power procurement.
Each should have a domestic cost and an explicit production/heating mechanism,
rather than another generic emissions percentage.
