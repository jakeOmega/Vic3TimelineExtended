# Demographics

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Demographics (`demographics_rule`): the census (phase 1; phase 2 steps 1, 2 and 4; phase 3: event wave, late game and Family Limitation)

A yearly census of every state's people by single year of age and by sex, shown in a Demographics tab of the Population panel, a Demographics tab of the Census Data window (any country's census; § The Census Data window) and a Demographics subtab of the state panel. It also keeps Wealth Concentration per state and computes the Gini and the national urban pattern. **From phase 2 step 4 the census sets each state's births and deaths** (under Full; § The census's births and deaths): the engine's events become the model's plus the terms added on top of the census (a state-wide one acting on the census's rate, a pop's own on its bare curve), and the ring follows what the engine did. The laws', technologies' and institutions' own birth and mortality lines are script-only and reach a state through the census or, without it, through Laws and Institutions (§ The census's births and deaths, "The moved lines"). **From phase 2 step 2 it sets each state's workforce too** (under Full): who is of working age and who works, with a pension setting, a pension bill and a health bill (§ Phase 2 step 2). Plan: `docs/superpowers/archive/plans/2026-10-10-demographics-phase2-engine-rates.md`. Two changes outside population ride with it: Wealth Concentration is now kept per state with four new target terms (a fifth, the economic laws, came with the phase 2 balance pass) (§ Wealth Concentration per state), and the tactical nuclear strike counts its soldiers among the dead. Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-demographics-design.md` (§ numbers below are its sections); plan: `docs/superpowers/archive/plans/2026-10-08-demographics-phases-0-1.md`; engine evidence: `docs/testing/demographics-probe-results-2026-10-08.md`, and the engine's own births and deaths per pop: `docs/testing/demographics-growth-probe-results-2026-10-09.md`; harness results: `docs/testing/demographics-harness-2026-10-08.md`. The model, harness and spec corrections are PR 1 (#828); the census is PR 2 (branch `demographics-phase1`).

**Built (calibration step 2):** Late Marriage and Birth Spacing, two customs that lower children per woman (§ Late Marriage and Birth Spacing).

**Built with it:** Primogeniture's farm births under Full (the late-marriage spec's call 4; the late-marriage plan, Task 9, Plan A): Late Marriage takes their place.

**Built (phase 2 step 3a):** the Family Policy measures, their buttons in the tab's Family Policy section, their upkeep and terms, and the family laws' AI weights (§ Family Policy measures). The census reads the measures' six new types into its birth and sex-ratio terms (step 3b).

**Built (phase 3, 2026-10-12):** the national urban pattern's effects and the Plan a New Capital decision, under Full only (§ The urban pattern's effects and Planned Capital).

**Built (phase 3, 2026-10-19):** the per-state metropolitan share (spec §5.2), shown under Display only and Full, its effects under Full only (§ The metropolitan share).

**Not built yet.** Phase 2 steps 1 to 4 are built (step 2, the workforce, pensions and health costs, since 2026-10-12: § Phase 2 step 2), along with calibration steps 1–3. The step 4 conscription, youth-bulge and sex-balance effects were enabled under Full by the migrant booking (2026-10-12). **Release precondition (owner, 2026-10-10, amended): nothing changes outside Full.** Step 4 of the engine-rates plan edits no engine birth or mortality line: the lines the census absorbs stay on their laws, techs and institutions, and only M cancels them, under Full. Step 5, a retune of the global defines, is dropped (owner, 2026-10-10): it would change every game, census on or off. Its only job was to keep M small so the terms on top count at the right weight; if that ever matters, it is handled inside M under Full. Wealth Concentration's effects, split by scope, are built (§ Inheritance, the state modifiers). Phase 3's urban pattern and Planned Capital, event wave, late game, and Family Limitation and Cultural Hegemony's fertility drift are built; the per-state metropolitan share is built too (§ The metropolitan share). Wealth Concentration's remaining shocks, the r > g term and the Inequality effect are built (2026-10-19: § Wealth Concentration per state, § The Inequality effect). Internal Resettlement's programme profiles and the US frontier's 1836 start were built on 2026-10-19 (§ Internal Resettlement in the census, and the US frontier). Map modes stay blocked until a per-state source exists (the `te_map_mode_*` hijack is switched off, `docs/audits/open_issues.md`).

**The late game** (approved design, 2026-10-11; **built 2026-10-12**, § Phase 3: the late game): `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-11-demographics-late-game-design.md` scopes biological age, the pension age, fertility under life extension, who gets life extension, the pension and health costs and their display as one model, and lists what phase 2 must build now so its workforce, pension and health work reads age through calendar and biological clocks rather than a fixed 65.

**Uploaded minds** (the owner's calls of 2026-10-19; **built 2026-10-19**, § Uploaded minds): `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-16-demographics-uploaded-minds-design.md`. Under Full, in a country with Mind Backups and a Mind Upload Nexus, people in longevity care facing death from illness or old age upload instead, within the Nexus's capacity, into a pool of minds beside the census; the Nexus's output follows the minds it hosts; and Mind Backups' −50% restore reaches only those in care.

**Phase 2's consequences** (design, 2026-10-12; all four steps built): `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-12-demographics-phase2-consequences-design.md` designs the workforce, pensions and health costs, the Family Policy measures, conscription, the youth bulge and sex balance in four steps, and lists what changes outside Full (the base-law fixes only). Step 1, the late game's interfaces, changing no number, is built (§ Phase 2 step 1: the interfaces; plan `docs/superpowers/archive/plans/2026-10-12-demographics-phase2-interfaces.md`), and so is step 3a, the Family Policy measures without the census reading them (§ Family Policy measures). Step 4, conscription, the youth bulge and sex balance, is built (§ Phase 2 step 4: conscription, the youth bulge and sex balance; built from the spec, owner 2026-10-12); it shipped its three effects held back behind switches, and the migrant booking (2026-10-12; § The census, "Flows") turned them on. Step 2, the workforce, pensions and health costs, is built too (§ Phase 2 step 2).

### Game rule

`demographics_rule`: `demographics_full` (default), `demographics_display_only`, `demographics_disabled`, labelled Full / Display only / Disabled under the rule name "Demographics" ("Demographics (Experimental)" until 2026-10-25). Full by default since 2026-10-25 (owner); it was off by default from 2026-10-10 while the census was experimental. Every check is written positively through `te_demog_cohorts_run` (Full or Display only) and `te_demog_effects_run` (Full), the only reads of the rule, so a save from before the rule, which stores no value for it, reads as Disabled; a negated check would start the census in a running campaign (the tax code's rule, for the same reason). 1.2.0 is the first release to ship the census (owner, 2026-10-26); 1.1.8 was cut without it. A release that ships without the census runs `scripts/release/comment_out_census_inputs.py` on its release branch (never on `main`): it comments out every line that sets one of the census's script-only modifier types, which render on tech, law and institution tooltips although the engine never applies them, along with France's Family Limitation, phase 3's census line of it and the rule itself (`test_release_census_inputs.py`). Full applies the census's births and deaths terms (§ The census's births and deaths), which take over the absorbed lines while it is on; Display only and Disabled clear them at each state's next step, and nothing changes outside Full (owner, 2026-10-10). Disabled runs no cohorts and hides the tab and subtab. It does **not** stop the pop and building walks, Wealth Concentration, the war shock or the national figure: inheritance (#822) owns those and must keep working. No setting gates Wealth Concentration's five modifiers either, the two national ones or the three per state (owner, 2026-10-09). Keys: `rule_demographics_rule`, `setting_demographics_*` (and `_desc`) in `te_game_rules_l_english.yml`.

### The census

- **Ring.** Each census state keeps `te_dg_f<k>` and `te_dg_m<k>` for k = 0–299, the birth year mod 300 (300 one-year slots, women and men apart; 150 until issue #1075, § The ring's 300 years), plus a pool (`te_dg_pf`, `te_dg_pm`, mean age `te_dg_pa`) for ages 300 and over, and `te_dg_ring` (300) to say so. A slot nobody holds has no variable, and the step skips it. **People, not shares**: values are stored in units of 1e-5, and a one-year cohort of 90-year-olds is a share near 1e-5, so its deaths would round away. `te_dg_scale` ties the ring to the engine's population and is applied lazily at the next sweep, while the display bands and class sums are scaled at once.
- **Seed.** The state's own life table times a growth factor read from a table by net reproduction rate gives the stable structure of its rates (§2.6), scaled to `state_population`; the seed's raw ring (radix 100,000) is normalised to people in two multiplications (see `scripting_best_practices.md`, § Writing a Yearly Per-Age Model in Script). Every 1836 state is seeded at game start and seeded again at its first pulse, so its first step, a year after that, compares a year's population change with a year's natural change (a step on a census taken off the pulse would compare 12 to 24 months with one year, and read the difference as migrants). A state without a census, or with one more than a year old, is seeded at its pulse. The US frontier's 1836 seeds give its men the 1840 census's ratios by age (§ Internal Resettlement in the census, and the US frontier).
- **Step** (once a year per state): `te_demog_step_begin` (the year's rates into locals, then the flows) and `te_demog_step_run` (the sweep over the ring, then the year's totals and the scale). The sweep is two guarded passes in age order (generated, `te_demog_sweep`); each age takes its group's death probability from five causes (infection and hunger, work, accidents and violence, maternal, chronic and old age), each a base schedule times a multiplier from literacy, standard of living, technology, laws and institutions; the newborn enter at 0 from fertility (§2.3: wealth, then education, child survival and urban life lower the desired number, and the means to plan a family close the gap). Flows are applied by **rate age** (last year's age), so the war window (the conscription pool's ages, 20–39 until phase 3: phase 2 step 4) and the migrant classes use that age.
- **Flows** (§2.5). War dead: the monthly pulse sums each war's `num_country_dead` for the owner, keeps the positive change, and shares it at once among the states by soldiers; a state's step takes what gathered since its last step (`te_dg_war_in`), 95% men, from the conscription pool's ages (calendar 20 or more, biological 39 or less; the women's 5% from the women of those ages; phase 2 step 4, the consequences design §4.2), so the dead cover the same months as its population change (shared at the country's yearly pulse, a state stepping in October would read March's battle dead as emigrants, then as immigrants the next year). Known kills: nuclear strikes and Violent Hostility record their dead on the state (`te_demog_note_kills`); the tactical strike now tallies the soldiers' half too, so the figure `nuclear_weapon_events.5`/`.6` print rises. Since 2026-10-19 Internal Resettlement's deaths in transit are known kills too, booked by their programme's profile (§ Internal Resettlement in the census, and the US frontier). Migration is the residual: the population change less the engine's own natural change (under Full, the trapezoid's window, § The census's births and deaths), war dead, kills and, since 2026-10-19, Internal Resettlement's known moves, which are booked by their programmes' profiles instead. The natural change is estimated in the pop walk from each pop's curves × its own multiplier, `te_demog_pop_birth_mult` / `_death_mult`. Those take the state's read plus literacy, starvation, class and workplace terms, the workplace's group reads carrying working conditions at the building's scale (`gen_demographics.py`, `engine_rate_terms`; growth probe, `docs/testing/demographics-growth-probe-results-2026-10-09.md`; the table's constants on top counted it twice until the self-check `working_conditions_read`, 2026-10-17). The state read alone made Closed Borders countries read −6 per 1,000 a year as emigrants. A residual inside 0.3% of the population is model error and becomes 0; otherwise, however large (a move over 25% in a year re-seeded the state until 2026-10-20), it is shared over migrant classes by a profile built from the destination's jobs, women's share of its workers, transport and the origin's crisis (`te_demog_crisis`: devastation, turmoil and war). Transport is a census-only type on its carriers since 2026-10-19 (modifier-types stage 3), so its tooltip says what it does: **Families Among Migrants** (`state_family_migration_add`) adds to the family kind's weight, 0.2417 before it: Paddle Steamer and Railways +0.10, Combustion Engine +0.15 (`te_demog_tech_injections.txt`), Commercial Aviation and Jet Engine Technology +0.05 each (`era_6.txt`, `era_7.txt`; the aviation pair is new, owner 2026-10-19); it was a has_technology_researched ladder from `demographics_params.py`'s table, at the same values bar the aviation pair. The labour kind's women's share is 0.1 + 0.5 × the women's job share × women's share of the state's workers ÷ 0.5 (women working as much as men), within 0.1–0.6 (`te_dg_labour_f`). Women's share of the workers is the census's own count at the last count (`te_dg_fws_wk`), else, before a count, what the sexes' rates give equal numbers of women and men (`te_demog_women_worker_share`); it also splits work deaths and feeds Late Marriage. **Until 2026-10-25** the women's-rights laws carried their own share as **Women's Work Outside the Home** (`state_female_work_share_add`, 0.1 + 0.15 to 0.38), which the labour kind, Late Marriage and, outside Full, the work-death split read, and which over Protected Class's 0.48 added up to 0.2 to the family kind's weight. It duplicated the census's own count of women at work (the participation model already reads the same laws' women's-work lines), and the families' term had no ground: women who can move on their own account don't make families move more. The line and the term went (owner, 2026-10-25); the 0.0417 the term gave No Women's Rights stayed in the families' base, so a state under that law books families as before. **The booking keeps the structure plausible** (2026-10-12; `docs/testing/demographics-migrant-booking-2026-10-12.md`): the residual is the engine's own migration (a save's per-state weekly migration statistics agree with it, in starving states too), and the engine moves whole pops, which have no age or sex, so the profile is the census's overlay. Booked the same way every year it hollowed out states that lost 2–7% of their people a year for decades (Hill Punjab 2 men per 100 women by 1870, Hyderabad 45) and piled men up where they arrived (Durango 199). So a step's profile adds to a class and sex at most 5% of its people and takes at most 3% (`MIGRANT_CAP_IN`, `_OUT`); what is left over is booked at one flat rate over every class and sex, whole families (`te_demog_migrant_rates`, generated; `demographics_model.migrant_rates`). And the labour kind's women's share moves to 0.5 as the state's men per 100 women aged 20–59 fall from 95 to 91 (departures) or rise from 105 to 115 (arrivals) (`LABOUR_BALANCE_*`; `te_demog_labour_balance`, `labour_balance_weight`). Below the cap and outside the ramps the booking is the profile's, as before. Re-walked with the run's own residual (`demographics_harness.py rebook`), the 2026-10-12 run's states stay within 90–111 men per 100 women to 1901, against 2–225.
- **Figures.** Each state stores bands (5 years, 0–4 to 85+; `te_dg_bf<b>`, `te_dg_bm<b>`) and, where anyone is past them, the people past each of the pyramid's row starts past 85 (§ The ring's 300 years), median age, age shares, dependency ratio, sex balance, children per woman, life expectancy at birth and 65, infant mortality, births and deaths per 1,000, and net migration. The country's census sums the bands, weights the rates (children per woman by women aged 15–49, life expectancy by people), counts urban people (everyone outside a rural workplace, Peasants counting as rural) and sets primacy, the effective number of cities and the pattern (Primate 40% or more, Dominant 25%, Balanced 10%, Dispersed). The player's country alone also gets the outline (`te_dg_pjf<b>`, `te_dg_pjm<b>`): its bands carried twenty years ahead, five years at a time, on its own average rates with no migration.
- **Trend arrows.** Each state and country keeps `te_dg_prev_<name>` from a step; `te_dg_stepped` is 1 when the figures came from a step and `te_dg_trend_ok` is 1 only when this census and the one before were steps. The step and the seed say which ran (the local `te_dg_from_step`), and a country has stepped when any of its states has. Until 2026-10-16 the flag read births above 0, so a step with none looked like a seed: the late game's lost births (fixed the same day) froze those states' births and deaths terms, since `te_demog_rates_refresh` skips a seed. A seed writes no births or deaths, so the first step's arrows would compare with nothing: arrows start at the second step (in an 1836 start, the country's yearly census two or three years in). The panel draws an arrow only while `te_dg_trend_ok` is 1 and hides births and deaths per 1,000 while `te_dg_stepped` is 0.
- **Lists** on the country (variable lists the panel draws, `te_demog_country_lists`): `te_dg_states` (its census states, largest first by the people their own census counted, the figure each row shows, at most 40), `te_dg_states_top` (its first ten, the States section's rows until Show All; `te_dg_states_n` counts them all), `te_dg_cities` (its three census states of the largest cities by `state_city_size_rank`). The number of effective cities is the scalar `te_dg_n_cities`; the list keeps `te_dg_cities` because the engine may keep a variable and a list in one namespace. A revolution's winner inherits `te_dg_census_year` but no list, so the civil-war repair (`inh_repair_after_civil_war`) draws them again at once.
- **History.** `te_demog_history_record` keeps a yearly sample (median age, children per woman, life expectancy, Gini, Wealth Concentration; since issue #1029 also births, deaths and net migration per 1,000 from a stepped census, each cause's share of the deaths, and three marker pips: war dead, a famine month, a demographic event) in a container per year on `te_dg_hist` for countries `te_history_country_is_tracked` accepts (the player and major powers), at most 100 years. The first yearly census overwrites the game-start sample when both fall in the same year. A famine month (`te_demog_history_note_famine`, the monthly pulse) and an event of the wave (`te_demog_history_note_event`) note their year on the country, and a famine month or an event after the country's census marks the stored sample at once.

- **Return on capital against growth** (country, `te_demog_wc_rg_term`, owner 2026-10-19; Piketty's r > g): 2 × (the real policy rate − GDP growth averaged over about five years), in percentage points, at most ±5, and 0 until the country's banking cycle has started (`te_banking_cycle_running`). Script has no logarithm, so it is the atanh series 2(z + z³/3 + z⁵/5 + z⁷/7 + z⁹/9), z = (r − 1)/(r + 1), each odd power kept as 100 zᵏ for the fixed point: within 0.0002 log points for r from 0.5 to 2, 2% short at 0.1 or 10, rising with r throughout. **A territory jump takes no sample** (owner, 2026-10-20, in place of a ±10 clamp on each year): every year's growth counts, however large, except a year in which the country's states changed (or an old save's first, with no count) and its GDP moved more than a tenth (the ratio under 0.9 or over 1.1). That year folds nothing into the average but re-bases the GDP, so next year's growth is measured on the new land. A colony's year and a real boom count; a conquest's jump doesn't. A GDP of 0 or below, this year's or last, takes no sample either and is re-based. A won revolution's winner takes the loser's count with its GDP. **The hovers show a compound annual rate**, 100 × (e^(g/100) − 1) (`te_demog_wc_g_shown`, display only, by an eight-term series with every term positive; a negative g takes the reciprocal): a halving's average reads −50% and a doubling's +100%, where the log figure reads −69 and +69. r > g itself compares the log figure. **The rate is the real one** (the build's call on the owner's "policy rate"): the engine has no nominal prices, so GDP grows in real terms, and the nominal rate (median 9.6% in a 2055 save) put 55 of 84 running cycles at +5; on the real rate the same save's high-inflation countries sit at −5 (median inflation 22%), which is what inflation does to bond fortunes. In an 1849 save the 22 running cycles average about 0. Under the simplified banking setting `te_inflation` stays 0. **The real rate it reads moves with #1084** (owner, 2026-10-20: the monetary deficit term capped and the dial's reach raised): in a 2057 save 72 of 91 countries sat at the −5 floor, most of them high-inflation microstates; at the 3–4% inflation the fix aims for, the investigation put about 47 at −5 and 34 at +5, so any Wealth Concentration calibration done before it should be re-read.

### Late Marriage and Birth Spacing

Two customs that lower children per woman (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-10-demographics-late-marriage-design.md`, approved by the owner 2026-10-11; plan `docs/superpowers/archive/plans/2026-10-11-demographics-late-marriage.md`; fit `docs/testing/demographics-late-marriage-2026-10-12.md`). Each is a share of a state's people, 0–1 (`te_dg_pr_lm`, `te_dg_pr_bs`), and multiplies children per woman by 1 − cut × share, outside the means: children per woman = wealth × natural × (1 − 0.10 × Late Marriage) × (1 − 0.20 × Birth Spacing) × (1 − means × (1 − desired)).

- **Start (geography, not culture).** Late Marriage is 1 in western, northern and central Europe and Occitania, 0.9 in north-western Iberia, 0.8 west of the Hajnal line in the east (the Baltic, Prussia, Posen, Slovenia), 0.6 in the rest of Iberia, 0.5 in Italy, 0.4 along the gradient (Mazovia to Slovakia), 0.2 in Kola and East Karelia, 0 elsewhere. Birth Spacing is 1 in Japan (Ryukyu excepted), 0.06 in China, Manchuria and Korea (the smallest share that brings China's projected peacetime growth inside +3 to +6 per 1,000), and 0 in India and elsewhere: with no raise of the wealth curve there is nothing for India's held share to cancel. A strategic region sets a value and a state region overrides it (`te_demog_pr_start_<P>`).
- **Drift.** Each census step moves a share 2.5% of the way to its target: the start less what its conditions have eroded, plus Late Marriage's drivers, within 0–1 (`te_demog_pr_target_<P>`). Contraception beyond traditional methods erodes both (the techs' `state_contraception_add` × (0.3 + 0.7 × literacy), from 0 at 0.1 to 70% at 0.3). Health care erodes Birth Spacing by up to 30%, read as the access medicine has reached above its base (`te_dg_acc` − 0.4, full at 0.5; a default flagged for the owner, 2026-10-12). Primogeniture or Undivided Farm Succession add 0.15 × the farm share to Late Marriage's target (their `state_late_marriage_farms_add` line since 2026-10-17, a census-only type on the law and on the amendment; the amendment sits only on Forced Heirship and Customary Inheritance, so the two never add to 0.30), and women's paid work adds 0.3 × the women's job share × women's share of the workers (`te_demog_women_worker_share`, § The census, Flows) ÷ 0.5 (the women's-rights laws' own share ÷ 0.48 until 2026-10-25). Since phase 3 each step also closes 0.5% of the gap to its neighbouring states' people-weighted mean, both ways (`PRACTICE_SPREAD`; one walk a step, `te_demog_practices_neighbours`, filling a local per practice), the late-marriage spec's §4.4: an edge state moves about a fifth of the way in 50 years, and in the overnight run's saves the spread moved no state's share more than 0.09 by 1887 (0.13 with straits).
- **Seed.** A state with no share (an old save's, a new or split state's, a rule switched on mid-game) takes the people-weighted mean of the other states in its state region that hold one, else its start less what its conditions have eroded. A neighbour across a border plays no part, and a census re-seed keeps the shares. A change of owner moves the people to a new state object, which carries none of the old one's variables. So each step also copies the shares onto the state region (`te_dg_pr_<P>_r`, the last state to step), and the seed takes that copy when no sibling holds a share: a whole region taken at once, by a revolution's winner say, keeps its shares. Its ring and medicine still re-seed, as before. They run wherever the census runs (Full and Display only) and reach the engine only through M, under Full.
- **The panel.** Both children-per-woman tooltips list Late marriage and Birth spacing beside the other terms, with women's mean age at first marriage (19 + 7 × the share); the Population tab's hover adds the country's figures. Option l logs the shares, their targets and the absorbed births sum.
- **The fit (2026-10-12; Stop A).** No raise passes the regional growth gate (#904's peacetime births less deaths by region), so the wealth curve stays at 6.0 and the cuts are the ones that fit children per woman best with no raise. India, Africa, the USA and Canada, the Middle East, South America and Southeast Asia stay outside their bands, which is calibration step 3's (a geographic mortality term).

### The census's births and deaths (phase 2 step 4)

- **The engine's arithmetic.** Each pop's births and deaths are its SoL curve (`bare`) × max(0, 1 + every term added together). The census's term M is M = target / bare − 1 − replaced / bare − on_top × (1 − target / bare) + q × target / bare, for births and for deaths. replaced is each pop's bare curve × the terms the engine still applies per pop that the census models (literacy's −0.1 and mild starvation's), summed by the walk into `te_dg_w_ebr` / `te_dg_w_edr` (the generated `te_demog_absorbed_walk_init` and `_pop`, under Full only). on_top is the engine's state-level lines left on top: the state read less the census's term, fast mode's and the moved lines' pairs, floored at −1, past which the engine gives no events (pollution, devastation, global warming, a decree, an event); taking it × (1 − target / bare) off M makes each act on the census's rate, as its tooltip reads (owner, 2026-10-11). A pop's own lines (severe starvation, workplace lines) stay at the bare curve's weight: one state-wide M can't scale them without moving the other pops' rates. q is the moved lines' on-top family (below), script-only, which the census adds at its rate. The refresh keeps replaced / bare as `te_dg_rep_b` / `_d`, target / bare as `te_dg_tb_*`, on_top as `te_dg_os_*` and q as `te_dg_q_*` (console option n). The target is the model's own rate (`te_dg_cbr_model`, `te_dg_cdr_model`) at today's people, in the walk's units (events a month × 100,000). M is refreshed only at each census step, so a state line that changes between steps (devastation clearing, a decaying event modifier) leaves M's share for it in force until the next step (review of #885).
- **Starvation, per pop.** The engine applies both starvation penalties × each pop's starvation value, clamp((0.4 − food security) × 2.5, 0, 1), which the save stores as `food_security={ starvation= }`: mild (`starvation_penalty`, +0.6 deaths, −0.7 births) up to 0.5, severe (`severe_starvation_penalty`, +1.0, −0.9) from 0.5 at food security 0.2 to 1 at 0. The walk took severe flat until the famine probe (2026-10-11; #894), so its famine deaths ran 1.5–1.7× the engine's and the residual booked the difference as immigrants. It now reads both in 0.05 steps of food security at each step's middle (`gen_demographics._starvation_buckets`): severe deaths +0.5625 / +0.6875 / +0.8125 / +0.9375 and births −0.50625 / −0.61875 / −0.73125 / −0.84375 at food security ≥ 0.15 / ≥ 0.10 / ≥ 0.05 / below. The model and `predict` take each pop's exact value (`demographics_model.starvation_value`). Mild is absorbed (below); severe stays on top at the bare curve's weight (owner). Evidence: `docs/testing/demographics-phase2-engine-rates-2026-10-10.md`, "The severe term".
- **The floor.** The state read less the census's, fast mode's and the moved lines' own terms, plus M, stays at or above −1, the engine's own per-pop floor (a plain pop's events reach 0 there; Q7's −3 stopped births and the growth probe's −5 deaths); when that read alone is past it, M stays at 0. A floored state sets `te_dg_rate_clamped`. The floor binds only where the absorbed per-pop lines (mild hunger's) alone pass the census's rate, or a state line takes the read past −1. M has no ceiling: target / bare is at most the model's rate over the curves' lowest, and a walk under one person at the lowest curve (`te_demog_k_rate_bare_min`, 80) leaves the terms as they are. Until 2026-10-20 the floor was −0.8 (the probes' measured −0.9 less literacy's −0.1) and M at most +3; in the owner's 2057 game the floor held about 260 late states, 1.9 billion people, at a fifth of the bare curve, so the engine killed 2.3 times the census's target (owner: "replacing something that might be wrong with something that is definitely wrong"). The engine's line from −0.9 to −1 is unmeasured: the self-check `rate_below_measured` watches it (`docs/testing/demographics-playtest.md`).
- **Applied as** a fixed-sign pair per kind, `te_demog_census_births_up`/`_down` and `te_demog_census_deaths_up`/`_down` (`te_demog_modifiers.txt`, ±1 fields, multiplier |M|, at most one of each pair), and Private Health Insurance's wealth line on `te_demog_census_wealth_up`/`_down` (multiplier |`te_dg_w`|), refreshed only by `te_demog_rates_apply`. `te_demog_rates_refresh` runs straight after the census, from the yearly state pulse and from fast mode's clock step (`te_demog_events.2`): ROOT is the state both times, as the multiplier needs. A seed leaves M alone (a seed also clears the target and the scales); Display only and Disabled clear M at the state's next step. The six carry the mod's blank `modifier_system.dds`, so the state panel hides them (by name, `gui/states_panel.gui`, and in `gui_state_hidden_modifier_count`); the birth-rate and mortality breakdowns name them "Census: Births", "Census: Deaths" and "Census: Deaths by Wealth".
- **The ring follows the engine.** Each step scales its children per woman and its five cause multipliers by how far the engine's expected events over the window ran from the target (`te_demog_on_top_scales`: bz, dz, at least −1, no events, and no ceiling since 2026-10-20, each age's probability already capped at 1; Full only, once a target exists). Births added on top are newborns. Deaths added on top fell by the model's age pattern (owner, 2026-10-10) until 2026-10-22; since, each natural cause's multiplier takes its own share of them (§ Causes of Death, "Deaths on top by cause"), so pollution's land on the old and famine's on children, and the rest (war and devastation, the census's lag, anything no tag names) still falls by the model's pattern. The step stores the model's own rates without the scales as the next target; where 1 + bz or 1 + dz is under 0.01 (the engine gave under 1% of the model's events) the last target stays, since the ring's events are too few to read it back from. Since 2026-10-20 the walk's per-pop death multiplier (`te_demog_pop_death_mult`) also takes the state's wealth line × the pop's wealth inside the floor at 0, as the engine does (probe P6), so the window's deaths, dz and the residual see what the census's pair (Private Health Insurance's line at its rate, the late game's slope) takes away; before, they ran high by the line × the mean wealth, about 0.3 of the census's rate at the slope's bound, and the residual booked the gap as immigrants. The start rate swaps the line in force for the refresh's new `te_dg_w`, on the bare deaths × wealth it reaches (`te_dg_w_edw`). A late-game state skips the calendar estimate of deaths by cause, which its own count replaces: calendar ages put an immortal state's deaths fifty times too high, which a famine's dz could take past the fixed-point range. The panel's children per woman, CBR and CDR are therefore the realized figures, and follow the window's events below; life expectancy, e65 and infant mortality stay the census's own life table, and so does the player's twenty-year outline (`te_dg_tfr_model`).
- **The window's events: the trapezoid** (owner, 2026-10-11; #894; reworked after the review of #907). The walk reads the engine's rate on the step's day, and the step books it for the whole window that ends that day. Germany's famine of 3 December 1897 was booked for all of Hannover's year to February 1898, and the residual read the deaths that never happened as 933k immigrants. Under Full the walk's tail (`te_demog_window_events`) now books the window at the mean of the engine's rate at its two ends: today's, and the rate per head the last step left in force (`te_dg_sr_b`, `_d`). The refresh stores that rate right after it sets M: the walk's rate, with its new M for the old one and without the moved lines' pairs it clears, on the bare curves those reach (`te_dg_w_eb0m`, `_ed0m`: the pops the engine doesn't floor at 0; severe starvation's births floor a pop's in a famine's lower food-security steps: below 0.10 in Germany's 1898 famine, at M about −0.28). Fast mode moves it by its own term's change. M changes only at a step and the fast term at a clock step (option k, which takes it off between steps, drops the start rate), so both ends carry them. Each end is the engine's whole rate, so every line that moved inside the window counts at the engine's own weight: severe starvation, the mild starvation M nets, a state line on every pop's bare curve, and the bare curves themselves. A first version averaged only an "on-top" share and missed the absorbed mild line and part of every state line's change. The model's own rate drifting is in neither end; it stays in dz's comparison with the target. The residual, the scales and console option n read the window (`te_dg_eb`, `te_dg_ed`). Births and deaths alike. With no start rate (the first step after a seed, a new state, an old save, fast mode's re-seed) the window is the snapshot. A refresh that sets no term (a seed) drops the start rate, so the next window takes the snapshot. A step with no births sets M like any step: `te_dg_stepped` says a step ran, not that it had births (#937's follow-up, 2026-10-16). A second pulse in one year refits M and stores the start again mid-window, as the refit itself already did. Twin: `demographics_model.window_events`; weight `WINDOW_START_WEIGHT` (0.5).

- **The moved lines** (owner, 2026-10-11; `docs/superpowers/archive/plans/2026-10-11-demographics-engine-lines-script-only.md`). - **Absorbed by the census** (the absorbed family, `state_demog_absorbed_*`): under Full the engine no longer applies these, and the census's own terms carry their effect: the SoL curves and literacy's −0.1 (per pop); the starving slope below SoL 4 (owner: the poverty term replaces it); mild starvation (per pop, its food security in the walk's 0.05 steps; owner amendment B: the poverty term was fitted without it); the health laws' mortality (access × treatment); child labour's class lines (owner: the census's smaller work term is the intended size); the women's-rights birth lines (natural fertility); the Pill's, State-Sponsored Family Planning's, Modern Vaccines', Antibiotic Mass Production's and Consumer Protection's lines (§8.4); Pro-Natalist Policy's +10% and Population Control Measures' −10% (phase 2 step 3b: the laws' force under Full is desired family size and the measures); the augmentation laws' mortality (flat chronic treatment: Medical Only +0.10, the other three +0.05; chronic cap 0.99 since 2026-10-20, 0.85 before, reaching only as far as health-care access does). The age-149 cohort and the pool's people join the last migrant class's sums; the conscription pool's sums wait for the step's count. The console's replay and benchmark re-file before they turn the census year back. `demographics_rebook.ring_from` re-files a save's ring the same way.
- **Cost:** in an ordinary game slots 150–299 stay empty, and each still costs its guards in the sweep. In the test interpreter (statements and trigger tests counted on one state with nobody past 150) a plain step rises from about 16,200 to 20,900 (+29%) and a late step from 50,500 to 58,400 (+16%), the rows past 85 included. At the spec's 1836 rate (about 1 ms a plain step) that is about 0.3 ms more per state, a fifth of a second per game year world-wide; option d measures it in game. A late-game state with people past 150 runs up to twice the occupied slots. Variables: up to 12 rows (6 pairs) per state where people pass 100, 14 shown rows and a top where the pyramid splits, as many again for the biological pyramid; up to about 300 more ring variables in a state with people past 150.

### Fast mode

A test tool for iterating on the census: `event te_debug_demog.1` option h, i or j runs demography, research and construction at 4×, 6× or 12× (`te_demog_fast_effects.txt`). It isn't for play, and nothing starts it but the console. Wars, diplomacy, laws, literacy and pop wealth keep the calendar's pace, so a fast game differs from a real one; check a result in a normal game once it looks right.

- **The clock.** `te_demog_year` reads `global_var:te_demog_clock` once the first switch creates it. The global monthly pulse advances it a year every N months (3, 2 or 1) and steps every state's census at once through the hidden state event `te_demog_events.2`; each country takes its census a day later (`te_demog_events.3`). Every window is whole calendar months, so it holds exactly N of each pop's monthly growth updates and the walk's expected births and deaths (`te_demog_step_months`) cover it. The states' own pulses are 30-day timers, which a one-month window can miss.
- **The speed-up.** `te_demog_fast_births`/`_deaths` (state, `state_birth_rate_mult`/`state_mortality_mult`) are sized at each step to (K − 1) × the state's average multiplier from the walk, less the term already applied, so its engine births and deaths run at K× rather than diluting its other terms. From phase 2 step 4 the census's last term comes off that average too and its new term goes in, both from `te_demog_rates_refresh`'s locals (it runs first in the clock step), so the engine runs K × (the census's target plus the terms on top); the term is clipped at 0. `te_demog_fast_speed` (country) adds K − 1 to research speed, technology spread and construction.
- **The trapezoid** (§ The census's births and deaths, the window's events) books the clock's window at the mean of the engine's rate at its two ends, as a yearly step books its year. The fast term is set at a clock step, where the start's rate moves by the term's change, so both ends carry the same one; and both cover the walk's months (`te_demog_step_months`), so a switch between two fast speeds is booked at the months it covered. Option k takes the term off at once, between two steps: `te_demog_fast_clear_rates` then drops the start's rate, which held the term (about K times the engine's rate after k), and that window takes the snapshot, as before.
- **Use.** In a new game on day 1: option b (census log), then h, then `observe`; archive the logs, then run `demographics_observer_report.py`. Census lines carry the clock's year, `date=` (the calendar date, which `--closed-borders` matches to saves) and `lag=` (states not at the census year; the report flags any).
- **Caveats.** The first switch re-seeds every census at the clock's first step. A later switch keeps the rings. Normal speed (k) takes the rate modifiers off at once, so the next step misreads migration once. The clock never stops, so the census year stays ahead of the calendar. Wealth Concentration and inheritance stay on the yearly pulse.

### Compatibility (§11.5)

The Demography mod models ages too, so running both applies two sets of corrections (no detection; the rule is the way out) and it replaces `states_panel.gui`. The Population panel is a new full-file override (`pops_overview.gui`), so any other mod that replaces it collides with this one. The state panel already collided with Demography; the subtab adds nothing new.
