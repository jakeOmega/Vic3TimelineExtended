# Global Warming and Resource Transition

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Global Warming (`je_global_warming`)

> See also: `docs/systems/journal_entry_systems.md` for full JE system documentation.

- **Source capture:** `gen_carbon_capture_pms.py`, via `pm_carbon_capture.py`, discovers all coal/oil-consuming source groups. Eligible stationary groups get independent 25/50/75% controls (including automation and company sites), with explicit transport/feedstock/synthetic-credit exceptions in `carbon_capture_coverage.md`. Workforce-scaled negative building emissions mirror the negative state industrial contribution; net state industry includes source capture, while the dashboard capture row counts only atmospheric removal. Managed Fossil Phaseout requires era-10 Carbon Capture and Storage and at least Tier II on eligible sources. The monthly law-consistency walk repairs a held phaseout law without its required technology.

- **Building-driven annual climate total:** `gw_state_greenhouse_gas_emissions` reads generated state mirrors of building fuel emissions minus source capture and synthetic-fuel credits, applies the state owner's industrial cut, adds the state's household fossil-heating estimate, and subtracts atmospheric removal at full credit. No gross market-goods consumption proxy remains. Household demand interpolates current buy-package heating at state `average_sol` and uses population (peasants discounted to 5%), a baseline consumption-equivalent fraction and weighted market heating supply. Green Building Codes/Renewable Investment/Fossil-Fuel Divestment cut households by 60/25/15%, stacked and floored at zero; national cuts follow the state's owner.

- A persistent journal entry (never completes: `complete = { always = no }`, `can_deactivate = no`).
- Tracks `temperature_anomaly_display` script value against a 4°C progress bar. Since Geoengineering it is the felt anomaly, the warming less the aerosol mask (§ Geoengineering); `gw_unmasked_temperature` is the warming emissions have caused. The goal is frozen at activation, which is why `goal_add_value` is `4 - temperature_anomaly_display` and not a flat `4` — see `journal_entry_systems.md` before touching it.
- Auto-activates from `is_shown_when_inactive` (game rule) + `possible` (anomaly ≥ 0.1°C), or, for a country holding a restrictive Resource Transition law, from that law alone, with the rule on or off (§ Resource Transition). There is **no** `should_be_involved` block; an earlier version of this line claimed one.
- 7 temperature tiers (negligible → apocalyptic), defined **once** in `gw_severity_text` / `gw_severity_short` (`common/customizable_localization/global_warming_custom_loc.txt`). `status_desc` is a single key that calls it.
- Uses 22 scripted buttons (`gw_*`) for the eleven climate policies, each carrying `is_ai = yes` so only the AI sees the grid. Their `possible`/`effect` bodies live in `common/scripted_triggers/global_warming_triggers.txt` and `common/scripted_effects/global_warming_effects.txt`, shared with the widget's scripted GUIs.
- **Renewable Investment's running cost (2026-10-20, owner).** The policy used to force subsidies on Renewable Energy Plants (`country_building_renewable_energy_plant_require_subsidies_bool`, retired with its modifier type and loc); a bill that moved with every plant's losses. It now costs a fixed 0.5% of GDP a year: `renewable_investment_cost_modifier` (`country_expenses_add = 1`, Budget Breakdown: Climate Policy) on the *country*, since the multiplier reads `gdp`, which a journal-entry modifier's multiplier (evaluated at the entry's scope) can't reach without an `owner` wrapper. The multiplier is `gw_renewable_investment_cost_value` (`global_warming_cost_values.txt`: `gdp` × 0.005 ÷ 52, weekly). It goes on through `gw_renewable_investment_cost_apply` from the button, the widget and treaty 109, off with `gw_effect_remove_renewable_investment`, and `gw_refresh_policy_costs_effect` (monthly, from `global_warming_events_on_action`, the single refresh site, climate rule on or off) re-applies it as GDP moves (`gw_renewable_investment_cost_apply` removes the copy it holds before adding the new one, since a same-name `add_modifier` stacks) and reconciles the two modifiers when something else added or removed the policy (the debug console, a lost entry). The policy's bonuses (reworked 2026-10-20, owner): Renewable Energy Plants +20% throughput, hydro, nuclear, fusion and renewable plants +25% construction efficiency (`building_group_bg_clean_power_construction_efficiency_add`), conventional power plants −10% throughput. Construction speed only exists per building group, so the clean plants sit in their own group, `bg_clean_power` (`extra_building_groups.txt`), a child of `bg_power`: the four buildings moved there, the coal and oil Power Plant stays in `bg_power`, and `bg_power` modifiers still reach all of them through the parent. To retune the cost, change the one constant.
- **AI policy choice** is one table, `common/script_values/global_warming_ai_values.txt`. It gives each country a climate will per policy and each policy a threshold on it. Adopt scores at or above the threshold, repeal only 15 below it, so each AI holds the policies its conditions support rather than all of them. Every will is a shared core (temperature, the environment ministry, an environmentalist in government) plus the same five signals under per-policy weights: laissez-faire, industrialists in government, the environmental movement, relative standard of living, and fossil exports. So wealth delays Climate Adaptation but speeds a carbon tax, and exporters resist divestment hardest. The weight table is in the file's header, and a test checks it against the code. Details and thresholds: `journal_entry_systems.md` → **How the AI chooses**. Calibrate with `event te_debug_gw.1` option **l** (`GW_AI_WILL` lines in `debug.log`). While available authority is negative, the eight repeal buttons for authority-costing policies also score `gw_ai_authority_deficit_repeal_weight`, so an AI in the red sheds what it can (low authority cuts bureaucracy and can start a bankruptcy cycle). The Cultural Hegemony and Colonial Empire disable buttons do the same through `te_ai_authority_deficit_repeal_weight`.
- **Player surface: the climate panels**, three widgets from `gui/journal_entry_widgets/global_warming_widget.gui` in `custom_widget_container_1/_2/_3`, handled by `common/scripted_guis/global_warming_sguis.txt` and laid out to `docs/guides/gui_style_guide.md`: an always-shown overview (tier, market role, penalty scale, a 0–4°C temperature bar showing the ten-year projection and a next-threshold line, the yearly emissions table, share and cut pies), then Mitigation Policies (open), Top Emitters (open: the five countries emitting most, with annual, share and cumulative emissions, for choosing whom a climate agreement should bind), History (open) and How Global Warming Works (collapsed). The overview replaces the status line, which prints only while the entry is inactive, and the goal bar, which the bars-on-top marker hides (#582). Each section is a type, so a tab can reuse them. The warming ladder is defined once, in `gw_disp_tier_code`. Full documentation, op table and editing rules in `journal_entry_systems.md` → **Climate Panels**; the icons (`gfx/interface/icons/gw_icons/`) are recorded in `docs/systems/global_warming_gui_icons.md`.
- **Market tab (2026-09-30; not yet seen in game).** The same sections also show in a fifth tab of the Market panel, *Climate* (`gui/market_panel.gui`, whose strip is now `te_tab_buttons_six`), under `datacontext = "[GetPlayerJournalEntry('je_global_warming')]"`: the overview, the live sections and the reference sections, then a link back to the entry. It shows on every market the panel opens. On another market, the overview's market cells (role, treaty, the market's emissions and capture, the share and cut pies) and the Mitigation Policies show that market, read from its leader as a `Country` datacontext (`MarketPanel.GetMarket.GetOwner`, through the types' `gw_market_country` block, whose default is the entry's own country; not the leader's journal entry, which did not work in game: GUI guide gotcha #35), gated on `te_market_global_warming_leader_sgui`, with Adopt and Repeal greyed on `Country.IsLocalPlayer` and the handlers' effects requiring `is_player`; the temperature, Top Emitters, History and How Global Warming Works stay the player's. Labels that say "our" switch wording on the same test (the `_ours` / `_theirs` keys). The tab is greyed until the entry is active; its tooltip lists the unlock test, `gw_entry_unlocked` (the entry's `possible` as one line). The policy rows and the section toggles work there as in the journal: their scripted GUIs root on the section's `Country`, the entry's own country. The Strategic Reserve is the sixth tab, *Reserve*, shown on the player's own market only (`strategic_reserve_system.md` §5).
- **National and market emissions:** `country_greenhouse_gas_emissions_script_value` sums a country's own state emissions for Top Emitters. `market_greenhouse_gas_emissions_script_value` sums the same formula across market members for the overview, map, history and UN; its snapshot stays on the market leader and members read it through `market_capital.owner`.
- **Carbon weights and display units:** coal weighs 2 and oil 1.74 (`common/script_values/greenhouse_gas_factors.txt`). Synthetic-fuel credits are generated from PM outputs by `gen_carbon_capture_pms.py`, with factors evaluated by the generator. Formatted emission amounts scale by 1000 and use K/M notation; snapshots, shares and temperature keep internal units. Electric Arc Process and the two substitution furnaces use 10 coal, with electricity replacing the saved coal at equal base-price cost. The Coal Mine's coal pumps and Steam Donkey burn coal that vanilla nets out of their output; `pm_emissions.NETTED_FUEL` supplies it. `pm_emissions.EXEMPT_METHODS` lists the one method whose coal/oil flow is not fuel, the Strategic Reserve hub's: it carries no emissions line and no capture controls. Production tiers are implemented; expanded regression and balance checks remain pending in `building_emissions_design.md` §0.
- **Display figures are snapshots.** Country capitals take annual national snapshots (`gw_disp_country_emis`) and add exactly those figures to both the world and national cumulative totals (`gw_disp_country_emis_cum`). Market capitals retain annual market snapshots for the overview, map, history and UN. Top Emitters ranks countries, including market members; its five capital bridges refresh monthly for players. Ten policies are national; only fossil-fuel tariffs belongs to the market leader. See `docs/systems/country_climate_policies.md` for scope, saved-game counting and tariff lifecycle.
- **History charts:** two series (`gw_temp`, `gw_share`) recorded by `common/scripted_effects/te_history_global_warming_effects.txt`. Both step once a year because that is the cadence emissions move at. Temperature is global but stored per tracked country, because `te_history_chart`'s datamodel is hard-coded to the country list — see **History Store and Charts**. No markers this wave.
- Events: `environmentalism_events.txt` — threshold events at 0.5°C, 1.0°C, 2.0°C, 3.0°C (`.1`–`.4`) and 4.0°C (`.22`, "Four Degrees", the apocalyptic tier). `.22` has no good option. The crossing lands three harms on top of the `global_warming` modifier: on the people (+10% mortality, −2 SoL), the harvest (−15% farm output) and the economy (−5% throughput). Each option is a 10-year decaying modifier made only of harms that spares one of the three and adds its own cost. Survival Footing spares the people for −20% bureaucracy, Fortress Nation spares the harvest for −10% prestige and −25% migration pull, and Carrying On spares the economy for very large radicals.
- **Climate damage sits on the country, not on a state.** `environmentalism_events.9` adds `coastal_flooding_modifier` or `coastal_relocation_modifier` to the country; no state carries a flood marker. A system that needs per-state damage reads the owner's modifier plus a state property: the Settlement Authority's Managed Retreat takes coastal states of a country carrying either (`resettlement_state_is_damaged`). Per-state climate damage is a candidate future expansion of this system.
- Cooling/reversal support: `global_warming_events_on_action` also fires one-time recovery events when temperatures decline below 4.0°C (`.23`), 3.0°C, 2.0°C, 1.0°C, 0.5°C, and 0.1°C (`.17`–`.21`). Each needs its warming event to have fired first. `.23` does not touch `.22`'s modifiers: they are the damage the crossing did, and they decay.
- `.7` (pollution scandal, "These Dark Satanic Chimneys") has weight 8 in the monthly roll, below the 10–15 of the other entries, because it needs no warming, only a level-3 coal mine or oil rig. With its five-year cooldown that is one scandal every ~24 years per qualifying country (it was ~15 at weight 15).
- **Disabled rule = no climate change.** `global_warming_update_on_action` accumulates emissions only under `global_warming_enabled`, and `global_warming_events_on_action` runs the display snapshots and the threshold events (`.1`–`.4`, `.22`, `.17`–`.21`, `.23`) only then. Its `else` holds `global_var:greenhouse_gas_emissions` at 0, which also clears the warming an older disabled-rule save built up, so every outside reader of `temperature_anomaly_display` (UN docket, election events, movements, treaty article 109) sees 0. The JE activates only for a country holding a restrictive Resource Transition law, and then shows only its Fossil Transition section (the climate sections gate on `gw_rule_enabled_sgui`); every recurring event also needs the `global_warming` modifier or warming, so none fires. `.7` (pollution scandal) is about pollution, not warming, and fires under both settings. There are no `*_no_gw_modifier` fallbacks any more.
- `.13` (climate summit) is the fallback for a world without a UN. It needs `united_nations_disabled` or no `un_founded`, because while a UN exists the accord is `un_events.17`.
- Test console: `event te_debug_gw.1` (`events/te_debug_gw_events.txt`).
- The JE sits in `je_group_internal_affairs`. A `je_group_environment` is declared in `common/journal_entry_groups/timeline_extended_je_groups.txt` and unused; moving this entry there was deliberately left out of scope.

## Geoengineering (Stratospheric Aerosol Injection)

The era 12 society technology `geoengineering` (after Environmental Movement and Globalization; era 13 spec § 7.5, call 10: some of it is possible now, so it stands for the will to use it) unlocks an eleventh climate policy, Stratospheric Aerosol Injection (key `aerosol_injection`, row "Aerosol Injection"). It carries a display marker, `country_gw_aerosol_injection_bool` (`global_warming_modifier_types.txt`), so the tech tree names the policy; it shipped without one and read as a technology that did nothing (2026-11-01). The policy's gate reads the technology, as every climate policy's does, so a grayed Adopt names it. It also carries weather modification (cloud seeding, hail suppression; owner, 2026-11-01): droughts −10% impact and duration, hailstorms −20% impact, in every state whether or not the country runs the policy. That sits below Reforestation Subsidies' −25% drought, a policy that costs, and within vanilla's 5–20% for harvest-condition technologies; against the Climate Warming modifier's +25% drought a degree it offsets about 0.4 °C, and it softens a deployer's own Weakened Monsoon. Terraforming (era 13) requires it.

- **The policy.** National, on the journal entry like the others (`aerosol_injection_modifier`: Environmental Movement radicalism +0.10, the one policy that stirs the movement; the movement's "could adopt" factors leave it out). Needs the technology and 1.0 °C beneath any haze (`gw_unmasked_temperature`, so a country can join others' injections after they have pushed the felt reading down). No authority cost and no treaty lock (Enforce Emissions Reduction binds emissions policies, and this cuts none). Its running cost is 1% of GDP a year: `aerosol_injection_cost_modifier` on the country, multiplier `gw_aerosol_injection_cost_value` (`gdp × 0.01 ÷ 52`), applied by `gw_aerosol_injection_cost_apply` (removal first) and kept in step by `gw_aerosol_injection_cost_sync` in `gw_refresh_policy_costs_effect`, Renewable Investment's pattern; Budget Breakdown: Climate Policy. The policy is mirrored in the country variable `gw_aerosol_injection_policy` and rebuilt by `gw_restore_aerosol_injection_policy_effect` on the entry's monthly pulse, so a civil war's winner keeps it (Carbon Removal Support's pattern).
- **The mask.** One world figure, `global_var:gw_g_aerosol_mask`, in degrees to 0.01 (`common/script_values/global_warming_aerosol_values.txt`, constants in its head). `temperature_anomaly_display` (`extra_script_values.txt`) is `gw_unmasked_temperature` less the mask, floored at 0 only while there is a mask, so the Climate Warming modifier, the tiers, the threshold and recurring events, the movement, elections, the UN docket and treaty 109 read the masked temperature (spec: "the harms read the masked temperature"). Emissions keep accumulating in `greenhouse_gas_emissions`. The target is the deployers' share of world GDP × 5 °C (`gw_aerosol_degrees_per_world_gdp`: a fifth of world GDP masks a degree), capped at 2 °C (`gw_aerosol_max_mask`) and at the unmasked warming (`gw_aerosol_ceiling`). World GDP and the deployers' GDP ride `gw_refresh_global_counts_effect`'s sweep (`gw_g_gdp_sum`, `gw_g_aerosol_gdp`, `gw_g_n_aerosol_injection`). `gw_aerosol_update_mask_effect`, the one writer, runs right after it in `global_warming_events_on_action` under the rule: the stored target (`gw_g_aerosol_target`), then the mask moved `gw_aerosol_mask_rate` (0.08) of the gap, at least 0.01 so the rounded mask always moves (63% in a year, 95% in three). With the rule off, `gw_aerosol_clear_mask_effect` removes both.
- **Termination shock.** When the deployers fall to 0 with at least 0.25 °C masked (`gw_aerosol_shock_min_mask`), the update fires `environmentalism_events.24` for every non-decentralized country, once until someone flies it again (`gw_g_aerosol_shock_fired`). The masked warming returns as fast as it built; the event's one option adds `gw_termination_shock_modifier` (decaying, five years, multiplier the mask returning: farms −5%, heatwave impact and duration +25%, drought impact +25% a degree), the speed of the change on top of the warming.
- **The free driver and its objectors.** One country can cool everyone. `gw_aerosol_objects` (country): not running it, and a capital in a monsoon region (`gw_country_in_monsoon_region`: North and South India, the Himalayas, Indochina, Indonesia, South China, West Africa) or an AI will a band below its threshold (`gw_ai_repeal_margin_aerosol_injection > 0`). An objector loses 10 relations with a country that starts it (`gw_effect_aerosol_injection`) and 5 more each January (the update), both `NOT = { THIS = deployer }`-guarded.
- **Weakened Monsoon.** `gw_aerosol_monsoon_modifier` on the entry of a monsoon-region country, re-applied monthly on the entry's pulse with multiplier = the mask (−5% farms, drought impact and duration +25% a degree: half the farm relief and none of the drought relief the mask brings there). Like the coastal damage, it reads the capital, not each state.
- **Cooling events and the AI read the unmasked warming.** `gw_fire_cooling_threshold_event` compares `gw_unmasked_temperature`: the recovery events tell of emissions under control, which a haze is not. The AI's shared urgency (`gw_ai_will_urgency`) reads it too, so a deployer whose own mask works does not repeal (a degree of mask is 20 points, more than the 15-point band) and re-adopt after the shock. Aerosol Injection's row flips the signs: laissez-faire +10, industrialists +8, fuel exports +10, wealth +5, movement −20; threshold 60. The AI does not start it in default or once the mask is at its ceiling (`gw_aerosol_has_room`), and stops it in default.
- **Panel.** The policy row (placeholder icon, `docs/systems/global_warming_gui_icons.md`), Around the World's count, an Aerosol Mask line in the overview's emissions table while there is a mask or a deployer (`gw_aerosol_shown_sgui`; hover: the warming beneath, the deployers and the target), and a Geoengineering note in How Global Warming Works. The ten-year projection still assumes the mask stays as it is.
- **Not built (spec candidates).** No UN docket item on geoengineering: objection runs through relations alone. Weakened Monsoon is the regional side effect.
- **Debug:** `event te_debug_gw.1`, option **j** adopts it with the rest, **n** runs twelve monthly steps of the mask (`GW_AEROSOL` in `debug.log`). **l** logs its will (`aerosol=`).
- **Tests:** `test_gw_geoengineering.py` (the wiring, and the monthly step run in Python); the policy counts in `test_global_warming_layout.py`, `test_gw_ai_policy_table.py`, `test_gw_policy_tech_gates.py`. Play-test list: `docs/testing/era-13-playtest.md` § Phase 3: Geoengineering.

## Resource Transition (`lawgroup_resource_transition`, #660)

- **Laws** (`common/laws/resource_transition_laws.txt`, Economy group beside the Ministry of the Environment): Unrestricted Extraction (default; **first in the file**, which is what an unseeded country and an old save get), Fossil Expansion Moratorium (Environmental Movement + the ministry, `unlocking_laws`), Managed Fossil Phaseout (Clean Energy Technologies + the ministry, and `can_enact` the ministry funded to level 3). One technology per law, as agreed in the issue. `gen_law_consistency` drops a restrictive law back to Unrestricted Extraction when the ministry is abolished; a later cut in funding does not repeal the phaseout. No law carries a modifier, and none touches emissions: the laws work through supply (the issue forbids an emissions bonus). **Text:** each law's description is flavor (a short paragraph, no figures: the enactment popup's box does not scroll); the rules are `on_enact` `custom_tooltip` lines (`rt_law_moratorium_tt`, `rt_law_phaseout_tt`, `rt_law_phaseout_rule_tt`, `rt_law_emissions_tt`), the way Collective Governance shows its Distribution of Power amendment, and the Fossil Transition section's law-line hover repeats the same lines (`rt_law_effects`). `test_resource_transition.py` pins the pairing.
- **Construction ban** on `building_coal_mine`, `building_oil_rig` and `building_power_plant` (every fuel method of the power plant burns coal or oil): `can_build_government`, `can_build_private` and `should_auto_expand` in their `INJECT:` blocks in `common/buildings/extra_buildings.txt`, all reading `rt_fossil_construction_allowed_here` (state scope, the law of the state's owner, so foreign investment is covered). `should_auto_expand` overrides the group's, so it repeats `default_auto_expand_rule = yes`. Unverified in game: what happens to levels already queued when the law passes.
- **Out of scope, on purpose:** hydro, nuclear, fusion and renewable plants; the synthetics plants; every other mine. Two edge cases found in the audit and left out: `building_graphite_mine` runs the coal mine's production methods and so outputs the coal good, and the whaling station outputs oil; neither is a fossil-fuel building in fiction. Company buildings that dig coal (Anzin Colliery and three others) or pump oil (Aramco Abqaiq and three others), and the coal-burning company power stations, are capped at a level or two by their company's prosperity, so they are no construction bypass. No live mod script creates or expands the three buildings: `expand_building_private_construction` can start a power plant, but its only caller chain (`expand_random_building` ← `queue_more_private_investment`) is never called, and whether `start_privately_funded_building_construction` honours `can_build_private` is unverified.
- **Retirement programmes** (Managed Fossil Phaseout only): one per industry, toggled in the Fossil Transition section or by the AI's `rt_start_/rt_stop_<key>_button` (both through `rt_possible_start` / `rt_possible_stop`, `common/scripted_triggers/resource_transition_triggers.txt`). A running programme is the country variable `rt_prog_<key>` holding months since its last closure; `rt_country_monthly_update` (`common/scripted_effects/resource_transition_effects.txt`, from `on_monthly_pulse_country` in `common/on_actions/resource_transition_on_actions.txt`, not the JE's pulse) counts it and every `RT_RETIRE_INTERVAL_MONTHS` (3) runs `rt_retire_smallest`: the state whose building has the fewest levels (`ordered_scope_state` on the negative level) loses the whole building (`remove_building`; vanilla's `law_extraction_economy` dismantles private industry the same way), the country pays `RT_COMPENSATION_SHARE` (0.25) of its construction value (levels × `bt:<type>.required_construction` × `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_SERVICES_PRICE` × `te_construction_market_goods_per_point`), the state gets `rt_transition_assistance` for 5 years (−50% radicals and loyalists from SoL change, +25% qualifications), and a player gets a feed notice (`common/messages/resource_transition_messages.txt`). Losing the phaseout stops every programme (the two other laws' `on_activate` and the monthly pulse). Programme state is variables only, so a civil war's winner carries it. Compensation leaves the treasury and reaches no owner, domestic or foreign; the loc says so. Unverified in game: removal of company- and foreign-owned buildings, and of a building with levels under construction.
- **Panel:** the Fossil Transition section of `je_global_warming` and the Market panel's Climate tab (`journal_entry_systems.md` → Climate Panels). The game concept `concept_fossil_transition` explains the system wherever the laws and the section name it; the phaseout's description carries the decision-time facts (whole building, any owner, a quarter of build cost from the treasury, no emissions cut by itself), and each Start button's tooltip the next and total compensation. Figures are the monthly `rt_d_*` snapshot for players, read by the guarded `rt_disp_*` values in `common/script_values/resource_transition_values.txt`.
- **AI** (`resource_transition_values.txt`, `resource_transition_buttons.txt`): the moratorium's weight is global warming's divestment will above 50 (`gw_ai_will_fossil_fuel_divestment`, so fuel exporters and laissez-faire markets resist hardest), with no fuel shortage in the market and a quarter of the power fleet clean; the phaseout's needs the moratorium, the will above 65 and half the fleet clean. Under a restrictive law, a market short of coal, oil or electricity weighs Unrestricted Extraction +400. A programme starts only when the market makes 1.25× the coal (oil) it burns and the good is neither short nor 10% above base price (power plants: 60% of generating levels clean and electricity not short or dear), and the country is not in default with debt under half its ceiling; it stops when the good (electricity) goes short or 40% above base, or the country defaults. IG stances mirror each ideology's Ministry of the Environment stance (`transition_constructor` in `ideology_modifications.py`; Environmentalists and Corporate by hand in `extra_ideologies.txt`). With the Global Warming rule off the will has no temperature term, so the AI rarely adopts either law.
- **Tests:** `test_resource_transition.py`; the panel's layout in `test_global_warming_layout.py`.
