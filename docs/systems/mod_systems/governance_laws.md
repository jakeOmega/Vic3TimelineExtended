# Governance, Bureaucracy and Policing Laws

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Collective Governance (`law_direct_democracy`)

The governance principle `law_direct_democracy` is displayed as **Collective Governance**: no individual holds supreme executive power. The key is historical (the law was Direct Democracy) and kept because renaming a law breaks saves. Spec: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-09-26-collective-governance-design.md`.

- **What it means depends on Distribution of Power**, expressed as one amendment on the law per group: the voting laws → *Direct Democracy* (the old referendum package, with a soft movement gate), Single-Party State → *Collective Leadership*, Technocracy → *Collegial Administration*, Anarchy → *Free Federation*, Oligarchy/Organic Regulation → *Patrician Council*, Algorithmic Governance → *Algorithmic Commons*. Autocracy can't hold the law. The law itself carries only the "no head" base (legitimacy from broad coalitions, less authority, slower enactment).
- **Soft movement gate (2026-10).** Direct Democracy and Free Federation used to carry `country_must_have_movement_to_enact_laws_bool`, which blocks every law no movement backs. Four mod law groups (Privacy Rights, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Consumer Protection, Urban Planning) have no movement stance at all, and the likeliest reading of "backs" is comparative (the movement rates the law above the current one), so under Anarchy the anarchist movement, already content, backs nothing and every law and exit needed a movement hostile to the regime. Both now carry a flat `country_law_enactment_success_add = -0.1` instead. A movement backing a law adds its support to the law's success chance natively (`LAW_ENACTMENT_MOVEMENT_SUPPORT_PASS_EFFECT = 1.0`; its activism can't drop below the 25% it needs while the law is enacted), so backed laws make up the 10% and unbacked laws pass slowly, or can't start under 11% success from the interest groups (`LAW_MIN_SUCCESS_CHANCE = 0.01`). Direct Democracy's +25% success went with the gate. Free Federation adds `state_loyalists_from_political_movements_mult = 0.5` (25% → 37.5% of a backing movement's supporters turn loyal on passage, `MOVEMENT_LOYALISTS_ON_APPROVED_ENACTMENT`) and `country_bolster_cost_mult = -0.5` (offsets Anarchy's −50% authority for bolstering). `test_no_movement_gate` keeps the bool off the law and every amendment. No modifier can make the gate per law: `political_movement_enact_support_mult` exists only in vanilla loc, and the movement-support defines are global.
- **Script-attached, never negotiated.** `te_refresh_collective_governance_amendment` (`collective_governance_effects.txt`) keeps exactly the matching amendment, derived from current laws on every call: the law's `on_activate` (hidden), `on_law_activated` (after the consistency cascade) and the monthly country pulse. The amendments' `possible` and `can_repeal` ask the same triggers (`collective_governance_triggers.txt`), so the player can't repeal the one their Distribution of Power calls for.
- **Preview:** the law's `on_enact` names the amendment the current Distribution of Power will give (the language-reform pattern).
- **Government types** (`timeline_extended_governments.txt`) run most specific first, because the first match wins: Noble Commonwealth (Oligarchy + Feudal Contracts) before Patrician Council, with `gov_collective_governance` as the catch-all. Algorithmic Commons comes before `gov_algorithmic_directorate`, which keys on Algorithmic Governance alone.
- **"Democracy" readings** (the liberal-democratic cultural-hegemony model, the referendum events `extra_law_events.24/.60/.84`) also require `collective_governance_is_popular`.
- **Every allowed Distribution of Power law must be holdable with it**, down the chain of laws it needs. Anarchy needs Cooperative Ownership, which vanilla unlocks only with Council Republic or Corporate State, so `common/laws/modified.txt` adds `law_direct_democracy` to its `unlocking_laws`. Before that, an anarchist Council Republic that enacted the law lost Cooperative Ownership to the consistency walk, then Anarchy, Collectivized Agriculture and Possession by Use. `test_gen_law_consistency.PrerequisiteHoldableTest` walks the chain for every law's prerequisites.
- **Adding a Distribution of Power law:** add it to its row of `EXPRESSIONS` in `test_collective_governance.py` (or add a row); the failures are the checklist.

## Bureaucracy Laws: Who Staffs the Administration (`law_contracted_administration`, `law_communal_administration`, `amendment_spoils_system`)

Beside vanilla's Hereditary, Appointed and Elected Bureaucrats and Automated Bureaucracy (next section), two human-staffed bureaucracy laws (`common/laws/extra_laws.txt`) and one amendment (`common/amendments/extra_amendments.txt`). Each law's identity is who staffs the Government Administrations: its own PM in vanilla's `pmg_government_administration_bureaucrat_professionalism`, which replaces Professional Bureaucrats while the law holds (`INJECT:pm_professional_bureaucrats` `disallowing_laws` lists all three mod laws), plus clout. They avoid Neocameralism's and Devolution's levers, so they stack with those laws without doubling up. Spec §7–12: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-01-bureaucracy-laws-design.md`.

- **Contracted Administration** (Supply Chain Management; `unlocking_laws` Laissez-Faire or Interventionism; disallowed by Council Republic and Algorithmic Governance): capitalists +15% and bureaucrats −25% political strength, +25% institution size change speed, +5% institution bureaucracy cost (contract overruns), no Legislative Override Capacity. PM `pm_service_contractors`: 250 bureaucrats a level plus 20 Services, a local good, so a state with few Urban Centers pays more and can go short.
- **Communal Administration** (no technology; `unlocking_laws` Council Republic, Anarchy or Collective Governance; disallowed by Technocracy, Elder Council and Algorithmic Governance, Elected's vanilla exclusions plus machine rule): bureaucrats −25%, Trade Unions and Rural Folk +10% political strength, −5% movement radicalism, −10% tax capacity, −2 Legislative Override Capacity. PM `pm_recallable_delegates`: 250 bureaucrats and 250 clerks a level.
- **Progressiveness and the consistency walk.** Contracted is −2: below 0 keeps Appointed as Elected's fallback, and above −5 makes Appointed, not Automated (−10), Contracted's own (−5 ties and goes to the ideology tiebreak). Communal is 75, so a Council Republic or Collective Governance that loses Elected lands on Communal, and losing Communal falls back to Elected, else Appointed. Re-read `te_fix_inconsistent_lawgroup_bureaucracy` after changing any bureaucracy law's progressiveness or constraints.
- **Spoils System** (`amendment_spoils_system`): parent Appointed, allowed on Appointed and Elected (the US starts with Elected), −10% tax capacity, sponsor +15% political strength. Sponsored by IGs in government or led by a Jacksonian Democrat, and offered by `extra_law_events.87` ("To the Victor Belong the Spoils", debate checkpoint) while either law is enacted, sponsored by the governing IG with the most clout. Vanilla's `amendment_preserved_bureaucratic_caste` is the template and is left unchanged.
- **Adding a bureaucracy law:** `disallowing_laws = { law_algorithmic_governance }` (or machine rule evicts it), a progressiveness checked against the cascade, its PM in the professionalism group plus the `pm_professional_bureaucrats` INJECT, Appointed's two IG-shift `on_enact` tooltips, an inline Legislative Override Capacity value or a comment saying why none, ideology stances, loc, a law icon (a `law` entry in `scripts/image_pipeline/icon_prompts.py`), the player guide.

## Machine Administration and Machine Rule (`law_automated_bureaucracy`, `law_algorithmic_governance`)

Bureaucracy decides who administers the state; Distribution of Power decides who rules it. **Automated Bureaucracy** (Bureaucracy, AI Agents) hands administration to machines under human authority, and any government can hold it. **Algorithmic Governance** (Distribution of Power, Artificial Intelligence) hands the machines the authority itself. Spec: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-01-bureaucracy-laws-design.md`. The human-staffed laws beside it are in the Bureaucracy Laws section above.

- **Automated Bureaucracy:** −20% institution bureaucracy cost, +50% institution size change speed, −25% bureaucrat and +15% engineer political strength, +1 Legislative Override Capacity. Progressiveness −10, so `gen_law_consistency` still moves a country that loses Elected Bureaucrats to Appointed. Its staffing is `pm_automated_casework` in vanilla's `pmg_government_administration_bureaucrat_professionalism`: 150 bureaucrats and 100 engineers a level instead of 500 bureaucrats, plus Digital Access and Digital Assets. That PM replaces Professional Bureaucrats while the law holds (`disallowing_laws` INJECTed on `pm_professional_bureaucrats`), as vanilla's Hereditary Bureaucrats PM does.
- **Install and lock.** Algorithmic Governance's `on_activate` enacts Automated Bureaucracy. Appointed and Hereditary Bureaucrats (and the Crownland Diets variant) get `disallowing_laws = { law_algorithmic_governance }` (`common/laws/modified.txt`), Contracted and Communal Administration carry it in their own definitions, and Elected needs a voting law, so Automated is the only valid bureaucracy law under machine rule. Don't put `unlocking_laws = { law_automated_bureaucracy }` on Algorithmic Governance: Distribution of Power is fixed before Bureaucracy in the consistency walk, so any bureaucracy change would evict the governing law, and old saves would lose it.
- **Algorithmic Governance** keeps no administrative effects. Its legitimacy is a flat `country_legitimacy_base_add = 70` on the law plus `algorithmic_mandate` (`sol_expectations_modifiers.txt`): +1 base legitimacy times `algorithmic_mandate_value`, which is 5 × the cached SoL expectations gap, capped at ±40. The base is there because every other Distribution of Power law carries legitimacy of its own (clout 25–120, votes 40–110, Elder Council a flat 20) and this one has no clout, votes or head-of-state term: with the mandate alone, a Presidential Republic on very low taxes sat at 42 (Unstable Government) with the mandate at its old +25 cap, and a first fix of base 50 with the cap unchanged still topped out at 92 there. Base plus cap is sized for headroom, not for the neutral case: 70 + 40 = 110 before taxes (`tax_modifier_*`: +10 very low, +5 low, 0 medium, −10 high, −20 very high), the head of state's group and timed modifiers. At the +40 cap a Presidential Republic reaches 100 through High taxes and 97 at Very High; at +25 (a gap of 5) it reaches 100 through Medium and falls to 92 and 82. A country on a steady standard of living sits near 70 plus those terms. The tooltip on enactment (`ALGORITHMIC_GOVERNANCE_TT_MANDATE`) explains the mandate and quotes the 5 and the 40; `test_algorithmic_mandate.py` pins them to the script value. The mandate is re-applied from `sol_expectations_monthly_on_action` right after the update caches the gap; that is its only refresh site, and the law's `on_deactivate` removes it. The gap is zero once expectations catch up, so the mandate pays for improvement and costs for decline, not for a level of wealth. Together with the doubled radicals and loyalists from SoL change, a falling standard of living is what threatens machine rule.
- **Events:** `society_technology_events.27` (AI Agents, which unlocks the law; Generative AI until the era 13 spec) and `post_scarcity_events.3` belong to Automated Bureaucracy. `extra_law_events.83`, the dispute over who has the last word, is Automated Bureaucracy's enactment event, and `.23`/`.59` fire for either law.

## Policing: Private Policing and Its Amendments (`law_private_policing`, `amendment_corporate_security_powers`, `amendment_civilian_police_oversight`, #628)

`lawgroup_policing` answers who provides ordinary enforcement, who pays and whom it answers to. Beside vanilla's No Police, Local Police Force (public constabularies under local notables), Dedicated Police Force and Militarized Police Force, the mod adds one law (`common/laws/extra_laws.txt`) and two amendments (`common/amendments/extra_amendments.txt`). Internal Security (regime protection) and Criminal Justice (punishment and rehabilitation) are separate groups. The group's and the four vanilla laws' descriptions are overridden in `localization/english/replace/` to say who pays and answers. Every effect is aggregate: coverage that follows money is modelled within states (strata standard of living) rather than as a per-state modifier, because every state has rich and poor districts (owner call). Spec, with the decision on every candidate in the issue: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-04-policing-laws-design.md`.

- **Private Policing** (Computer Networks; `unlocking_laws` Laissez-Faire or Interventionism; disallowed by Council Republic). Keeps `institution_police` (displayed as Ministry of Public Safety) at −40% institution cost, vanilla Private Schools' and Private Health Insurance's value. Per investment level: upper strata +0.25 and middle strata +0.1 SoL, `state_turmoil_effects_mult` +0.05 (half the institution's own −0.1), `goods_output_services_mult` −0.01 (owners' funding: the guards are service workers, so Services buyers pay through prices; an investment-pool cut was rejected as too harsh on the corporate economies most likely to enact it). Flat: Trade Unions −10% political strength, Labor Movement pop attraction +15%, Legislative Override Capacity −0.5 (No Police's value). No IG clout bonus per level: the issue warned against moving Local Police's landowner bonus to the Industrialists.
- **Who favours it.** Only Corpocrat (strongly approve) and Corporate Rule (approve); `ai_will_do` needs a Corpocrat ruler (Neocameralism's rule). Market ideologies are left neutral. The disapproving vanilla ideologies are in `ideology_modifications.py` (`private_policing_*`). Every ideology with a Policing block states a stance on it (`ideology_lawgroup_audit`), and one the block is new to also states the four base-game police laws.
- **Consistency walk.** Progressiveness 25 equals Dedicated Police Force, so `te_fix_inconsistent_lawgroup_policing` moves a country that loses its market economy or becomes a Council Republic to Dedicated Police Force (nationalization; the shared institution keeps its investment), or No Police without Law Enforcement.
- **Corporate Security Powers:** parent Private Policing (so the same corpocrat-only stance), allowed on Local, Dedicated and Militarized Police Force; Corporate Charters and not Command Economy, Cooperative Ownership or Industry Banned; −10% police institution cost, Trade Unions −10%, Labor Movement +15%, sponsor +10% political strength. Sponsored by the Industrialists or any IG approving the parent, and offered by `extra_law_events.89` ("Guards at the Mill Gate", debate checkpoint) with the Industrialists as sponsor. Not allowed on Private Policing, which already delegates the whole force.
- **Civilian Oversight:** parent Guaranteed Liberties, allowed on Local, Dedicated and Private Policing (not Militarized); Civil Rights Movement; +10% police institution cost, Legislative Override Capacity −0.5, radicalism increases −10% for Second-Rate Citizen and Open Prejudice pops, turmoil effects −5%, sponsor +10% political strength. The 2026-10-17 rebalance replaced Civil Rights Movement pop attraction −15%, which worked against the reforming governments that adopt it (spec §3). Sponsored by the Intelligentsia or any IG approving the parent, and offered by `extra_law_events.90` ("Who Watches the Watchmen", debate checkpoint) with the Intelligentsia as sponsor.

## Penal Labor Camps (`law_penal_labor_camps`)

The authoritarian end of `lawgroup_criminal_justice` (`common/laws/extra_laws.txt`), beside Punishment-Focused (progressiveness 0, the default), Restorative (25, `behaviorism`) and Rehabilitation-Focused (50, `mental_health_awareness`).

- **Gate:** `mass_surveillance` plus one of `law_autocracy` / `law_bakufu` / `law_neo_absolutism` / `law_single_party_state` / `law_outlawed_dissent`. The two Autocracy variants are listed because `unlocking_laws` matches exact keys. A country that loses every gate is moved to Punishment-Focused by the generated `te_fix_inconsistent_lawgroup_criminal_justice` (closest progressiveness; the law is -50). The sweep runs from `on_law_activated`, so when the replacing Distribution of Power or Free Speech law activates, and on the monthly country pulse. `cultural_hegemony.16` never presses the camps on a country: its `activate_law` would skip the gate.
- **Modifier:** +100 authority, -10 legitimacy, -0.10 suppression attraction factor (Shinsengumi's value; vanilla multiplies a suppressed movement's attraction by -0.5 plus the factor, so under Outlawed Dissent, itself -0.5, suppression goes from -1.0 to -1.1 × base), +10% civil-rights movement support, and a flat +5% throughput for mines, logging camps, plantations and rubber plantations. The throughput is deliberately flat (owner call): nothing scales it with radicals, turmoil or prisoners. There is no activism cut: repression feeds the opposition as a cost, so the movement levers are the civil-rights support and the movement stances below.
- **Interest groups:** by ideology stance only (`ideology_modifications.py`; the six custom-religion society ideologies in `extra_ideologies.txt`). Liberal, progressive and moderate criminal-justice ideologies strongly disapprove (so the Intelligentsia and Trade Unions); regressive ones approve, one step below their Punishment-Focused stance, so they don't push for camps. The ideologies that rank the camps above Punishment-Focused, and so push for them, are the fascist leader and movement (strongly approve) and the authoritarian leader (approve); none has a Punishment-Focused stance. The totalitarian society strongly approves both laws, and the imperial-cult and theocratic societies approve both, so they are indifferent between them. The liberal, radical and anarchist movement ideologies strongly disapprove, which is how the law feeds those movements. `test_penal_labor_law.py` pins that every ideology with a Criminal Justice stance has one on the camps.
- **Where else it is listed:** the UN human-rights violator test (`un_regime_human_rights_violator`) and the human-rights vote lean, refusal and withdrawal weights beside Outlawed Dissent; the Mental Health JE's fail condition and the punitive AI weights beside Punishment-Focused (`mental_health_events.3`, `social_tensions_events.5`).
- **Not listed** in the reform-debate enactment events (`extra_law_events.12/.30/.48/.72`) or either Criminal Justice amendment, which are written for the reform laws and Punishment-Focused.

## Frontier AI Control (`lawgroup_frontier_ai_control`)

Who builds the most capable AI systems: the power structure third of the era 13 trio (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-era-13-technology-tree-design.md` § 7.1, call 12), beside Bodily Autonomy and Automation Policy. Laws in `common/laws/te_frontier_ai_laws.txt`, group in `extra_law_groups.txt` (Internet Governance's weights: opinion impact 0.25, carried by regime change). **Keep the keys:** Superintelligence (spec § 3.5) reads this group as a country's stance on racing for capability or investing in alignment.

- **Opening.** `law_no_frontier_ai_policy` is first in the file (an unseeded country's and an old save's law) and seeded for every country in `extra_history.txt`. `te_frontier_ai_fix_law` (`te_frontier_ai_effects.txt`, called from `fix_incompatible_laws`: every law change, every acquired technology, the yearly country pulse) moves a holder with AI Agents to `law_private_ai_labs`, the default, as No Internet Policy moves to Unregulated Internet. The placeholder's `can_enact` (no AI Agents) closes it afterwards; the five other laws need `ai_agents`.
- **The trade.** Innovation (`country_weekly_innovation_mult`, never the cap: § Calibrated modifier budgets) against exposure to covert technology theft (`country_covert_defense_economic_add`, the axis Industrial Espionage meets), authority and intelligence capacity:

  | Law | Progressiveness | Lines | Backers |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Private Labs | 50 | innovation +10%, economic covert defense −10, Industrialists +10% political strength | Industrialists (market) |
  | National Licensing | 40 | innovation +5%, economic covert defense +5, intelligence capacity +1 | most groups' second choice, the Devout's and Trade Unions' first |
  | State Monopoly | 25 | innovation −5%, authority +100, intelligence capacity +3, economic covert defense +10, Armed Forces +10% political strength | Armed Forces (military, statist) |
  | International Oversight | 65 | innovation +5%, diplomatic reputation +2, UN Authority Target +0.5 | internationalists, liberals |
  | Open Weights | 80 | technology spread +10%, economic covert defense −10, Intelligentsia +10% pop attraction; the world share below | Intelligentsia (liberal) |

  Figures borrow their neighbors': ±10 covert defense is No IP and Strict IP Protection's, +10% spread Open Source Innovation's, +2 reputation War Crimes Forbidden's.
- **International Oversight** needs UN membership while held (`te_frontier_ai_un_member`: the membership modifier or its civil-war mirror, `te_frontier_ai_triggers.txt`). `can_enact` checks it at enactment, and `is_visible` hides the law with the United Nations rule off. Leaving and dissolution run `un_membership_end_effect`, whose last line (`te_frontier_ai_on_un_membership_end`) drops the law to National Licensing at once; `un_leave_organisation` shows that line in the leave button's tooltip, since the membership block sits inside a `custom_tooltip`. Any other way out of the UN (absorption, a released country inheriting the law) is caught by `te_frontier_ai_fix_law`. Its UN Authority Target grant is pinned in `test_un_policy_pillar.py` and listed in `un_redesign_design.md` § 0.11.
- **Open Weights' world share.** The global monthly pulse (`te_frontier_ai_on_actions.txt`) counts holders of major power rank or greater into `global_var:te_frontier_ai_open_weights_count`; `te_frontier_ai_open_weights_spread` (`te_frontier_ai_values.txt`) reads it at 100 each, up to 300, and is the last line of `global_tech_spread_script_value`. A read, not a sweep: that value already sweeps 55 technologies across the world. The law's `on_enact` tooltip quotes the 100 and the 300.
- **Consistency walk.** No law has `unlocking_laws` or `disallowing_laws`, so `gen_law_consistency` emits no helper for the group. The placeholder is closed with `can_enact`, not Internet Governance's `disallowing_laws`, so it stays a constraint-free, technology-free fallback should a later law gain a constraint; its progressiveness (0) keeps it furthest from the five real laws in a cascade. The group is in `LAWGROUP_PRIORITY` (tier 3) for that day.
- **Ideologies.** Every block names all six laws (placeholder neutral): `FRONTIER_AI_LISTS` and `FRONTIER_AI_STANCES` in `ideology_modifications.py` for 76 vanilla ideologies, and the same lists by hand in `extra_ideologies.txt` for nine mod ideologies. `test_frontier_ai_control.py` pins the lists and checks that the Armed Forces', Industrialists' and Intelligentsia's ideologies peak at State Monopoly, Private Labs and Open Weights.
- **Icons:** all six borrow other laws' icons (`# Placeholder Icon`), with unreviewed `law` prompts in `scripts/image_pipeline/icon_prompts.py`.
