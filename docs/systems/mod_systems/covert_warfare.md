# Covert Warfare

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Covert Warfare System

> Supersedes the earlier *Information Warfare (Cyber Power)* design (`je_information_warfare`, `cyber_*` diplomatic actions, Digital Sovereignty), which was removed from the mod in `45cd7d8`. Nothing from that design remains in script.

**Purpose:** Adds an espionage/covert operations layer to the Cold War+ era. Countries can run covert operations against rivals (election interference, sabotage, espionage, etc.) using pact-based diplomatic actions. Operations consume operation slots, cost GDP-scaled expenses, and (all but the one that supports a friend) carry detection risk. A target that catches an operation may expose it or let it run and watch it; being caught spying on a friend costs the attacker at home. Design for the 2026-10-16 additions: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-16-covert-counterintel-backlash-design.md`; their play-test list: `docs/testing/covert-counterintel-playtest.md`.

**Gate:** `has_game_rule = covert_warfare_enabled` (shows the entry). It activates once `covert_operation_max_slots` is one above the rank baseline: 4 for a great power, 3 for a major power, 2 for anyone else (`je_covert_warfare.txt` `possible`). Slots (`country_covert_operation_slot_add`) come from the base 1 every country has (`base_values`), the rank bonus (great power +2, major power +1, `extra_country_ranks.txt`), four technologies (`mainframe_computers`, `computer_networks`, `cyber_warfare`, `quantum_computing`, +1 each) and the Ministry of Intelligence and Security institution (`law_ministry_of_intelligence_and_security`, +1 per level). No technology gates the entry directly; the first extra slot usually comes from `mainframe_computers` or the ministry.

**Technology gates (2026-10).** Seven operations need a technology to launch, a `has_technology_researched` `custom_tooltip` first in their `possible` (`covert_op_tech_<type>_tt`; the action's `_desc` and `concept_covert_operations_desc` name it too): influence campaign `mass_media` and comms disruption `cryptography` (era 6); election interference `television_broadcasting`, ideological subversion `pop_culture` and regime change `satellite_communications` (era 7); financial subversion `computer_networks` (era 8); destabilization `social_media` (era 9). The aim is that the operations which can break a great power sit where a small country can't rush them before the great powers have built services of their own: vanilla's ahead-of-time penalty charges for every skipped earlier-era tech in the same tree, and `satellite_communications`, the deepest era-7 node (through `nuclear_weapons`, `ICBMs` and `space_exploration`), costs about 425k innovation from the end of era 5, as much as era 9's `social_media` (400k); `computer_networks` costs 685k. The rest stay ungated: cultivate assets, Counterintelligence Support (defence shouldn't wait on technology its protector may not need), both technology espionage types (technology theft is the laggard's catch-up; their gate is having something to steal, § Technology theft), infrastructure sabotage (needs a war or play), and space espionage, nuclear sabotage, secure loose material and the two colony operations, which have gates of their own. Launch only, like the Tradecraft unlocks. `test_covert_tech_gates.py` holds the table, and every registry type must be either gated or listed `UNGATED`.

**What a launch shows (2026-10-27).** Each operation's diplomatic action keeps one name, the operation's (`iw_op_name_<type>` spliced after "Covert: "), and its `accept_effect` tooltip ends with `covert_op_launch_summary`: our network inside that target and the head start it gives (`covert_launch_net_tt`, or `covert_launch_net_none_tt`), the weekly upkeep the operation adds at priority 1 at today's funding (`covert_launch_added_upkeep`), then the detection warning. The phase line before it is one sentence naming `concept_operation_phase`, which carries head start, priority and exposure cost for the tooltip, the rows and How Covert Warfare Works alike.

**Military tab (2026-09-30; not yet seen in game).** The panels also show in a fifth tab of the Military panel, *Covert* (`gui/panel_military.gui`), under `datacontext = "[GetPlayerJournalEntry('je_covert_warfare')]"`: the overview, the live sections and How Covert Warfare Works, then a link back to the entry. The tab is greyed until the entry is active; its tooltip lists the unlock test, `covert_warfare_entry_unlocked` (`covert_warfare_triggers.txt`: the entry's `possible` as one line, a verbatim copy held to it by `MilitaryCovertTabTest`). The Funding stepper, the priority steppers, Stand down and the collapse toggles work there as in the journal: their scripted GUIs root on `JournalEntry.GetCountry`.

### System Architecture
- **Operations as Pacts:** Each operation type is a togglable diplomatic action (like `increase_relations`). Effects applied monthly via JE `on_monthly_pulse`.
- **Operation state as script containers (1.13.10+):** Each running operation is one container tagged `iw_op` + `iw_op_<TYPE>`, parented to the operator country, holding `iw_target` (country), `iw_duration` (months), and JE display values (`iw_target_capital`, `iw_detect`, `iw_tgt_ic`, `iw_tgt_td`; since 2026-10-27 `iw_exposure_tier`, set by `covert_op_refresh_exposure_tier` from the tier triggers, and `iw_detect`'s terms `iw_det_*`, see **Detection**), plus `iw_known` while its target watches it and `iw_catch_pending` while its target is deciding (§ Watching a caught operation). The operator lists them in its `iw_ops` variable list, and every loop walks that list instead of `every_container`. Pacts remain the source of truth: `accept_effect` calls `covert_op_start`, `manual_break_effect`/`auto_break_effect` call `covert_op_end`, the detection event's `covert_op_burn` removes the pact and container together, and `covert_ops_sync_all` runs at the top of the monthly pulse to create containers for pacts that lack one (month 0) and destroy containers whose pact is gone. Saves made before containers keep their pacts, which restart at month 0; their old `iw_*_<type>_<n>` slot variables are left inert (clearing them would log "used but never set" warnings on every load).
- **Per-type cap:** `covert_ops_type_below_cap` compares the live pact count against `covert_ops_max_per_type` (1, +1 `mainframe_computers`, +1 `cyber_warfare`, +1 at Veteran Tradecraft — slice 5). That script value is the only place the cap lives; storage has no per-type limit.
- **Phases:** `covert_op_is_established` (`covert_op_effective_duration >= covert_op_phase_2_month`, 6) and `covert_op_is_fully_operational` (`>= covert_op_phase_3_month`, 12), where the effective duration is `iw_duration` plus the whole-month head start the target's network gave the operation at creation (`iw_net_head_start`, slice 4; missing reads as 0), evaluated on the container. The two named values are the only copy of the thresholds: the triggers and `covert_op_refresh_phase` test them, and the loc prints them (the launch tooltip's phase line, `concept_operation_phase`; 2026-10-27). `covert_op_refresh_phase` projects them onto `iw_phase` (1/2/3) and `iw_phase_months_left` for display, from the tail of `covert_ops_age_all_durations` (immediately after the monthly `+1`, so they are never a month stale) and once from `covert_op_create`. Target-side effects use each operation's own phase; self-side effects use the operation with the largest phase × priority product (see **Priority** below).
- **Priority (2026-09, covert slice 3):** each container carries `iw_priority`, 1–3 (missing reads as 1); written by `covert_op_create` and backfilled by `covert_op_sync` before `covert_op_refresh_detection` runs, so an old-save container never reaches detection scaling without one. Priority multiplies what an operation does, what it costs and how exposed it is:

  | priority | effect × | upkeep weight | raw detection | slots filled | reached by |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 1 | 1 | +0 | 1 | default; every new and every pre-slice-3 operation |
  | 2 | 1.65 | 1.6 | +2 points | 2 | stepper, or AI when rivalled with the target |
  | 3 | 2 | 2.4 | +4 points | 3 | stepper, or a great-power AI at war with the target |

  **Slots (2026-10-31, owner):** an operation fills one operation slot per level of its priority, so raising one competes with launching another. `covert_operation_slots_used` (country) is the pact count plus each `iw_ops` container's `iw_priority − 1` (a pact the sync has not yet given a container counts 1; uncapped, so an agency over its slots reads no free one), and `covert_operations_available_slots` subtracts it from `covert_operation_max_slots`. Every step up needs a free slot (`covert_possible_priority_up`, `iw_priority_needs_free_slot_tt`), which binds the stepper and the AI alike; stepping down is never gated, and losing slots leaves operations at their priorities, as it leaves operations over the count running. The count is live, so a step frees or fills its slot on the click, and the AI's second step in a month sees its first. Per slot a new operation buys more than a priority step (×1 against +0.65 and +0.35); priority is for the target that matters most, once the per-type cap or the targets run out. The overview's slot icons and `je_iw_slots_detail` count slots filled (`covert_slots_used_display`); the unused-slot counterintelligence bonus (`iw_domestic_defense`) reads the free slots, so it falls as priority rises. `test_covert_priority.SlotTests`.

  Effect multiplies the phase multiplier (establishing = 1, fully operational = `covert_op_phase_full_mult` = 2 — the literal `multiplier = 2` this slice retired), so the six live multipliers are 1 / 1.65 / 2 establishing and 2 / 3.3 / 4 fully operational (×1.35 / ×1.6 until 2026-10-31, when priority began to fill slots and the owner made priority 3 double the effect); preparatory stays at no effect whatever the priority. Every apply site goes through `covert_op_add_scaled_modifier`, which branches phase × priority onto six **constant** script values `covert_op_mult_p{2,3}_pri{1,2,3}` — constant because `add_modifier`'s `multiplier` is re-evaluated against ROOT on later ticks, so a value that reads the operation's container cannot be one. Self-side modifiers now come from the operation with the largest `covert_op_effect_mult` (phase × priority, 0 while preparatory) via `ordered_in_list … position = 0` in `covert_op_apply_self_effect`, not merely the best phase as before. Espionage's technology theft is the exception: it runs for every established operation, not the strongest (§ Technology theft, 2026-10; it replaced military espionage's spread modifier, which the strongest operation whose target was ahead used to carry). Destabilization's movement pressure rides on its one country modifier, `covert_destabilization_resist` (+20 % activism and +20 % attraction at base, reaching every movement from the country as vanilla's home-affairs institution modifiers do); until 2026-09-25 the same totals were split across a second country modifier and a per-movement one, now retired (PENDING REMOVAL in `legacy_modifier_cleanup.txt`). Ideological subversion's movement pressure (`ch_apply_hegemon_movement_pressure`) scales by priority only, through the establishing-phase column `covert_op_mult_p2_pri{1,2,3}` at every phase — it was already phase-flat, so this slice didn't add a phase axis to it. The election-interference confidence hit (`covert_op_election_confidence_effect`, `-0.05`/`-0.1` by phase) is **not** priority-scaled: it is evaluated from the target's side over every attacker's operation with a literal `add_electoral_confidence`, unlike the operation's own `covert_election_interference` modifier, which is scaled normally.

  The row stepper is two fail-closed scripted GUIs (`covert_priority_up_sgui` / `covert_priority_down_sgui`), each taking the row's container as the saved scope `iw_op` (`AddScope('iw_op', ScriptContainer.MakeScope)`) — an engine link with no vanilla precedent, unproven at the time of writing; both handlers gate on `covert_priority_op_is_ours` (`exists`, `iw_op` tag, membership of the country's own `iw_ops`) so the worst case if the link fails is a permanently greyed stepper, never a change to the wrong operation. **Named contingency, do not build unless the in-game check fails:** replace the two handlers with per-type handlers in the stand-down shape (18 handlers, one per type per direction). **When a step takes hold:** detection moves on the click (the step re-runs the sync, which re-stages every operation's detection figure); raising priority also raises upkeep on the click; the effect modifiers follow only at the next monthly pulse, because re-applying them per click would re-roll infrastructure sabotage's `random_scope_state` pick onto a fresh target state every time. Upkeep is charged at the **higher** of the current priority and the priority the running effects were applied at — `covert_ops_apply_all_phase_effects` snapshots `iw_priority` into the container's `iw_priority_applied` before applying and calls `covert_refresh_priority_cost` after, which weights each operation through `covert_op_charged_priority_at_least` — so lowering priority lowers upkeep only once the effects have followed. Without that, raising to 3 just before the pulse and lowering the day after would buy a month of ×1.6 effect for a day of 2.4-share upkeep.

  Doc and console-harness detail: `journal_entry_systems.md` § Covert Warfare → Panels; `docs/superpowers/archive/plans/2026-09-22-covert-per-op-priority.md` § Stepper.

  AI rule: `covert_ai_manage_priorities`, run for every `is_player = no` country before `covert_ops_sync_all` in the monthly pulse, sets a per-operation wanted level — 1 in default or bankruptcy, 3 for a great power at war with the target, 2 against a rival, else 1 — and steps `iw_priority` toward it at most twice per direction per month through the same `covert_possible_priority_up`/`_down` gates the stepper uses.
- **JE display:** no `status_desc` since the style-guide pass (2026-09-29): the five-tier intelligence-standing verdict is the overview's standing icon's tooltip and the empty-state pointer is the Operations section's. The overview is `widget_je_covert_overview` (`custom_widget_container_1`); the live sections are `widget_je_covert_status` (`_2`), whose Operations rows' datamodel is `JournalEntry.GetCountry.MakeScope.GetList('iw_ops')` and Agent Networks rows' `GetList('iw_nets')`; How Covert Warfare Works is `widget_je_covert_reference` (`_3`, below the buttons). Full layout, op table and editing rules: `journal_entry_systems.md` § Covert Warfare → Panels.
- **Intelligence Capacity (IC):** Base 5 (from `INJECT:base_values`) + rank bonus (GP +25, Major +10 from `INJECT:country_ranks`) + literacy component (`literacy_rate × intelligence_capacity_literacy_max`, i.e. × 50) + GDP component (`gdp / global_gdp × 100 × intelligence_capacity_gdp_points_per_percent` (2), capped at `intelligence_capacity_gdp_max` = 50, a quarter of the world economy) + modifiers (`country_intelligence_capacity_add`), the whole sum then scaled by `1 + country_intelligence_capacity_mult`.
  - **Scale (2026-10).** Rank and GDP share are what give a large country's counterintelligence an edge over a small, literate one's (literacy is up to 50 for anyone). They were +10 / +5 and one point per percent capped at 25, which left a great power only about 2 detection points ahead of a literate minor; they were raised alongside the technology gates. The covert defences (`country_covert_defense_*_add`) were scaled ×5 at the same time (laws ±1–2 → ±5–10, the education principle tiers 5 / 10, Heightened Vigilance 25, the intelligence-sharing tip-off 10 and failure −5, the UN's shared intelligence 5 per step and electoral observers 15 per strength, the cultural exchange article −5): they add to a capacity of ~100–160, so ±1–2 moved detection by a fraction of a point. The detection formula, its factor (15) and cap (20) are unchanged on purpose, so a fully funded agency, which costs as much as a large army, still hides from almost anyone. Worked example (era 8; shared purchasable capacity 49; Britain 85 % literacy and 8 % of world GDP, the US 90 % and 22 %, Sweden 95 % and 0.8 %), a Swedish operation's base detection: against Britain 12 % → 15 % at funding 1, 4 % → 7 % at funding 3, 0.1 % at funding 5 either way; against the US 14 % → 20 %, 6 % → 12 %, 0.1 % → 0.9 %. Side effects: a great power now reaches the cap of the Intelligence Capacity modifier (`intelligence_capacity_modifier_mult`, separatism and coup resistance, full at 125) sooner, and the two AI launch checks that compare capacities (election interference, financial subversion) rarely let a minor target a great power.
- **Operation Slots:** Single modifier-driven value: `modifier:country_covert_operation_slot_add`. Base 1 (`INJECT:base_values`) + rank bonus (GP +2, Major +1) + tech modifiers (`mainframe_computers`, `computer_networks`, `cyber_warfare`, `quantum_computing`, +1 each) + the Ministry of Intelligence and Security institution (+1 per level). Capped at 10. An operation fills one per level of its priority (`covert_operation_slots_used`, **Priority** above).
- **Funding:** **6 levels**, 0 – `iw_funding_level_max` (= 5): 0 Dormant, 1 Operational, 2 Professional (named Tradecraft before slice 5), 3 Covert Network, 4 Black Budget, 5 Deep State. **Level 0 does not idle operations, it ends them** — every covert action's `requirement_to_maintain` demands `iw_funding_level >= 1`, so dropping to 0 lapses every running pact (which is why the AI's own funding branch floors at 1 while it has operations, and why the widget's decrease control warns before the step). Cumulative detection reduction and counterintelligence IC by level: L2 −3 % / +5, L3 −8 % / +10, L4 −13 % / +15, L5 −19 % / +20, applied through the `iw_funding_defense` static modifier scaled by `covert_ops_funding_ci_mult` (= level − 1). The widget reads these from `covert_funding_detect_reduction_at_N` / `covert_funding_ci_ic_at_N`, which are sums of the Section 1 constants rather than a second copy of the numbers.
- **Cost:** `country_expenses_add` with GDP-scaled multiplier: `(Σ priority weights + 1) × funding_level × covert_operations_cost_scale` (`gdp × 0.00005`). The sum is `iw_priority_cost_sum`, refreshed by `covert_refresh_priority_cost` from `covert_op_create`, `covert_op_destroy`, the tail of `covert_ops_sync_all`, every priority step (`covert_apply_priority_change`), and the end of `covert_ops_apply_all_phase_effects`; it falls back to the pact count (`covert_operations_active`, the pre-slice-3 formula) for a save loaded before the first refresh. Each operation contributes 1 / 1.6 / 2.4 depending on its own priority — or rather the higher of its current priority and the one its running effects were applied at (`iw_priority_applied`), so raising charges more on the click while lowering charges less only from the next pulse, when the effects follow (the pulse re-refreshes the sum after applying; see **Priority** above) — example: three operations at priority 1 cost 4 units; raising one to priority 3 costs 5.4 units, +35 % total upkeep for +100 % on that one operation's effect (and two more slots), the diminishing return that keeps "everything at 3" from being the answer. The `+1` ensures a base maintenance cost even with 0 active operations (you pay for defensive IC benefits like counterintelligence). Cost modifier applies whenever `iw_funding_level >= 1`, regardless of active op count.
- **Influence (2026-10-12):** on top of the money, each operation's pact `cost` is influence by exposure tier: mild 25, moderate 50, severe 100, war 0 (the engine scales pact costs by rank: ×2 great power, ×1.5 recognized major). Vanilla's `orchestrate_coup` and `support_separatism` cost 100. The AI launches a costed operation only through `covert_ai_can_spare_influence` (can afford the pact and holds `covert_ai_influence_floor`, 300), and the breakable operations' `will_break` also fires, once settled, while influence is in deficit; the floor sits above what any break frees, so a dropped operation is not relaunched at once. `test_international_influence_costs.py` holds the tier table.
- **Detection:** each running operation carries its own monthly chance, `iw_detect` = `(base 10 − funding stealth − network cover + target counterintelligence penalty + their network inside us + (priority − 1) × covert_op_priority_detect_add) × covert_detection_efficiency_factor (1 − covert efficiency, floored at 0.2) × covert_ops_detection_multi_op_scale` (scale default 1) — the priority term (2026-09, covert slice 3) adds raw points before efficiency, so a busier operation is more exposed but better tradecraft still hides most of it: at base 10 % with no efficiency, priority 1/2/3 reads 10/12/14 %, and at the late-game efficiency floor (0.2) the +4 raw points becomes +0.8. The priority is staged per operation onto the operator as `iw_priority_staging` by `covert_op_refresh_detection` (from `PREV.var:iw_priority` on the container — reading `scope:iw_op.var:iw_priority` directly isn't valid) before `covert_operation_detection_chance` is computed, then removed, clamped to a max of `covert_ops_detection_ceiling` = 50 % and a floor of `covert_ops_detection_floor` = 0.1 % (2026-09, covert slice 2) — reachable at high funding against a target with no counterintelligence, an "almost with impunity" but never "safe" floor; the operation row shows the chance to one decimal (`|1`, not `|0`) so that 0.1 % doesn't render as "0%" and read as safe — refreshed by `covert_ops_sync_all` at the top of the pulse. The refresh also copies the terms onto the container (`iw_det_ci`, `iw_det_their_net`, `iw_det_net`, `iw_det_prio`, `iw_det_fund`, `iw_det_eff`, staged on the operator like the rest, from the same values the chance adds), so the row's risk hover is a table that adds up to `iw_detect` (2026-10-27). Display only: the roll reads `iw_detect`. `covert_ops_roll_detection_all` (last in the pulse) rolls every hostile operation (`covert_op_is_hostile`) that its target is not already watching (`iw_known`) or deciding on (`iw_catch_pending`) against `covert_op_roll_chance` (= `iw_detect` directly — the multi-op scale is already folded in above, so the widget row and the roll agree by construction), collects the successes in a temporary list and catches **at most one** per month, chosen at random among them, with the container in `scope:iw_burned_op`. Nothing is rolled while an exposure of that attacker is in flight (`covert_exposure_in_flight`). The catch goes to its target first (§ Watching a caught operation); an exposure fires `covert_warfare.1`, only ever through `covert_exposure_fire`. With N equal operations at c each, P(any burn) = 1 − (1 − c)^N: 10 % → 19 % at two, 27 % at three. ### Per-Target Networks (2026-09, covert slice 4)
- **What:** every country an operator has run operations against gets a persistent network, 0 – `covert_net_max` (100), that outlives the operations. One script container per (operator, target), tagged `iw_net`, parented to the operator, listed in the operator's `iw_nets`; vars `iw_target`, `iw_target_capital`, `iw_net_strength`, `iw_net_ops`, `iw_net_trend` (1 growing / 2 decaying / 0 holding at max), `iw_net_head_start_offer`. Type-agnostic for hostile types: adding one adds no network site (a friendly type is excluded by `covert_op_is_hostile`, § Counterintelligence Support).
- **Monthly pass:** `covert_nets_sync`, called **only** from the JE pulse, between `covert_ops_sync_all` and `covert_ops_age_all_durations` — never from `covert_ops_sync_all` itself, which also runs on every funding and priority click and would grow networks per click. It (1) creates a network for any hostile operation's target without one, and for the attacker of every operation we watch (§ Watching a caught operation), (2) counts hostile operations per network by zeroing `iw_net_ops` and walking `iw_ops` once, then sums what the operations we watch add (`iw_net_watch_gain`), (3) grows each network with ≥ 1 operation, or any watch gain, by `covert_net_gain_base` × (1 + 0.5 × (ops − 1), ops counted to 3) × `covert_net_gain_scale` (1 below 50, falling to 0.1 at 100 — the `un_standing_gain_scale` shape), or decays it by `covert_net_decay_base` = 1.5 a month (halved at funding ≥ `covert_net_maintain_funding_level` = 3 — there is deliberately no "maintain presence" pact; funding is the paid lever and the AI already manages it), and (4) reaps networks whose target is no longer alive (`is_country_alive`) or that sit at 0 with no operations and nothing watched (`iw_net_watch_gain <= 0`). A network is also created by `covert_op_create`, so the row appears the day the first operation starts.
- **Consumers:** (a) **head start** — `covert_op_create` copies the network's `iw_net_head_start_offer` (`covert_net_head_start_value` = strength × 0.05, rounded, capped at 5) onto the new operation as `iw_net_head_start`, fixed for its life; the phase triggers and `covert_op_refresh_phase` measure `covert_op_effective_duration`. The cap is under 6, so the preparatory phase is never skipped: a full network puts a new operation one month from establishing. (b) **cover** — `covert_op_refresh_detection` stages the target network's strength on the operator as `iw_net_strength_staging` (0 without one), and `covert_operation_detection_chance` subtracts `covert_net_detect_reduction` = strength × 0.05 raw points (−5 at full) after the funding terms and before efficiency. The staging happens inside `covert_ops_sync_all`, before the monthly network pass, so detection uses last month's strength — a one-month lag, accepted. (c) **burn** — `covert_op_burn` takes `covert_net_burn_loss` = 25 off the target's network (unscaled — about a year of single-operation rebuilding).
- **Counterintelligence (2026-10):** a network also works against its target's operations. `covert_op_refresh_detection` looks in the *target's* `iw_nets` for the network whose `iw_target` is the operator (zeroed first, the write done inside the `random_in_list`, like the cover lookup), stages its strength on the operator as `iw_tgt_net_strength_staging`, and `covert_operation_detection_chance` adds `covert_net_catch_penalty` = strength × `covert_net_catch_factor` (0.1, so +10 at full) raw points after the target's counterintelligence penalty and before efficiency. Twice the cover factor on purpose: a penetrated service is the decisive counterintelligence advantage, and +10 is the one term that lifts a fully funded operation (raw −9 against an equal) off the 0.1 floor; two services holding full networks inside each other each run +5 on balance. A target without the journal entry has no networks (they come from its own operations, which need funding ≥ 1, or from a retaliation seed, which option b gates on the entry), so the lookup misses. The network row's country hover shows the figure (`je_iw_net_row_catch` inside `je_iw_net_row_tt`, `covert_net_catch_display` in network scope); the operator's row tooltip only says a network of theirs may sharpen their counterintelligence and prints no number for it. The row's `iw_detect` does include the term, so a player who works the published formula against the inputs the tooltip and overview print can infer the leftover — a weaker side channel to the defender rule than the tier-2 count, accepted (owner, 2026-10-07: a vague line, no number). AI: `covert_warfare.2` option c (expel) takes ×0.2 when the defender's network inside the perpetrator has reached intelligence tier 1 (`iw_net_intel_tier >= 1`, strength 50), because the expulsion is answered in kind and would cost it 25 of that network. Console: `event te_debug_covert.2` option k (every target gets a full network inside us).
- **Lookup rule:** every "network for target X" lookup does its work **inside** the `random_in_list` (`limit = { var:iw_target ?= X }`), never through a saved scope afterwards. Several run inside loops, where an unmatched pick would leave the previous iteration's network in a saved scope, and several sit on paths the engine also walks to render tooltips (the steppers' `covert_refresh_funding_state`, `covert_warfare.1`'s `after`), where a saved scope may be unset. `iw_net_head_start_offer` is restated by `covert_net_clamp` after every strength write, so a burn shrinks the head start a same-day relaunch gets.
- **Projection** (months from 0): strength 25 in 12.5 / 8.3 / 6.3 months with 1 / 2 / 3 operations; 50 in 25 / 16.7 / 12.5; 75 in 42 / 28 / 21; 90 in 65 / 44 / 33. Decay from 50 with no operations: 33 months (67 at funding ≥ 3).
- **Console:** `event te_debug_covert.2` options i (every network to full strength) and j (one network pass now).

### Network intelligence (2026-09, covert slice 7)
- **What:** a network strong enough reports on the service it has penetrated — display only, nothing in script reads the report back. Tier 1 (strength ≥ `covert_net_intel_tier_1_strength`, 50): the target's intelligence capacity and technology count, each printed beside our own. Tier 2 (≥ `covert_net_intel_tier_2_strength`, 75): also how many covert operations the target is running against us. With one operation a network reaches tier 1 in ~25 months and tier 2 in ~42; cultivate assets alone in ~17 / ~28.
- **The defender rule's one exception:** nothing else tells a country about hostile operations it has not caught (`covert_op_burn`'s header). The tier-2 count is that exception on purpose — it is earned by holding a strong network inside the other service, not given to the defender — and it says how many, never which operations or what they do.
- **Tier code:** `covert_net_intel_tier_value` (network scope) is the only place the two thresholds are compared; `covert_net_clamp` writes it to `iw_net_intel_tier` (0 / 1 / 2) after every strength write, beside the head-start offer, so a burn that drops a network below 75 or 50 hides that part of the report the same day. `covert_net_create` seeds 0. The widget reads only the code.
- **Report:** `covert_net_refresh_intel` (network scope, needs `scope:iw_net_operator`), called from `covert_nets_sync` step 3 straight after the strength write and from the console harness — never from a tooltip-walked path, because it uses saved scopes. At tier ≥ 1 it writes `iw_net_intel_ic` (the target's `intelligence_capacity_total`) and `iw_net_intel_techs` (`techs_researched`); at tier 2, `iw_net_intel_ops`, counted by accumulation over the target's hostile covert pacts (`is_hostile_covert_operation_pact`: Counterintelligence Support is not an operation against us) with `first_country` = target and `second_country` = us. Pacts, not the target's `iw_ops`: the containers only sync while the target's own journal entry is active. Every phase counts. Below a tier its values stay stored but hidden. Strength only rises in the monthly pass (or the harness), which refreshes right after, so a tier is never shown with values that were not written at it. Our own figures are live (`intelligence_capacity_total`, `covert_techs_researched_display`); theirs are last month's report.
- **Network row, not operation row:** the spec said "operation/network row". The report sits on the network row because the network outlives its operations. The operation row's detection line already printed the target's capacity (`iw_tgt_ic`) as a detection input before this slice, and still does, but only while an operation is running there.
- **Old saves:** a network from before this slice has no `iw_net_intel_tier` until its first monthly pass; every report line is gated on `ScriptContainer.HasVariable('iw_net_intel_tier')`, so for that month the row shows none.
- **Console:** `event te_debug_covert.5` — a: every network to 50; b: to 80; c: every network's target starts cultivate assets against us (target funding floored at 1) and the reports refresh, so a tier-2 row's count goes up by one. `te_debug_covert.2` option i (strength 100) now refreshes the report too.

### New operations (2026-09, covert slice 6)

AI: a major power or above proposes it against any target with a loose line (score 5, +5 for a neighbour; evaluation 0.05).

Both need that rule (`sr_colonies_contested_enabled` in `potential`, so the actions are absent otherwise) and a target that holds a colony (`sr_has_space_colony`, `iw_target_holds_space_colony_tt`), in `possible` and as a `requirement_to_maintain`, so an operation ends by itself when the target's last colony goes. AI: a fleet power (`sr_fleet_power`) against a rival or an enemy holding a colony, not a friend; it breaks when no longer a fleet power, when hostility is gone outside a war, or in an influence deficit.

### Technology theft (2026-10)
- **What:** every industrial espionage operation (production technologies) and military espionage operation (military technologies) that is established steals each month on its own, from its own target: `covert_ops_steal_tech_all` (`covert_warfare_effects.txt`, called from `covert_ops_apply_all_phase_effects`). Progress goes to our current research if it is in the category, the target has it and we hold its prerequisites (a save from before a prerequisite was added can be researching without one, and the engine refuses that progress: #1244), else to a random technology in the category that the target has, we lack and `can_research`, else nowhere. It replaced the +15 % production and military technology-spread modifiers (`covert_industrial_espionage`, `covert_military_espionage`: kept defined so saves load cleanly and stripped monthly by `legacy_modifier_cleanup.txt`, PENDING REMOVAL one release after 2026-10-06). Design: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-06-covert-tech-theft-design.md`.
- **Theft only, while there is something to steal (2026-10-07, owner's call):** the theft is all either type gives its operator; the target still carries its −3 authority marker. Industrial espionage's +5 weekly innovation (`covert_espionage_base`) and military espionage's +5 % unit offense and defense (`covert_military_espionage_unit`, which helped against every enemy, not only the target) are retired the same way as the spread modifiers (PENDING REMOVAL one release after 2026-10-07). Both types launch (`possible`) and run (a `requirement_to_maintain`, as space programme espionage does with its target's lead) only while `covert_tech_stealable_<category> = { TARGET = scope:target_country }` holds, so an operation ends by itself once the target has nothing left we could take, the player's and the AI's alike; the AI's `will_break` is `always = no`. Before this only industrial espionage had a launch gate, and neither had a keep gate (the AI's `will_break` was the only one), so a player could launch military espionage anywhere and keep either running with nothing to steal.
- **Amount:** `covert_tech_theft_share` (0.05) × the technology's era cost (`common/technology/eras/00_eras.txt`) × the operation's phase × priority multiplier: 5 % at base, 10 % fully operational, 20 % at priority 3. `add_technology_progress` takes a literal only, so `scripts/generators/gen_covert_tech_theft.py` writes the amounts: twelve `covert_tech_grant_era_N`, each branching on phase × priority the way `covert_op_add_scaled_modifier` does.

The name comes from `type = container` custom loc called as `[ScriptContainer.GetCustom('covert_stolen_tech_name')]`, the mod's first use of either; confirmed rendering in game on 2026-10-06 (owner's check after #780).

### Friends and domestic backlash (2026-10-16)
- **Friend** (`covert_is_friend = { TARGET = X }`, `covert_warfare_triggers.txt`): the one definition every rule below asks. Relations with X at least cordial (20) **and** a tie (treaty alliance, defensive pact, the same power bloc, guarded by `is_in_power_bloc = yes` so two blocless countries don't match, or one the other's overlord or subject, through `is_subject_of`, which also counts a subject's subject), **or** relations at least amicable (50) with no tie. A tie never counts alone: two members of one bloc who dislike each other are not friends. Relations are read as `X.relations:this`, not `:root`, so the trigger answers from any country scope and the same from either side (the defender's backlash calls it with ROOT the attacker). Consumers: the attacker's backlash, the defender's backlash scaling, the AI's watch odds, Counterintelligence Support's `possible` and `requirement_to_maintain`, and the AI's launch and break checks.
- **The relations block is a cost.** The gates that refused six operations at good relations are gone: amicable on election interference, financial subversion, infrastructure sabotage, communications disruption and ideological subversion (`iw_no_good_relations_tt`), cordial on destabilization (`iw_no_cordial_relations_tt`, and its second `requirement_to_maintain`, which ended it at cordial). Any operation may be run against a friend, and a catch costs the attacker at home and abroad. The intelligence-sharing treaty gate (`iw_no_intel_sharing_tt`) stays on every hostile operation: it is a treaty obligation, not the relations block. Each hostile action's launch preview adds `covert_friend_target_warning_tt` when the target is a friend (an `if = { limit = { covert_is_friend … } custom_tooltip }` in `accept_effect`, after the detection warning). The AI side is under § AI Behavior.
- **Attacker's home backlash:** `covert_backlash_home = { SHARE = full|half }` is the last line of each `covert_warfare.1` option: Acknowledge pays the full share, Deny half. A gate, then a dispatch (`covert_backlash_home_$SHARE$`) onto two internals that hold the literals. It does nothing unless `iw_burned_friend = 1` (copied in `.1`'s `immediate` from `covert_is_friend` against the target, with the other `iw_burned_*` copies, removed in `after`) and the exposure is not costless (`covert_exposure_is_costless`). Tier columns are mild / moderate / severe; a war-tier code that is not costless (caught in the run-up to war) takes the severe values, and an unrecognised code the moderate:

  | share | appeasement | legitimacy (`covert_friend_spying_scandal` multiplier) |
  |---|---|---|
  | full (Acknowledge) | −3 / −5 / −8 | −3 / −5 / −8 |
  | half (Deny) | −2 / −3 / −4 | −2 / −3 / −4 |

  Appeasement: the `lobby_pro_country` with the target as its `target`, or `lobby_pro_overlord` when the target is the overlord of the lobby's country (`covert_backlash_pro_lobby_of`, the one definition both halves use; at most one lobby matches, since a country has one lobby of a type per target and the two types exclude each other), pinned in `.1`'s `immediate` as `scope:iw_home_lobby` and changed through that scope so the option's appeasement line names it (vanilla's `CHANGE_APPEASEMENT_THIRD`, as its lobby events show it), through `change_appeasement` with the factor `appeasement_covert_spying_exposed` ("Caught Spying on Them", `common/political_lobby_appeasement/te_covert_appeasement.txt`). The factor is `is_always_usable = yes`, because vanilla's lobby types list their own factors and an unlisted, not-always-usable one is rejected (`Appeasement change failed`); `appeasement_special_events_negative` is the fallback. The lobby's interest groups lose approval through the lobby itself, so there is no separate approval hit. With no such lobby, or one disbanded since the event opened, `covert_backlash_no_lobby_tt` replaces the appeasement line and the legitimacy hit still lands. Legitimacy is the static modifier `covert_friend_spying_scandal` (`country_legitimacy_base_add = -1`, decaying, `days = short_modifier_time`, 913, about 2.5 years) with a **literal** multiplier per branch: `multiplier` is re-evaluated against ROOT on later ticks and `iw_burned_type_code` is gone by then, so a value reading it would fall to zero (`modifier_multiplier_var_audit`). Abroad, the existing infamy and relations costs apply, with the relations hit ×1.5 when the target was a friend (`covert_exposure_friend_relations_mult`, applied once in `covert_exposure_relations_deny`; Acknowledge's half derives from it, so applying it in both would give ×2.25).
- **Defender's public reaction:** `covert_backlash_public`, called in `.1`'s `after` as `scope:detected_by_country = { covert_backlash_public = yes }`, after the burn chain and before `covert_tradecraft_burn_loss` and `.2`. Every exposure passes it, whoever chose it (the target's `.3` or panel click, an AI, or a target with no service), and it sits there rather than in `.2`, whose 24-month cooldown would skip it. Each member of each lobby that favours the attacker (`covert_backlash_pro_lobby_of` with `TARGET = ROOT`, ROOT being the attacker) takes `covert_foreign_ties_discredited` (`interest_group_pol_str_mult = -0.05`) with a literal multiplier 1 / 2 / 3 by tier (severe or non-costless war tier takes 3), ×1.5 when the attacker was a friend (1.5 / 3 / 4.5), for `short_modifier_time`, decaying. Nothing when the exposure is costless or the defender has no such lobby. It sets `iw_last_exposed_public = 1` on the defender when it applied, and removes it otherwise (nothing else clears the exposure record, so a stale flag would print the line on a later burn), for `.2`: a second `first_valid` child of `desc` (`covert_warfare.2.d_public` / `_unnamed`, "At home, public anger has turned on X's friends."). The `probe_result_events.txt` shape it copies has no in-game confirmation; the flavor chain is the fallback (play-test list). `.2` after an exposure the defender itself ordered still opens "Our counterintelligence services have uncovered evidence…" although it has known for months: text only, left as is. A watched operation causes none of this until it is exposed.
- **Named lobbies (2026-10-30).** The exposure events name the lobby the backlash reaches and its interest groups, as vanilla's lobby events name theirs: each pins the lobby in `immediate` with `random_political_lobby` filtered by `covert_backlash_pro_lobby_of`, and its text reads `[SCOPE.sPoliticalLobby('<scope>').GetName]` and `.GetMembersDesc` (the member list vanilla's lobby-created notifications print). `.1` pins `iw_home_lobby` (at home, favouring the target) and `iw_abroad_lobby` (in the target, favouring us); its `desc` is one `first_valid` of four variants, which splice `covert_warfare.1.d_home_line` and `_abroad_line` onto `covert_warfare.1.d` with `$key$` so the main text exists once, each gated as its half of the backlash is (home: a friend, not costless; abroad: not costless). `.2` pins `iw_exposed_lobby` when `iw_last_exposed_public` is set, and `covert_warfare.2.d_public_lobby` heads the public paragraph's `first_valid`, ahead of the two texts that name no lobby. `.3` pins `iw_catcher_lobby`, and Expose adds `covert_warfare.3.a_lobby_tt` when it exists and the exposure would not be costless (`covert_code_tier_war` on `iw_caught_type_code` and `has_war_with` the attacker, since `covert_exposure_is_costless` reads copies `.1` has not made yet), for an exposure now or one that waits its turn. `GetMembersDesc` is unconfirmed in event text (play-test list).

### Watching a caught operation (2026-10-16)
- **Flow.** `covert_ops_roll_detection_all` (the operator's JE pulse) saves the catch as `scope:iw_burned_op`, its target as `scope:iw_catch_target` and the operator as `scope:iw_catcher_attacker` (operations carry no operator, and the target's event needs one). Then by target: with `covert_can_watch` (the game rule on and `je_covert_warfare` active, the test `covert_warfare.2`'s Retaliate in kind applies) and a **player**, the operation gets `iw_catch_pending = 1` (the roll skips it) and `covert_warfare.3` fires on the target (`duration = 3`, which is months, so a player may leave it open that long): **Expose them** (default) or **Let it run, and watch**; with an **AI**, `covert_watch_ai_decide` watches by chance, else the operation is exposed at once; with **no service**, exposed at once, today's flow. **Exposing** is `covert_exposure_fire` on the attacker, which opens `.1` (the attacker's Acknowledge or Deny, `after` burns the operation and fires `.2` on the target). Costs are computed from the operation's phase at the time of exposure, so a long-watched operation pays the ×1.5 fully operational factor. **Watching** is `covert_watch_start` on the target: `iw_known = 1` on the operation (the attacker's panel never shows it and it gets no event), `covert_net_create` for a network inside the attacker if there is none (no seed amount), and a record. `.3`'s options read the operation inside `hidden_effect` under a `custom_tooltip` and guard every use with `exists`, since the operation can end while it is open.
- **One exposure in flight per attacker.** `.1` keeps its burned operation on the attacker (`iw_burned_type_code` and siblings), so two open at once would overwrite each other. `covert_exposure_fire` is **the only trigger site of `.1`** (the roll, the AI's catch, `.3` Expose and every later exposure call it, so each pays the backlash, infamy and Tradecraft loss `.1` resolves). It takes the lock, `iw_expose_inflight = 1` on the attacker, before `trigger_event` (the event's `immediate`, which sets the lock's other half `iw_burned_type_code`, may not run inside the calling effect); `.1`'s `after` clears it. `covert_exposure_in_flight` is either variable. While held, the roll skips that attacker for the month, the Known Foreign Operations Expose greys with a reason, `.3` Expose while locked watches the operation with `iw_expose_ordered = 1` and fires `covert_warfare.4` four days later, a retry: a player attacker may leave `.1` open for up to its month, so the target's monthly pass retries any order still waiting. Failsafe (`covert_watch_attacker_monthly`): a pass that finds the lock with no `.1` open raises it, and the second such pass removes it, so a `.1` that never ran costs one to two months. A `.1` that opened and never closed is caught the same way: a revolution that wins while it is open hands its copies to the winner, whose lock would then hold for good, since the loser's event and its `after` die with the loser. Each pass that finds `iw_burned_type_code` counts itself on `iw_burned_seen` (which `.1`'s `after` removes with the copies); the pass that brings the count to `covert_open_event_stale_passes` (5) removes the copies, the lock and the count. An event's `duration` is in months: `.1` is `duration = 1` (a longer event would let a player attacker stall its own detection roll by leaving it unanswered; at one month a stall skips at most one roll per catch), `.3` is 3, and five passes come four months after either opened at the soonest. A panel click is never queued: the button stays greyed until `.1` has closed, which a player attacker may take weeks over; `.3`'s Expose is the one that waits.
- **Records.** One container per watched operation, on the **defender**: tags `iw_known_rec` + `iw_known_<TYPE>`, parent the defender, listed in its `iw_known_ops`; variables `iw_attacker`, `iw_attacker_capital` (restated monthly, for the row's flag and name), `iw_type_code`, `iw_watch_months`, `iw_watch_net` (our network's strength inside the attacker, restated monthly, which the AI's expose chance and the row hover read), `iw_expose_ordered` (an exposure is ordered or waiting) and `iw_expose_sent` (`.1` has been opened for it). A record names its operation by (attacker, type code), not by a reference to the attacker's container: the no-duplicate rule makes the pair unique per operator, and `covert_watch_op_matches` (`iw_known`, target, type code, the code compared through a staged country variable, never `PREV` or the container's own `var:`) finds the live operation. A relaunch of the type carries no `iw_known`, so an old record never adopts it. **Reaping:** `covert_op_destroy` and `covert_op_sync`'s direct reap call `covert_watch_forget = { ATTACKER TYPE }` for an `iw_known` operation (burn, stand-down, a broken pact and a relaunch clear all remove the row the same day); `covert_watch_monthly` reaps what is left (a dead attacker's culled containers). `covert_watch_attacker_monthly` also clears an `iw_catch_pending` that reaches `covert_open_event_stale_passes` (four months after the roll, past `.3`'s three: `.3` never ran) and an `iw_known` whose target holds no matching record, so a revolution (the winner inherits neither lists nor containers: a government overthrown loses what it watched, owner's call) makes the operation catchable again. Old saves: every read of `iw_known_ops` sits behind `has_variable_list`.
- **The target's monthly pass** (`covert_watch_monthly`, from the JE pulse straight after `covert_nets_sync`, which has just counted the records into the networks: growth has no other clock, so a defender who loses the entry keeps its records and stops growing): reap, age (`iw_watch_months`) and restate, retry waiting exposures, and the AI's exposure roll.
- **Network growth.** Each watched operation not already ordered for exposure adds `covert_net_watch_gain_p1` / `_p2` / `_p3` = 4 / 6.6 / 8 a month (4 × the priority effect multipliers 1.65 and 2, derived from them, read from the attacker's live operation) to `iw_net_watch_gain` on our network inside the attacker, summed in `covert_nets_sync` step 2. It reaches the strength through `covert_net_watch_tick_gain` (× our Tradecraft and the UN host's multipliers, not the cultivate-assets factor) and `covert_net_gain` (so the diminishing return above half strength applies). Compare 2 a month for an operation of our own and 25 once for the Retaliate-in-kind seed: one watched operation outgrows the seed in about six months, and the price of watching is the harm the operation keeps doing, at full strength, meanwhile. A watched network does not decay. The figures are starting values, to tune in play.
- **AI odds** (`covert_ai_watch_chance`, percent, at the catch): watch 60 for a mild, moderate or war-tier operation, 15 for a severe one (`covert_code_tier_severe`), +20 when the attacker is a friend, flat 10 once our network inside the attacker has reached 75 (the other terms drop out). Each month, per record with no exposure ordered (`covert_ai_watch_expose_chance`): expose with 2 + 0.1 per point of network strength, 25 once it reaches 75, +5 for a severe operation. The choice is the AI-specific part only; no outcome branches on `is_ai`.
- **Known Foreign Operations panel and Expose button:** `journal_entry_systems.md` § Covert Warfare → Panels. The button only orders the work (sets `iw_expose_ordered`, fires `covert_warfare.4`); `.4` is hidden, copies every ordered record to a temporary list (an exposure can forget a record) and calls `covert_watch_expose_record` on each, which forgets a record whose operation or attacker is gone, leaves one waiting while the attacker is locked, and otherwise opens `.1` from inside the attacker's lookup. The exposure is not in the scripted GUI's effect because the engine walks those to render tooltips (the trophies pattern, `monument_trophy_events.10`–`.13`).
- **Self-checks** (`TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN <name>` in `debug.log`, once a game each, separate OK and BROKEN flags): `covert_catch_decision` (OK in `.3`'s `immediate`: the roll's saved scopes crossed attacker to defender; BROKEN from the attacker's pending watchdog), `covert_exposure_lock` (BROKEN only, from the stale-copy failsafe above: a `.1`'s `after` never ran) and `covert_watch_expose` (OK in `.1`'s `immediate` when the operation had `iw_known`: the target's order crossed defender to attacker; BROKEN from the target's monthly pass when an exposure was sent, its operation lives and its attacker is not locked). ### Counterintelligence Support (2026-10-16)
- **What:** `covert_counterintel_support_action`, type 14 (registry row `("counterintel_support", 14, "ideological", "mild", "covert_counterintel_support")`; mild is nominal). It costs an operation slot, upkeep (a priority share), a pact cost of 25 influence (the mild tier) and counts against the per-type cap. Ungated by technology (`UNGATED`). `possible`: funding ≥ 1, a free slot, under the cap, no duplicate, `covert_action_valid_target` (not decentralized), `covert_is_friend`, and no other protector. `requirement_to_maintain`: funding ≥ 1 and `covert_is_friend`, so it lapses with the friendship. No truce check and no `iw_no_intel_sharing_tt`: that gate is for hostile operations. It has a priority stepper, a Stand down button and no Detection Risk figure (the row says "Detection Risk: none, an open arrangement").

Unproven in game (play-test items 9 and 10: the mark surviving a month, and whether the shared no-duplicate gate, which passes no `is_initiator`, blocks mutual protection).
- **The protector gets nothing** (owner). Every consumer that treats an operation as a threat or a foothold reads `covert_op_is_hostile` (container) or `is_hostile_covert_operation_pact` (pact), which exclude the type by tag: the detection roll (never caught, never watched, never burned: `.1`'s chain keeps a row for code 14 that is unreachable, like `covert_caught_type_name`); `covert_nets_sync` steps 1 and 2 (no network inside the ally, no growth); `covert_op_create` (no head start and no network: it sets the protector mark instead); the tier-2 count of operations against us (`covert_net_refresh_intel`); `covert_tradecraft_monthly` (no gain, and no help against idle decay); `covert_ai_manage_priorities` (the war and rivalry escalation sits behind `covert_op_is_hostile`, so it stays at priority 1); cultivate assets' AI `propose_score` bonus for another operation against the target; and the console harness's detection and hostile-network helpers. The UN dossier and docket, election confidence and the backlash read exposure records or type codes only, which it never produces.
- **Intelligence-sharing shield:** `update_intel_sharing_defense` reads the parties' defence modifiers. The ally's +8 counts once in its own base defence, and a partner of the ally can take up to half the gap through the shield at the next yearly pulse. No loop (shields are stripped before comparing) and no double count on the ally.

### Election Interference & Electoral Confidence
- **Gate:** Election interference (`covert_election_interference_action`) requires the target to have an elected legislature (Landed/Wealth/Census/Universal Suffrage). If the target abolishes elections, the operation auto-cancels via `requirement_to_maintain`.
- **Electoral Confidence Effect:** On `on_election_campaign_end`, the system checks if any country has an active election interference operation against the country holding the election. Based on the **worst** (most advanced) operation among all attackers:
  - **Preparatory phase** (<6 months): No effect.
  - **Establishing phase** (6–11 months): `-0.05` electoral confidence.
  - **Fully Operational** (12+ months): `-0.1` electoral confidence.
  - Both are halved while the country hosts a UN electoral observer mission with any strength (`un_mission_hosts_with_strength`): a Moribund UN's mission has none and halves nothing.
- **Operation matching:** For each attacker with an election-interference pact against ROOT, reads the attacker's `iw_ops` container tagged `iw_op_election_interference` whose `iw_target` is ROOT, and takes its phase.
- **On-action:** `covert_warfare_on_actions.txt` → `on_election_campaign_end` → `on_actions = { covert_warfare_election_end }` → calls `covert_op_election_confidence_effect` scripted effect.
- **Notification:** `iw_election_interference_confidence` message sent to the affected country.

### AI Behavior
- **Evaluation chance:** Low (0.02–0.05) to prevent spammy toggling. Aggressive rulers get +0.02–0.03, cautious rulers get ×0.25–0.5.
- **Will propose guards:** `in_default = no`, `country_rank >= rank_value:major_power` (peacetime ops), rivalry/antagonistic/domineering attitude required, IC advantage check for some ops.
- **Target size (2026-10):** upkeep scales with the operator's GDP (`covert_operations_cost_scale`), not the target's, so against a much smaller country an operation costs more than it returns. Every operation whose payoff scales with the target's size carries `covert_ai_target_worth_the_upkeep = { TARGET = scope:target_country }` (`covert_warfare_triggers.txt`) in `will_propose`: the target passes unless our GDP is at least `covert_ai_much_weaker_gdp_factor` (10) times its own (`covert_ai_target_much_weaker`), and a much weaker target still passes when we have money to burn (`covert_ai_has_money_to_burn`: gold reserves at least `covert_ai_spare_reserves_ratio` (0.5) full — a tenth of a year's GDP, since the limit is 20 % — and `net_fixed_income > 0`). Gated: election interference, financial subversion, influence campaign, ideological subversion, destabilization, regime change, cultivate assets, and the two wartime operations, which skip the test while `is_losing_war_against` the target. Exempt, because the payoff is our own progress or a threat removed: industrial, military and space programme espionage, nuclear programme sabotage, secure loose material; and the two colony operations, whose payoff is the target's colonies, not its size. Launch only — a running operation never lapses for it, and humans never see it. `test_covert_ai_targets.py` makes every type sit on one list.
- **Friends (2026-10-16):** the six operations the old relations block covered (election interference, financial subversion, infrastructure sabotage, communications disruption, ideological subversion, destabilization) carry `NOT = { covert_is_friend = { TARGET = scope:target_country } }` in `will_propose`, so the AI does not start them against a friend; so do the two colony operations (2026-10-26), written that way, though the AI does not call them off when the target turns friend. The other hostile operations have no friend test (regime change and nuclear programme sabotage need a rivalry or a hostile attitude to launch): the AI can still run espionage, an influence campaign, cultivate assets or secure loose material against a friend, and pays the backlash when caught.
- **Watching (2026-10-16):** an AI target with a covert service decides at the catch in script (`covert_watch_ai_decide`) and exposes watched operations by a monthly chance; figures in § Watching a caught operation. Counterintelligence Support's AI rule is in § Counterintelligence Support.
- **Propose score:** Scales with rivalry (+10), great power rank (+5–7), aggressive ruler (+5–8).
- **Calling an operation off (2026-10):** an operation does nothing for six months and reaches full strength at twelve, while upkeep runs from the first month, so every covert action spells out its AI `will_break` rather than leaving it to the engine's default. Before this, none had one, and the launch tests in `will_propose` (a free slot, intelligence capacity, target size, `in_default`) were the only AI test an operation had: if the engine falls back to "break once `will_propose` fails", filling the last slot made every running operation breakable. Now the AI keeps an operation until it is fully operational, `covert_ai_op_settled = { TYPE TARGET }` (`covert_warfare_triggers.txt`, on `covert_op_is_fully_operational`; a pact without its container yet reads as not settled). After that it ends it only once its reason has gone, judged more loosely than at launch. Election interference, financial and ideological subversion, destabilization, regime change and nuclear programme sabotage stop at `covert_ai_hostility_gone`: no rivalry either way, no antagonistic, belligerent or domineering attitude, and relations above poor, where launch needs only our rivalry or one of two attitudes. The first four (election interference, financial and ideological subversion, destabilization) also stop once the target is a friend (`covert_is_friend`, a third branch of the `OR` in `will_break`, behind the same settle wait); infrastructure sabotage and communications disruption keep `always = no`. Space programme espionage stops when we run no milestone of our own; the two colony operations when we have no stake in space left (no colony and no milestone running), and sabotage colony also at `covert_ai_hostility_gone`. Cultivate assets stops once its network reaches `covert_cultivate_ai_net_done` (75, intelligence tier 2), whatever its age, since it pays from its first month; a network decays back to the launch ceiling of 50 in ~17 months. It also stops once settled if the hostility has gone. The wartime operations, influence campaign, secure loose material and industrial and military espionage carry `will_break = { always = no }`: their `requirement_to_maintain` already ends them when their purpose does. Launch-only tests never break anything. `test_covert_ai_targets.py` makes every type take one of these rules.
- **AI Funding Management:** The JE monthly pulse sets `iw_funding_level` outright for every `is_player = no` country — this, not a button, is the AI's path into funding. Branches, in order: in default or bankrupt → 1 if it has operations (dropping to 0 would lapse them all and waste the setup), else 0; otherwise, if major power or above **and** (rivalled or at war) → at war: `iw_funding_level_max` for a great power, else 3; at peace: 3 for a great power (whether or not it already runs 3+ operations), else 1; otherwise 1 if it has any operation running, else 0. A human's level comes from the widget's stepper and is never overwritten.
- **AI Priority Management (2026-09, covert slice 3):** `covert_ai_manage_priorities`, run for every `is_player = no` country immediately before `covert_ops_sync_all` in the monthly pulse (so the sync sees the new priorities the same tick). In default or bankrupt, the wanted level is 1 (never raises while the treasury is in that state); otherwise a great power at war with the operation's own target wants 3, a country with a rivalry pact against the target wants 2, and everything else wants 1. Each operation's `iw_priority` steps toward its wanted level at most twice per direction per month, through the same `covert_possible_priority_up`/`_down` gates the human stepper uses, so slice 5's Tradecraft requirement for priority 3 binds the AI too.
- **Wartime ops:** Higher eval chance (0.05), no rank requirement, `in_default = no` guard only. `propose_score` 15, +10 rivalry pact with the target, +5 GP, +5 when `is_losing_war_against = { ENEMY = scope:target_country }` (battlefield read from `common/scripted_triggers/nuke_triggers.txt`; see § War Support Feeds).
- **Slice-6 operations (2026-09):** regime change — rivalry plus an antagonistic or domineering attitude, major power or above; score 5, +5 great power, +8 aggressive ruler; evaluation 0.02. Nuclear programme sabotage — target proliferating, we are a nuclear power or a great power, and rival or antagonistic; score 5, +10 when the target's programme is at 75 % or more, +5 great power. Space programme espionage — target ahead in space and we are pursuing a milestone; score 10, +5 great power. Cultivate assets — rival or antagonistic, and our network there below `covert_cultivate_ai_net_ceiling` (50); score 5, +5 when we already run another operation there; evaluation 0.05. **The AI will rarely launch the two severe operations:** `possible` binds it, so it needs Seasoned Tradecraft, and one moderate operation at 10 % detection averages 57.

### Cancellation Conditions
- **All 14 peacetime ops** (election interference, financial subversion, industrial espionage, military espionage, influence campaign, ideological subversion, destabilization, slice 6's regime change, nuclear programme sabotage, space programme espionage and cultivate assets, secure loose material, and the two colony operations) have truce checks in both `possible` and `requirement_to_maintain`, so they auto-cancel if a truce with the target begins. None of them has a war guard (the actions say "Allowed during war"): a war with the target does not end them.
- **Slice-6 operations also lapse** when their own condition goes: regime change when the rivalry ends; nuclear programme sabotage when the target's programme is paused by treaty, given up or gone (`nuclear_program_is_standing` — not when its funding merely drops to 0); space programme espionage once the target is no longer ahead; cultivate assets when the target becomes decentralized; secure loose material when the target's line has no warhead left unaccounted for; the two colony operations when the target holds no colony.
- **Counterintelligence Support** has no truce check and no intelligence-sharing gate; it lapses when funding drops below 1 or its target stops being a friend (`covert_is_friend`). Relations end no operation (Destabilization does not lapse at cordial); the AI alone calls the four breakable formerly blocked ones off once the target is a friend (§ AI Behavior).
- **Wartime ops** (infrastructure sabotage, communications disruption) require active war with the target **or** a diplomatic play against them under way (2026-09, covert slice 2, Task 8 — see § Graduated exposure blowback); they auto-cancel once neither holds, e.g. the play resolves without war, or peace is achieved.
- **Script value scope:** All covert warfare SVs use `owner = {}` wrapping to access country-scope data from JE scope (the `on_monthly_pulse` context). This is required because the JE monthly pulse runs in journal entry scope, not country scope.
- **Stand down:** `covert_effect_stand_down` (called only from the widget's seventeen `covert_stand_down_<type>_sgui` handlers) ends one operation on demand: `remove_diplomatic_pact` plus `covert_op_destroy`. That reaches the same end state as a manual break in the diplomacy outliner, whose `manual_break_effect` is `covert_op_end` → the same `covert_op_destroy`; the effect's `any_in_list` guard makes it idempotent, so it is correct whether or not `remove_diplomatic_pact` also fires the break hook. No infamy and no relations hit — those belong to being caught, not to calling an operation off.

## Intelligence Sharing Defense Shield

- **Purpose:** Countries with an intelligence sharing pact benefit from their partner's covert defense strength. The weaker partner receives a shield equal to 50% of the difference between their combined base defense and their strongest partner's, spread evenly over the three defense types (a sixth of the gap on each; the boost is multiplied by 0.5 and divided by 3).
- **Mechanism:** Uses the "prior variable subtraction" pattern for modifier recalculation. Each country stores `intel_shield_mult` (the multiplier currently applied to their shield modifier). When recalculating:
  1. Reads own total defense from `modifier:country_covert_defense_*_add` (3 types summed).
  2. Subtracts `3 * intel_shield_mult` to get base defense (since the shield adds multiplier to each of 3 types).
  3. For each partner, reads their total defense and subtracts `3 * PREV.var:intel_shield_mult` to get their base.
  4. Computes `boost = (partner_base - own_base) * 0.5`, takes the best across all partners.
  5. Removes old modifier, applies new one with the computed multiplier, stores it for next tick.
- **Key modifier:** `intelligence_sharing_defense_shield_modifier` in `common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt` (each defense type = 1, applied with multiplier).
- **Key effect:** `update_intel_sharing_defense` in `common/scripted_effects/treaty_article_effects.txt`.
- **Wiring:** Called from `on_yearly_intel_sharing_defense` on_action, triggered by `on_yearly_pulse_country` in `common/on_actions/treaty_article_events_on_actions.txt`.
- **Critical pattern:** Modifier values are NOT recalculated within a single effect block, so `modifier:X` reads after `remove_modifier` still include the old value. The `intel_shield_mult` persistent variable allows subtracting the prior contribution. See `docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md` § "Modifier Values Are NOT Recalculated Within a Single Effect Block".
