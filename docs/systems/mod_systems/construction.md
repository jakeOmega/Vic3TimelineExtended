# Construction

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Construction Cost Scaling

- **Purpose:** Makes construction more expensive for rich countries whose workforce is fully employed (GDP per capita, and slack: peasants plus unemployed over the workforce). Keeps a rich country from outbuilding its population, lets one with idle labour build cheaply until it is used, and provides catchup for poorer nations.
- **Modifier:** `construction_cost_scaling` in `common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt` — `goods_input_construction_mult = 1`.
- **Script values:** `common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt` — search for `CONSTRUCTION COST SCALING`.
- **On action:** `construction_cost_scaling_on_action` in `common/on_actions/extra_on_actions.txt`, wired to `on_yearly_pulse_country`. It first calls `te_cc_yearly_update` (`common/scripted_effects/te_construction_cost_effects.txt`), which stores the country's slack (`te_cc_slack`, `te_cc_peasants`), moves the applying share (`te_cc_slack_factor`), stores the GDP per capita and GDP part the scaling uses (`te_cc_gdppc`, `te_cc_gdp_part`) for the Nation tab, moves the capped factor (`te_cc_scale`) and stores the small-economy factor (`te_cc_small_factor`). The on_action then calls `te_cc_apply_scaling` (the same file), the modifiers' one refresh site, which the game-start pass and a switch of `free_market_construction_rule` also call.
- **Tuning:** `construction_cost_gdppc_reference`, `construction_cost_floor_ratio` (1x), `construction_cost_ceiling_ratio` (100x), `construction_cost_max_mult` (10 = +1000%); `construction_cost_slack_full` (0.03), `construction_cost_slack_none` (0.15), `construction_cost_slack_ease` (a third). The `scripts/analysis` tools read them all; re-run `econ_construction_counterfactual.py` after a change. `construction_cost_max_yearly_move` (0.1) caps the yearly move of the scaling's own factor. The optional small-economy rule's: `construction_cost_small_economy_mild_factor` (0.5), `_standard_factor` (0.2) and `_strong_factor` (0.1), the factor at no GDP of each strength; `construction_cost_small_economy_full_gdp` (10 million), where the full price returns under all three; and `construction_cost_small_economy_min_factor` (0.1), the floor of the price and goods per point, which must stay the lowest of the three.
- **Curve:** X = S × d − 1, where S is the scaling's own factor, one plus the GDP part × the share that applies, capped a year, and d is the small-economy factor (1 unless its rule is on).
  - **The GDP part:** linear from 0 at the floor to `max_mult` at the ceiling, about 10 points per £1 of GDP per capita above £1 and +1000% from £100 (`construction_cost_scaling_gdp_mult`).
  - **The share that applies:** 1 at slack `slack_full` or less, 0 at `slack_none` or more, a straight line between. It moves `slack_ease` of the way toward that at each yearly pulse, from 0 before a country's first (`te_cc_slack_factor_now`), so a save from before the rule doesn't jump to the whole GDP part.
  - **The yearly cap** (owner, 2026-11-13). S is stored at the pulse (`var:te_cc_scale`) and moves toward one plus the GDP part × the share by at most `construction_cost_max_yearly_move` of itself, up or down (`te_cc_scale_next`). The share goes on easing as before, so S arrives where it would have, later, and the steady state is unchanged. A country with no stored S first gets the scaling in force, read before the share moves (`te_cc_scale_target`: 1 in a new game, the old figure on a save from before the cap), so no save jumps. Why: the scaling multiplies every use of the construction good, maintenance on 75 building types included, so a step in the share of a rich country moved the country's whole demand for the good on one day; `docs/systems/economy_growth_sim.md` had the eased cost moving up to 23% a year in arithmetic that understates the game.
  - **The small-economy factor** (`te_small_economy_construction_rule`, off by default; owner, 2026-11-13; three strengths since 2026-11-16). d runs in a straight line from the setting's factor at no GDP (`te_cc_small_economy_min_factor`: mild 0.5, standard 0.2, strong 0.1, 1 with the rule off) to 1 at `construction_cost_small_economy_full_gdp` (`te_cc_small_economy_factor`, read only by the pulse and stored as `var:te_cc_small_factor`, with the minimum it was worked out from as `var:te_cc_small_min`; `te_cc_small_factor_now` is the one in force, 1 before a pulse has stored one). The cap does not touch it. It makes X negative for a small poor country, down to −0.9 under strong, so the adjusted multipliers have no floor at 0, the modifier is added whenever its multiplier is not 0, and the price and goods per point are floored at `construction_cost_small_economy_min_factor`, the lowest any setting gives, not at 1 (nor at the setting in force's minimum: a country switched to a milder setting keeps its factor until its pulse, and the price must follow it there). It rides the scaling's modifier, so it also cuts a small economy's construction maintenance and its investors' construction, and it applies under both the market and the direct setting, to players and AI alike. Why: the mod removes the base game's free construction, so in a market game a country with a small budget pays the full price of every point (Workshop reports that a minor country could hardly build).
    - **The settings.** `te_small_economy_construction_disabled` (default), `_mild`, `_standard`, and `_enabled`, which is the strongest: it was the rule's one "on" setting from 2026-11-13 to 2026-11-16 and keeps its name, so a preset or a save made with it on still means a tenth. Read only through the triggers in `te_construction_cost_triggers.txt`: `te_small_economy_construction_on` (any strength) and one per strength (`_mild_on`, `_standard_on`, `_strong_on`), all positive, so a save from before the rule reads as off.
    - **How the strengths are sized** (owner, 2026-11-16: "a magnitude option", on a report that a small AI government could afford a quarter of a point a week, a 100-point building in seven years). The bar is the base game's free construction, `country_gdp_construction`'s 10 points a week, which the mod empties. Vanilla gives it whole to a player under its default Base Construction Capacity setting and to everyone under Maximum, and to an AI under the default 10 × (0.2 + 0.8 × GDP ÷ 2 million), at most 10 (`00_defines.txt`, `COUNTRY_GDP_CONSTRUCTION_MODIFIER_*`; read from the defines, not measured). The figures come from the AI telemetry probe's two unattended runs of February and March 1836 (`docs/testing/ai-probe-runbook.md`; 76 country snapshots of GDP and weekly income), with a surplus of a third of income (the snapshots' median is 0.36) and a point at 1,250 (their smoothed price, `cm_price`, median 1,334). A setting is compared with the base game's free points plus the same surplus spent at full price:
      - **Strong**, the old "on": a country under it buys about what a player of the same income had in the base game, and no more. 75 of the 76 snapshots get fewer points a week than that; the one above is Tibet (income 9,294 on a GDP of 725,000, high for its size), by 20%. On a smooth line through the snapshots' GDP for each income, strong reaches 97% of the bar at about 10,000 a week and stays below it elsewhere. A minimum of 0.05 puts 12 of the 76 above it, by up to 67%. Against what the base game gave an AI, strong gives 48 of the 76 more, up to twice as much, and the 1,000-a-week AI exactly as much.
      - **Standard** is about what the base game gave an AI: 70 of the 76 get no more, the worst (Tibet again) 30% more, and the 5,000-a-week AI exactly as much.
      - **Mild** halves the price with almost no GDP, and still cuts the 1,000-a-week government's wait for a 100-point building from 7.2 years to 3.6.
      - **One full-price GDP for every strength.** At 10 million (an income of about 47,000 a week in 1836) a third of income buys about 12 points a week at full price, more than the base game's whole free base, so the country needs no help whatever the strength. The settings differ most for the smallest economies, which is where the shortfall is: a 20,000-a-week government builds a 100-point level in 19 weeks with no discount. A later full price for strong would push it past the bar where it is tightest (incomes of 10,000 to 15,000 a week); a sooner one for mild would make it do nothing above that GDP. One number is also one fewer for a player to keep in mind.
      - A 100-point Government Administration or Construction Sector level (both `construction_cost_very_low`), its cost at 1,250 a point and the years it takes on a third of income. GDP is the snapshots' for each income (a fit of GDP against income, 8 × income^1.3):

        | 1,000 | 65,000 | 125,000: 7.2 years | 62,900: 3.6 | 25,700: 1.5 | 13,200: 0.76 | 2.3 free a week and 0.27 bought: 0.76 | 10 free and 0.27 bought: 0.19 |

        Later in a game a government collects a smaller share of its GDP (AI revenue was 8% of GDP in the 1958 save study; in the 1836 snapshots weekly `income` was a median two-thirds of weekly GDP), so the same income sits at a higher GDP and the discount is smaller: 1.1 years for the 1,000-a-week government under strong at the 1958 share, 1.8 under standard. At the market's ceiling price (about 2,000 a point) every time is 1.6 times as long. The AI's purchase is its surplus over the price only once PR #1854 lands; before it, the 10-point floor sets an AI's purchase, and the discount cuts the floor's bill instead.
    - **The Nation tab** names the minimum the factor in force came from (`te_cc_small_min_now`), not the setting chosen now, so the two agree between pulses, and after a switch adds that the new setting applies from the next yearly update (`te_cc_small_setting_changed`: the stored minimum is not the chosen setting's).
    - **At game start** the rule's factor is applied to every country at once (`te_cc_game_start`, from `on_game_started_after_lobby` after the direct conversion, through the hidden country event `te_construction_market_recalc_events.3`, because the modifier's multiplier resolves against ROOT). A country whose `gdp` reads 0 that day tries again a week later. The self-check `small_economy_start` reads the country's whole construction-cost modifier two days on, from the hidden event `te_construction_market_recalc_events.5` (a modifier set in an effect is not visible inside it): below 0 means a discount is in force, so `gdp` read on the first day and the multiplier resolved against the country; against a wrong ROOT it resolves to 1, the static modifier's +100%.
    - **Switched in a running game**, on, off or from one strength to another, the rule reaches each country at its next yearly pulse (`test_construction_cost_scaling.py`, `TheStrengths`).
    - **A country made during play** (a release, an uprising's rebels) has no stored factor. With the rule on, the weekly pulse gives it the yearly steps once (`te_cc_weekly_new_country`, called first in `te_construction_market_on_weekly_pulse`, which runs in the country's own event under either construction setting), so it does not pay full prices until its first yearly pulse. It needs a GDP above 0 and queues no event.
  - **A stale prior.** `var:prior_construction_scaling` is what the adjusted multipliers back out of the modifier read, so it must be the contribution the country holds. A revolution's winner inherits the variable and not the modifier; backing it out then shrank a positive scaling for a year, and with a small economy's negative prior carried the multiplier past −1 (X = −0.8 gives −0.8 × 1.8 = −1.44). `te_cc_apply_scaling` first sets a prior that is not 0 to 0 where the country does not hold its modifier, in each branch, so the winner gets X × (1 + the other sources) at once (`test_construction_cost_scaling.py`, `test_a_revolutions_winner_gets_the_scaling_it_is_owed`). Set, not removed: the direct modifier's multiplier is the variable itself.
  - **Readers:** `construction_cost_scaling_mult` is X, which every reader takes (the construction market's goods per point and price, the adjusted multipliers). It reads stored figures only once the pulse has run, so the price and the modifier in force agree between pulses.
  - **Slack:** the sum over the country's states of the yearly walk's counts (§ Unemployment as a Share of the Workforce): peasants' workforce plus every other pop's with no workplace, over the workforce. `te_country_slack_share` and `te_country_peasant_share` iterate every state, so only the pulse reads them, and the Nation tab reads the stored figures.
- **History:**
  - **#1037** (2026-10-18) capped the line at +100% from £11, after the economy growth sim's data counterfactual (`docs/systems/economy_growth_sim.md`) found that its late steepness, up to 11x the price of a point, accounted for much of the late stall in rich economies, which kept a third of their workforce idle. A collapse in service jobs ran alongside it (79M in France's core in 1998, 41–64M from 2023).
  - **The slack part** (the owner's proposal, the same day) replaced the cap and restored the line. Against the same data, with the ministries at +1% a level:
    - It leaves the EU as a whole at 3.5% slack in 2045, with no labour shortage. The cap would have run France's core short of labour from 2033.
    - The slack is read over the whole owner country. A rich core inside a country with poor annexed or colonial states keeps cheap construction until the whole country's slack falls. Without migration into it, France's core runs short from 2004, about 31% by 2045. If the brake followed the core instead, the core would end at 1.6% and the EU at 29%.
    - The owner kept the country-wide read (2026-10-18), pending a play-test of a rich core with a poor periphery (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`). Weighting by state GDP or building levels is the next step if the core runs short while the periphery idles.
- **Nation tab:** the Construction Cost row shows the scaling's own effect, `te_cc_scaling_factor` − 1. Its tooltip gives the cost of a point as factors: the scaling (the GDP part, the share that applies, the slack, peasants and unemployed) times the rest. The total is the cost in force, one plus every construction-cost modifier (`te_cc_total_factor`), and the rest is the total over the scaling, so the product holds after a mid-year change. The engine's per-source list (`GetValueWithBreakdownFor`) closes it. The scaling shown is the one in force, S × d (`te_cc_scaling_factor` = 1 + X): the tooltip gives S, where it is heading and that it moves at most a tenth of itself a year, and, while d is below 1, the small-economy factor.
- **Self-check:** `construction_slack_read` (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`): the stored slack against a fresh walk of the country's pops. `small_economy_start` (the same list): whether the small-economy factor was applied on the first day.
- `goods_input_construction_mult` affects both construction project costs AND ongoing building maintenance.
- With `free_market_construction_rule` disabled there is no construction good to scale, so the on_action applies `construction_cost_scaling_direct` (`country_construction_goods_cost_mult = 1`, adjusted by `construction_cost_scaling_direct_adjusted_mult`) instead. See § Free Market Construction off. The modifier is set by `te_cc_apply_scaling` (`te_construction_cost_effects.txt`), from the yearly pulse and from a switch of the rule in a running game: the switch clears both modifiers and `var:prior_construction_scaling` (`te_direct_construction_clear_cost_scaling`) and applies the new model's scaling at once from its first-run branch (`te_direct_construction_switch_cost_scaling`). It must not wait for the yearly pulse: the market's price and goods per point multiply by the live `construction_cost_scaling_mult` whether or not the modifier is in force.

## Excess Private Construction

- **Purpose:** Gives an investment pool that the private queue can't spend a way to spend. The engine caps the private queue at 1,000 buildings and each project's weekly progress at `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`, so a rich pool can outgrow what investors can build and keep depositing forever. The modifier raises the per-project cap and charges a construction-efficiency penalty for it, which is the sink. Overinvestment is the second stage.
- **Modifiers** (`common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt`): `too_much_private_construction`, per unit `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add = 1` and `state_construction_mult = -0.0001` (−90% at the 9,000 cap). `overinvestment_modifier` sets every pop type's `state_*_investment_pool_contribution_add = -1`.
- **Script values** (`common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt`, `target_queue_length` through `too_much_private_construction_script_value`). `too_much_private_construction_overflow` is target − base: the per-project cap raise at which 500 projects could spend the pool's gross income. It is positive exactly when 500 projects at base speed couldn't, and 0 while `private_construction_used` is 0 or less, i.e. an empty queue or no private share (which also keeps `curr_cost_per_construction_point` from dividing by 0). `too_much_private_construction_script_value` is the overflow, moving at most ×1.2 or ÷1.2 a year from last year's value (`var:too_much_private_construction_cached`); the cap is 9,000. While Overinvestment is in force and the private queue has levels, it returns last year's value instead. A new episode starts at the lesser of the overflow and 10, or, if higher, the value the modifier had when it was removed in the last five years (`too_much_private_construction_resume`, reading `var:too_much_private_construction_last`).
- **On action:** `excess_private_construction_on_action`, on `on_yearly_pulse_country`, checked every year, also while the modifier is in force. The modifier is applied or kept while one of these holds:
  - **Entry** (no stored value above 0): more than 500 private levels queued and `investment_pool_net_income > 0`.
  - **Stay** (`var:too_much_private_construction_cached` above 0; it is the modifier's multiplier, so normally the modifier is in force): a non-empty private queue and `too_much_private_construction_overflow > 0`.
  - Overinvestment is in force.

  While one holds, the modifier is re-applied at the script value. Otherwise it is removed, its value kept for five years in `var:too_much_private_construction_last` (a timed country variable), and the variable set to 0. A re-entry within those five years resumes at the lesser of the kept value and the overflow, so a short dip in pool income (a banking panic cuts capitalist contributions for 14–18 months; a default or bankruptcy) doesn't restart it at 10 and let the pool build up for decades. After five years the kept value expires and a new episode ramps from 10 as designed, so a re-entry decades later doesn't jump straight to a large penalty. The kept value is also removed once the modifier is back. The tests read the variable rather than the modifier because a civil war's winner inherits variables but no modifiers (`docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md` § What a Civil War's Winner Inherits). A winner holding the variable rebuilds the modifier and continues the nation. On that first pulse `base_construction_per_week` subtracts a value the winner's modifiers don't include, so the overflow reads high by that much; the ×1.2 ceiling caps the rebuilt value at 1.2× the inherited one. The stay test can't be the entry test. Net income turns negative the moment the modifier starts draining the pool, so the modifier would remove itself by working and the pool would refill until it came back. The queue can also fall to 500 or below while the modifier is still needed: the engine's own autonomous investment fills it, and faster projects shorten it. Gross income doesn't depend on the modifier, and under Free Market Construction, while the pool's funds don't bind, the cost per point (`curr_cost_per_construction_point`, spending ÷ `private_construction_used`) is the market price, because the market sizes the private share to what the pool buys. So the modifier can't end its own need by working. The estimate does include the modifier's (base + value) × levels cap through `private_construction_used`; where the pool's funds bind it reads low and the overflow high (the direct-construction exception below). Outside Overinvestment it fails exactly when the ramp target reaches 0, so the ÷1.2 floor can't keep a spent modifier alive.
- **Overinvestment** is added the same pulse the variable is above 1,000 and `investment_pool > gdp`. It keeps the construction modifier because it cuts every pop's pool contribution, so gross income, and the overflow with it, falls to about 0; removing the modifier then would end Overinvestment after one year, since it needs the variable above 1,000. Kept, the value is **held at last year's** while Overinvestment is in force and the private queue has levels (owner's ruling, PR #514), so Overinvestment is re-added until its own exit holds, the pool at or under GDP, even with the value above 1,000. The year after it lifts, the stay test and the normal ramp take over. With an empty private queue nothing drains the pool, so there the value falls ÷1.2 as before and Overinvestment ends within 13 years. Meanwhile the private queue keeps spending the pool, under direct construction through the law's allocation and under Free Market Construction through the private purchase, a 24th of the pool a week up to what the private queue can absorb (§ Construction as a Market Good). **Limits of the hold.** It freezes growth as well as decay: with gross income cut the overflow reads −base, so the need can't raise the value during Overinvestment, and when the queue cap limits the drain the frozen value is the limit (PR #514's toy model has a 2M/week country run 13 straight years at 1,145). Episodes can be long, even indefinite, when inflows that aren't pop contributions outpace a capped drain: government-dividend reinvestment, or a pool left far above GDP by a GDP collapse. (The cooperative boom and frenzy phases were one until 2026-10-05, when their `country_weekly_investment_pool_mult` became a share of the pool's gross income rather than of its balance; Overinvestment cuts gross income, so they no longer feed it. `banking_cycle_simulation.md` §21.) One queued private level is enough to keep the hold. If play shows long episodes, the candidate follow-ups are capping the hold's consecutive years, or holding only while the per-project cap binds (`private_construction_used` at or above queued levels × `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`). Two earlier behaviours made Overinvestment fail to drain the pool. Until 2026-09-26 the market formula was gross × pool ÷ (24 × gross) with a fixed 2.4M divisor at gross ≤ 0, so it went to 0 or below exactly when Overinvestment cut gross income, and the market bought nothing for the private queue. Until 2026-09-27 the value fell ÷1.2 in each Overinvestment year, which lifted Overinvestment every other year and kept the value, and so per-project capacity, near 1,000 while the pool stayed above GDP. What still limits a very rich pool is the owner's 9,000 cap.
- **Direct construction with an emptied pool (known exception, accepted for now).** Under direct construction nothing throttles the private queue to pool ÷ 24. It draws its allocation, `min(construction × private share, (base + value) × levels)`, and stalls at what the pool can fund. If that allocation costs more than the pool's income, the modifier drains the pool to 0. From then on spending equals gross income, but `private_construction_used` doesn't see the stall, so the cost estimate falls below the price and the overflow reads about private allocation ÷ 500 − base instead of the real need. The modifier climbs to that and stays while the allocation exceeds 500 × base, even after the real need is gone (a toy model in PR #514's review put it at twice the real need, and kept it after the need turned negative). This is off the default rule (Free Market Construction), and the gate before 2026-09-26 kept the modifier regardless. Follow-up: give the stay test a cost per point that doesn't depend on spending: `te_construction_market_price` under Free Market Construction, a goods cost per point under direct construction. The same fix covers the estimate's `min = 1` floor, which makes the overflow read huge for one pulse after a week of almost no spending (for example after `te_mon_currency_reform_pool_wipe` or `te_mon_union_exit_pool_hit`).
- **Invariant:** the modifier's multiplier is `var:too_much_private_construction_cached`, written first, so `base_construction_per_week` (the per-project cap without this modifier) can subtract it. Set the variable to 0; never `remove_variable` it. `var:too_much_private_construction_last` exists only while the modifier is absent, for up to five years after a removal.
- **History:** until 2026-09-26 the gate was `OR = { <conditions>  has_modifier = too_much_private_construction }` and the value could only fall ÷1.2 a year. Once the modifier existed, the conditions were never checked again, and it stayed above 0 for 76–114 years. The first draft of the fix (PR #514) re-checked the entry test every year and removed the modifier when it failed. Review found that this removes it the pulse after it starts draining the pool, so the stay test is separate.

## Batched Private Investment

- **Purpose:** game speed in the late game. The engine's weekly investment pass queues private levels one at a time and scores every candidate building again after each. For the USA of 2050 that is 1,000 decisions and 22 s of every weekly tick, two thirds of all tick work (`docs/audits/late_game_performance_2050.md`, findings 1 and 4). Script cannot shorten a decision; it makes each one count for more. When the investors of a very large economy queue a level at home, the mod queues more levels of the same building beside it, so the pass finds its queue nearer full and makes fewer decisions (owner, 2026-11-26: "make the investment AI pick 100 levels at a time instead of 1").
- **Status:** built 2026-11-27. **One short session in game** the same night read its four engine facts as holding and the pass at a quarter to a third of its time (§ the first run, below). Not yet seen: a country switching on by itself, who owns and pays for a batch, and what a long game does with it (`docs/testing/perf-fixes-playtest.md` § Batched private investment).
- **Files:** `common/scripted_effects/te_batch_investment_effects.txt` (the hook, the monthly pass, the self-checks), `common/script_values/te_batch_investment_values.txt` (tuning and the batch's size), `common/scripted_triggers/te_batch_investment_triggers.txt` (the rule), `common/on_actions/te_batch_investment_on_actions.txt`, and two generated files (`scripts/generators/gen_batch_investment.py`): `te_batch_investment_generated.txt`, a branch for each of the 93 buildings that get batches, since `start_privately_funded_building_construction` takes a literal building type, and `te_batch_investment_generated_triggers.txt`, the 12 of them that make a local good.
- **The hook.** The engine fires `on_start_expanding_building` (ROOT = the building) as a level is queued, by anyone. `te_bi_on_start_expanding` turns away every country that is not watched with one variable test. For a watched country `te_bi_hook` reads whether its investors placed the level (next bullet) and, if the country is batching, sizes a batch and queues it through the generated `te_bi_queue_batch`.
- **Who placed the level.** Script has no "who queued this", so it reads the queue: a watched country keeps the length of its private queue as of the last hook call in one of its states (`var:te_bi_seen`), and a call that finds the queue longer is a level its investors queued (`te_bi_note_pick`). A government level leaves the private queue as it was; another country's investors queue into their own (assumed, fact 4 below). Two things it gets wrong, both small: the first pick after a week's levels finish reads as nobody's (completions shortened the queue), and a pick abroad lengthens the queue without a call at home, so the next call at home is taken for the investors' even if it was the government's or a foreign investor's. That one needs room under the ceiling, which the investors' pass has just filled.
- **A batch** (`te_bi_batch_extra`, building scope): one pick of the engine's stands for at most 100 levels, its own included (`te_bi_batch_max`), and a building never has more than a tenth of its built levels queued (`te_bi_share`); for a building that makes a good no market carries (electricity, transportation, services, digital access), 10 a pick and a fiftieth queued (`te_bi_local_batch_max`, `te_bi_local_share`; owner, 2026-11-26: the overshoot risk is "mainly transportation and network infrastructure"). What is queued for the building already, in any queue, comes off the room (`level_after_queued_constructions`), the engine's own level included. So a new building and one under 20 levels get no batch, each further pick of a large building in the same pass adds another hundred until its tenth is queued, and however long levels take, batches cannot stack past that tenth. Until the first run the hundred was also the most a building could have queued; the engine picks its best few buildings over and over, so more than half its picks were then single levels of buildings that already held their hundred (§ the first run). The batch is clipped to the room under `te_bi_ceiling` (900) and skipped when `can_queue_building_levels` says the building cannot take that many (counted as `capped`).
- **Which buildings.** Every building investors can build (not government funded, expandable, not `can_build_private = { always = no }`), less three whose private gate counts levels, which a batch could overrun and `can_queue_building_levels` does not read: the Extraplanetary Base and the Resort Colony (room) and the Trade Center. The generator stops on a building with a `can_build_private` gate nobody has read (`GATES_READ`, `EXCLUDED`).
- **The reserve.** The engine stops at 1,000 queued (`te_bi_engine_queue`). Batches stop at 900, so the engine fills the last 100 slots (`te_bi_reserve`) a level at a time: about 2 s a week for a country at the limit, against 22. The slots do two jobs. They keep some investment abroad: script can only queue at home (the effect takes a state and no investor, so a level queued in another country's state would be that country's), **picks abroad stay single levels**, and with batches filling the queue at home there is room for fewer of them. And they are how script sees that the engine still fills the queue to its limit (`var:te_bi_maxq` is read at the engine's picks), so the reserve must stay above 10.
- **The engine's limit is probably each country's own.** NAI's `CONSTRUCTION_MAX_NUM_PRODUCTION_BUILDING_CONSTRUCTIONS` defines give 1 + 0.05 for each point of construction a country makes a week, the scaled part at most 999: 1,000 from about 20,000 construction a week, fewer below. That matches the 2050 save's 1,000 and is not confirmed as the source. The mod does not set them; if it ever does, `te_bi_engine_queue` must follow (`test_batch_investment.py` fails).
- **Who batches.** Each monthly pulse (`te_bi_monthly_on_action`): a country with 200 private levels queued becomes watched (`te_bi_watch_start`), which means the hook counts its picks. A watched country starts batching when two things hold for the month (`te_bi_month_end`): its investors placed 800 levels **at home**, about 200 a week (`te_bi_on_levels`; owner: "once we're building hundreds of levels a week"), and its queue reached 990 at one of the engine's picks (`te_bi_engine_queue_near`). It stops under 400 at home (`te_bi_off_levels`; the count takes in the levels the batches added, so batching does not end itself) or when the queue no longer reaches 990. A country not batching whose queue falls under 100 is no longer watched (`te_bi_clear`).
- **Why only at the limit.** Where the engine itself refills the queue to 1,000 every week, batches change which levels are queued and not how many, so `te_construction_market_private_use_target`'s cap (queued levels, at most 1,000, times the per-project cap) and Excess Private Construction's tests read what they read before. A country held under the limit, by its pool's money, by the pass's own update count or by a lower limit of its own, would have its queue lengthened by batches instead, and with it the construction bought for it. So it never starts batching. One gap: a batching country whose pool income later falls keeps a full queue for as long as its pool can pay, since each of its fewer decisions still fills a hundred slots.
- **Why home picks only in the count.** A country whose investors build mostly abroad would have its few home batches crowd out the rest.
- **What it rests on** (read once, in the first run):
  1. `on_start_expanding_building` runs as the engine queues a level, inside its pass, with the level already counted. The construction sites have followed this hook since the construction market was added, so it fires; when in the tick is unread.
  2. The pass reads the queue's length again after each level, so script's levels count against its 1,000.
  3. `start_privately_funded_building_construction`, run from the hook, queues a level the investment pool pays for and its investors own. The 2050 probe ran it from an event: the queue went from 1,000 to 4,060, the levels were built, and a resource-capped building was refused. Nothing in the mod has queued construction from inside a construction hook before.
  4. A level a country's investors queue abroad counts in their own private queue, not the host's.
- **If one is wrong:**
  - *1:* no pick is seen, or the queue is already full when the hook runs. Nothing is batched, and `batch_invest_hook` or `batch_invest_month` says so.
  - *2:* the engine places its thousand whatever script adds, so the first batching pass leaves up to 900 levels too many in the queue, which get built. The first pick that finds more than 1,000 queued trips batching off in every country (`te_bi_trip_limit`, `batch_invest_limit`), so that happens once.
  - *3:* `batch_invest_queue` says what the effect did. Its START line prints before the first batch, so a crash there leaves it last in `debug.log`.
  - *4:* a watched country takes every foreign investor's level in its states for its own and batches it; a play-test row reads the queue.
  - In every case script queues nothing past the ceiling by its own reading, a level that comes back through the hook while a batch runs is never batched (`global_var:te_bi_placing`), and a batch that finds more levels queued than it asked for trips batching off (`te_bi_trip_runaway`). A trip lasts until the rule is switched off and on again.
- **Self-checks** (the header of each effect gives the verdicts and the fallback):

  | Line | When | BROKEN means |
  |---|---|---|
  | `batch_invest_queue` | Once a game, the first batch of two levels or more (START before it, verdict after) | The effect is refused from the hook, or queues nothing there. No verdict and the game gone: the engine does not take a level queued from inside its own hook |
  | `batch_invest_reentry` | The same batch | The hook runs before its level is counted, so picks are read one call late. `NO_VERDICT`: none of the batch's levels came back while it ran |
  | `batch_invest_hook` | OK once a game; BROKEN once for each country | That country's sixth month at the engine's limit, the pool paying, with under ten picks seen at home or under a twentieth of the queue's growth: the hook runs after the pass or not for private levels, and the country never batches |
  | `batch_invest_month` | Each country's first whole month of batching | The batches queued no more levels than were refused (`noroom`, `small`, `capped`, `unknown` and `short` say why) |
  | `batch_invest_limit` | Only ever BROKEN | The engine queued a level with more than 1,000 queued: it does not count script's levels. Batching is off until the rule is switched off and on |
  | `batch_invest_runaway` | Only ever BROKEN | A batch queued more than it asked for. Batching is off until the rule is switched off and on |
  | `batch_invest_flag` | Only ever BROKEN | `te_bi_placing` was still set at a monthly pulse; cleared |

  `TE_BI on` and `TE_BI off` mark each switch, and `TE_BI month` prints a batching country's month for its first six months and then each January: `picks_home` and `picks_other` (the engine's decisions) against `added` (the levels the batches stood for), with `short`, `over`, `noroom`, `small`, `capped`, `unknown`, `came_back`, `maxq` and `queue`.
- **The fallback if the hook is the wrong place** (`batch_invest_hook` BROKEN, or `batch_invest_month` with every pick `noroom`): batch from a pulse instead. Each week walk the country's states for buildings `is_under_construction` with less than their tenth queued, queue each one's batch, and hold the ceiling above the engine's limit so its pass rests in the weeks between. The 2050 probe measured that: with 4,060 queued the pass took 0.49 s. It cannot tell a government level from a private one, and levels queued past 1,000 are built on top of the engine's thousand a week, so it changes how much is built and is the owner's call.
- **The first run in game** (2026-11-27, harness session `20261127T082220Z`, `/mnt/d/vic3te-data/batch-invest-2026-11-27/`): the 2050 USA copy on the deployed build of `7d353f90a` with this branch's files as a variant mod, batching forced on for the USA by a setup (`te_bi_watch_start`, `te_bi_on`), 25 days. It ran the first batch rule, a hundred as the most a building could have queued.
  - **The pass:** 5.25 s and 8.05 s on the two full weekly ticks, against 24.9–25.2 s and 20.1–20.2 s on the same ticks of the report's three unchanged runs. Wall time over 14 February to 1 March: 25.7 s a week against 39.9–40.4. The cheap first tick after the load was 1.86 s, as before.
  - **Decisions:** 598 picks in the two passes, where the engine made about 2,000: 19 took batches that added 1,448 levels (`short`, `over`, `capped`, `unknown` all 0), 263 found the queue at the ceiling (the reserve's hundred a pass), and 315 were turned away as `small`. 13.3 s over 598 picks is 22 ms a pick, the report's figure, so the pass's cost does follow its decisions.
  - **The 315** are why the batch rule changed: the first batch was for a building of 6,579 levels, and with a hundred as the most it could have queued, every further pick of it was a single level. The test's model shows the same when three buildings take nearly all the picks (220 to 320 decisions a pass under the first rule, about 120 under this one). The rule as it stands has not been timed in game; by the model the pass should be near 2.7 s.
  - **The four facts:** the hook ran inside the pass (the first batch found 16 levels queued); the engine never queued with more than 1,000 queued (`maxq` 1,000, the queue 1,000 at the pulse, no trip); the effect queued all 99 levels asked from inside the hook, and nothing crashed; and of 85 countries, 4 held 805 more queued levels in their states than in their own queues while 7 held 999 more in their queues than in their states, so a level placed abroad sits with the country that placed it. (The two sums differ by 194, unexplained; the walk counted buildings `is_under_construction`.)
  - **Script's own levels do not come back through the hook** while the batch runs (`batch_invest_reentry` NO_VERDICT, `came_back` 0).
  - **The USA of 2050 invests almost nothing abroad:** 1 pick in 598 lengthened its queue without a call at home, and 999 of its 1,000 queued levels were in its own states. The loss of investment abroad below is then no cost to it; other countries do hold levels abroad (the 7 above).
  - **A save begun before the rule read the rule's default:** `has_game_rule = te_batch_investment_enabled` yes, `_disabled` no.
- **What it changes in play:**
  - **Less investment abroad** from a batching country. By the arithmetic, not a measurement: a country that turns its whole queue over each week placed 1,000 levels a week at whatever share abroad its investors choose, and now places about 115 engine picks at that share, so about a tenth as many levels abroad with the reserve at 100. The test's model, with three picks in ten abroad, gives 11 to 15%. The monthly line prints home and other picks, so the share can be read; the reserve is the lever, at 22 ms a slot a week.
  - **Slower privatization.** The same pass buys government-owned levels instead of building (`BUILDING_PRIVATIZATION_CHANCE`), and a purchase does not fill the queue. With about a ninth of the decisions, a batching country's investors privatize about a ninth as fast.
  - **Bigger steps.** A building can have up to a tenth of its size queued at once, a hundred levels a pick. One that draws local goods (electricity, transportation, services) raises its state's demand for them by as much at once, while the buildings that make them there grow by a fiftieth.
  - **The engine's own checks are made once a batch.** It weighs staffing, infrastructure and profit before each level; the levels of a batch ride on one such check.
- **Rule:** `te_batch_investment_rule` (enabled by default), read through `te_bi_rule_on` / `te_bi_rule_off`, both written against the disabled setting so a game begun before the rule plays with batching on. Switched off mid-game, the hook stops batching at the next level queued and each watched country's variables go at its next monthly pulse, which also re-arms a trip.
- **A revolution's winner** inherits the country's variables, which is all the system keeps (no modifier, no list, nothing on a state or a building), so it carries on (`test_batch_investment.py`, `ARevolution`).
- **Tests:** `test_batch_investment.py` runs the script's own text through a small interpreter against a model of the weekly pass: as the script assumes the engine behaves (the pass makes under 15% of its decisions, the queue never passes 1,000, every self-check reads OK; two giants investing in each other; a country under the limit never batches), and with each assumption turned off in the model (the script stays bounded, the matching line says BROKEN, and where the engine ignores script's levels batching trips after one pass). The model is the test's, not the engine's.
- **History:** an independent review before the first PR (2026-11-27) found that the first draft kept batching when the engine did not count script's levels, switched on for countries under the limit and for investors who build mostly abroad, could stack batch on batch where levels are slow, and could print every self-check OK from a small country while a giant gained nothing. The trip, the two gates, the queued-level cap and the per-country verdicts answer those.
- **Unused since:** `queue_more_private_investment`, `expand_random_building`, `expand_building_private_construction` and `needed_private_levels` (`extra_effects.txt`, `extra_script_values.txt`), the older bulk pass nothing calls. It picks for itself (a random profitable building over 90% occupancy), which never founds a building and gates on occupancy, which flat crews make meaningless. Left in place; removing it is the owner's call.

## Construction as a Market Good (FMC architecture)

> Cross-reference: `docs/vanilla/vanilla_economy_reference.md` § 9 establishes the vanilla two-FCFS-queue model (government queue from treasury, private queue from IP, allocation set by `country_private_construction_allocation_mult`). This mod adds a market layer underneath without replacing the queues. Based on the third-party *Free Market Construction* mod (credited in `README.md`); every file is prefixed `te_construction_market_*`.

**Why:** Decoupling construction *capacity* (the vanilla `country_construction` country-resource that the FCFS queues spend) from construction *output* (what the construction sector actually produces) lets per-state economics influence national construction throughput. A goods-side stockout in one region propagates through the price loop instead of disappearing into a black-box country resource.

**Architecture (3 layers):**

1. **Construction-sector building, REPLACE'd.** `building_construction_sector` (vanilla) is REPLACE'd in `common/buildings/te_construction_market_site.txt` (its group `bg_construction` is REPLACE'd as ordinary heavy industry in `extra_building_groups.txt`, so it is no longer government-funded), and its tier PMs are REPLACE'd in `common/production_methods/extra_pms.txt` (`pm_wooden_buildings`, `pm_iron_frame_buildings`, `pm_steel_frame_buildings`, `pm_arc_welded_buildings`, plus the mod tiers `pm_reinforced_concrete_buildings`, `pm_advanced_construction`, `pm_nanomaterial_buildings` and `pm_self_replicating_construction` (Von Neumann Machines, era 13) INJECTed into `pmg_base_building_construction_sector`). Each tier now outputs `goods_output_construction_add = N` (1, 2, 3.5, 5, 6, 10, 16, 24 across tiers) — i.e. the construction sector produces the **`construction`** market good rather than direct country construction points. State-level `state_construction_mult` is preserved so per-state efficiency still matters. Each tier takes only goods its era can make: Advanced Construction (era 7) uses automobiles, engines and electricity rather than Software, which no ordinary building makes before the Software Industry (`computer_networks`, era 8). Software enters construction with Computer-Aided Design automation and Nanomaterial Buildings. `test_construction_tiers.py` (`TierInputsExistByTheirEra`) checks every tier, automation method and Construction Site twin (2026-11-03). Barracks under the Engineering & Logistics principle (`pm_principle_engineering_and_logistics`) also produce 0.2 per level.
2. **The `construction` good itself.** Defined in `common/goods/timeline_extended_extra_goods.txt`. `cost = 1000`, `category = industrial`, `traded_quantity = 0.1`, `consumption_tax_cost = 400` (Authority cost if the player consumption-taxes it). High base cost makes it a load-bearing market input.
3. **Auto-placed consumer building: `te_construction_market_site`.** Defined in `common/buildings/te_construction_market_site.txt`, group `te_construction_market_bg` (government-funded, hidden from the outliner). `common/defines/te_construction_market_defines.txt` points `NCountry.CONSTRUCTION_CAMP_BUILDING` at it, which is what makes the engine charge its inputs only for the points actually spent — to the treasury or the investment pool, by queue. Flags: `buildable = yes` and `expandable = yes` for direct games, but `can_build_government` is false in a market game, so nobody builds one (script's own placement raises `te_cm_placing_site`, which the trigger accepts); `downsizeable = yes`, also for direct games, so a player or the AI can remove a site — capacity is spread over the remaining staffed sites each pulse, and `te_construction_market_ensure_site` re-places a capital site at the end of the pulse if none is left; `min_raise_to_hire = -1.0`, `levels_per_mesh = -1`. Placed automatically by `te_construction_market_sector_placement_effects.txt` in each state where construction starts, and one always stands in the capital. Its single PM (`pm_te_construction_market_base` in `common/production_methods/te_construction_market_pms.txt`) consumes 1 `goods_input_construction` per workforce and produces 1 `country_construction_add` — i.e. it converts the construction good back into the vanilla queue currency. Each weekly pulse re-applies `te_construction_market_capacity_modifier` (`building_throughput_add`) to every site that has its crew, at one multiplier for all of them, so those sites together deliver the week's public + private purchase (**Sizing the sites** (`te_construction_market_apply_per_site_modifier`, values in `te_construction_market_sector_placement_values.txt`). A site delivers its throughput times its staffing, and the government queue spends whatever the sites deliver at the treasury's cost, so the rule is never to size past the purchase.

- **A site's crew.** A site is placed with ten laborer jobs a level. A building hires a tenth of its jobs a week, whatever its group's hiring rates say (measured 2026-11-13 in a 1945 game; `docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`, "First verdict"), so in its first week a site takes one laborer a level. The crew pass (`te_construction_market_site_crew`, run before every sizing) then gives the site's state `te_construction_market_crew_modifier`, which cuts the group's jobs there by 90%, to that one laborer. The pass marks the state (`var:te_cm_site_crew`) once the site is settled, and the mark is what the sizing counts. This is the Free Market Construction mod's device; this copy dropped it on 2025-09-02 and has it back since 2026-11-13. All of the pass's state is in state variables (`te_cm_site_crew_cut` says the modifier is there), because a building holds no variables and `has_modifier` does not see a modifier added or removed earlier in the same effect.
  - **What the engine does around the cut** (read in the first game on it, 1947; "Second verdict"). The jobs are cut at once. The site still takes one more laborer at the engine's next weekly employment update: ten of twelve sites held two laborers on one job a week after their cut. The engine lets laborers above a site's jobs go only at an update after that. From its fourth week a site is steady at one laborer of one (131 of the 132 counted sites four to ten weeks old).
  - **A site is counted only once a weekly pass after its cut finds it at 105% staffed or less.** Until then it settles: not counted, at −100% throughput, so it delivers nothing while it holds more laborers than jobs. A new site is therefore counted about three weeks after it is placed, and the capital's site carries the purchase meanwhile. Every site with a laborer is cut, a site of a save from before this rule with its ten too (owner, 2026-11-13: the engine lets them go). If a site is still over at its eighth weekly pass, the cut comes off and it is counted on its ten jobs.
  - A cut made by a purchase button, between two weekly passes, is not read until a weekly pass at least eight days on: the next one may come before the engine's employment update, and would find the site at one of one with its second laborer still to arrive.
  - A marked site found over 105% loses its mark and settles again, before the sizing.
  - A site with no laborer loses the cut and the mark, so it has its ten jobs again and can hire one. A cut site whose laborer left would otherwise hold one job and a hiring limit of a tenth of a laborer.
  - Staffing is read off a site only in the weekly pulse (`var:te_cm_weekly_pass`), never when a purchase button sizes the sites.
  - The modifier is bookkeeping and hidden: it has the mod's blank icon (`modifier_system.dds`), so the state panel's modifier rows leave it out by name and `gui_state_hidden_modifier_count` counts it (`test_hidden_state_modifiers.py`).
  - The modifier sits on the state and cuts the whole group, so `te_construction_market_clear_crew` takes it off wherever a market site is placed or removed, and off every state on a switch to direct construction, where the site is a sector in the same group and would run at full output on a tenth of its workers. A state whose site was downsized away keeps it until a site stands there again.
- **Sizing.** Every crewed site gets the same throughput, `te_construction_market_site_throughput`: the purchase over the crewed sites' levels (`te_construction_market_site_levels`; the market places level-1 sites, but one kept from a direct game can have more). A site without a crew gets no modifier and is not counted, so it neither thins the others' share nor adds to it when it hires. **Staffing is not read** by the values (owner, 2026-11-13): they read the mark, which the crew pass sets.
- **The capital's site** (owner, 2026-11-14). One site always stands in the capital (`te_construction_market_ensure_site`, from the weekly pulse and wherever a site is removed) and keeps its laborer, so a country never waits for a new site's first hire: when construction starts in a new state, the capital's site carries the purchase while the new one hires. So that it does not then take a share from the sites where the building is, it stands by (`var:te_cm_site_standby` on the capital, set by `te_construction_market_capital_site_standby` between the crews and the count: not counted, −100% throughput) while another site has a crew and nothing is being built in the capital itself. A site at −100% keeps its staff: in three saves of 1945, sites at −100% for want of a purchase were as well staffed as the rest (19 of 23 full). Until 2026-11-14 the capital's site was removed when construction started elsewhere, and a country whose only site moved built nothing for a week or two each time.
- **A state that changes hands** (`on_state_owner_change`, every path: `te_construction_market_site_arrived`). A site that arrives with a state brings its laborer, its mark and a throughput that the engine reads from then on off its new owner's variable, so with n sites of its own and k arrived ones the new owner would get (n + k) / n times its purchase until its next weekly sizing. The hook takes the site out of the count at once: it goes if nothing is being built in the state and the state is not the new owner's capital, and otherwise it settles at −100% until the new owner's next weekly pass counts it. Nothing is sized from the hook, since one peace deal changes many states' owners.
- So the sites never deliver more than the purchase, plus a tenth of a point for a site that has just hired. They deliver less, and what is not delivered is not paid for, in two cases: no site has a crew (a new game's first weeks, or a country whose capital's site has lost its laborer: nothing until a site has hired, been cut and settled, three or four weeks), and a crewed site was removed between pulses (the others keep their share until the next one; if it was the only one beside the capital's, which is then standing by, nothing is delivered until that pulse, a week at most).
- One value for all the sites also ends the leak the old per-site value had: the multiplier is read from one country variable (`te_cm_capacity_mult`) that the engine re-reads between pulses.
- **How it got here.** Until 2026-11-13 each site got its share divided by its own staffing, and a site under 10% staffed got ten times its share, each value through that one country variable (Workshop report: millions spent on a purchase of 50,000, the sites' throughput at 5,000%, 2,000% and 15,000% in three successive weeks). #1770 gave every site one throughput and took each to fill within its first week; in play a country whose construction moves from state to state got a fifth of its purchase (Russia 18%, 19% and 0% at three quarterly readings). `test_construction_market_site_sizing.py` runs the script itself: a small interpreter executes the crew pass, the sizing and the self-checks over a model of the sites, week by week, with the engine letting a surplus go at once, at its weekly update or never, the weekly hiring before or after the pulse, and the cut taking or not. Four self-checks read it in the game (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`): `construction_site_crew` (a site cut at one laborer of ten reads full at a later weekly pass), `construction_site_surplus` (the engine lets a cut site's surplus laborers go, and after how many weekly passes), `construction_site_delivery` (fully staffed crewed sites deliver the purchase) and `construction_site_unstaffed` (no site with a crew eight weeks running, while the delivery check has no verdict).

**The chain end-to-end:**

```
Construction Sector (vanilla, REPLACE'd) → produces construction good
   ↓
Market: construction good supply meets construction good demand at a price
   ↓
te_construction_market_site (auto-placed, the engine's construction camp) consumes construction good
   ↓ produces
country_construction_add (vanilla queue currency)
   ↓
Vanilla two-FCFS queues (government from treasury / private from IP) spend it
   ↓
New buildings get built / expanded
```

**Purchases and the queue split.** The government purchase is `te_construction_market_public_setting`, capped each week at what the government queue can absorb (`te_construction_market_public_use_target`: queued government levels × `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`). The setting has two modes. *Fixed Quantity* (the default, and the AI's only mode) reads the country variable `te_construction_market_public_target`, set by the player in the construction panel or by `te_construction_market_ai_update_buy` for the AI (The AI's purchase, below). *Fixed Budget* (`te_construction_market_budget_mode_on`: the country variable `te_construction_market_budget_mode`) reads `te_construction_market_budget_quantity`, which the weekly pulse (`te_construction_market_update_budget_quantity`, before the sites are sized) moves 1/N of the way (`te_construction_market_budget_shift_weeks`) toward the player's weekly budget `te_construction_market_public_budget` ÷ `te_construction_market_price`, and onto it once within a point. The partial step damps the loop through the price: the government's purchase moves the price it is divided by, and if a 1% larger purchase raises the price by e%, each week leaves 1 − (1 + e)/N of the gap, so N = 1 + e closes it at once to first order and a larger N approaches without overshooting. N is read off the engine's price formula, `price = base × (1 + PRICE_RANGE × clamp(imbalance / (F − 1), −1, 1))` with `imbalance = (BUY − SELL) / min(BUY, SELL)` and `F` = `BUY_SELL_DIFF_AT_MAX_FACTOR` (the mod's two defines are mirrored as `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_PRICE_RANGE` and `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_BUY_SELL_DIFF_AT_MAX_FACTOR`, and `test_construction_market_budget_step.py` pins them to `extra_defines.txt`). It is taken from the chord between the purchase in force and the one the budget wants at today's price, N = 1 + T/(T − Q) × (price at T ÷ price now − 1), the two prices coming from that formula with the government's goods (points × `te_construction_market_goods_per_point`) added to everyone else's buy orders; the chord, unlike the slope at either end, sees the price stop moving at its floor and ceiling. In a large market a small purchase barely moves the price (e ≈ 0.01 for the construction panel's 1,000 points against 325,000 goods wanted, so N ≈ 1 and the quantity reaches the budget in a week); when the purchase is a large part of the market, or the price is near its floor, N grows. A raise is never spread over fewer than `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_BUDGET_MIN_RAISE_WEEKS = 2` weeks, because an overshoot on a raise spends past the budget and the model cannot see other buyers reacting, the site's local price or an engine lag; a cut has no floor, because an overshoot there spends less than the budget. The model sets the speed, not the destination: each week's target is still the budget ÷ the price the market shows, so the quantity settles where spending equals the budget whatever the model gets wrong. While the quantity is still moving, the panel says how much of the gap closes this week (`te_construction_market_budget_stepping_sgui`, `te_construction_market_display_budget_step_percent`). A budget cut to 0 zeroes the quantity at once (`te_construction_market_clear_budget`). The mode buttons carry the purchase over: into Fixed Budget, the budget is the in-force purchase (or the setting, while none is in force) × the price, to the nearest 1,000, and the quantity starts there (`te_construction_market_budget_seed[_quantity]`); into Fixed Quantity, the target is the budget quantity, rounded. The AI's weekly purchase clears the mode, so a country a player left in Fixed Budget returns to the AI's quantity. The private purchase (`te_construction_market_private_use_target`) is a 24th of the investment pool a week (`te_construction_market_private_spending_target`), so the pool settles at 24 weeks of gross income, divided by the smoothed price (`te_construction_market_price_stabilizer`) and capped by the private queue. It reads the pool alone, so it keeps spending when gross income is 0 or less, as under Overinvestment (§ Excess Private Construction); an earlier gross × pool ÷ (24 × gross) form went to 0 or below there. The private share of construction (`te_construction_market_private_share_modifier`, `country_private_construction_allocation_mult = 0.01` × the private share in percent) is set weekly from those two purchases; `common/laws/construction_system_law_injections.txt` cancels the economic-system laws' vanilla allocations so nothing else moves it.

**The AI's purchase.** An AI government's purchase is set each week by `te_construction_market_ai_purchase_target` (`te_construction_market_ai_values.txt`), which `te_construction_market_ai_update_buy` writes to `te_construction_market_public_target` from the weekly pulse, for AI countries in a market game only. A player's purchase is never touched, a direct game has no purchase, and a country a player takes over keeps the AI's last figure until the player changes it; that figure is now often a fraction of a point, which the panel shows as it stands (cosmetic). The value is 0 while the government queue is empty, and while the engine has paused the country's construction (`is_construction_paused`), whatever its bureaucracy (*Through the engine's pause*, below, is the record of an exception that was tried and removed). Otherwise it is the larger of two amounts, either of which can be nothing, capped at what the queue can take (queued government levels × `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`):

- **The surplus.** `net_fixed_income` (the budget before construction, which the engine counts as a temporary expense) over the smoothed price, times 1 + reserves − debt (`scaled_gold_reserves`, `scaled_debt`), moved by the interest rate (*The rate*, below), less a war brake and a banking brake while reserves are thin, never under 0.1. At a middling rate, with no reserves and no debt it spends the surplus exactly; reserves raise it and are drawn down; debt lowers it and is paid off. At a high rate it holds part of the surplus back until it keeps a reserve, and at a low one it borrows a little.
- **The floor** (`te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor`, 2026-11-15), for a government whose surplus buys less:
  - a twentieth of `fixed_income` over the smoothed price, never more than 10 points;
  - paid from reserves: whole while they are a tenth of their limit or more above the reserve the rate asks for (none at a low or middling rate, up to a tenth at a high one), less in a straight line below that, and nothing with none to spare;
  - with no reserves, a country that has a surplus is still held to a tenth of that twentieth (a 200th of fixed income, at most one point) whatever its debt and the brakes take off the surplus term, and never to more than the surplus itself, so the surplus pays it. No part of the floor is on credit (owner, 2026-11-16);
  - while `bureaucracy < 0`, a fifth of fixed income over the smoothed price higher (`te_construction_market_ai_bureaucracy_purchase`), whatever the reserves and the debt. So a country short of bureaucracy buys **at least a fifth** of its fixed income's worth. It is a floor, not a fifth on top of the surplus: where the surplus term already buys more, the shortage adds nothing. The fifth is the only part of the floor that can go on credit; the surplus term goes on credit only at a low rate (*The rate*, below).
  - The war and banking brakes cut the surplus term only. The floor ignores them.
- **Nothing at all** for a government with no surplus, no reserves and no shortage of bureaucracy. It cannot pay for anything, so it buys nothing, and its queued level waits where it stands until one of the three returns: a market game has no base construction (the mod empties `country_gdp_construction`; Base Construction is a direct game's). That is intended.
- **Not rounded.** The purchase was rounded to whole points, which the old floor hid. A small economy now buys a fraction of a point a week: a surplus of 143 buys 0.11 of a point at no debt, and 0.08 at Kunduz's debt of 32% of its ceiling.

*The rate* (#1877; owner, 2026-11-16: "If interest rates are high, requiring a reserve makes sense. If low, no need, and you could even allow spending in (small amounts of) debt."). The rule reads the rate the country pays (`var:te_rate_paid_pts`) over the world reference rate (`var:te_mon_era_base_now`). Over 4 points it asks the purchase to keep a reserve, rising to a tenth of the limit at 7: the surplus term saves twice the shortfall's share of the surplus and draws down only the reserves above it, and the floor draws only those. Under 4 it allows a debt, rising to a tenth of the debt ceiling at 2, and never more than the debt whose interest at the rate 7 over the reference is a third of the surplus: the surplus term adds twice the credit not yet used, so a country with a surplus and no reserves borrows a fifth of its surplus a week at first and settles at two-thirds of the allowance. With a level always queued that debt stands (the factor is 1 there), and while a government holds debt the engine queues nothing for it that it does not count critical, so the pass below is what builds for it. The credit's switch is `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_CREDIT_MAX`: at 0 there is none and nothing else changes. At 4, and where the monetary update has not run, it is the rule as above. A financial crisis reads as 7 at least, and there is no credit at war or while bubble pressure is 50 or more. The rate is a real rate (headline inflation subtracted), what the treasury pays. A monthly pass queues one level for an AI government the engine has left with nothing queued for a month, where the rate allows it the means: the first that applies of a Government Administration while net bureaucracy is under 2% of its usage, the country's first power plant, a railway where a state is short of infrastructure, a university while innovation is under nine-tenths of its cap, and a highway or an airport in a major industrial state, each only where the surplus pays for it within two years. The curve, the read, the evidence, the debt's cost and the pass: `ai.md` § AI Government Construction; every number with its reason: `te_construction_market_ai_values.txt`, THE RATE and THE PASS.

*Why the floor is paid from reserves and not on credit.* (The rate's credit, above, is on the surplus term and sized on the surplus, so a country with no surplus borrows nothing at any rate.) A floor on credit, however small, is the old floor again for a country whose surplus is under it: a few points now, interest from then on, and less built in every later year. A first draft of this change let the floor borrow up to 10% of the ceiling (where the engine's AI starts to call debt significant, `CHANGE_TAX_SIGNIFICANT_DEBT_THRESHOLD`); in the model below a country with a surplus of 3% of income had built less than under the reserve rule by year 20, and was in default in year 56. Until 2026-11-16 one part was left on credit, a tenth of the floor with no reserves, so that a queued level was never left standing. For a country with 3,000 a week and no surplus it bought about 0.6 points a year, so a 100-point level would have taken about 160 years, and after thirty years the country owed a fifth of its ceiling for about 19 points, most of the debt being interest. The PR's reviewer recommended dropping it where net fixed income is zero or below and the owner agreed. The tenth is now capped at the surplus, which is the same rule without a step at zero: a surplus of nothing pays for nothing, a surplus under a 200th of income pays for what it covers, and a larger one for the 200th. At exactly no reserves, then, the floor is the smaller of a tenth of the twentieth and the surplus itself; it exceeds the surplus term only where debt or the brakes cut that term under it.

*The bureaucracy clause.* A country short of bureaucracy loses tax in proportion (vanilla's `administrative_overburden`), and a Government Administration level costs 100 points, so the clause lets it spend at least a fifth of its income on construction whatever its surplus. It was written on 2024-08-26 as income ÷ the price held between 500 and 1,500 ÷ 5, when the construction good's base price was 1,000. On 2024-09-03 the base price was halved and the bounds became 50 and 150 (a tenth, not a half); on 2026-03-19 the base went back to 1,000 and the bounds stayed. Since then the clause divided by 150 at every price in play: a fifth of income bought at 150 a point is 1.7 times the country's whole income at 1,250. Sokoto, 4 bureaucracy short on an income of 12,261, was set to 26 points (20 after the queue's cap, about 27,000 a week). The clause now divides by the smoothed price like the surplus term, and reads `fixed_income`: `income` also counts temporary income, which in the probe's rows was about 200 a week on small countries, a tenth of a point of private construction at 1,999 that the investment pool repays the treasury. It stays outside the reserve rule on purpose: the engine's own pause for a construction it calls critical is 99% of the ceiling (`CONSTRUCTION_DEBT_PAUSE_CRITICAL_CONSTRUCTION`), not 40%. Because it is part of the floor, it changes the purchase only where the surplus term buys less than about a fifth of fixed income, and the 1836 median surplus is a third: for most countries a shortage changes nothing. Sokoto's 4.49 points in the table below are its surplus term (6,174 ÷ 1,369 × 0.996); its floor with the clause is 0.04 + 1.72 = 1.77. Whether the clause should instead be a fifth on top of the surplus term is the owner's open decision (`ai.md` § AI Government Construction).

*The surplus at zero or below.* The surplus term is then 0 and only the floor buys anything: a twentieth of income while reserves last, nothing without them, and at least a fifth when short of bureaucracy. That is intended (owner, 2026-11-16). A government that cannot cover its fixed bills has nothing to build with, and its reserves are about to go on those bills.

*The cap, and the engine's queue.* The purchase is capped at queued government levels × the weekly maximum per level: 10 at the start, 20 with Urbanization, 40 by the base game's last technology that raises it, 140 for Germany in the 1958 save. How many levels an AI queues is the engine's. `CONSTRUCTION_MAX_NUM_GOVERNMENT_BUILDING_CONSTRUCTIONS_BASE` 1 and `_SCALED` 0.05 allow one government building plus one per 20 points of "construction production"; the production buildings a government builds have an allowance of the same size, and military buildings follow the army the AI wants. In the probe's 1836 runs the government queue held one level in 20 and 22 of the 38 AI countries and 2 to 5 in 12 and 13, small ones among them (an income of 667 with 3 queued), so at the start the cap is seldom what binds: the United States bought 26 and 32 points with 3 and 4 levels queued, and only the countries short of bureaucracy were held to it (Great Britain at 25, one level's maximum). Late in the owner's games it binds for the rich: in 1958, 110 of 239 AI countries had exactly one government item queued and 52 none, and Germany bought 140, one level's maximum, on a GDP of 3.1 billion. A country that could afford more than one level's weekly maximum is held to it unless the engine queues a second level, and two engine rules decide that. One is the allowance (what "construction production" reads in a market game is unread; the save study took it for near zero). The other is the reserve gate: no new government building unless reserves stay above the gate after its cost (`CONSTRUCTION_RESERVES_NEW_CONSTRUCTIONS`: a tenth in vanilla, a fiftieth in the mod since #1877). The script bears on the second, because the surplus term spends reserves down (× (1 + reserves)) whenever a level is queued: an AI finishes a level, saves until it passes the gate, then queues the next. A country in debt has no reserves and cannot queue at all, apart from what the engine counts critical (an administration while short of bureaucracy is queued with none: `ai.md`), which was one more cost of the old floor. The levers are the two defines and how far the surplus term draws reserves. The reserve gate went to 0.02 on 2026-11-16 (#1877; the evidence, vanilla's second gate of the same tenth and the fallbacks are in `ai.md` § AI Government Construction); the allowance and the surplus term are unchanged. How the engine prices "the cost of a new construction" in a market game is unread.

**Construction-cost-scaling layer.** The `construction_cost_scaling` static modifier (above) applies `goods_input_construction_mult = 1` (multiplied by a per-country GDPpc-driven multiplier). This scales how much *construction good* every consumer burns — each construction site per point delivered, and every building's maintenance PM. So a richer country burns more construction good per same building → competes more for the same supply → stockouts and higher prices → fewer net `country_construction_add` produced → vanilla queues stall on availability rather than treasury.

**Buildings consume the construction good as maintenance.** Each maintenance group (`pmg_maintenance` and its `_light`, `_heavy`, `_intensive`, `_mine` and `_graded` variants, all hidden in PM displays) carries a rate of `goods_input_construction_add` per level, set by the kind of building and, for mines, power plants, railways and ports, by the method it runs (§ Maintenance tiers); it is on 75 building types — industry, power, transport infrastructure (railways, ports, airports, highways), services, mines — not farms, plantations or urban centres, and most mines only once they are mechanized. A few company buildings in `unique_pms.txt` consume more (0.5–1). Construction is therefore not just an upfront build cost — it's an ongoing maintenance load tied to current economic activity.

### The sector's workforce and margin (2026-11-16)

Every tier of `pmg_base_building_construction_sector` hires a third of what it did, each profession divided by three to the nearest 5, and Wooden Buildings takes 30 wood instead of 40. The middle of the ladder then takes one input change a tier so that the first five tiers' wage breakevens climb evenly, about 1.6 times a step from 0.10 to 0.63: Iron-Frame 18 iron (was 20), Steel-Frame 25 steel (20), Arc-Welded 30 transportation (33.5). Each automation method in `pmg_construction_automation` cuts the same third, so it removes the same share of a tier's workforce as before, and takes about a third of its goods, so it pays for itself at about the wage it did; the principle group hires no one. The Construction Site's direct tiers copy these figures (§ Free Market Construction off).

**Why** (owner, 2026-11-16, the middle of three options put to him: "Workers per level cut to a third, plus wood 40 → 30 on Wooden Buildings"). The sector used 12 to 21 times the base game's labour per point (Wooden Buildings 6,000 a level for one unit, where vanilla's hires 1,000 for two points; Iron-Frame 6,500 for two, vanilla's 1,000 for five), and its margins at base prices could not pay it: a wage breakeven of 0.00 on Wooden Buildings, 0.04 on Iron-Frame and 0.12 on Steel-Frame, where vanilla's first textile method leaves about 0.11. An unattended AI run from 1836 to 1925 (build dab9335f1) showed what follows in the 20 largest economies: their manufacturing levels under half staffed matched their Construction Sector levels almost exactly all game (Great Britain in 1836, 80 against 82), so the sectors were very likely what was under-staffed; the construction good sold at 1.3 to 1.5 times base in every decade; sector levels per £1M of GDP halved (1.57 to 0.66); and the base game's AI does not expand a building under 80% employment (`PRODUCTION_BUILDING_LOW_EMPLOYMENT_THRESHOLD`). Private investors, who do 75–89% of a big economy's construction and spend a fixed 24th of the pool a week, got that many fewer points. Wages come out of value added, so the workforce per unit decides both whether the sector can staff and how large a share of GDP it is: a margin large enough for 6,000 workers would have made construction services a large share of GDP, which the owner did not want ("feels weird"). The tiers still hire 4 to 7 times vanilla's labour per point (Wooden Buildings 2,000 a point against 500, Arc-Welded 467 against 67).

**The even climb** (owner, the same day, on reading the cut's figures: "Seems like we might need to make steel frame a bit worse, iron frame a little better, and/or arc welded a bit better"). After the cut alone the first five tiers paid 0.10, 0.12, 0.36, 0.36 and 0.63: Steel-Frame three times Iron-Frame, and Arc-Welded no better than Steel-Frame. One input a tier, with outputs and workforce untouched, sets them at 0.10, 0.16, 0.25, 0.40 and 0.63, and the margin a level still rises at every step (200, 340, 595, 940, 1,475), so each tier is still worth switching to. `test_construction_tiers.py` (`test_the_ladder_climbs`) fails if a tier's breakeven or margin a level is not above the one before.

**The sector's share of GDP** (estimates: the probe has no GDP-by-sector reading). Construction spending is about 9–18% of a big economy's GDP. The sector's GDP is the share of the price it keeps over its inputs: about a fifth on Wooden Buildings at base price, so roughly 2–4% of GDP. A margin added for 6,000 workers at the same wage would have kept about two thirds of the price, 6–12% of GDP. At the baseline's 1.3 to 1.5 times base Wooden Buildings would keep 38–47%; the change should bring the price toward base.

**Against same-era manufacturing.** The median wage breakeven of the base methods of manufacturing buildings (under `bg_manufacturing`, company and exotic buildings left out) unlocked in the tier's era, from the same formula: era 1 (Wooden and Iron-Frame) 0.15, era 3 (Steel-Frame) 0.34, era 5 (Arc-Welded) 0.54 over only three methods (0.45 with the eras either side), era 7 (Reinforced Concrete and Advanced) 0.66, era 12 (Nanomaterial) 4.16 over seven widely spread methods (2.18 with the eras either side), era 13 (Self-Replicating) 2.60. So Iron-Frame and Reinforced Concrete pay about their era, and Wooden Buildings a little under it (0.10 against 0.15); Advanced Construction and Self-Replicating Construction pay well above theirs. On the even climb Steel-Frame (0.25 against 0.34, 0.30 with the eras either side) and Arc-Welded (0.40 against 0.54, 0.45 with the eras either side) pay below theirs: the climb puts the middle tiers between the two ends rather than at their era. If the sectors still do not staff in eras 3 to 5, those two are the next lever.

**The AI's sector value** (`te_construction_sector_ai_value`) is unchanged and reads the construction good's price, not profit. A sector with a margin at base price adds the engine's own profit terms to it: before, they had nothing to add at base price. If the price falls toward base as expected, the script's part falls with it, from about 8,000–10,000 at the baseline's 1.3 to 1.5 times base back to 5,000, so the two move against each other; below base both fall, which is what should stop a glut.

**Unverified:** everything above that happens in the game. That the sectors now staff, the price falls, the sector grows with GDP and investors' points per pound rise is for the next observer run (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md` § The Construction Sector's workforce). The first, one game from 1836 to 1850 (2026-11-16), read the 20 largest economies' sectors still under-staffed (81–93% of their levels under half staffed, a mean staffing of 32–37%) and the good at 1.23 to 1.30 times base, with no earlier reading of the sector's own staffing to set beside it (the list's "First reading").

### Maintenance tiers

`pmg_maintenance` was one rate (0.1 per level) for every building that had it. It is now six groups. Four are a single PM plus `pm_no_maintenance`; the fifth, for mines, changes with the equipment, and the sixth, for power plants, railways and ports, with the base method (below):

| Group / PM | Per level | Buildings |
|---|---|---|
| `pmg_maintenance_light` / `pm_maintenance_light` | 0.05 | Trade center, art academy, state youth centers, software industry; the four tech metals mines (lithium, nickel and cobalt, rare earths, platinum group metals) |
| `pmg_maintenance` / `pm_maintenance` | 0.1 | Everything else that had it: light, heavy, military and high-tech industry, synthetics and biotech plants, shipyard, skyscraper, tourism, construction sector, public housing (2026-10-31: the government building that is all plant), grand monument (at level 1; `gm_upkeep` charges the levels its monuments record, § Grand Monuments) |
| `pmg_maintenance_heavy` / `pm_maintenance_heavy` | 0.15 | Highway, airport, network infrastructure, grid transfer station, HVDC undersea interconnector; hydro and renewable plants; oil rig |
| `pmg_maintenance_intensive` / `pm_maintenance_intensive` | 0.2 | Nuclear and fusion plants, ocean mine, space mine |
| `pmg_maintenance_mine` / `pm_maintenance_traditional` or `pm_maintenance_mechanized_mine` | 0 or 0.05 | The base game's coal, iron, lead, copper, sulfur and gold mines, and the nine mod discoverable mines that run their equipment or copies of it |
| `pmg_maintenance_graded` / `pm_maintenance_graded_light`, `_standard` or `_heavy` | 0.05, 0.1 or 0.15 by base method | Power plant (fossil), railway, port |

The principle: upkeep follows the physical plant that wears with use, not the building's price or output. Offices, exchanges and institutions carry little; transport and power networks are all plant; hostile sites (reactors, the sea floor, vacuum) leave nothing to run down. Heavy industry keeps the original 0.1. Maintenance is charged per level, so a building whose level grows several times over up its method ladder pays by method instead (power plants, railways and ports, below).

- **Mines follow their equipment.** `pmg_maintenance_mine` lists `pm_maintenance_traditional` (free, first, so the default and the fallback), `pm_maintenance_mechanized_mine` (0.05) and `pm_no_maintenance`. The paid method's `unlocking_production_methods` names the equipment PMs of the six mines from the condensing engine pump on (condensing, diesel, high-pressure hydraulic, continuous miners, smart miners); picks and shovels and the atmospheric engine pump are the hand-worked tier. The free method carries `replacement_if_valid = pm_maintenance_mechanized_mine`, the vanilla mechanism that moves the train methods onto their principle variants, so the swap happens by itself when the equipment does. It is deliberately tied to the building's own PM, not to a technology: a technology-gated method would swap every mine in the country at once when the tech arrived, and each swap applies `pm_retooling`; here the swap comes with the equipment switch that already paid it. Vanilla drops a PM that depends on another PM when the other is swapped out, provided another PM in the group still fits, so going back to picks and shovels returns the mine to the free method. Nine of the mod's discoverable mines run those mines' equipment ladders, picks and shovels included, so they take this group too. Phosphate, potash and salt share the sulfur mine's groups, whose PMs the paid method's list already names. Bauxite, chromium, manganese and specialty alloys (the iron mine's ladder), precious metals (the lead mine's) and graphite (the coal mine's) run their own copies, which split the output across several goods (#1419, [Production Methods](production_methods.md) § The mod's mines and their co-products); `gen_mine_coproducts.py` lists the copied pumps in a generated tail of the paid method's list. They were on `pmg_maintenance_light` until 2026-10-23 on the belief that their equipment was all mechanized. The four tech metals mines run copies of the tech metals family's equipment, which starts at the condensing engine pump, and stay on `pmg_maintenance_light`. The test pins the unlock list to the equipment PMs; unverified in game: the automatic swap in both directions, and the default on a newly built mine.
- **Power plants, railways and ports follow their base method** (`pmg_maintenance_graded`, 2026-10-23). A level of these buildings grows several times over up its ladder while maintenance is per level, so the heavy tier's flat 0.15 crushed the early methods: at base prices a fossil plant level sells £750 (early plant) to £4,200 (modern oil), against £13,500–360,000 for a renewable, hydro, nuclear or fusion level, and 0.15 was 20% of the early plant's revenue and 10% of the coal-fired plant's; early trains add £135 a level over their inputs and the basic port £90. Measured as the multiple of base construction cost at which a method's profit at era wages reaches zero (cost scaling times the construction price over £1,000), the early plant broke even at ×1.1, coal-fired ×3.0, oil-fired ×4.7, steam trains ×1.4, the industrial port ×2.7, against medians of 12–30 for factories; a labour-tight country pays ×2.3 at £14 GDP per capita and ×3.4 at £25 (§ Construction Cost Scaling). The tiers: **light 0.05** for early and steam trains (and the steam variant), the anchorage, basic and industrial ports, and the early and coal-fired plants; **standard 0.1** for electric and diesel trains (and their variants), Centralized Traffic Control, the modern port, and the oil-fired and both modern fossil plants; **heavy 0.15** for containerized cargo, automated loading, autonomous trains and Vactrain Freight, and container, global and magnetic-drive ports. That puts coal-fired at ×9.0, oil-fired ×7.1, modern coal ×10.3, modern oil ×15.8, steam trains ×4.2, diesel ×7.9, the industrial port ×8.2, the modern port ×6.6. The early plant (×3.4) and early trains without passengers (×0.9) stay thin: their margins are £170 and £45 a level before maintenance. The mechanism is the mines': `pm_maintenance_graded_light` is first and needs no other method (the default and the fallback) and carries `replacement_if_valid = pm_maintenance_graded_standard`, which carries `replacement_if_valid = pm_maintenance_graded_heavy`. The standard method's `unlocking_production_methods` lists its own tier's base methods and the heavy tier's, the heavy method only its own, so a jump from a light-tier method straight to a heavy-tier one steps light → standard → heavy, and going down the dearer method loses its last unlocking method and falls back. The Transport-principle variants sit in their base method's tier, so the bloc's principle swap (§ Market settings without retooling or maintenance, Railways) changes no maintenance. Renewable, hydro, nuclear and fusion plants are separate buildings and keep their flat tiers. `test_maintenance_tiers.py` pins each base method's tier. Unverified in game, beyond the mines' questions: the two-step swap up from light to heavy (§ Market settings without retooling or maintenance, Play-test list, item 5).
- **Adding a tier or a building.** Give the building the group whose rate fits (`INJECT:` or the building's own `production_method_groups`). A new *rate* needs a PM, a group in `extra_pm_groups.txt`, `disable_<pm>` in the `no_maintenance` and `disabled` settings of `free_market_construction_rule`, loc for the PM and group, and the group's key in the six `ProductionMethodGroup.GetKey` checks (six keys now, `And(And4(...), And(Not(...), Not(...)))`) that hide maintenance in `gui/production_methods.gui`, `building_details_panel.gui`, `building_browser_panel.gui` and `goods_state_panel.gui`. `test_maintenance_tiers.py` checks the rule flags, the shared `pm_no_maintenance` and that every maintenance group is hidden in those panels.
- **Left without maintenance on purpose.** Farms, plantations, ranches, logging, fishing and whaling (land-based, and their tools and machines are already inputs). Farms and plantations could take the same equipment-driven split as mines (their harvesting PMs, such as steam threshers and tractors, are shared across crops, so one paid method could list them), but they are the largest sector and need their own balance decision. Also left out: universities and government buildings (paid from the budget, not built to earn); barracks, fortifications and bases; urban centers; canals (one level, not expandable, so the upkeep would be a rounding error); monuments and wonders (caretaker PMs); company buildings (their own recipes); the mod's late-game megastructures and the Strategic Reserve buildings (their own construction and cost rules). The original mines are not left out: they pay from the first mechanized pump, so an 1836 economy's hand-worked mines stay free while construction supply is scarcest.

**Retooling.** The engine puts the `pm_retooling` static modifier on a building that switches production methods (`NEconomy.RETOOLING_WEEKS = 260` in `extra_defines.txt`); this mod REPLACEs it with `goods_input_construction_mult = 10`, so a retooling building pays +1000% on its construction input. Two `free_market_construction_rule` settings waive the retooling cost, and one of them maintenance as well (§ Market settings without retooling or maintenance).

**AI production-method switching.** Vanilla's AI PM hysteresis (`NAI.PRODUCTION_METHOD_STICKINESS_UPGRADE` 0.95, `_DOWNGRADE` 0.75, `PRODUCTION_METHOD_CHANCE_TO_CHANGE` 0.1: the score of a method the building doesn't run is multiplied by the stickiness, so a switch needs a 5% or 33% better score) assumes switching is free. Under retooling it isn't: a switch costs 1,300 construction per 1 construction a week of maintenance, £195,000 per level of a heavy-tier building at base price (a port, railway or power plant until 2026-10-23; they now pay by method, a third or two thirds of that on their early and middle methods). An AI-only observer game to 2015 had 3,039 of 13,965 buildings retooling (ports 69%), and AI budgets ran 25–50% subsidies, mostly ports. So `extra_defines.txt` sets 0.9 / 0.6 / 0.05: an upgrade needs an 11% better score and a downgrade 67%, and the AI looks half as often. Retooling spread over five years for a downgrade (usually a price swing that reverses) is worth a 23–150% better score per level of those heavy-maintenance buildings at base prices, and over twenty years for an upgrade 6–38%; factories (0.1 maintenance, larger output) need only 13–33% and 3–8%, so they get more hysteresis than their retooling warrants. `pmg_water_personal_transport` (port ferries and cruise ships; no workers, ships and fuel into transportation) takes `ai_selection = most_productive` like vanilla's `pmg_passenger_trains`: under most_profitable AI ports flipped between a ferry tier and none at the transportation break-even price, 174 times on 443 ports in four months. `pmg_emissions_control` (ports, power plants, steel mills, automotive industries) gets the same fix. Its three tiers only cost, and AI ports switched between Basic and Advanced 35 times in four months of the 2015 run. Its tiers also carry `ai_value` 1000 / 3000 / 9000, so the AI should move up a tier and never down (owner ruling 2026-10-06). That is unconfirmed in game, because what `ai_value` does is undocumented (`scripting_best_practices.md` § Production Method `ai_value`). Stickiness cannot hold flips driven by the score's multiplicative factors: `PRODUCTION_METHOD_EMPLOYMENT_CHANGE_FACTOR` (2.0) multiplies or divides by 1 + 2 × the share of labor saved, 2 for a method that halves staffing (Centralized Traffic Control against diesel trains, already `most_productive`), and an input shortage multiplies by 0.01. Only the lower chance slows those. Urban centers, fishing wharves, ranches and logging camps have no construction input, so their switches show the Retooling modifier but cost nothing. Power plants' base group (`pmg_base_building_power_plant`) takes `most_productive` too (#933), as vanilla gives the port and railway base groups. Under most_profitable the AI moved a plant down to the least-loss method (the early plant) whenever its own output pushed the local electricity price down, and back up when the price recovered; a 4× fast run's 1912 save had 70% of AI power plants retooling against 38% of railways and 29% of ports, those plants carried 92% of the plants' subsidies, and the base-group switches (about 70 a year among 623 plants) came without an input shortage. The AI subsidises its power plants (vanilla's `must_have`; 604 of 630 flagged), so the retooling lands on its treasury. `edited_default_strategy.txt`'s INJECT of `nice_to_have` never applied: an INJECT can't change a key vanilla's `subsidies` block already holds (`scripting_best_practices.md`, read in game 2026-10-16). Since #981 every vanilla administrative strategy sets `building_power_plant = should_remove` (`te_ai_subsidy_strategies.txt`), a priority below `nice_to_have` that makes the AI drop the subsidy, so an AI coal or oil plant now carries its own losses (nuclear, fusion and renewable plants are separate buildings no strategy subsidizes). In the owner's late-game save, 3½ months took the AI's subsidized share of power plants from 99% to 1–19% by strategy, railways steady at 98%. The plants' automation and carbon-capture groups still switch (about 28 and 20 a year in that save): the first is partly the employment factor, and `most_productive` on the second would stop the AI ever capturing, since capture only costs. Check in game: `docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`.

**Player UI.** The domestic tab of the construction panel (`gui/construction_panel.gui`, the `te_construction_market_section` block) carries the Fixed Quantity / Fixed Budget mode buttons (`te_construction_market_set_{quantity,budget}_mode`; the read-only `te_construction_market_budget_mode_sgui` tells the GUI which controls and tooltips to show), the purchase control (+/- buttons bound to `te_construction_market_{increase,decrease}_target_*` in `common/scripted_guis/te_construction_market_scripted_gui.txt`, click / shift / ctrl / alt steps 1 / 10 / 100 / 1,000, right-click +10,000 or reset; in Fixed Budget, `te_construction_market_{increase,decrease}_budget_*`, steps 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 / 1,000,000, right-click +10,000,000 or reset), a live read-out (government purchase in force and its treasury cost, in Fixed Budget what the budget buys at today's price and, while the purchase is still moving toward it, how much of the gap closes this week, private purchase and share, price per point, market supply vs demand with a shortage flag). The explanations are on the Help page, How the Construction Market Works (`te_construction_market_help_popup`, its content `te_construction_market_help_body`, both in `construction_panel.gui`, shown in `gui/te_popups.gui`), which the "?" at the right of the section's header opens (`test_te_help_pages.py`); the section, and so the "?", shows only while the rule runs the market. The figures come from `common/script_values/te_construction_market_display_values.txt` (display-only, every `var:` read guarded); the text is the `TE_CM_*` keys plus the `concept_te_construction_market` game concept.

**AI building the sector.** In a market game the Construction Sector is ordinary heavy industry, so the AI and the investors weigh it like any factory: its `ai_value` plus profit terms. In play they underbuilt it, since the profit terms don't seem to credit that a level costs 100 construction points against 600–800 for the base game's factories and 2,000–5,000 for the mod's heavy industry. `te_construction_sector_ai_value` (`te_construction_market_ai_values.txt`) gives it 5,000 at base price (an ordinary factory scores 1,000), plus 10,000 per 100% the construction good's price stands above base in the state's market (about 15,000 at the ceiling). Below base it is 5,000 × price ÷ base price, so about 0 at the floor (0.1% of base, where sell orders are four times buy orders), with the old −500 each for war and banking stress, and the total is floored at 0. It reads the price, not the queue (owner's ruling, 2026-10-11): both purchases are divided by the price, so the queue lengthens as the price rises and only echoes it, and the old count of queued government levels (+2,000 above 30, −1,500 below 5) also followed the country's size, leaving a small country's sector at 1,000. Not yet watched in game: whether the boost overshoots into a glut (the price then falls and the value with it), and whether the sectors it adds stay staffed. An unattended run to 1925 found them under half staffed all game, and since 2026-11-16 a level hires a third as many and makes a margin at base price (§ The sector's workforce and margin).

**Implications for mod authors:**

- **The two FCFS queues still apply unchanged** — government queue from treasury, private queue from IP. The mod hasn't replaced the queue model; it's added an upstream market that determines whether the queue's currency is actually being produced, and drives the allocation between the queues from the two purchases.
- **A goods-side stockout stalls both queues** regardless of treasury/IP balance. This is the design intent: construction is gated by economic capacity, not just by money.
- **When mod-adding new buildings**, give them a maintenance group of the tier that fits (§ Maintenance tiers), or a realistic `goods_input_construction_add` in their own maintenance PMs. Skipping this makes the building free to maintain in capacity terms, undermining the system.

### Market settings without retooling or maintenance

`free_market_construction_rule` has five settings. Four run the market (`te_free_market_construction_on` is true for all four, so the market itself works the same):

| Setting | Maintenance (`pmg_maintenance*`) | Retooling (`pm_retooling`) |
|---|---|---|
| `free_market_construction_enabled` (default) | `pm_maintenance` tiers | applies |
| `free_market_construction_no_ai_retooling` | `pm_maintenance` tiers | removed by script only for AI-owned buildings |
| `free_market_construction_no_retooling` | `pm_maintenance` tiers | removed by script |
| `free_market_construction_no_maintenance` | `pm_no_maintenance`, forced (the direct setting's flags for that group) | removed by script (it would multiply nothing) |
| `free_market_construction_disabled` | `pm_no_maintenance`, forced | applies, multiplies nothing (§ Free Market Construction off) |

Everything else in the three alternative market settings is the enabled setting's: the Construction Site's market method, and the company buildings (`pm_disney_world`, `pm_generic_industrial_city`, `pm_generic_monument_to_industry`) and Engineering & Logistics barracks keep their construction-good recipes, which are production, not upkeep.

**No retooling cannot be a PM flag.** `pm_retooling` is a static modifier the engine applies for `RETOOLING_WEEKS`; neither a static modifier nor a define has a rule gate, and a cancelling modifier would have to be put on each retooling building by the same script that can simply remove the penalty (`scripting_best_practices.md`, "Gating a Mechanic on a Game Rule When It Lives in a Define, PM or Building Group"). So `te_building_retooling_waived` (`te_construction_market_triggers.txt`) gates `te_remove_waived_pm_retooling` (`extra_effects.txt`, building scope). It combines `te_pm_retooling_waived` (the two global settings), the AI-only setting with `owner = { is_ai = yes }`, and the railway exception below. All rule checks name their settings positively, so a pre-rule save keeps paying. The effect removes the modifier:

- from `on_production_method_changed` (`te_on_production_method_changed_retooling`, `te_construction_market_on_actions.txt`; root = the building), at once and again a day later through `te_construction_market_building_events.3`, since whether the engine applies the modifier before or after the hook fires is unverified;
- from the weekly sweep `minor_events_timelineextended.100` (every building of every state), which catches any PM change the hook misses, such as one the engine makes itself.

The AI-only check reads the building's current owner each time, including the delayed event and weekly sweep. Human-controlled subjects still pay; AI subjects of a player are exempt. Taking control of an AI country does not restore a penalty already removed, but subsequent PM changes pay normally. No persistent country waiver or extra pulse is needed.

The same effect keeps its older job in every setting: removing the modifier from level-0 buildings (also from `on_start_expanding_building`).

**Railways: the Transport principle swap is free in every setting** (owner ruling, #511). A railway's steam, electric or diesel method switches itself into its `pm_*_trains_principle_transport_3` variant through `replacement_if_valid` when the bloc reaches tier 3, and back when the bloc loses tiers 3–5. The player chose neither switch. On each railway PM change the hook runs `te_railway_note_train_method` (`extra_effects.txt`) first, and so does the weekly sweep for every state with a railway (`te_state_note_railway_train_method`), which catches a swap the hook missed and keeps every railway recorded. It compares the state's `te_state_railway_train_code` (steam 1, electric 2, diesel 3, +10 for the tier-3 variant; Centralized Traffic Control through Autonomous Trains 4–7, Vactrain Freight 8; `pm_early_trains` 0) against the last recorded `var:te_rail_train_code` (a state variable, since buildings don't hold variables reliably; one railway per state). Only the swap itself (`te_state_railway_tier3_swap`) sets a two-day `te_rail_retool_waive` marker on the state, which `te_building_retooling_waived` reads. It covers three cases: the same method moving +10; no record yet with the railway on a variant; or, after the bloc lost the principle, a recorded variant moving exactly −10 or landing on `pm_early_trains`, the group's fallback. A new train method, the passenger group or Centralized Traffic Control and later still pay. Residuals, accepted under the owner's ruling:

- **The waiver strips the whole `pm_retooling`,** including time left from an earlier player switch. A bloc leader can clear its members' railway retooling by dropping the transport principle to tier 2 and re-adopting tier 3.
- **The first weekly sweep on an old save, or on a new state object,** waives every railway already on a variant, whatever its retooling came from. A railway not yet recorded that jumps from a lower method straight onto a variant is waived once.
- **Another PM change within the two days rides along.** If the hook missed the swap, it rides along until the next sweep.
- **If losing the tier drops a railway to `pm_early_trains`,** the player's switch back pays full retooling. No waiver is built for that yet.

The sweep is a fallback only for a swap the hook missed. If the hook fired and the engine applied `pm_retooling` more than a day later, only the day-later re-check helps.

**Play-test list.**
1. *No retooling, switch a PM* on a factory with maintenance: the Retooling modifier does not appear on the building (or is gone the next day), and its construction input stays at 0.1 per level (times cost scaling). An AI country's buildings show no Retooling modifier after a few weeks.
2. *No maintenance, day 1*: `pmg_maintenance` shows No Maintenance only; the construction market read-out's demand is the government and private purchases plus the company buildings. Construction Sites, sectors and the panel work as in the default game.
3. *Default game*: switching a PM still applies Retooling (+1000% construction input).
4. *No AI retooling*: switch a PM on completed factories in a player country and an AI country, including subjects. Only the AI factory loses Retooling (at once or the next day), while both retain normal maintenance. Let the weekly sweep clear an existing AI penalty. Take control of the AI country and switch again: the new penalty stays. Repeat with two human players in multiplayer; both pay.
5. *Graded maintenance, default game*: a railway on steam trains shows Light Maintenance (0.05 construction a level, times cost scaling). Switching it to electric trains moves it to Standard Maintenance (0.1) by itself, and on to containerized cargo to Heavy Maintenance (0.15); switching back steps it down. Switch a steam railway straight to containerized cargo: it should settle on Heavy within a few days (light, standard, heavy). The same for a port (industrial, modern, container port) and a power plant (coal-fired to oil-fired: Light to Standard). A bloc's Transport tier 3 swapping electric trains into their principle variant leaves the maintenance method alone. A newly built railway starts on Light, or on the tier of the method it is built with. In the no-maintenance setting all three show No Maintenance.
6. *Discoverable mines*: a bauxite (or copper, graphite, potash) mine on picks and shovels shows Traditional Workings and pays no construction; a condensing engine pump moves it to Mechanized Workings (0.05), as on an iron mine. A lithium mine shows Light Maintenance whatever its pump, and can't take the high-pressure hydraulic pump before Modern Materials.

### Free Market Construction off (direct construction)

`free_market_construction_rule` (default enabled) turns the whole market off for base-game-style construction, at game setup or in a running game (§ The rule changed in a running game, below). Script asks `te_free_market_construction_off` / `_on` (`common/scripted_triggers/te_construction_market_triggers.txt`), which test the **disabled** setting so a save from before the rule keeps the market.

**The Construction Site is the construction sector.** `CONSTRUCTION_CAMP_BUILDING` is a define, so no rule can move it off `te_construction_market_site`. In a direct game the site therefore *is* the Construction Sector: buildable and expandable by the government (`can_build_government` = the rule; `possible` = Urbanization, as vanilla's sector), with tier PMs whose recipes are written out. Being the camp, it gets vanilla's whole treatment: the engine buys its inputs only for the points actually spent, and the AI's construction planning (below) works on it. `building_construction_sector` can't be built in a direct game. Its direct tiers copy the sector's, so the 2026-11-16 cut reaches a direct game too (§ The sector's workforce and margin): a site level hires a third of the people, whose wages the government pays whether or not its points are used, the AI's planning, which scores a government building down where no workforce is available (`GOVERNMENT_BUILDING_NO_AVAILABLE_WORKFORCE_FACTOR`), has a third as many jobs to fill, and a Wooden Buildings point costs 800 in goods instead of 1,000. Points per level are unchanged.

**The rule's PM flags do the switching** (`common/game_rules/extra_game_rules.txt`, the vanilla `disable_<pm>` / `force_<pm>` mechanism of `monument_effects`; `scripting_best_practices.md`, "Locking Production Methods"). No script activates or corrects a PM during play; each setting disables the other's methods (all `is_hidden_when_unavailable`):

| Group (buildings) | Market (rule on) | Direct (rule off) |
|---|---|---|
| `pmg_base_te_construction_market_site` (site) | `pm_te_construction_market_base`: 1 construction good → 1 point | `pm_te_direct_construction_<tier>`, 8 tiers: the Construction Sector tier of the same name (`extra_pms.txt`) with its construction-good output as `country_construction_add` — the same inputs, employment, `state_construction_mult`, mortality and required inputs. Per level: 1 / 2 / 3.5 / 5 / 6 / 10 / 16 / 24 points. `ai_selection = most_productive`; the tech gates are the sector's |
| `pmg_construction_automation`, `pmg_construction_principle` (sector and site) | on the sector only in practice: each gated PM's `unlocking_production_methods` lists sector tiers with their direct twins (all 8 for prefabricated components, tower cranes and the principle; Arc-Welded on for computer-aided design and 3D printing, whose cuts the early tiers' staff can't take), never the market base PM. A new tier goes in every list that names the tier below it; `test_construction_tiers.py` checks that, the twins and every place a tier is listed (the conversions in both directions and the site guard among them) | the same PMs, on the site |
| `pmg_maintenance`, `_light`, `_heavy`, `_intensive`, `_mine`, `_graded` (75 building types) | `pm_maintenance` (0.1 construction per level) or its `_light` (0.05), `_heavy` (0.15) or `_intensive` (0.2) variant, the mines' traditional (0) or mechanized (0.05) method, or the graded methods (0.05 / 0.1 / 0.15 by base method); `pm_no_maintenance`, forced, in the no-maintenance setting | `pm_no_maintenance`, forced |
| `pmg_disney_world`, `pmg_generic_industrial_city`, `pmg_generic_monument_to_industry` | the original PM (consumes the construction good) | `pm_<same>_direct`, forced: the recipe without the good |
| `pmg_principle_engineering_and_logistics` (barracks) | the principle PM (0.2 construction good per level) and its no-effect fallback | `_direct` twins: 0.2 `country_construction_add` per level instead |

**Script left.**
- *Conversion* (`te_direct_construction_convert_all`): every `building_construction_sector` becomes a site of the same level (capped at 200, the largest `te_construction_market_build_specified_level` can create) on the matching direct tier, a sector still under construction is cancelled, and every country gets Base Construction. It then sets the global `te_direct_construction_converted`, which closes the sector's `can_build_*` — until then they stay open so the history's own `create_building` seeding works whatever the rule. It runs at game start (`te_direct_construction_convert_sectors`, from `on_game_started_after_lobby`, when the rules are final) and whenever the rule is switched to Disabled in a running game (below).
- *Private share*: `te_direct_construction_law_private_share` restores the economic-system laws' vanilla allocations (25 / 50 / 75 / 35 / 10 %), which `construction_system_law_injections.txt` cancels for the market; keep the two in step. Applied at country setup (`te_direct_construction_country_setup`) and refreshed weekly (`te_direct_construction_on_weekly_pulse`).
- *AI site guard* (`te_direct_construction_keep_ai_sites`, weekly). Play-testing showed AI countries removing every Construction Site they had. No script removes a site in a direct game, so this is the AI downsizing them, and script can't steer that. The site stays `downsizeable` for players, so the guard works after the fact. Each week every state records its site's level and tier (state variables `te_dc_site_level` / `te_dc_site_tier`, recorded for players too, so a player's own downsizing is never undone); a conversion writes the record itself, so the guard holds from that day (AI countries removed 23 of 378 new sites in the week after one, 2026-11-13). An AI country then gets back any levels it lost since the last pulse. A site that is still there is raised with `create_building`, so it keeps its workers (the engine's documented "larger of the two levels"). If it comes out above the record (the disputed "adds levels" reading), it is rebuilt at exactly the record. A rebuilt or re-created site is moved back to its recorded tier. The guard skips an AI country `in_default`, whose record follows it down, and one without Urbanization (the site's `possible`). The AI can still add levels and sites and choose tiers.
- *Base construction* (`te_direct_construction_ensure_base`). Every country that is not decentralized holds `te_direct_construction_base_modifier` ("Base Construction", `country_construction_add = 10`): added by the conversion and from the weekly pulse to any country without it (a country made in play, a revolution's winner), and removed when the market is switched back on and, weekly, from any country that holds it in a market game (`te_direct_construction_remove_base`). Country setup does not add it: setup runs from history, where a rule read is not final, and a lobby that then picked a market setting would have left every country the modifier. A country with no sites (the AI in default, a player who downsized everything, a country before Urbanization) can therefore still build: in a new direct game Greece queued a second site level on 5.1 points and had it in 18 weeks, and a country made in play queued its first site (2026-11-13). The value is the base game's maximum, 10 (vanilla `country_gdp_construction`, which the mod empties), flat for every country: what a player gets in the base game under two of the three settings of its Base Construction Capacity rule (#1768; it was 5 until 2026-11-13, when the readings above were taken). That is ten wooden site levels' worth, small beside a working construction sector. Its points carry no wages. Whether they draw on the sites' material purchases (the engine buys the camp's inputs per point spent) is unverified.
  - **Why script and not the rule.** Until 2026-11-13 the Disabled setting applied the modifier with `apply_modifier = all:<modifier>` (vanilla `game_rules.md`; the base game never uses it). The engine does not apply that line by the rules of the save being played (read directly): a save whose rule said Enabled got the Disabled setting's modifier while the last lobby said Disabled, and a second rule behaved the same (`docs/testing/direct-construction-playtest.md`). It follows the setting the player last applied in a lobby on their machine. That was read through the harness, and by hand at loads from the main menu (#1767), where a direct game lost the modifier after a lobby set to Enabled, in the same session and after a fresh launch. A market game gaining it at a main-menu load was not tried. That the engine keeps the setting in `player/game_rules/presets.txt` is all but settled, not proved. So a direct game could lose its base at a load, which is what players reported, and a market game could gain it. No rule setting of the mod may carry the line (`test_construction_rule_switch.py`).
  - **The base game's Base Construction Capacity rule** (`free_construction`; Scaled (All), Scaled (Player Exemption), Maximum) scales vanilla's `country_gdp_construction` (+10) to GDP: its two flags, `free_construction_scaled_player_exemption` and `free_construction_unscaled`, are read by the engine, and no setting has an `apply_modifier`. The mod empties that modifier (`REPLACE:country_gdp_construction = {}`, `extra_modifiers.txt`, in every game, market or direct), so the rule moves nothing: a played Greece without Base Construction read 0 construction a week (2026-11-13, `docs/testing/direct-construction-playtest.md`). Since #1768 the mod cuts the rule to one setting, No Effect, which keeps the name and flag of vanilla's default and says that Base Construction comes from Free Market Construction (`common/game_rules/00_a_te_free_construction.txt`). A save on one of the other two settings loses it at load. The one script reader left is vanilla's AI construction target, which names a setting the copy drops (so it may now take Maximum's branch, +10 for every country) and which the mod's INJECT sets to 0; whether that replaces vanilla's value is #1771. How the copy overrides vanilla's rule, and why it isn't hidden: `game_rules.md` § A base-game rule the mod cuts down.
- *Market placement is off*: `te_construction_market_state_on_start` / `_on_end`, `te_construction_market_country_setup`'s capital site and `te_construction_market_on_border_changes` only act in market games.
- *Construction Cost Scaling*: `construction_cost_scaling_direct` (`country_construction_goods_cost_mult`, vanilla's lever on construction-material cost), same curve, same multiplicative adjustment.
- *Construction panel*: `te_direct_construction_section` shows construction per week (`modifier:country_construction_add`) and the law share; `te_construction_market_rule_on_sgui` picks the section and hides every purchase handler. The vanilla "no construction sector" prompt offers the site instead of the sector.
- *Build lens*: `te_construction_market_bg` has `lens = development`, so the production lens's Development tab offers the Construction Site, first in its row, in a direct game. The lens hides a building whose `can_build_government` is false, so a market game's lens has no Construction Site (screenshots of both, 2026-11-13). Before, the site could only be queued from a state's view.

**The rule changed in a running game** (`te_construction_market_follow_rule`, 2026-11-13). A rule can be changed from the screen that changes country. The global `te_direct_construction_converted` says which model the buildings are in, and the daily pulse (`te_construction_market_pulse_events.1`, before the weekly bodies) makes them follow the rule when the two disagree:
- *Switched to Disabled* (rule off, not converted): the conversion above. While it runs, `te_dc_from_market` is up and a site counts one level less (`te_direct_construction_convert_target_level`): the market places its sites at level 1, a converter and not capacity, so a state gets its sector's levels plus whatever its owner built on top of the market's own. Each site is removed and made new (`te_direct_construction_convert_state`), which is what gets rid of `te_construction_market_capacity_modifier`: sized for the market's last weekly purchase and never refreshed in a direct game, it left France's capital site at −100% throughput and France with Base Construction alone, whatever it built. A state with a site and no sector loses the site. An owner without Urbanization, which a direct game's site needs and which a country can lack only for a sector that came to it with a state (a conquest, say), is granted it before the site is built, as the history's seeding does (`extended_timeline_build_specified_building_level`), so `create_building` cannot refuse the site and lose the levels (#1765).
- *Switched back to a market setting* (rule on, converted; `te_direct_construction_revert_all`): every site becomes a government-owned sector of the same level and tier (capped at 200, the largest `extended_timeline_build_specified_building_level` can create; 100 until #1766), a site still under construction is cancelled, Base Construction goes, and each country gets the purchase variables. A site whose owner lacks Urbanization stays as a market site, since `create_building` checks the sector's technology, and is set to level 1 as the market's sites are (each is sized for a share of the week's purchase and gives that times its level). No site is placed by the switch: each country's weekly pulse ends with `te_construction_market_ensure_site`, after sizing.
- *Every fourth week, in a converted game*: any sector that has appeared is taken into its state's site, and one under construction is only cancelled, so a staffed site keeps its workers. The walk reads every building in the world, and nothing can queue a sector once the conversion has closed it, so weekly would buy nothing. An AI queued a sector on a new game's first day, before the conversion closed it, and the construction never finished.
- Both switches clear Construction Cost Scaling's modifiers and its variable and apply the new model's at once (§ Construction Cost Scaling; read live, 2026-11-13: a country with a scaling of 0.0029 held the market's modifier at that size the day after a switch to the market, and the direct one after a switch to Disabled).
- Both conversions leave the levels with the government: a sector's levels become government sites whoever owned them, and the way back makes government-owned sectors, so a switch to Disabled and back nationalizes every sector (#1764). Both also forget every state's site-guard record first (`te_direct_construction_forget_site_record`), so one left on a state with no site cannot have the guard build a site from nothing after a later switch.
- No retooling follows a switch to the market (read live, 2026-11-13): the rule's own move of 935 buildings from No Maintenance to Maintenance put `pm_retooling` on none of them, and neither did the scripted tier of the 375 re-created sectors.
- A save that was switched before this existed is converted on its first day on the new build, by the same test.
- `TE_SELF_CHECK … construction_rule_switch` logs each switch and `… direct_base_construction` the base, with fallbacks in `docs/testing/direct-construction-playtest.md`.

**Why the AI can run it.** Every piece the base game's AI uses is present in its vanilla shape: the camp is the buildable, government-funded building whose PMs carry `country_construction_add` (`ai_value = 500` in `00_modifier_types.txt`) and goods inputs, as vanilla's sector does. So the engine's construction planning applies unchanged: `MONEY_SPENDING_CONSTRUCTION_CRITICAL_THRESHOLD` / `_EXCESSIVE_THRESHOLD` (actual-to-wanted construction), `GOVERNMENT_BUILDING_STATE_POP_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_IMPORTANCE_*` (first level in populous states), `GOVERNMENT_BUILDING_NO_AVAILABLE_WORKFORCE_FACTOR` (staffing), and the debt pauses. The site's `ai_value` in direct games is weighted by the government queue (the market-mode sector's formula until 2026-10-11, when the sector moved to the construction price, which a direct game doesn't have) and 0 in market games. Tier choice is `most_productive` over the same shape of PMs as vanilla's `pmg_base_building_construction_sector`. None of this has been watched in game yet; see the play-test list.

**Deliberate differences from the base game and from the market-mode sector.**
- *Downsizing is allowed in both modes.* A direct game needs it: a site's wages are paid whether or not its points are used, so overbuilding must be reversible. In a direct game the AI's downsizing is undone at the next pulse by the AI site guard (above), except in default. In a market game it lets the player or the AI remove a site; `te_construction_market_ensure_site` puts back the last one, so the worst case is about two weeks without construction (a week to be re-placed, a week to hire and be sized).
- *Base construction.* Direct games give every country a flat 10 construction points a week (above), the base game's maximum. The base game scales its own base to GDP unless its Base Construction Capacity rule says otherwise; here that rule has one setting, No Effect (above).
- *Building-group properties.* `te_construction_market_bg` has no economy of scale (like vanilla's sector), no infrastructure use (vanilla 2/level, the market sector 0.1) and no urbanization (vanilla 5), and is hidden from the outliner.
- *Buffs that name the sector.* Throughput aimed at `building_construction_sector` or `bg_construction` (banking-event booms, `we_se_astronaut_industry`) doesn't reach the site, and `company_construction_power_bloc`, whose `possible` needs a level-5 sector, can't form.
- *Market-mode sites list two empty groups.* The site carries the automation and principle groups for direct games; on a market site their methods are unavailable (they need a sector or direct tier), so it shows No Construction Automation and No Effect.
- *Company buildings pay nothing* for the construction they consumed; the twins drop the input rather than price it in materials.
- *A converted site starts empty.* The sector's workers are released when it is removed; the site hires them back a tenth of its jobs a week (the rate measured on market sites, 2026-11-13; the group's hiring rate of 1.0 has no effect), so after a conversion a direct game's construction should take about ten weeks to reach full strength. Not watched in a direct game.
- *Saves from the first version of this rule* (#394, weekly capacity script) aren't migrated. Start a new direct game.

2. *Direct game, day 1.* No Construction Sector anywhere; a Construction Site of the same level on the same tier stands where each was. The panel's construction per week matches the sites' levels × tier points (plus base construction). The private share matches the law.
3. *Direct game, build.* Queue a new site from the production lens's Development tab (listed there, 2026-11-13) and from a state's view: it starts on a researched tier (which one the engine picks is unverified; any researched tier is acceptable). Expand one, then downsize it: its wages drop with it.
4. *Direct game, AI (observer, 10–20 years, watch 3–4 AIs of different size).* (a) Countries without a site build one once they have Urbanization; populous states get the first levels. (a2) No AI country outside default ends up without sites: levels an AI downsizes are back at the next weekly pulse, at the same tier. Check that a site that loses a level comes back at exactly its old level (not above it), and that the restored level rehires. (b) Levels keep up with the construction queue (the panel's construction per week grows with GDP). (c) Sites move to higher tiers as the techs arrive. (d) Sites stay staffed (occupancy near 100% outside labour shortages). (e) Treasury and debt: no country sits paused on construction debt for long because of site wages or materials.

6. *Direct game, base construction.* From day 1 every country, player and AI, shows Base Construction (+10) in its modifier list and its construction tooltip, and the panel's construction per week includes it (every country held it and read 5 or more through the harness on 2026-11-13, when the base was 5; 10 or more now).
