# Construction

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Construction Cost Scaling

- **Purpose:** Makes construction more expensive for rich countries whose workforce is fully employed (GDP per capita, and slack: peasants plus unemployed over the workforce). Keeps a rich country from outbuilding its population, lets one with idle labour build cheaply until it is used, and provides catchup for poorer nations.
- **Modifier:** `construction_cost_scaling` in `common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt` — `goods_input_construction_mult = 1`.
- **Script values:** `common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt` — search for `CONSTRUCTION COST SCALING`.
- **On action:** `construction_cost_scaling_on_action` in `common/on_actions/extra_on_actions.txt`, wired to `on_yearly_pulse_country`. It first calls `te_cc_yearly_update` (`common/scripted_effects/te_construction_cost_effects.txt`), which stores the country's slack (`te_cc_slack`, `te_cc_peasants`), moves the applying share (`te_cc_slack_factor`), and stores the GDP per capita and GDP part the scaling uses (`te_cc_gdppc`, `te_cc_gdp_part`) for the Nation tab.
- **Tuning:** `construction_cost_gdppc_reference`, `construction_cost_floor_ratio` (1x), `construction_cost_ceiling_ratio` (100x), `construction_cost_max_mult` (10 = +1000%); `construction_cost_slack_full` (0.03), `construction_cost_slack_none` (0.15), `construction_cost_slack_ease` (a third). The `scripts/analysis` tools read them all; re-run `econ_construction_counterfactual.py` after a change.
- **Curve:** X = the GDP part × the share that applies.
  - **The GDP part:** linear from 0 at the floor to `max_mult` at the ceiling, about 10 points per £1 of GDP per capita above £1 and +1000% from £100 (`construction_cost_scaling_gdp_mult`).
  - **The share that applies:** 1 at slack `slack_full` or less, 0 at `slack_none` or more, a straight line between. It moves `slack_ease` of the way toward that at each yearly pulse, from 0 before a country's first (`te_cc_slack_factor_now`), so a save from before the rule doesn't jump to the whole GDP part.
  - **Readers:** `construction_cost_scaling_mult` is the product, which every reader takes (the construction market's goods per point and price, the adjusted multipliers).
  - **Slack:** the sum over the country's states of the yearly walk's counts (§ Unemployment as a Share of the Workforce): peasants' workforce plus every other pop's with no workplace, over the workforce. `te_country_slack_share` and `te_country_peasant_share` iterate every state, so only the pulse reads them, and the Nation tab reads the stored figures.
- **History:**
  - **#1037** (2026-10-18) capped the line at +100% from £11, after the economy growth sim's data counterfactual (`docs/systems/economy_growth_sim.md`) found that its late steepness, up to 11x the price of a point, accounted for much of the late stall in rich economies, which kept a third of their workforce idle. A collapse in service jobs ran alongside it (79M in France's core in 1998, 41–64M from 2023).
  - **The slack part** (the owner's proposal, the same day) replaced the cap and restored the line. Against the same data, with the ministries at +1% a level:
    - It leaves the EU as a whole at 3.5% slack in 2045, with no labour shortage. The cap would have run France's core short of labour from 2033.
    - The slack is read over the whole owner country. A rich core inside a country with poor annexed or colonial states keeps cheap construction until the whole country's slack falls. Without migration into it, France's core runs short from 2004, about 31% by 2045. If the brake followed the core instead, the core would end at 1.6% and the EU at 29%.
    - The owner kept the country-wide read (2026-10-18), pending a play-test of a rich core with a poor periphery (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`). Weighting by state GDP or building levels is the next step if the core runs short while the periphery idles.
- **Nation tab:** the Construction Cost row shows the scaling's own effect, `te_cc_scaling_factor` − 1. Its tooltip gives the cost of a point as factors: the scaling (the GDP part, the share that applies, the slack, peasants and unemployed) times the rest. The total is the cost in force, one plus every construction-cost modifier (`te_cc_total_factor`), and the rest is the total over the scaling, so the product holds after a mid-year change. The engine's per-source list (`GetValueWithBreakdownFor`) closes it.
- **Self-check:** `construction_slack_read` (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`): the stored slack against a fresh walk of the country's pops.
- `goods_input_construction_mult` affects both construction project costs AND ongoing building maintenance.
- With `free_market_construction_rule` disabled there is no construction good to scale, so the on_action applies `construction_cost_scaling_direct` (`country_construction_goods_cost_mult = 1`, adjusted by `construction_cost_scaling_direct_adjusted_mult`) instead. See § Free Market Construction off.

## Excess Private Construction

- **Purpose:** Gives an investment pool that the private queue can't spend a way to spend. The engine caps the private queue at 1,000 buildings and each project's weekly progress at `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`, so a rich pool can outgrow what investors can build and keep depositing forever. The modifier raises the per-project cap and charges a construction-efficiency penalty for it, which is the sink. Overinvestment is the second stage.
- **Modifiers** (`common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt`): `too_much_private_construction`, per unit `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add = 1` and `state_construction_mult = -0.0001` (−90% at the 9,000 cap). `overinvestment_modifier` sets every pop type's `state_*_investment_pool_contribution_add = -1`.
- **Script values** (`common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt`, `target_queue_length` through `too_much_private_construction_script_value`). `too_much_private_construction_overflow` is target − base: the per-project cap raise at which 500 projects could spend the pool's gross income. It is positive exactly when 500 projects at base speed couldn't, and 0 while `private_construction_used` is 0 or less, i.e. an empty queue or no private share (which also keeps `curr_cost_per_construction_point` from dividing by 0). `too_much_private_construction_script_value` is the overflow, moving at most ×1.2 or ÷1.2 a year from last year's value (`var:too_much_private_construction_cached`); the cap is 9,000. While Overinvestment is in force and the private queue has levels, it returns last year's value instead. A new episode starts at the lesser of the overflow and 10, or, if higher, the value the modifier had when it was removed in the last five years (`too_much_private_construction_resume`, reading `var:too_much_private_construction_last`).
- **On action:** `excess_private_construction_on_action`, on `on_yearly_pulse_country`, checked every year, also while the modifier is in force. The modifier is applied or kept while one of these holds:
  - **Entry** (no stored value above 0): more than 500 private levels queued and `investment_pool_net_income > 0`.
  - **Stay** (`var:too_much_private_construction_cached` above 0; it is the modifier's multiplier, so normally the modifier is in force): a non-empty private queue and `too_much_private_construction_overflow > 0`.
  - Overinvestment is in force.

  While one holds, the modifier is re-applied at the script value. Otherwise it is removed, its value kept for five years in `var:too_much_private_construction_last` (a timed country variable), and the variable set to 0. A re-entry within those five years resumes at the lesser of the kept value and the overflow, so a short dip in pool income (a banking panic cuts capitalist contributions for 14–18 months; a default or bankruptcy) doesn't restart it at 10 and let the pool build up for decades. After five years the kept value expires and a new episode ramps from 10 as designed, so a re-entry decades later doesn't jump straight to a large penalty. The kept value is also removed once the modifier is back. The tests read the variable rather than the modifier because a civil war's winner inherits variables but no modifiers (`docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md` § What a Civil War's Winner Inherits). A winner holding the variable rebuilds the modifier and continues the nation. On that first pulse `base_construction_per_week` subtracts a value the winner's modifiers don't include, so the overflow reads high by that much; the ×1.2 ceiling caps the rebuilt value at 1.2× the inherited one. The stay test can't be the entry test. Net income turns negative the moment the modifier starts draining the pool, so the modifier would remove itself by working and the pool would refill until it came back. The queue can also fall to 500 or below while the modifier is still needed: the engine's own autonomous investment fills it, and faster projects shorten it. Gross income doesn't depend on the modifier, and under Free Market Construction, while the pool's funds don't bind, the cost per point (`curr_cost_per_construction_point`, spending ÷ `private_construction_used`) is the market price, because the market sizes the private share to what the pool buys. So the modifier can't end its own need by working. The estimate does include the modifier's (base + value) × levels cap through `private_construction_used`; where the pool's funds bind it reads low and the overflow high (the direct-construction exception below). Outside Overinvestment it fails exactly when the ramp target reaches 0, so the ÷1.2 floor can't keep a spent modifier alive.
- **Overinvestment** is added the same pulse the variable is above 1,000 and `investment_pool > gdp`. It keeps the construction modifier because it cuts every pop's pool contribution, so gross income, and the overflow with it, falls to about 0; removing the modifier then would end Overinvestment after one year, since it needs the variable above 1,000. Kept, the value is **held at last year's** while Overinvestment is in force and the private queue has levels (owner's ruling, PR #514), so Overinvestment is re-added until its own exit holds, the pool at or under GDP, even with the value above 1,000. The year after it lifts, the stay test and the normal ramp take over. With an empty private queue nothing drains the pool, so there the value falls ÷1.2 as before and Overinvestment ends within 13 years. Meanwhile the private queue keeps spending the pool, under direct construction through the law's allocation and under Free Market Construction through the private purchase, a 24th of the pool a week up to what the private queue can absorb (§ Construction as a Market Good). **Limits of the hold.** It freezes growth as well as decay: with gross income cut the overflow reads −base, so the need can't raise the value during Overinvestment, and when the queue cap limits the drain the frozen value is the limit (PR #514's toy model has a 2M/week country run 13 straight years at 1,145). Episodes can be long, even indefinite, when inflows that aren't pop contributions outpace a capped drain: government-dividend reinvestment, or a pool left far above GDP by a GDP collapse. (The cooperative boom and frenzy phases were one until 2026-10-05, when their `country_weekly_investment_pool_mult` became a share of the pool's gross income rather than of its balance; Overinvestment cuts gross income, so they no longer feed it. `banking_cycle_simulation.md` §21.) One queued private level is enough to keep the hold. If play shows long episodes, the candidate follow-ups are capping the hold's consecutive years, or holding only while the per-project cap binds (`private_construction_used` at or above queued levels × `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`). Two earlier behaviours made Overinvestment fail to drain the pool. Until 2026-09-26 the market formula was gross × pool ÷ (24 × gross) with a fixed 2.4M divisor at gross ≤ 0, so it went to 0 or below exactly when Overinvestment cut gross income, and the market bought nothing for the private queue. Until 2026-09-27 the value fell ÷1.2 in each Overinvestment year, which lifted Overinvestment every other year and kept the value, and so per-project capacity, near 1,000 while the pool stayed above GDP. What still limits a very rich pool is the owner's 9,000 cap.
- **Direct construction with an emptied pool (known exception, accepted for now).** Under direct construction nothing throttles the private queue to pool ÷ 24. It draws its allocation, `min(construction × private share, (base + value) × levels)`, and stalls at what the pool can fund. If that allocation costs more than the pool's income, the modifier drains the pool to 0. From then on spending equals gross income, but `private_construction_used` doesn't see the stall, so the cost estimate falls below the price and the overflow reads about private allocation ÷ 500 − base instead of the real need. The modifier climbs to that and stays while the allocation exceeds 500 × base, even after the real need is gone (a toy model in PR #514's review put it at twice the real need, and kept it after the need turned negative). This is off the default rule (Free Market Construction), and the gate before 2026-09-26 kept the modifier regardless. Follow-up: give the stay test a cost per point that doesn't depend on spending: `te_construction_market_price` under Free Market Construction, a goods cost per point under direct construction. The same fix covers the estimate's `min = 1` floor, which makes the overflow read huge for one pulse after a week of almost no spending (for example after `te_mon_currency_reform_pool_wipe` or `te_mon_union_exit_pool_hit`).
- **Invariant:** the modifier's multiplier is `var:too_much_private_construction_cached`, written first, so `base_construction_per_week` (the per-project cap without this modifier) can subtract it. Set the variable to 0; never `remove_variable` it. `var:too_much_private_construction_last` exists only while the modifier is absent, for up to five years after a removal.
- **History:** until 2026-09-26 the gate was `OR = { <conditions>  has_modifier = too_much_private_construction }` and the value could only fall ÷1.2 a year. Once the modifier existed, the conditions were never checked again, and it stayed above 0 for 76–114 years. The first draft of the fix (PR #514) re-checked the entry test every year and removed the modifier when it failed. Review found that this removes it the pulse after it starts draining the pool, so the stay test is separate.

## Construction as a Market Good (FMC architecture)

> Cross-reference: `docs/vanilla/vanilla_economy_reference.md` § 9 establishes the vanilla two-FCFS-queue model (government queue from treasury, private queue from IP, allocation set by `country_private_construction_allocation_mult`). This mod adds a market layer underneath without replacing the queues. Based on the third-party *Free Market Construction* mod (credited in `README.md`); every file is prefixed `te_construction_market_*`.

**Why:** Decoupling construction *capacity* (the vanilla `country_construction` country-resource that the FCFS queues spend) from construction *output* (what the construction sector actually produces) lets per-state economics influence national construction throughput. A goods-side stockout in one region propagates through the price loop instead of disappearing into a black-box country resource.

**Architecture (3 layers):**

1. **Construction-sector building, REPLACE'd.** `building_construction_sector` (vanilla) is REPLACE'd in `common/buildings/te_construction_market_site.txt` (its group `bg_construction` is REPLACE'd as ordinary heavy industry in `extra_building_groups.txt`, so it is no longer government-funded), and its tier PMs are REPLACE'd in `common/production_methods/extra_pms.txt` (`pm_wooden_buildings`, `pm_iron_frame_buildings`, `pm_steel_frame_buildings`, `pm_arc_welded_buildings`, plus the mod tiers `pm_reinforced_concrete_buildings`, `pm_advanced_construction`, `pm_nanomaterial_buildings` INJECTed into `pmg_base_building_construction_sector`). Each tier now outputs `goods_output_construction_add = N` (1, 2, 3.5, 5, 6, 10, 16 across tiers) — i.e. the construction sector produces the **`construction`** market good rather than direct country construction points. State-level `state_construction_mult` is preserved so per-state efficiency still matters. Barracks under the Engineering & Logistics principle (`pm_principle_engineering_and_logistics`) also produce 0.2 per level.
2. **The `construction` good itself.** Defined in `common/goods/timeline_extended_extra_goods.txt`. `cost = 1000`, `category = industrial`, `traded_quantity = 0.1`, `consumption_tax_cost = 400` (Authority cost if the player consumption-taxes it). High base cost makes it a load-bearing market input.
3. **Auto-placed consumer building: `te_construction_market_site`.** Defined in `common/buildings/te_construction_market_site.txt`, group `te_construction_market_bg` (government-funded, hidden from the outliner). `common/defines/te_construction_market_defines.txt` points `NCountry.CONSTRUCTION_CAMP_BUILDING` at it, which is what makes the engine charge its inputs only for the points actually spent — to the treasury or the investment pool, by queue. Flags: `buildable = yes` and `expandable = yes` for direct games, but `can_build_government` is false in a market game, so nobody builds one (script's own placement raises `te_cm_placing_site`, which the trigger accepts); `downsizeable = yes`, also for direct games, so a player or the AI can remove a site — capacity is spread over the remaining staffed sites each pulse, and `te_construction_market_ensure_site` re-places a capital site at the end of the pulse if none is left; `min_raise_to_hire = -1.0`, `levels_per_mesh = -1`. Placed automatically by `te_construction_market_sector_placement_effects.txt` in each state where construction starts (with a capital fallback). Its single PM (`pm_te_construction_market_base` in `common/production_methods/te_construction_market_pms.txt`) consumes 1 `goods_input_construction` per workforce and produces 1 `country_construction_add` — i.e. it converts the construction good back into the vanilla queue currency. Each weekly pulse re-applies `te_construction_market_capacity_modifier` (`building_throughput_add`) to every site so the sites together deliver exactly the week's public + private purchase. Employment is tiny (10 laborers per level, level_scaled).

**The chain end-to-end:**

```
Construction Sector (vanilla, REPLACE'd) → produces construction good
   ↓
Market: construction good supply meets construction good demand at a price
   ↓
te_construction_market_site (auto-placed, the engine's construction camp) consumes construction good
   ↓ produces
country_construction_add (vanilla queue currency)
   ↓
Vanilla two-FCFS queues (government from treasury / private from IP) spend it
   ↓
New buildings get built / expanded
```

**Purchases and the queue split.** The government purchase is `te_construction_market_public_setting`, capped each week at what the government queue can absorb (`te_construction_market_public_use_target`: queued government levels × `country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add`). The setting has two modes. *Fixed Quantity* (the default, and the AI's only mode) reads the country variable `te_construction_market_public_target`, set by the player in the construction panel or by `te_construction_market_ai_update_buy` for the AI. *Fixed Budget* (`te_construction_market_budget_mode_on`: the country variable `te_construction_market_budget_mode`) reads `te_construction_market_budget_quantity`, which the weekly pulse (`te_construction_market_update_budget_quantity`, before the sites are sized) moves 1/N of the way (`te_construction_market_budget_shift_weeks`) toward the player's weekly budget `te_construction_market_public_budget` ÷ `te_construction_market_price`, and onto it once within a point. The partial step damps the loop through the price: the government's purchase moves the price it is divided by, and if a 1% larger purchase raises the price by e%, each week leaves 1 − (1 + e)/N of the gap, so N = 1 + e closes it at once to first order and a larger N approaches without overshooting. N is read off the engine's price formula, `price = base × (1 + PRICE_RANGE × clamp(imbalance / (F − 1), −1, 1))` with `imbalance = (BUY − SELL) / min(BUY, SELL)` and `F` = `BUY_SELL_DIFF_AT_MAX_FACTOR` (the mod's two defines are mirrored as `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_PRICE_RANGE` and `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_BUY_SELL_DIFF_AT_MAX_FACTOR`, and `test_construction_market_budget_step.py` pins them to `extra_defines.txt`). It is taken from the chord between the purchase in force and the one the budget wants at today's price, N = 1 + T/(T − Q) × (price at T ÷ price now − 1), the two prices coming from that formula with the government's goods (points × `te_construction_market_goods_per_point`) added to everyone else's buy orders; the chord, unlike the slope at either end, sees the price stop moving at its floor and ceiling. In a large market a small purchase barely moves the price (e ≈ 0.01 for the construction panel's 1,000 points against 325,000 goods wanted, so N ≈ 1 and the quantity reaches the budget in a week); when the purchase is a large part of the market, or the price is near its floor, N grows. A raise is never spread over fewer than `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_BUDGET_MIN_RAISE_WEEKS = 2` weeks, because an overshoot on a raise spends past the budget and the model cannot see other buyers reacting, the site's local price or an engine lag; a cut has no floor, because an overshoot there spends less than the budget. The model sets the speed, not the destination: each week's target is still the budget ÷ the price the market shows, so the quantity settles where spending equals the budget whatever the model gets wrong. While the quantity is still moving, the panel says how much of the gap closes this week (`te_construction_market_budget_stepping_sgui`, `te_construction_market_display_budget_step_percent`). A budget cut to 0 zeroes the quantity at once (`te_construction_market_clear_budget`). The mode buttons carry the purchase over: into Fixed Budget, the budget is the in-force purchase (or the setting, while none is in force) × the price, to the nearest 1,000, and the quantity starts there (`te_construction_market_budget_seed[_quantity]`); into Fixed Quantity, the target is the budget quantity, rounded. The AI's weekly purchase clears the mode, so a country a player left in Fixed Budget returns to the AI's quantity. The private purchase (`te_construction_market_private_use_target`) is a 24th of the investment pool a week (`te_construction_market_private_spending_target`), so the pool settles at 24 weeks of gross income, divided by the smoothed price (`te_construction_market_price_stabilizer`) and capped by the private queue. It reads the pool alone, so it keeps spending when gross income is 0 or less, as under Overinvestment (§ Excess Private Construction); an earlier gross × pool ÷ (24 × gross) form went to 0 or below there. The private share of construction (`te_construction_market_private_share_modifier`, `country_private_construction_allocation_mult = 0.01` × the private share in percent) is set weekly from those two purchases; `common/laws/construction_system_law_injections.txt` cancels the economic-system laws' vanilla allocations so nothing else moves it.

**Construction-cost-scaling layer.** The `construction_cost_scaling` static modifier (above) applies `goods_input_construction_mult = 1` (multiplied by a per-country GDPpc-driven multiplier). This scales how much *construction good* every consumer burns — each construction site per point delivered, and every building's maintenance PM. So a richer country burns more construction good per same building → competes more for the same supply → stockouts and higher prices → fewer net `country_construction_add` produced → vanilla queues stall on availability rather than treasury.

**Buildings consume the construction good as maintenance.** Each maintenance group (`pmg_maintenance` and its `_light`, `_heavy`, `_intensive`, `_mine` and `_graded` variants, all hidden in PM displays) carries a rate of `goods_input_construction_add` per level, set by the kind of building and, for mines, power plants, railways and ports, by the method it runs (§ Maintenance tiers); it is on 74 building types — industry, power, transport infrastructure (railways, ports, airports, highways), services, mines — not farms, plantations or urban centres, and most mines only once they are mechanized. A few company buildings in `unique_pms.txt` consume more (0.5–1). Construction is therefore not just an upfront build cost — it's an ongoing maintenance load tied to current economic activity.

### Maintenance tiers

`pmg_maintenance` was one rate (0.1 per level) for every building that had it. It is now six groups. Four are a single PM plus `pm_no_maintenance`; the fifth, for mines, changes with the equipment, and the sixth, for power plants, railways and ports, with the base method (below):

| Group / PM | Per level | Buildings |
|---|---|---|
| `pmg_maintenance_light` / `pm_maintenance_light` | 0.05 | Trade center, art academy, state youth centers, software industry; the four tech metals mines (lithium, nickel and cobalt, rare earths, platinum group metals) |
| `pmg_maintenance` / `pm_maintenance` | 0.1 | Everything else that had it: light, heavy, military and high-tech industry, synthetics and biotech plants, shipyard, skyscraper, tourism, construction sector, grand monument (at level 1; `gm_upkeep` charges the levels its monuments record, § Grand Monuments) |
| `pmg_maintenance_heavy` / `pm_maintenance_heavy` | 0.15 | Highway, airport, network infrastructure, grid transfer station, HVDC undersea interconnector; hydro and renewable plants; oil rig |
| `pmg_maintenance_intensive` / `pm_maintenance_intensive` | 0.2 | Nuclear and fusion plants, ocean mine, space mine |
| `pmg_maintenance_mine` / `pm_maintenance_traditional` or `pm_maintenance_mechanized_mine` | 0 or 0.05 | The original coal, iron, lead, sulfur and gold mines, and the ten discoverable mines that run their equipment groups |
| `pmg_maintenance_graded` / `pm_maintenance_graded_light`, `_standard` or `_heavy` | 0.05, 0.1 or 0.15 by base method | Power plant (fossil), railway, port |

The principle: upkeep follows the physical plant that wears with use, not the building's price or output. Offices, exchanges and institutions carry little; transport and power networks are all plant; hostile sites (reactors, the sea floor, vacuum) leave nothing to run down. Heavy industry keeps the original 0.1. Maintenance is charged per level, so a building whose level grows several times over up its method ladder pays by method instead (power plants, railways and ports, below).

- **Mines follow their equipment.** `pmg_maintenance_mine` lists `pm_maintenance_traditional` (free, first, so the default and the fallback), `pm_maintenance_mechanized_mine` (0.05) and `pm_no_maintenance`. The paid method's `unlocking_production_methods` names the equipment PMs of the five mines from the condensing engine pump on (condensing, diesel, high-pressure hydraulic, continuous miners, smart miners); picks and shovels and the atmospheric engine pump are the hand-worked tier. The free method carries `replacement_if_valid = pm_maintenance_mechanized_mine`, the vanilla mechanism that moves the train methods onto their principle variants, so the swap happens by itself when the equipment does. It is deliberately tied to the building's own PM, not to a technology: a technology-gated method would swap every mine in the country at once when the tech arrived, and each swap applies `pm_retooling`; here the swap comes with the equipment switch that already paid it. Vanilla drops a PM that depends on another PM when the other is swapped out, provided another PM in the group still fits, so going back to picks and shovels returns the mine to the free method. Ten of the 14 discoverable mines run the original mines' equipment groups, picks and shovels included (bauxite, chromium, manganese and specialty alloys the iron mine's; copper and precious metals the lead mine's; phosphate, potash and salt the sulfur mine's; graphite the coal mine's), so they take this group too: the paid method's list already names their equipment, which is the same PMs. They were on `pmg_maintenance_light` until 2026-10-23 on the belief that their equipment was all mechanized. The four tech metals mines have their own equipment group, which starts at the condensing engine pump, and stay on `pmg_maintenance_light`. The test pins the unlock list to the equipment PMs; unverified in game: the automatic swap in both directions, and the default on a newly built mine.
- **Power plants, railways and ports follow their base method** (`pmg_maintenance_graded`, 2026-10-23). A level of these buildings grows several times over up its ladder while maintenance is per level, so the heavy tier's flat 0.15 crushed the early methods: at base prices a fossil plant level sells £750 (early plant) to £4,200 (modern oil), against £13,500–360,000 for a renewable, hydro, nuclear or fusion level, and 0.15 was 20% of the early plant's revenue and 10% of the coal-fired plant's; early trains add £135 a level over their inputs and the basic port £90. Measured as the multiple of base construction cost at which a method's profit at era wages reaches zero (cost scaling times the construction price over £1,000), the early plant broke even at ×1.1, coal-fired ×3.0, oil-fired ×4.7, steam trains ×1.4, the industrial port ×2.7, against medians of 12–30 for factories; a labour-tight country pays ×2.3 at £14 GDP per capita and ×3.4 at £25 (§ Construction Cost Scaling). The tiers: **light 0.05** for early and steam trains (and the steam variant), the anchorage, basic and industrial ports, and the early and coal-fired plants; **standard 0.1** for electric and diesel trains (and their variants), Centralized Traffic Control, the modern port, and the oil-fired and both modern fossil plants; **heavy 0.15** for containerized cargo, automated loading and autonomous trains, and container, global and magnetic-drive ports. That puts coal-fired at ×9.0, oil-fired ×7.1, modern coal ×10.3, modern oil ×15.8, steam trains ×4.2, diesel ×7.9, the industrial port ×8.2, the modern port ×6.6. The early plant (×3.4) and early trains without passengers (×0.9) stay thin: their margins are £170 and £45 a level before maintenance. The mechanism is the mines': `pm_maintenance_graded_light` is first and needs no other method (the default and the fallback) and carries `replacement_if_valid = pm_maintenance_graded_standard`, which carries `replacement_if_valid = pm_maintenance_graded_heavy`. The standard method's `unlocking_production_methods` lists its own tier's base methods and the heavy tier's, the heavy method only its own, so a jump from a light-tier method straight to a heavy-tier one steps light → standard → heavy, and going down the dearer method loses its last unlocking method and falls back. The Transport-principle variants sit in their base method's tier, so the bloc's principle swap (§ Market settings without retooling or maintenance, Railways) changes no maintenance. Renewable, hydro, nuclear and fusion plants are separate buildings and keep their flat tiers. `test_maintenance_tiers.py` pins each base method's tier. Unverified in game, beyond the mines' questions: the two-step swap up from light to heavy (§ Market settings without retooling or maintenance, Play-test list, item 5).
- **Adding a tier or a building.** Give the building the group whose rate fits (`INJECT:` or the building's own `production_method_groups`). A new *rate* needs a PM, a group in `extra_pm_groups.txt`, `disable_<pm>` in the `no_maintenance` and `disabled` settings of `free_market_construction_rule`, loc for the PM and group, and the group's key in the six `ProductionMethodGroup.GetKey` checks (six keys now, `And(And4(...), And(Not(...), Not(...)))`) that hide maintenance in `gui/production_methods.gui`, `building_details_panel.gui`, `building_browser_panel.gui` and `goods_state_panel.gui`. `test_maintenance_tiers.py` checks the rule flags, the shared `pm_no_maintenance` and that every maintenance group is hidden in those panels.
- **Left without maintenance on purpose.** Farms, plantations, ranches, logging, fishing and whaling (land-based, and their tools and machines are already inputs). Farms and plantations could take the same equipment-driven split as mines (their harvesting PMs, such as steam threshers and tractors, are shared across crops, so one paid method could list them), but they are the largest sector and need their own balance decision. Also left out: universities and government buildings (paid from the budget, not built to earn); barracks, fortifications and bases; urban centers; canals (one level, not expandable, so the upkeep would be a rounding error); monuments and wonders (caretaker PMs); company buildings (their own recipes); the mod's late-game megastructures and the Strategic Reserve buildings (their own construction and cost rules). The original mines are not left out: they pay from the first mechanized pump, so an 1836 economy's hand-worked mines stay free while construction supply is scarcest.

**Retooling.** The engine puts the `pm_retooling` static modifier on a building that switches production methods (`NEconomy.RETOOLING_WEEKS = 260` in `extra_defines.txt`); this mod REPLACEs it with `goods_input_construction_mult = 10`, so a retooling building pays +1000% on its construction input. Two `free_market_construction_rule` settings waive the retooling cost, and one of them maintenance as well (§ Market settings without retooling or maintenance).

**AI production-method switching.** Vanilla's AI PM hysteresis (`NAI.PRODUCTION_METHOD_STICKINESS_UPGRADE` 0.95, `_DOWNGRADE` 0.75, `PRODUCTION_METHOD_CHANCE_TO_CHANGE` 0.1: the score of a method the building doesn't run is multiplied by the stickiness, so a switch needs a 5% or 33% better score) assumes switching is free. Under retooling it isn't: a switch costs 1,300 construction per 1 construction a week of maintenance, £195,000 per level of a heavy-tier building at base price (a port, railway or power plant until 2026-10-23; they now pay by method, a third or two thirds of that on their early and middle methods). An AI-only observer game to 2015 had 3,039 of 13,965 buildings retooling (ports 69%), and AI budgets ran 25–50% subsidies, mostly ports. So `extra_defines.txt` sets 0.9 / 0.6 / 0.05: an upgrade needs an 11% better score and a downgrade 67%, and the AI looks half as often. Retooling spread over five years for a downgrade (usually a price swing that reverses) is worth a 23–150% better score per level of those heavy-maintenance buildings at base prices, and over twenty years for an upgrade 6–38%; factories (0.1 maintenance, larger output) need only 13–33% and 3–8%, so they get more hysteresis than their retooling warrants. `pmg_water_personal_transport` (port ferries and cruise ships; no workers, ships and fuel into transportation) takes `ai_selection = most_productive` like vanilla's `pmg_passenger_trains`: under most_profitable AI ports flipped between a ferry tier and none at the transportation break-even price, 174 times on 443 ports in four months. `pmg_emissions_control` (ports, power plants, steel mills, automotive industries) gets the same fix. Its three tiers only cost, and AI ports switched between Basic and Advanced 35 times in four months of the 2015 run. Its tiers also carry `ai_value` 1000 / 3000 / 9000, so the AI should move up a tier and never down (owner ruling 2026-10-06). That is unconfirmed in game, because what `ai_value` does is undocumented (`scripting_best_practices.md` § Production Method `ai_value`). Stickiness cannot hold flips driven by the score's multiplicative factors: `PRODUCTION_METHOD_EMPLOYMENT_CHANGE_FACTOR` (2.0) multiplies or divides by 1 + 2 × the share of labor saved, 2 for a method that halves staffing (Centralized Traffic Control against diesel trains, already `most_productive`), and an input shortage multiplies by 0.01. Only the lower chance slows those. Urban centers, fishing wharves, ranches and logging camps have no construction input, so their switches show the Retooling modifier but cost nothing. Power plants' base group (`pmg_base_building_power_plant`) takes `most_productive` too (#933), as vanilla gives the port and railway base groups. Under most_profitable the AI moved a plant down to the least-loss method (the early plant) whenever its own output pushed the local electricity price down, and back up when the price recovered; a 4× fast run's 1912 save had 70% of AI power plants retooling against 38% of railways and 29% of ports, those plants carried 92% of the plants' subsidies, and the base-group switches (about 70 a year among 623 plants) came without an input shortage. The AI subsidises its power plants (vanilla's `must_have`; 604 of 630 flagged), so the retooling lands on its treasury. `edited_default_strategy.txt`'s INJECT of `nice_to_have` never applied: an INJECT can't change a key vanilla's `subsidies` block already holds (`scripting_best_practices.md`, read in game 2026-10-16). Since #981 every vanilla administrative strategy sets `building_power_plant = should_remove` (`te_ai_subsidy_strategies.txt`), a priority below `nice_to_have` that makes the AI drop the subsidy, so an AI coal or oil plant now carries its own losses (nuclear, fusion and renewable plants are separate buildings no strategy subsidizes). In the owner's late-game save, 3½ months took the AI's subsidized share of power plants from 99% to 1–19% by strategy, railways steady at 98%. The plants' automation and carbon-capture groups still switch (about 28 and 20 a year in that save): the first is partly the employment factor, and `most_productive` on the second would stop the AI ever capturing, since capture only costs. Check in game: `docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md`.

**Player UI.** The domestic tab of the construction panel (`gui/construction_panel.gui`, the `te_construction_market_section` block) carries the Fixed Quantity / Fixed Budget mode buttons (`te_construction_market_set_{quantity,budget}_mode`; the read-only `te_construction_market_budget_mode_sgui` tells the GUI which controls and tooltips to show), the purchase control (+/- buttons bound to `te_construction_market_{increase,decrease}_target_*` in `common/scripted_guis/te_construction_market_scripted_gui.txt`, click / shift / ctrl / alt steps 1 / 10 / 100 / 1,000, right-click +10,000 or reset; in Fixed Budget, `te_construction_market_{increase,decrease}_budget_*`, steps 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000 / 1,000,000, right-click +10,000,000 or reset), a live read-out (government purchase in force and its treasury cost, in Fixed Budget what the budget buys at today's price and, while the purchase is still moving toward it, how much of the gap closes this week, private purchase and share, price per point, market supply vs demand with a shortage flag) and a collapsible "How does the construction market work?" explanation. The figures come from `common/script_values/te_construction_market_display_values.txt` (display-only, every `var:` read guarded); the text is the `TE_CM_*` keys plus the `concept_te_construction_market` game concept.

**AI building the sector.** In a market game the Construction Sector is ordinary heavy industry, so the AI and the investors weigh it like any factory: its `ai_value` plus profit terms. In play they underbuilt it, since the profit terms don't seem to credit that a level costs 100 construction points against 600–800 for the base game's factories and 2,000–5,000 for the mod's heavy industry. `te_construction_sector_ai_value` (`te_construction_market_ai_values.txt`) gives it 5,000 at base price (an ordinary factory scores 1,000), plus 10,000 per 100% the construction good's price stands above base in the state's market (about 15,000 at the ceiling). Below base it is 5,000 × price ÷ base price, so about 0 at the floor (0.1% of base, where sell orders are four times buy orders), with the old −500 each for war and banking stress, and the total is floored at 0. It reads the price, not the queue (owner's ruling, 2026-10-11): both purchases are divided by the price, so the queue lengthens as the price rises and only echoes it, and the old count of queued government levels (+2,000 above 30, −1,500 below 5) also followed the country's size, leaving a small country's sector at 1,000. Not yet watched in game: whether the boost overshoots into a glut (the price then falls and the value with it), and whether the sectors it adds stay staffed.

**Implications for mod authors:**

- **The two FCFS queues still apply unchanged** — government queue from treasury, private queue from IP. The mod hasn't replaced the queue model; it's added an upstream market that determines whether the queue's currency is actually being produced, and drives the allocation between the queues from the two purchases.
- **A goods-side stockout stalls both queues** regardless of treasury/IP balance. This is the design intent: construction is gated by economic capacity, not just by money.
- **When mod-adding new buildings**, give them a maintenance group of the tier that fits (§ Maintenance tiers), or a realistic `goods_input_construction_add` in their own maintenance PMs. Skipping this makes the building free to maintain in capacity terms, undermining the system.

### Market settings without retooling or maintenance

`free_market_construction_rule` has five settings. Four run the market (`te_free_market_construction_on` is true for all four, so the market itself works the same):

| Setting | Maintenance (`pmg_maintenance*`) | Retooling (`pm_retooling`) |
|---|---|---|
| `free_market_construction_enabled` (default) | `pm_maintenance` tiers | applies |
| `free_market_construction_no_ai_retooling` | `pm_maintenance` tiers | removed by script only for AI-owned buildings |
| `free_market_construction_no_retooling` | `pm_maintenance` tiers | removed by script |
| `free_market_construction_no_maintenance` | `pm_no_maintenance`, forced (the direct setting's flags for that group) | removed by script (it would multiply nothing) |
| `free_market_construction_disabled` | `pm_no_maintenance`, forced | applies, multiplies nothing (§ Free Market Construction off) |

Everything else in the three alternative market settings is the enabled setting's: the Construction Site's market method, and the company buildings (`pm_disney_world`, `pm_generic_industrial_city`, `pm_generic_monument_to_industry`) and Engineering & Logistics barracks keep their construction-good recipes, which are production, not upkeep.

**No retooling cannot be a PM flag.** `pm_retooling` is a static modifier the engine applies for `RETOOLING_WEEKS`; neither a static modifier nor a define has a rule gate, and a cancelling modifier would have to be put on each retooling building by the same script that can simply remove the penalty (`scripting_best_practices.md`, "Gating a Mechanic on a Game Rule When It Lives in a Define, PM or Building Group"). So `te_building_retooling_waived` (`te_construction_market_triggers.txt`) gates `te_remove_waived_pm_retooling` (`extra_effects.txt`, building scope). It combines `te_pm_retooling_waived` (the two global settings), the AI-only setting with `owner = { is_ai = yes }`, and the railway exception below. All rule checks name their settings positively, so a pre-rule save keeps paying. The effect removes the modifier:

- from `on_production_method_changed` (`te_on_production_method_changed_retooling`, `te_construction_market_on_actions.txt`; root = the building), at once and again a day later through `te_construction_market_building_events.3`, since whether the engine applies the modifier before or after the hook fires is unverified;
- from the weekly sweep `minor_events_timelineextended.100` (every building of every state), which catches any PM change the hook misses, such as one the engine makes itself.

The AI-only check reads the building's current owner each time, including the delayed event and weekly sweep. Human-controlled subjects still pay; AI subjects of a player are exempt. Taking control of an AI country does not restore a penalty already removed, but subsequent PM changes pay normally. No persistent country waiver or extra pulse is needed.

The same effect keeps its older job in every setting: removing the modifier from level-0 buildings (also from `on_start_expanding_building`).

**Railways: the Transport principle swap is free in every setting** (owner ruling, #511). A railway's steam, electric or diesel method switches itself into its `pm_*_trains_principle_transport_3` variant through `replacement_if_valid` when the bloc reaches tier 3, and back when the bloc loses tiers 3–5. The player chose neither switch. On each railway PM change the hook runs `te_railway_note_train_method` (`extra_effects.txt`) first, and so does the weekly sweep for every state with a railway (`te_state_note_railway_train_method`), which catches a swap the hook missed and keeps every railway recorded. It compares the state's `te_state_railway_train_code` (steam 1, electric 2, diesel 3, +10 for the tier-3 variant; Centralized Traffic Control through Autonomous Trains 4–7; `pm_early_trains` 0) against the last recorded `var:te_rail_train_code` (a state variable, since buildings don't hold variables reliably; one railway per state). Only the swap itself (`te_state_railway_tier3_swap`) sets a two-day `te_rail_retool_waive` marker on the state, which `te_building_retooling_waived` reads. It covers three cases: the same method moving +10; no record yet with the railway on a variant; or, after the bloc lost the principle, a recorded variant moving exactly −10 or landing on `pm_early_trains`, the group's fallback. A new train method, the passenger group or Centralized Traffic Control and later still pay. Residuals, accepted under the owner's ruling:

- **The waiver strips the whole `pm_retooling`,** including time left from an earlier player switch. A bloc leader can clear its members' railway retooling by dropping the transport principle to tier 2 and re-adopting tier 3.
- **The first weekly sweep on an old save, or on a new state object,** waives every railway already on a variant, whatever its retooling came from. A railway not yet recorded that jumps from a lower method straight onto a variant is waived once.
- **Another PM change within the two days rides along.** If the hook missed the swap, it rides along until the next sweep.
- **If losing the tier drops a railway to `pm_early_trains`,** the player's switch back pays full retooling. No waiver is built for that yet.

The sweep is a fallback only for a swap the hook missed. If the hook fired and the engine applied `pm_retooling` more than a day later, only the day-later re-check helps.

**Play-test list.**
1. *No retooling, switch a PM* on a factory with maintenance: the Retooling modifier does not appear on the building (or is gone the next day), and its construction input stays at 0.1 per level (times cost scaling). An AI country's buildings show no Retooling modifier after a few weeks.
2. *No maintenance, day 1*: `pmg_maintenance` shows No Maintenance only; the construction market read-out's demand is the government and private purchases plus the company buildings. Construction Sites, sectors and the panel work as in the default game.
3. *Default game*: switching a PM still applies Retooling (+1000% construction input).
4. *No AI retooling*: switch a PM on completed factories in a player country and an AI country, including subjects. Only the AI factory loses Retooling (at once or the next day), while both retain normal maintenance. Let the weekly sweep clear an existing AI penalty. Take control of the AI country and switch again: the new penalty stays. Repeat with two human players in multiplayer; both pay.
5. *Graded maintenance, default game*: a railway on steam trains shows Light Maintenance (0.05 construction a level, times cost scaling). Switching it to electric trains moves it to Standard Maintenance (0.1) by itself, and on to containerized cargo to Heavy Maintenance (0.15); switching back steps it down. Switch a steam railway straight to containerized cargo: it should settle on Heavy within a few days (light, standard, heavy). The same for a port (industrial, modern, container port) and a power plant (coal-fired to oil-fired: Light to Standard). A bloc's Transport tier 3 swapping electric trains into their principle variant leaves the maintenance method alone. A newly built railway starts on Light, or on the tier of the method it is built with. In the no-maintenance setting all three show No Maintenance.
6. *Discoverable mines*: a bauxite (or copper, graphite, potash) mine on picks and shovels shows Traditional Workings and pays no construction; a condensing engine pump moves it to Mechanized Workings (0.05), as on an iron mine. A lithium mine shows Light Maintenance whatever its pump, and can't take the high-pressure hydraulic pump before Modern Materials.

### Free Market Construction off (direct construction)

`free_market_construction_rule` (default enabled) turns the whole market off at game setup for base-game-style construction. Script asks `te_free_market_construction_off` / `_on` (`common/scripted_triggers/te_construction_market_triggers.txt`), which test the **disabled** setting so a save from before the rule keeps the market.

**The Construction Site is the construction sector.** `CONSTRUCTION_CAMP_BUILDING` is a define, so no rule can move it off `te_construction_market_site`. In a direct game the site therefore *is* the Construction Sector: buildable and expandable by the government (`can_build_government` = the rule; `possible` = Urbanization, as vanilla's sector), with tier PMs whose recipes are written out. Being the camp, it gets vanilla's whole treatment: the engine buys its inputs only for the points actually spent, and the AI's construction planning (below) works on it. `building_construction_sector` can't be built in a direct game.

**The rule's PM flags do the switching** (`common/game_rules/extra_game_rules.txt`, the vanilla `disable_<pm>` / `force_<pm>` mechanism of `monument_effects`; `scripting_best_practices.md`, "Locking Production Methods"). No script activates or corrects a PM during play; each setting disables the other's methods (all `is_hidden_when_unavailable`):

| Group (buildings) | Market (rule on) | Direct (rule off) |
|---|---|---|
| `pmg_base_te_construction_market_site` (site) | `pm_te_construction_market_base`: 1 construction good → 1 point | `pm_te_direct_construction_<tier>`, 7 tiers: the Construction Sector tier of the same name (`extra_pms.txt`) with its construction-good output as `country_construction_add` — the same inputs, employment, `state_construction_mult`, mortality and required inputs. Per level: 1 / 2 / 3.5 / 5 / 6 / 10 / 16 points. `ai_selection = most_productive`; the tech gates are the sector's |
| `pmg_construction_automation`, `pmg_construction_principle` (sector and site) | on the sector only in practice: each gated PM's `unlocking_production_methods` lists the 7 sector tiers and the 7 direct tiers, never the market base PM | the same PMs, on the site |
| `pmg_maintenance`, `_light`, `_heavy`, `_intensive`, `_mine`, `_graded` (74 building types) | `pm_maintenance` (0.1 construction per level) or its `_light` (0.05), `_heavy` (0.15) or `_intensive` (0.2) variant, the mines' traditional (0) or mechanized (0.05) method, or the graded methods (0.05 / 0.1 / 0.15 by base method); `pm_no_maintenance`, forced, in the no-maintenance setting | `pm_no_maintenance`, forced |

**Script left.**
- *Conversion at game start* (`te_direct_construction_convert_sectors`, from `on_game_started_after_lobby`, when the rules are final): every `building_construction_sector` the history placed becomes a site of the same level (capped at 100) on the matching direct tier. It then sets the global `te_direct_construction_converted`, which closes the sector's `can_build_*` — until then they stay open so the history's own `create_building` seeding works whatever the rule.
- *Private share*: `te_direct_construction_law_private_share` restores the economic-system laws' vanilla allocations (25 / 50 / 75 / 35 / 10 %), which `construction_system_law_injections.txt` cancels for the market; keep the two in step. Applied at country setup (`te_direct_construction_country_setup`) and refreshed weekly (`te_direct_construction_on_weekly_pulse`).
- *AI site guard* (`te_direct_construction_keep_ai_sites`, weekly). Play-testing showed AI countries removing every Construction Site they had. No script removes a site in a direct game, so this is the AI downsizing them, and script can't steer that. The site stays `downsizeable` for players, so the guard works after the fact. Each week every state records its site's level and tier (state variables `te_dc_site_level` / `te_dc_site_tier`, recorded for players too, so a player's own downsizing is never undone). An AI country then gets back any levels it lost since the last pulse. A site that is still there is raised with `create_building`, so it keeps its workers (the engine's documented "larger of the two levels"). If it comes out above the record (the disputed "adds levels" reading), it is rebuilt at exactly the record. A rebuilt or re-created site is moved back to its recorded tier. The guard skips an AI country `in_default`, whose record follows it down, and one without Urbanization (the site's `possible`). The AI can still add levels and sites and choose tiers.
- *Base construction* (no script). The disabled setting applies `te_direct_construction_base_modifier` ("Base Construction", `country_construction_add = 5`) to every country, player and AI, with `apply_modifier = all:<modifier>` (vanilla `game_rules.md`). It is always on and needs no refresh. A country with no sites (the AI in default, a player who downsized everything, a country before Urbanization) can therefore still build. The value is five wooden site levels' worth, small beside a working construction sector. Its points carry no wages. Whether they draw on the sites' material purchases (the engine buys the camp's inputs per point spent) is unverified.
- *Market placement is off*: `te_construction_market_state_on_start` / `_on_end`, `te_construction_market_country_setup`'s capital site and `te_construction_market_on_border_changes` only act in market games.
- *Construction Cost Scaling*: `construction_cost_scaling_direct` (`country_construction_goods_cost_mult`, vanilla's lever on construction-material cost), same curve, same multiplicative adjustment.
- *Construction panel*: `te_direct_construction_section` shows construction per week (`modifier:country_construction_add`) and the law share; `te_construction_market_rule_on_sgui` picks the section and hides every purchase handler. The vanilla "no construction sector" prompt offers the site instead of the sector.

**Why the AI can run it.** Every piece the base game's AI uses is present in its vanilla shape: the camp is the buildable, government-funded building whose PMs carry `country_construction_add` (`ai_value = 500` in `00_modifier_types.txt`) and goods inputs, as vanilla's sector does. So the engine's construction planning applies unchanged: `MONEY_SPENDING_CONSTRUCTION_CRITICAL_THRESHOLD` / `_EXCESSIVE_THRESHOLD` (actual-to-wanted construction), `GOVERNMENT_BUILDING_STATE_POP_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_IMPORTANCE_*` (first level in populous states), `GOVERNMENT_BUILDING_NO_AVAILABLE_WORKFORCE_FACTOR` (staffing), and the debt pauses. The site's `ai_value` in direct games is weighted by the government queue (the market-mode sector's formula until 2026-10-11, when the sector moved to the construction price, which a direct game doesn't have) and 0 in market games. Tier choice is `most_productive` over the same shape of PMs as vanilla's `pmg_base_building_construction_sector`. None of this has been watched in game yet; see the play-test list.

**Deliberate differences from the base game and from the market-mode sector.**
- *Downsizing is allowed in both modes.* A direct game needs it: a site's wages are paid whether or not its points are used, so overbuilding must be reversible. In a direct game the AI's downsizing is undone at the next pulse by the AI site guard (above), except in default. In a market game it lets the player or the AI remove a site; `te_construction_market_ensure_site` puts back the last one, so the worst case is about two weeks without construction (a week to be re-placed, a week to hire and be sized).
- *Base construction.* Direct games give every country a flat 5 construction points a week (above).
- *Building-group properties.* `te_construction_market_bg` has no economy of scale (like vanilla's sector), no infrastructure use (vanilla 2/level, the market sector 0.1) and no urbanization (vanilla 5), and is hidden from the outliner.
- *Buffs that name the sector.* Throughput aimed at `building_construction_sector` or `bg_construction` (banking-event booms, `we_se_astronaut_industry`) doesn't reach the site, and `company_construction_power_bloc`, whose `possible` needs a level-5 sector, can't form.
- *Market-mode sites list two empty groups.* The site carries the automation and principle groups for direct games; on a market site their methods are unavailable (they need a sector or direct tier), so it shows No Construction Automation and No Effect.
- *Company buildings pay nothing* for the construction they consumed; the twins drop the input rather than price it in materials.
- *A converted site starts empty.* The sector's workers are released when it is removed; the site hires them back at its hiring rate of 1.0, so a direct game's construction may start a week late.
- *Saves from the first version of this rule* (#394, weekly capacity script) aren't migrated. Start a new direct game.

Downsize the only site: a new capital site appears at the next weekly pulse, and construction resumes the pulse after. **AI:** watch for AI countries downsizing their sites; a country that does it repeatedly would keep losing construction, and would need a further guard.
2. *Direct game, day 1.* No Construction Sector anywhere; a Construction Site of the same level on the same tier stands where each was. Sites show the seven tiers (the unresearched ones hidden), with automation and the principle group. `pmg_maintenance` shows No Maintenance only. The panel's construction per week matches the sites' levels × tier points (plus base construction). The private share matches the law.
3. *Direct game, build.* Queue a new site: it starts on a researched tier (which one the engine picks is unverified; any researched tier is acceptable). Expand one, then downsize it: its wages drop with it.
4. *Direct game, AI (observer, 10–20 years, watch 3–4 AIs of different size).* (a) Countries without a site build one once they have Urbanization; populous states get the first levels. (a2) No AI country outside default ends up without sites: levels an AI downsizes are back at the next weekly pulse, at the same tier. Check that a site that loses a level comes back at exactly its old level (not above it), and that the restored level rehires. Watch whether an AI downsizes the same site week after week; if it does, the guard is holding against constant pressure and the AI's reason (budget, staffing) needs a look. (b) Levels keep up with the construction queue (the panel's construction per week grows with GDP). (c) Sites move to higher tiers as the techs arrive. (d) Sites stay staffed (occupancy near 100% outside labour shortages). (e) Treasury and debt: no country sits paused on construction debt for long because of site wages or materials.
5. *Direct game, costs.* Construction Cost Scaling shows as Construction Goods Cost on rich countries, and the materials the sites buy follow the points spent.
6. *Direct game, base construction.* From day 1 every country, player and AI, shows Base Construction (+5) in its construction tooltip, and the panel's construction per week includes it. A pre-rule save and a market game don't show it. Downsize every site as a player: construction stays at 5 or more, and new sites can be queued and built.
