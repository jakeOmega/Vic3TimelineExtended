# Production Methods

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Production Methods (PMs)

- PM group icons are displayed in several GUI panels: `production_methods.gui`, `building_browser_panel.gui`, `building_details_panel.gui`, `goods_state_panel.gui`.
- The mod adds a `pmg_maintenance` PM group that should be **hidden** from all PM icon displays using `visible = "[Not(EqualTo_string(ProductionMethodGroup.GetKey, 'pmg_maintenance'))]"`.
- PM icon displays use `scrollarea` + `flowcontainer` (not `fixedgridbox`) so hidden items collapse properly and scrollbars appear when > 4 PM groups. Max width: `208` pixels (4 × 52px icons).

### Drugs (vanilla `opium`) and Pharmaceutical Industries

Drugs are vanilla's `opium` good under a new name, so anything that adds Drugs supply or demand also touches vanilla's opium content. `building_synthetics_plant_opium` unlocks at vanilla `pharmaceuticals` (era 2) with a six-method ladder in `pmg_synthetic_opium`. Its design rule and wage-breakeven targets are in the comment above `pm_pharma_alkaloid_extraction` (`extra_pms.txt`): the era-2 method loses to a plantation so it can't undercut the British–Qing trade, and later methods beat the opium Mechanized Farm, trimmed to 55. What vanilla keys off the good, and how the mod handles it:

- **The Opium Trade (`je_opium`)** has no timeout and completes when another country's market imports ≥200 of the good, firing `opium_events.1` against that people. Modern Drugs imports would fire it in the 20th century, so `common/journal_entries/te_vanilla_opium.txt` `REPLACE`s it with a 23 January 1912 cutoff (re-diff on every patch).
- **Qing's Opium Crisis ban** (`add_banned_goods = g:opium`) also bans medicine. Pharmaceutical Industries carry the plantation's `is_banning_goods` build check.
- **Cultural obsession** (Han starts with opium). `obsession_demand_*` in a need only override the defines' defaults (`DEFAULT_OBSESSION_DEMAND_MIN/MULT`), and the ±25% shift in what an obsessed pop spends on a need holding the good is global (`OBSESSION_POP_NEED_EXPENSE_MULT`). So leaving the fields out of `popneed_healthcare` should not exempt it: expect Han pops to put about 25% more into Healthcare, taken from their other needs. Not yet checked in game (plan § 4).
- **Healthcare** (`popneed_healthcare`, `common/pop_needs/extra_pop_needs.txt`) is a Drugs-only need from wealth 16 (`healthcare_need` in `pop_needs_curves.py`). The owner's target: Pharmaceutical Industries at 1.5–2% of GDP in a modern rich country, overwhelmingly from healthcare, which is about 3% of consumption. A buy-package value is a cost at base prices, so the anchors (0 at 15, 10 at 20, 30 at 25, 85 at 30, 160 at 35, 290 at 40, 520 at 45, 900 at 50) hold Healthcare at 2.9–3.2% of the package from wealth 29 to 50. Past 50 it rises 76 a level (1,660 at 60, 4,624 at 99, 12,300 at 200): the share falls, but a richer pop still buys more. Wealth 100–200 come from the curve itself (`CURVES_PAST_99`), not the generator's power-law fit. `test_healthcare_need.py` pins the curve, the generated buy packages and the 3% band.
- **Leisure without Drugs.** `REPLACE:popneed_leisure` in `extra_pop_needs.txt` is vanilla's Leisure minus its `opium` entry. Leisure grows without limit (394 at wealth 40, 9,648 at 60), and a need's split follows each good's share of market sell orders, so a market full of Pharmaceutical Industries' output would pull ever more Leisure into Drugs. Intoxicants keeps Drugs at 0.75: the 19th-century British–Qing trade depends on it. Re-diff the replacement on every vanilla patch.
- **Plantation penalties.** Health-system laws (`INJECT:law_*_health_*` in `common/laws/modified_health_system.txt`) carry `building_opium_plantation_throughput_add` −0.02 / −0.03 / −0.05 per level of `institution_health_system` (in game the Ministry of Health; laws Charity Hospitals / Private / Public Health Insurance), and the Ministry of Consumer Protection −0.10 per level. Throughput is the plantation-only lever; `goods_output_opium_mult` applies per good and would cut Pharmaceutical Industries too. Only the enacting country's plantations shrink. `test_opium_plantation_penalties.py` pins the values.
- **Medicine Shortage** (`drugs_shortage.1`, `events/drugs_shortage_events.txt`): rolled yearly at 75% from `drugs_shortage_yearly_events` (`common/on_actions/drugs_shortage_on_actions.txt`), cooldown `short_modifier_time`. Its trigger copies vanilla's `market_goods_shortage_alert` for Drugs (`market_goods_shortage_ratio > 0.33`, `country_has_local_shortage`), not banning Drugs, and `drugs_healthcare_population_share >= 3` (percent of people at wealth 25+, `common/script_values/te_drugs_values.txt`). Under the revised Healthcare curve that gate matched "Healthcare is a third or more of the country's Drugs demand" in 57 of 63 of the probe's country-saves, so it stays; the value's comment has the reasoning. Options: buy abroad (`drugs_shortage_spending` × `sv_money_flow_event_medium`), ration (`drugs_shortage_rationing`, +3% mortality), a Pharmaceutical Industries drive (needs one; spending × large plus +25% throughput) or a poppy reprieve (needs a plantation; +30% plantation throughput, Intelligentsia −5 / Devout −3 / Landowners +3 approval). Modifiers in `common/static_modifiers/event_modifiers.txt`.
- **Measuring:** `event te_debug_drugs.1` (console only) logs every country's population share at wealth 10–50, Drugs buildings, price and ban to `debug.log` on `TE_DRUGS_PROBE` lines, plus world supply by method and buy/sell orders.
- **Field Hospitals** use Drugs as in vanilla; First Aid and Medevac Helicopters use liquor.
- Companies, prestige goods, Britain's plantation JE, the opium mobilization option and Free Trade's ban removal key off plantations or the good existing, not off demand levels.

- **Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs (INCB)**: Assembly business opens after any UN member researches `antibiotic_mass_production`, at authority ≥20. Major powers with `pharmaceuticals` may propose. Country-scoped parties carry `un_narcotics_control_modifier` at ×E: −15% `building_opium_plantation_throughput_add`, +5% `building_synthetics_plant_opium_throughput_add`, +3% prestige. No exporter regime term; see `test_un_convention_registry.py` and the approved narcotics spec.

### Barracks training methods

Five methods `INJECT`ed into vanilla's `pmg_training` (`common/production_methods/barracks_training_pms.txt`). The group has `ai_selection = most_productive`, which on a building with no output means the highest training rate. So `pm_officer_academies` (26/level, `combined_arms`) and `pm_neural_grafting` (120/level, `bioenhanced_soldiers` plus an enhancement-permitting augmentation law; buys `opium` and electronic components, +25% servicemen mortality) extend vanilla's ladder past Mobile Warfare Tactics (24) for the AI. `pm_expeditionary_logistics` (10, `predictive_logistics`, `building_mobilization_cost_mult` −0.1) and `pm_reserve_cadre` (12, `public_works_programs` and National Militia or Mass Conscription) train slower and are player choices. `pm_political_commissars` (20, `mass_media` and Single-Party State or Council Republic, hidden otherwise, 5% officers) trains slower too, but its count type carries `game_data = { ai_value = 5000 }`. The AI appears to score a barracks method by its modifiers' ai_values (`building_training_rate_add` is 100 a point), and with no goods every switch is a downgrade needing ×1.67 (`PRODUCTION_METHOD_STICKINESS_DOWNGRADE` 0.6), so a single-party AI moves to Commissars from Officer Academies on barracks of two levels or more, and to Neural Grafting from Commissars only on large ones.

Building-local effects sit in the PM. A national or state effect is a count in a custom modifier type (`barracks_training_modifier_types.txt`), declared `workforce_scaled` in the PM so staffing counts. A monthly pulse turns each count into a static modifier (`barracks_training_modifiers.txt`) through a never-removed variable. Each modifier has one refresh site, in `barracks_training_effects.txt`. A PM-borne country effect would otherwise stack once per barracks:

| Count (type) | Declared by | Pulse | Becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `country_academy_trained_battalions_add` | Officer Academies, 1 per staffed level | `te_barracks_doctrine_country_pulse` (monthly country) | `te_officer_academies_standing` × (count ÷ `army_size`, max 1): Armed Forces +20% political strength, +10% attraction at 1 |
| `country_commissar_battalions_add` | Political Commissars, 1 per staffed level | same | `te_political_commissars_indoctrination` × share: +15% in-government attraction at 1 |
| `state_reserve_battalions_add` | Reserve Cadre, 2 per staffed level | `te_reserve_cadre_state_pulse` (monthly state) | `te_reserve_cadre_conscription` (conscription rate = count × 1000 ÷ state population, max 0.25) and `te_reserve_cadre_capacity` (+1 conscription-center max level per reserve battalion) |

Any other source can declare these types (a law, a decree, a building) and the same pulse converts it; the tooltip breaks the count down by source. The reserve conversion assumes the engine sizes conscription from population (the `CONSCRIPTION_CENTER_LEVEL_POPULATION_DIVISOR` define says civilian population; the modifier text says workforce). If a reserve battalion turns out to buy only about half a conscription center in game, the engine uses workforce and the 1000 needs raising.

### Deep-Sea Mine and Extraplanetary Base logistics (Global Commons § 1)

The Deep-Sea Mine (`building_ocean_mine`) and the Extraplanetary Base (`building_space_mine`) both mine the five metal and mineral goods. Seabed licences cap the first, so each of its levels earns more than an Extraplanetary Base level until era 12's propulsion (spec `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-16-global-commons-design.md` § 1). Per level at base prices, the shared `pmg_maintenance_intensive` left out; a Deep-Sea profit is on Motor Ship Tenders / Magnetic Drive Tenders (support vessels, below):

| Method (era) | Construction | Staff | Profit | With Fusion Tugs | With both upgrades |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deep-Sea Mining (11) | 5,000 | 1,000 | 22,700 (103%) / 23,700 (113%) | | |
| Advanced Deep-Sea Mining (12, Advanced Nanofabrication) | | 1,000 | 63,400 (244%) / 64,400 (258%) | | |
| Automated Deep-Sea Mining (12, Artificial General Intelligence) | | 10 | 58,600 (190%) / 59,600 (200%) | | |
| Asteroid Mine (11) | 20,000 | 1,000 flat | 7,250 (24%) | 14,450 | 21,650 |
| Mining Colony (12, Space Colonization) | | 1,000 flat | 37,000 (33%) | 65,800 | 94,600 |
| Resort Colony (12, Space Colonization) | | 1,000 flat | 38,000 (34%) | 66,800 | 95,600 |
| Self-Replicating Miners (13, Von Neumann Machines) | | 500 flat | 104,050 (127%) | 123,250 | 142,450 |

- `pm_ocean_mine` mines the polymetallic-nodule mix (mostly manganese and nickel–cobalt, so Structural and Tech-Critical Metals 260 each) and covers every output of the Asteroid Mine on lower inputs, with a level's staff the size of the base's whole crew. The era-12 methods produce twice its output on their own inputs. The base method's margin stays under the late land tech-metal mines' (Smart Miners, 172%) on either tender; the era-12 ones exceed it (spec § 11 #3).
- **Support vessels** (`pmg_support_vessels_building_ocean_mine`, methods after the mine's in `extra_pms.txt`): the mine's ships, for every extraction method of both families, which carry none. Motor Ship Tenders (default, 40 Motor Ships a level, 3,600) and Magnetic Drive Tenders (`modern_material_science`, 20 Magnetic Drive Ships, 2,600). That technology makes Magnetic Drive Ships and gives ports, fishing wharves and whaling stations their magnetic-drive methods, but it is on a separate branch from Abyssal Plain Mining, so the mine starts on Motor Ships. A switch retools the mine (260 construction a level on `pm_maintenance_intensive`'s 0.2 a week), about 5 years of the 1,000 saving at base prices. Both methods only cost, so the group takes `ai_selection = most_productive` and the methods `ai_value` 1000 / 3000, `pmg_emissions_control`'s pattern for moving the AI up and never back (unconfirmed in game: `docs/testing/global-commons-playtest.md`).
- **Logistics groups** (`common/production_methods/te_global_commons_logistics_pms.txt`, groups in `te_global_commons_logistics_pm_groups.txt`): Launch to Orbit (Chemical Launch, default; Tethered Launch, `space_elevator`) and Transport in Space (Chemical Tugs; Fusion Tugs, `compact_fusion_reactors`). Each upgrade is `goods_input_launch_capacity_mult = -0.3` in `building_modifiers.unscaled` (registered in `mod_entity_modifier_types.txt`; vanilla registers that axis for six goods only). The two add to −60%. `space_elevator` requires `compact_fusion_reactors`, so Fusion Tugs always comes first. A method can't be gated on owning a building, so Tethered Launch needs the technology, not an elevator.
- **A flat crew** (owner, 2026-10-31): the Extraplanetary Base's five base methods hire their staff in `building_modifiers.unscaled`, the same at any level (800 on Regolith Processing, 500 on Self-Replicating Miners, 1,000 on the others), since a base needs its colonies' room to pay and its levels are off-world work. Each crew carries `building_job_attractiveness_mult = 5`, as every flat crew (`pm_ai_managed_*`, the Grid Transfer Station). `pm_costs.py` reads only `level_scaled` staff, so their cost comments read Employment 0; `test_deep_sea_rebalance.py` (`ExtraplanetaryBaseFlatCrew`) holds the crew.
- **Self-Replicating Miners** (`pm_self_replicating_miners`, Von Neumann Machines, era 13 spec § 7.10): seed miners that build their copies from the ore they dig. Against the Mining Colony, a level launches two thirds of the Launch Capacity (320) and adds 20 robotics, every output is a quarter larger, and the crew is half (400 engineers, 100 academics). A smaller launch bill makes the logistics upgrades worth less here: it out-earns every Deep-Sea method with no upgrade, and the Mining Colony on every combination (`test_deep_sea_rebalance.py`, `SelfReplicatingMiners`). The base has no automation or AI-managed group, so no other method names its base methods.
- **AI:** the groups keep the default `ai_selection` (most_profitable). An upgrade lowers only the Launch Capacity bill, so it is the more profitable method at every price and can't flip with the market.
- **Known gap:** Space Colonization (society) and Advanced Nanofabrication (production) are both era 12 and independent, so a country with only the first runs a Mining Colony (37,000) beside an era-11 Deep-Sea Mine (22,700). Per point of construction the Deep-Sea Mine still earns 2.4× as much.
- **Rule off keeps the old mine.** These figures belong to the capped mine. With `global_commons_disabled` the mine is uncapped (spec § 2.9, "uncapped as today"), so it keeps its pre-rebalance methods, `pm_ocean_mine_uncapped` and its two era-12 twins (6,200 / 30,400 / 25,600 a level on Motor Ship Tenders, 2,000 staff at the base method), on the same support vessels. The rule's settings switch the two families with `disable_pm_` flags (`extra_game_rules.txt`), every method `is_hidden_when_unavailable`, the pattern of § Locking Production Methods in `scripting_best_practices.md`. Unverified in game, as there: which method an existing building moves to when a mid-game rule change disables its own, and whether a save from before the rule gets either setting's flags.
- `test_deep_sea_rebalance.py` holds the orderings from the files (`--table` prints the table above); the cost comments in `extra_pms.txt` are `pm_costs.py`'s.

### Aneutronic Fusion's methods (era 13 spec § 7.8)

Aneutronic Fusion (era 13, military, after Compact Fusion Reactors) adds a base method to two buildings. Per level at base prices, from the `pm_costs.py` comments:

| Method (era) | Group | Input cost | Output | Staff | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compact Fusion Power (12) | `pmg_base_building_fusion_power` | 128,000 | 12,000 electricity | 1,100 | 232,000 (181%) |
| Aneutronic Fusion Power (13) | same | 152,500 | 16,000 electricity | 950 | 327,500 (215%) |
| Microgravity Alloying (12) | `pmg_steelmaking_process` | 21,400 (150 iron, 180 electricity, 50 launch capacity) | 600 steel | 3,000 | 8,600 (40%) |
| Fusion Torch Reclamation (13) | same | 20,000 (50 iron, 600 electricity) | 600 steel | 3,000 | 10,000 (50%) |

- **Aneutronic Fusion Power** (`pm_base_building_aneutronic_fusion_power`). Proton–boron fusion gives off charged particles, not neutrons, and its current is drawn from the plasma. Against Compact Fusion Power it takes a quarter of the engines (no turbines) and of the lead (almost no shielding), twice the tech metals, 500 electronic components and 15 advanced materials, and staffs 450 laborers, 300 machinists, 100 engineers and 100 academics a level. The mod has no radioactive-waste mechanic, so "no waste" is the lead cut and the method's description.
- **Its own AI-managed tier.** `pm_ai_managed_power_plant_reactor` cancels 500 laborers, 400 machinists, 100 engineers and 100 academics a level, exact for the three older fusion bases and the Nuclear Plant (#996). The new base staffs fewer, so the reactor method doesn't list it; `pm_ai_managed_power_plant_aneutronic` (hidden when unavailable, the same inputs and 1,000-engineer crew) cancels 450, 300, 100 and 100. A new power base method needs one of the AI-managed power methods to list it, with a cut that matches its staff. The two flat automation tiers (Centralized Control, Integrated Smart Systems) cut at most 420 laborers, 280 machinists and 40 engineers, which the new staff covers.
- **Fusion Torch Reclamation** (`pm_fusion_torch_reclamation`): the 1969 Eastlund–Gough fusion torch, which breaks scrap and waste back into its elements. It trades ore for electricity, with Microgravity Alloying's steel and staff and pollution 10 (Microgravity's 20). Bespoke Alloys lists it (its cut leaves 10 engineers, as on Microgravity), and `gen_carbon_capture_pms.py` puts it on the not-applicable capture method beside Microgravity, since it burns no coal.
