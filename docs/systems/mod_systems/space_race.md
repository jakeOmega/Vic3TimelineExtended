# Space Race

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Space Race (`je_space_race_*`)

Multi-stage competition system simulating a space race between great and major powers. Nine journal entries: seven milestones with a progress bar, the repeatable Solar System Colonization entry (34 globally-claimed colonies across 5 stages), and a passive entry that waits out the interstellar probe's transit. Semi-parallel progression, "The First" bonuses, Safe/Ambitious approach choices, and failure mechanics. Cross-system connections to the UN, private companies, buildings, and tourism. The per-entry table of techs and prerequisites is in `docs/systems/journal_entry_systems.md` → Space Race.

### Milestone Progression (Semi-Parallel)
```
Suborbital Flight (rocketry; great or major power)
    └→ Orbital Flight (guided_missiles)
        └→ Moon Landing (space_exploration)
               ├→ Outer Solar System Probe (space_exploration) [shown once Orbital is done, but `possible` also needs the Moon Landing]
               ├→ Moon Base [retired 2026-10-29: the first lunar colony takes its place]
               ├→ Solar System Colonization, the Moon (cislunar_infrastructure)
               └→ Mars Landing (knowledge_economy) [shown once Orbital is done, but `possible` also needs the Moon Landing]
                      ├→ Interstellar Probe (aneutronic_fusion; also needs the Probe)
                      │      └→ Interstellar Results (passive 132-month wait, fires after probe launch)
                      └→ Solar System Colonization beyond the Moon (stages 1 to 5)
                             [Repeatable: 6 stages from the Moon's, 40 colonies in all]
```

Every entry also needs the matching programme bool from the Space Programme building's production method (`building_space_program`, itself unlocked by `guided_missiles`): `pm_earth_orbit` for Suborbital and Orbital, `pm_moon_mission` (space_exploration) for the Moon Landing and the Probe, `pm_mars_mission` (knowledge_economy) for Mars, `pm_solar_colonization` (cislunar_infrastructure) for colonization, plus `pm_deep_space_exploration` (space_colonization) from colonization stage 3, and `pm_interstellar_mission` (aneutronic_fusion) for the Interstellar Probe. Each PM also carries all the lower tiers' bools. Losing the bool fails the entry (see *Programme loss* below).

**The Interstellar Probe on Aneutronic Fusion** (era 13 spec § 7.8, phase 3, 2026-10-31). The probe moved from Compact Fusion Reactors (era 12) to Aneutronic Fusion (era 13, military, after it): `pm_interstellar_mission`'s technology, `te_decision_space_mission_interstellar`'s `is_shown` and the entry's `is_shown_when_inactive` and `possible`. `country_sr_interstellar_mission_program_bool` stays the method's marker, not a technology's: a technology carrying it would let the entry run without the method. Compact Fusion Reactors keeps the fusion torch, its funding level and its other unlocks. Old saves: `te_repair_era_13_technologies` (`te_old_save_repairs.txt`, flag `te_era_13_aneutronic_fusion_repaired`) gives Aneutronic Fusion to every Compact Fusion Reactors holder whose Space Program runs the Interstellar Mission, the method being the test of a probe under way (the entry's `possible` and failure outcome read its bool, which only that method carries), and to every Microfusion Cells (`fusion_batteries`) holder, since Microfusion Cells now requires it.

**Technologies of the lunar colonization design** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-lunar-colonization-design.md` § 5, build 1, 2026-10-29): `cislunar_infrastructure` (era 10, military, after `reusable_rocketry`; +2 funding cap) opens `pm_solar_colonization` (10,000 Launch Capacity a week, sized for Reusable Rockets; it was 25,000 on Directed Energy Weapons) and Orbital Assembly. `in_situ_resource_utilization` (era 10, military, after `reusable_rocketry`; +1; the design's production category and Additive Manufacturing prerequisite would be cross-category under Asteroid Mining, § Tech Tree Authoring) opens `building_space_mine` on its default method, `pm_regolith_processing` (half the Asteroid Mine's inputs; £1,600 a level and 2.0 a worker at base prices). `asteroid_mining` now needs `in_situ_resource_utilization` and `directed_energy_weapons`, and gives `pm_space_mine`, the near-Earth room and propellant depots. `nuclear_thermal_propulsion` (era 11, after `cislunar_infrastructure`; +1) gives the smaller off-window premium (design § 9, *Launch windows* below) through `country_sr_nuclear_thermal_launches_bool`, which `sr_launch_has_ntp` reads (so the tooltip line and the effect share one source). Directed Energy Weapons keeps its funding level and its other unlocks. From the design's second build colonization needs the Moon Landing (the Mars Landing for the worlds beyond the Moon), and the method, like every mission method, is gated by its technology alone (a PM has no `possible`): a country with Cislunar Infrastructure can switch to it in the building panel before the milestones and pay the bill for no colonization, as a Directed Energy Weapons country could before. Old saves: `te_repair_lunar_technologies` (`te_old_save_repairs.txt`) gives Cislunar Infrastructure to every country with Directed Energy Weapons and a Space Program, and In-Situ Resource Utilization to every Asteroid Mining holder, once.

### The Space Program: founding and missions

Two kinds of decision (`common/decisions/extra_decisions.txt`) get a country into the race and up the mission ladder, for players and the AI alike. A player can still build the Space Program and switch its production method by hand.

**Why.** In the owner's 2073 player game every AI had every technology, yet only GBR, GER and SPA completed Suborbital Flight, only GBR and SPA Orbital Flight, none went further, and the only Space Program still standing was the player's. Every milestone after Orbital Flight needs a costlier mission, and the space programme's production method group used the default AI selection, `most_profitable`: every mission's goods are a pure cost, so the AI always kept the least-loss one, Earth Orbit.

- **Found a Space Program** (`te_decision_found_space_program`). Shown with the Space Race rule, `rocketry` (when Suborbital Flight appears), for a great or major power that is not a revolutionary or decentralized and has no programme; possible with `guided_missiles`. It places the building in the country's capital at once, free, on `pm_earth_orbit` (`create_building`'s `activate_production_methods`); the building's `possible` is `sr_space_program_site` (`space_race_triggers.txt`), `owner.capital ?= THIS`. Until 2026-11-03 the site was the market capital of a market the owner led, so a customs-union member could never found one (owner: that made no sense, and one rule, the capital, for everyone; the next release is save-incompatible, so a program in a market capital that is not the capital, such as the USA's New York, being removed costs nothing). Nothing reads where the program stands. The owner's ruling: the programme's expense is its use, not its construction.
- **Begin the next mission** (`te_decision_space_mission_<mission>`), one decision per mission, each `activate_production_method` on `building_space_program`. Each is shown once its production method's technology is researched and while the programme runs the mission it builds on, and is possible once the milestones before it are complete:

| Decision | Production method | Shown while the programme runs | Possible once complete |
|---|---|---|---|
| Begin the Moon Mission | `pm_moon_mission` (`space_exploration`) | Earth Orbit | Orbital Flight |
| Begin the Mars Mission | `pm_mars_mission` (`knowledge_economy`) | Moon Mission | Moon Landing, Deep-Space Probe |
| Begin Solar Colonization | `pm_solar_colonization` (`cislunar_infrastructure`) | Moon Mission or Mars Mission; while a lunar world is open, or after the Mars Landing while stage ≤ 2 or a colony beyond the Moon held | Moon Landing |
| Begin Deep Space Exploration | `pm_deep_space_exploration` (`space_colonization`) | Mars Mission or Solar Colonization; stage ≥ 3, no colony beyond the Moon held, worlds left | Mars Landing |
| Begin the Interstellar Mission | `pm_interstellar_mission` (`aneutronic_fusion`) | Mars Mission or above; Interstellar Probe not done | Deep-Space Probe, Mars Landing |

The first three follow the owner's rule, "the milestones the current mission serves are complete". The last two depart from it, because the ladder is not straight past Mars: `country_sr_deep_space_exploration_program_bool` is read only by Solar System Colonization's active branch at stage 3 or later, which a holder of a colony beyond the Moon bypasses, so Deep Space Exploration helps only a country holding none once the inner worlds are claimed (a lunar colony alone doesn't open the outer system); and the Interstellar Probe needs only the Deep-Space Probe and the Mars Landing, while colonization never "completes" for one country while worlds are left. The Interstellar Mission carries every lower bool, so switching to it never stops colonization. One more decision, Resume Solar System Colonization, switches no method: it runs a finished colonization programme again for the Moon (*The Moon Base retired* above).

- **AI weights.** Every decision weighs 0 unless `sr_ai_should_participate` (the top three by ranking: any other AI makes no progress but would pay the bill) and the country is out of default and not under the bankruptcy modifier (`sr_ai_space_budget_in_trouble`). Founding also needs `net_fixed_income` (the affordability reader `cultural_hegemony_buttons.txt` uses) over the whole Earth Orbit bill (`sr_mission_bill_earth_orbit`: the mission's Launch Capacity at full staff, `sr_mission_units_*`, held to the production methods by `test_space_program_decisions.py`, times our market's price, never below base) and that bill within the AI budget's climbing share (`sr_ai_mission_room_earth_orbit`). A mission decision needs the AI's Mission Limit ceiling to allow its mission (`sr_ai_mission_ceiling_allows_<mission>`): the monthly budget pass climbs AI programs itself, so the decisions only must not take one past its ceiling. A government building's goods are fixed expenses: vanilla's budget lesson says so (`lesson_budget_balance_how_4_desc`). Then 100. `ai_chance` is AI-only by nature, so nothing here is an `is_ai` rule.
- **`ai_selection = most_productive`** on `pmg_base_building_space_program`, as vanilla gives every building whose methods cost goods and output only country effects (university, government administration, every monument). **It climbs straight to a mission the AI cannot pay for** (the risk this note listed until 2026-11-15): in the owner's 1958 save nine AI programs ran Solar Colonization, eight of them, fully staffed, at 94% to 791% of their government's own revenue, most in countries that had never run a milestone. The group keeps `most_productive` (under `most_profitable` the engine would pull every AI program down to Earth Orbit, which nothing gates, failing its milestones), and a fifth group, **Mission Limit**, caps it: each mission above Earth Orbit lists in its `unlocking_production_methods` the ceilings that allow it, and an AI country's monthly budget pass sets the ceiling and the mission (`ai.md` § Space Program Mission Budget). Its methods carry nothing and are ratcheted (each unlocks only on itself), so the engine's AI can't move the group, and no panel shows it (the maintenance groups' filter; owner, 2026-11-16). A step down by the pass fails the milestones the higher mission carried, as any lowered method does (*Programme loss* below).
- **Why AI programmes may still vanish.** Two existing mechanics fit the 2073 game's lone surviving programme. `remove_invalid_buildings` (`extra_on_actions.txt`, monthly) and `te_on_state_owner_change` remove a Space Program that is not in its owner's capital (`sr_space_program_site`), once the capital link is known, so an AI that moves its capital loses it. Joining another country's market no longer does (it did until 2026-11-03). And `building_space_program` is downsizeable (the default; the hub and every vanilla monument set `downsizeable = no`), so the AI may demolish a building that only costs money.
- **Stays demolishable (owner, 2026-10-05).** Making it permanent would trap a player paying for a mission, since the group has no idle method. Watch item instead: in the next observer run, look for an AI that founds, demolishes and founds again. Since 2026-11-16 (owner) the AI's budget pass pulls down an AI program whose Earth Orbit bill has been over 20% of fixed income for a year, and the country founds or builds none for five years, nor while Earth Orbit would be over 10% (`ai.md` § Space Program Mission Budget), so that loop can't come from the budget.

### Key Mechanics
- **"The First" Bonus:** Global variables (`sr_global_first_*`) track first achiever. First nation gets ~2× rewards (prestige, innovation max, tech speed) permanently. Subsequent nations get smaller permanent modifiers. Each of the six milestones with a pair of rewards (suborbital through interstellar probe) has two completion outcomes (#1433, 1.15): *first* (goal reached, `sr_global_first_<m>` unset; header `je_space_race_<m>_first_outcome_header`, `_first_conclusion_desc`) and *later* (goal reached, set; `_completion_header`, `_complete_conclusion_desc`), ahead of the failure outcome, so the panel lists both rewards. Both run the same effect, and `sr_complete_milestone_effect_base`'s own check of the global variable still decides the reward: two countries reaching a goal on the same update may both conclude through *first* while only one is paid it. No outcome carries `historical` (vanilla puts it only on entries one country or region can get, and these are open to every great or major power; a flag is per outcome, not per country) or `fails_subgoal` (the mod defines no objectives, and a vanilla subgoal cannot name a mod entry). The names need no `_concluded` key: they read no scope, and the conclusion text already says which ending it was.
- **Approach Choice:** Standard, Safe or Ambitious, per entry. Standard is neither `sr_safe_<m>` nor `sr_ambitious_<m>`: every milestone starts on it, it has no modifier and no approach cost, and the player can return to it (scripted GUI op 4, `sr_effect_standard`; no AI button, as the AI never had a way back from an approach). It replaced, 2026-09-29, a no-approach state that drifted at a flat 0.5 a month with no roll (owner's play-test). Safe and Ambitious print their weekly innovation cost (`sr_approach_cost_<kind>_<m>`, from `sr_disp_cost_<kind>_<m>`) in their tooltips and the overview. The base monthly setback risk is a per-milestone script value — `sr_base_risk_<m>`, 5 for suborbital rising to 10 for solar colonization — and `sr_risk_pct_<m>` applies `country_space_race_risk_mult` to it (Safe contributes -0.5, Ambitious nothing). **The monthly roll and the widget's "Setback risk" line read the same script value**, so they cannot disagree; `sr_risk_shown_<m>` is the same figure under the panel's name (it read zero during the failure cooldown, retired 2026-10-31). There is no longer a `sr_base_risk` proxy variable or a `RISK =` parameter — a number copied into a variable one statement before it is rolled is readable by nothing else, which is why the risk could not be shown at all before.
- **Funding Levels:** 0 to `sr_max_funding_level` (base 3, increased by `country_space_race_max_funding_add` modifier from techs like `reusable_rocketry` +1, `space_colonization` +2; with every one a country has 13 by the end of era 10, 18 by era 11, 24 by era 12 and 26 in era 13, where Microfusion Cells (`fusion_batteries`) moved with the era 13 split of 2026-10-31, and tier V of Advanced Research adds 1). Each level costs innovation (-15/level via `sr_space_program_cost` consolidated modifier; `sr_innovation_per_cost_level` mirrors the 15 for the display, held equal by `test_space_race_approaches.py`). Solar colonization is billed only while it runs (`sr_milestone_billed_<m>`, 2026-09-29). Funding and approach are **per-JE** — you can fund moon landing heavily with safe approach while running a cheap ambitious probe.
- **Funding past the cap (2026-10-29, lunar colonization design § 5.1).** The cap is soft: `sr_possible_fund_up` tests `sr_max_funding_level_soft` (the cap plus `sr_funding_past_cap_steps`, 6). Each level past the cap adds less pace than the one before, 1/2 of a level for the first down to 1/7 for the sixth (`sr_funding_past_cap_step_<n>`), so six levels past it are worth 1.59. Every level still costs in full (`sr_<m>_cost` reads the variable) and adds its full +25% throughput, so the funding modifier is two: `sr_funding_progress` (`country_space_race_progress_add = 0.5`) at `sr_funding_<m>_effective` and `sr_funding_throughput` (`building_space_program_throughput_add = 0.25`) at `sr_funding_<m>_level`, each declared on the entry (see *Per-JE Cost*). `_effective` reads the country through `owner`, which in a declaration's country root is the country itself (`_within_cap` + `_past_cap_effective`, country-scoped, with `_past_cap` the raw excess). Nothing clamps a level to the cap: one that falls below it (a lost principle) leaves the level standing and the excess counts past the cap, and a level past the sixth step adds nothing. The AI stays within the cap (owner, § 15.6): its fund-up buttons ask `sr_possible_fund_up_within_cap`, and its fund-down buttons get +100 while `sr_funding_<m>_past_cap > 0`. The pace reads the cap, which technologies and Advanced Research V move with no button press; the declaration reads it live, so the pace follows a moved cap within days, while the stepper and the pace hover's Funding line read it at once (until #1404 a cached `sr_cost_funding_cap` and a monthly recalculation did this). Panel: past the cap the stepper's figure is `je_space_race_widget_funding_past_cap_<m>` ("9 / 7 (+0.8)", chosen by a `SelectLocalization` on `_past_cap`), the plus button's tooltip adds `SR_FUND_UP_PAST_CAP_<n>` from `sr_effect_fund_up`, and the pace hover gives `sr_disp_funding_within_cap` / `sr_disp_funding_past_cap`. `test_space_race_soft_cap.py` evaluates the values.
- **Per-JE Cost:** each active milestone's journal entry carries `sr_space_program_cost` with `multiplier = sr_<m>_cost` (that milestone's funding level + approach overhead: standard=0, safe=1, ambitious=2, times an era factor `sr_cost_factor_<m>` from 1x for suborbital to 12x for the interstellar probe; solar colonization's is 5 until the country's own Mars Landing, then 8, read through `owner`). **Declared, not added (1.15, #1404):** each of the seven billed entries declares `sr_space_program_cost` at `sr_<m>_cost`, `sr_funding_progress` at `sr_funding_<m>_effective` and `sr_funding_throughput` at `sr_funding_<m>_level` in `scaling_modifiers_while_active`, each 0 unless `sr_milestone_billed_<m>`, and `sr_safe_approach` / `sr_ambitious_approach` at 1 while `sr_safe_<m>` / `sr_ambitious_<m>` is set (on solar colonization, whose entry stays open for its colonies after the programme stops, also only while it is billed: until #1472 an idle programme's approach went on speeding or steadying every running milestone with nothing billed for it). The engine re-reads the values about every four days, so a click reaches the modifiers 2 to 5 days later, and the panel's figures that read them (pace, setback risk) follow then; the stepper and the cost lines read the variables and move at once. Participation (an AI's rank crossing 3), the solar production method, the funding cap and the Mars Landing's cost factor are read live too. Each running milestone's entry carries its own copies, so they add up across entries as before. Until then `sr_recalculate_cost` cleared and re-added them on every entry after each click, `immediate` and completion, and the monthly pulse recalculated when participation, the solar method or the cap had moved (cached in `sr_cost_participation`, `sr_cost_solar_running`, `sr_cost_funding_cap`) or an entry lacked one (`sr_cost_modifiers_missing`); `sr_refresh_milestone_statuses`, its tail, is what those call sites run now. `test_space_race_scaling_modifiers.py` evaluates every declaration. A single consolidated `sr_total_space_cost` script value used to exist for this and is gone — nothing read it after the per-JE split.
- **Failure:** Costs the failed milestone months of progress (`sr_setback_lose_months`, see *Progress in months* below): a minor setback (Safe, Standard) 1 to 4 months, the default answer 2 or 3; an Ambitious failure 4 months with the review, 5 or 6 for the answers that press on without it. Ambitious failures add decaying negative modifiers (`sr_mission_failure_effect`), and most answers the temporary safety review (−10% progress, −25% risk, decaying). All of it is applied by the failure event's chosen option, not in the pulse. Does NOT permanently block — just wastes time.
- **No post-setback cooldown (owner, 2026-10-31).** Until then an Ambitious failure set a six-month country-wide `sr_failure_cooldown` that skipped every milestone's roll while progress ran on, shown as "Shielded" (status codes 3 to 5). Switching every running milestone to Ambitious during it was free progress, so it went: every running milestone rolls every month.
- **Progress in months (2026-10-25).** Every event that moves a milestone's progress moves it by whole months of the programme's pace, `sr_progress`: setbacks through `sr_setback_lose_months = { MONTHS = N }` (the failed milestone), the in-progress events through `sr_boost_active_milestones = { MONTHS = N }` (every running milestone) or `sr_gain_progress_months` / `sr_lose_progress_months = { MILESTONE = m MONTHS = N }`. Each such event's `immediate` calls `sr_record_event_pace`, so a month is the pace when it fired (`sr_event_pace_value`) and changing funding while it waits changes nothing; the tooltips (`SR_SETBACK_LOSE_<N>_MONTHS_TT`, `SR_PROGRESS_ADD_<N>_MONTHS_TT`, `SR_PROGRESS_LOSE_<N>_MONTHS_TT`) print the months and the amount (`sr_event_months_<N>`). A loss never takes more than the milestone has, nor more than one goal. Why: until then a setback kept ×0.75 (Ambitious) or ×0.85 (Safe, Standard) of the progress, most options took a flat 3 to 10 on top, and the in-progress events gave a flat 2 to 30. The risk is a monthly roll, so a milestone sees setbacks in proportion to the months it takes, and each took a share of a progress that grows with the goal (50 to 650): a slow programme settled where its pace equalled its losses. In a simulation of the setback pools (no windfalls), Standard on the Mars Landing took 880 months on average at a pace of 4 (113 without setbacks) and at 2 never finished in 100 years; the flat amounts meant nothing past the first milestones. In months, the same simulation gives every milestone the same overhead at a given risk, whatever its goal or pace: about ×1.15 (5% risk) to ×1.4 (10%) on Standard, ×1.06 to ×1.18 on Safe, ×1.2 to ×1.37 of its faster base on Ambitious. The in-progress events' months follow their old points at the pace of their era (about 2 a month for Suborbital and Orbital Flight, 4 for the Moon Landing and the probe, 5 to 6 for the Moon Base and Mars, 10 for the Interstellar Probe and colonization), keeping each event's order between its options: 1 to 3 months, 4 for a gamble's best outcome. `test_space_race_progress_months.py`.
- **Every running milestone moves and rolls.** Whatever its approach, the pulse adds `sr_progress` and rolls `sr_risk_pct_<m>`; the setback kind follows the approach: Ambitious fails big (`sr_fire_ambitious_failure_event`), Safe and Standard suffer the minor setbacks (`sr_fire_safe_setback_event`). The flat `sr_progress_drift` for a milestone with no approach is gone (2026-09-29).
- **Failure events know which milestone failed.** The pulse calls `sr_fire_ambitious_failure_event = { MILESTONE = <m> }` / `sr_fire_safe_setback_event = { MILESTONE = <m> }`, which first `save_scope_as = sr_setback_<m>`. Saved scopes travel with `trigger_event`, so that marker is how the pool picks a theme (`.13` only for the Deep-Space Probe, not the interstellar probe, which stands on the pad until its milestone completes; `.14/.17/.71` only for Moon base / Mars / colonization, `.15` only for the Moon landing, …; shared triggers `sr_setback_is_deep_space_probe` / `sr_setback_is_crewed_outpost`) and how every option's helper — progress loss (`sr_setback_lose_months`), `sr_temporary_safety_review_effect`, `sr_clear_failed_milestone_flags` — finds its one milestone. Two journal entries failing in the same month cannot mix: each entry's pulse is its own script run. **Never dispatch twice in one script run** (the second event would carry both markers); `te_debug_sr_fire_setback` picks one milestone for that reason. A failure event with no marker at all (open in a save from before the marker existed, or fired from the console) falls back to the old behaviour: the first helper an option runs calls `sr_resolve_setback_markers`, which marks every milestone whose `sr_failed_<m>` is raised.
- **Failure Flags:** `sr_failed_<milestone>` is still raised by the pulse — it is the "setback not yet answered" bookkeeping — and each option clears the flag of its own milestone. Nothing reads it to decide *which* milestone an effect applies to any more.
- **"A more cautious approach"** (`.10.b`, `.15.c`) switches the failed milestone to the safe approach through `sr_switch_setback_milestone_to_safe` → `sr_effect_safe`, the journal-entry button's own body, so the approach flags, the approach modifiers (risk and speed), the cost and the widget status follow (`sr_mission_failure_effect` re-derives the status via `sr_refresh_milestone_statuses`). For solar colonization only while it is running, as the panel's own controls are.
- **Completion texts** (`.1/.3/.6/.7/.8`, and the flavour of `.3/.7`) claim a world first only under `has_variable = sr_was_first_<m>`; otherwise a `_later` variant ("…joins the nations that have…"). The rewards already split first from later (`sr_apply_milestone_reward_base`).
- **Achievement notifications (`.20`).** `sr_notify_milestone_achievement` adds the achiever's capital to a per-milestone *list* on every receiver (`sr_notify_achievers_<m>`) and queues one `.20`; each popup takes exactly one entry (`sr_take_milestone_notice_base`, an `else_if` chain) — the world first's entry before any other, since the list has no reliable order — and reads "world first" from the achiever's own `sr_was_first_<m>`. Two completions inside the 14-day delay — different milestones, or the same milestone by two countries — give two popups, each naming its own achiever, and option b's relations go to that achiever.
- **Programme loss (`.76`).** A lowered space-programme production method, or a lost / demolished building, fails every milestone its tier was carrying, and the journal entries notice on their own 4-day update schedule. Each failure outcome calls `sr_report_program_loss = { MILESTONE = <m> }`: it records the milestone and only the first queues `.76`, five days out, so one change gives one report whose option tooltip lists the closed programmes. The text is read at fire time: building still there → a refit (a choice, the original "Program Discontinued" text); building gone → "The Silent Gantry", an involuntary loss.
- **Starting again after a programme loss (2026-11-06).** The failure outcome concludes the entry, and the engine deletes a concluded record and never activates the type again (`docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md`, "A finished record is deleted"), so until then a milestone a refit closed never came back. A monthly pass, `sr_reentry_monthly` (`space_race_effects.txt`, from `space_race_on_action` after the inactive-milestone cleanup, players and AI alike while the race is on), adds `je_space_race_<m>` back once the country has been due it for a whole month: `sr_reentry_due_<m>` (`space_race_triggers.txt`) is no record of the type, active or inactive (the add is refused while one exists), not decentralized, not a revolution's rebels, then the entry's `is_shown_when_inactive` and `possible` word for word (`add_journal_entry` skips both; `test_space_race_reentry.py` holds them equal). - **Launch windows (Global Commons § 5, 2026-10-16; not yet seen in game).** Every colony site but the Moon's and Mercury's belongs to a window body (`gen_launch_windows.py`'s `BODIES`): Mars, the five asteroids (each its own), the Jupiter system, Venus, the Saturn, Uranus and Neptune systems, and the Kuiper Belt (one body on Pluto's orbit for all five stage-5 sites, Sedna included: each has a synodic period within days of a year). The Moon, Mercury, the Mars Landing and the probes are not gated.
  - **The sky.** A body's optimum is the Hohmann-phase departure (its true longitude at arrival is Earth's at departure + 180°, T half the transfer ellipse's period), from Standish's Table 1 (planets) and JPL SBDB osculating elements (asteroids, Pluto), 1900 to `END_DATE`; the window is the optimum's month ±1. The generated `sr_window_next_<body>` is one branch per cycle on `te_history_month_index` (optimum month, plus the months since the previous optimum as two decimals), from which `sr_window_months_to_next_<body>`, `sr_launch_offset_<body>` (signed months from the nearest optimum), the trigger `sr_window_open_<body>` and `sr_launch_premium_<body>` (a binary search on the offset) follow. The premium is `exp(extra Δv ÷ 3.7 km/s) − 1` for the month's best departure against the cycle's best window month, from a Lambert solver over all transfer times, averaged over 16 cycles, capped at 1000 (+100,000%). **Nuclear thermal** (lunar colonization design § 9): `sr_launch_premium_ntp_<body>` is the same extra Δv at 9 km/s, `exp(extra Δv ÷ 9) − 1` = `(1 + p)^(3.7/9) − 1` of the chemical p, rounded once from the Δv (on Mars +16% and +46% the first two months after a window, against +43% and +150%). Transit, drives, fleets, yards and refits don't change: nuclear thermal vehicles fly the same near-Hohmann transfers. `docs/engine/launch_windows_report.md` has every curve, both premiums with their cycle spreads, the off-window rule on Mars for each, the historical check and the Mars windows of era 11.
  - **The cache.** The global monthly pulse (`sr_launch_windows_on_action`, with the space race rule) stores per body `sr_window_is_open_<body>`, `sr_window_wait_<body>`, `sr_window_cost_<body>` and `sr_window_cost_ntp_<body>` (the two premium curves), and `sr_window_cache_month`; the entry's pulse refreshes it first if that month's is missing (a country pulse can precede the global one on the 1st). Nothing but the cache refresh and the self-check reads the generated chains, except that `sr_window_premium_now_ntp_<body>` reads its curve until a refresh has cached it (an old save loaded mid-month). The cached readers `sr_window_premium_now_<body>` and `sr_window_premium_now_ntp_<body>` are any scope; the country reader `sr_window_premium_country_<body>` picks the nuclear thermal one with `sr_launch_has_ntp` (`country_sr_nuclear_thermal_launches_bool`, which `nuclear_thermal_propulsion` carries), and is the only reader of the chemical cache (`test_launch_windows.py` holds it): `sr_launch_cost_<body>`, `sr_launch_target_cost_<body>` and so every colony fleet's cost and score, `sr_launch_months_cost_<body>` (the approach's test, for players and the AI alike) and Next Windows' premium all read it. The design named the country reader `sr_launch_premium_<body>`, but that is the chemical curve the global refresh reads.
  - **The rule** (`sr_launch_window_triggers.txt`). A site departs free in its window, or any month for a country with the fusion torch (`compact_fusion_reactors`' `country_sr_drive_torch_bool`) or the antimatter drive (`sr_launch_has_fusion`; or with the rule off). With `asteroid_mining`, a closed body departs at its premium (the nuclear thermal one with `nuclear_thermal_propulsion`, which makes off-window launches cheaper, never possible without depots) when the approach's test passes: not Safe, and goal × premium ÷ pace (`sr_launch_months_cost_<body>`, pace `sr_pace_rate_solar_colonization`) ≤ k × m (`sr_launch_months_allowed`; k 1 Standard, 2 Ambitious; m = `sr_window_stage_wait`, the stage's soonest window among its unclaimed sites, 0 while one is open or a world of the Moon or Mercury is left). `sr_launch_best_cost` is the cheapest over the stage; each site's `trigger` in `sr_establish_colony_effect` passes only when its body's `sr_launch_cost_<body>` equals it, so the draw can never found a site whose body is closed to that country. The pulse (`sr_launch_month`) founds at most one colony a month and spends goal × (1 + premium) only on a real claim. **A colony-less programme's staged fleets** (review L2): such a programme still restarts its bar when it stops (the failure outcome's cleanup when its method goes, the monthly cleanup when it deactivates, `immediate` when it re-activates or a revolution's winner inherits it), because progress carried into an activation would become the journal bar's baseline. `sr_launch_bank_staged_fleets` first keeps the whole goals apart (`sr_launch_banked_fleets`, additive, not once every world is claimed), and `sr_launch_restore_banked_fleets` adds them back, at the current stage's goal, at the start of the next `sr_launch_month`; only the remainder below a goal is lost. A colony holder never stops (the failure outcome needs no colony left, no deactivation, `immediate` keeps its progress).
  - **Setbacks** never take more than one goal (`sr_lose_progress_months` caps every loss at min(progress, goal)), so fleets staged in orbit are safe.

- **A revolution's winner continues the programme** (`docs/audits/civil_war_inheritance_audit.md` F5, #464). The winner inherits the loser's variables but none of its modifiers, and every entry it inherits active runs `immediate` again. So:
  - **`immediate` only creates what is missing.** Progress and funding are guarded with `has_variable`, so a milestone at 90 % stays at 90 %. That is safe for the native progress bar because an inherited record keeps the loser's bar: start date, baseline and goal are copied, not evaluated again (the German-revolution saves' `je_global_warming`: baseline 0.1 and goal 4.0 on the winner with the anomaly at 1.18). The monthly country pulse (`sr_sync_probe_data`, only for a country that has run a milestone: `sr_space_race_participant`) also records a probe result granted before the record existed, so old saves migrate themselves. The entries' modifiers need no rebuilding: the milestone entries declare their approach, choice, cost and funding modifiers, and solar colonization its colony modifiers, each from variables the winner inherits (`sr_safe_<m>` / `sr_ambitious_<m>`, the choice and funding variables, `<modifier>_held`, `sr_completed_solar_colonization` and the population and module records; 1.15 scaling modifiers, #1404; whether an inherited entry re-applies its declarations is the `scaling_inherited` self-check). ### The colony record

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-solar-colonization-holders-and-cession-design.md` (slices 0 and 1 of the scope in `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-25-solar-colonization-contest-scope.md`). Not yet seen in game; the self-checks and the play-test list are in `docs/testing/solar-colonies-playtest.md`.

- **The record is the truth, per world, in global variables:** `sr_colony_<key>` (claimed, as before), `sr_site_holder_<key>` (the holder, a country), `sr_site_spec_<key>` (1 or 2, the founding event's option), `sr_site_region_<key>` (the holder's capital state region, recorded monthly while it lives), `sr_site_mirror_<key>` (the country that shows the world), and per discovery `sr_site_disc_<d>` (1 or 2) and `sr_site_disc_<d>_pending`. Until 2026-10-25 nothing named a holder: a world was a claim flag and a modifier on its founder's journal entry, so annexing the founder removed the colony from the game.
- **The projection.** `sr_site_sync_<key>` makes the holder show the world and nobody else: the old mirror loses both specializations' `<modifier>_held`, the world's discovery outcomes and its discovery `_pending` / `_done`, each with its entry modifier behind `has_modifier`; the holder gains the chosen ones; both recount `sr_colony_count` from the record (`sr_sites_held_count`). So `<modifier>_held`, the Our Colonies panel, `sr_has_space_colony` and the revolution repair keep reading what they always read. The sync runs at every write and from the global monthly step for all 40 worlds.
- **Founding and choosing.** The draw sets the holder after the claim and syncs (it no longer adds to the count: the sync counts). Each founding and discovery event option calls `sr_site_choose_<modifier>`, a dispatcher with one generated effect per specialization and discovery outcome (120): its tooltip shows the modifier (`show_as_tooltip`, the multiplier 1 of a new colony's founding choice), and hidden, it marks `<modifier>_held`, writes the world's choice and syncs the world. `sr_seed_colony_discovery` also sets the world's pending flag; `sr_finish_colony_discovery` clears it.
- **The monthly step** (`sr_sites_global_monthly`, global `on_monthly_pulse`, under `sr_space_race_on`): per world, record the holder's capital region; pass a dead holder's world (fails `exists` or `is_country_alive`) to the owner of the most populous state in that region that can hold one (`sr_site_can_hold`: alive, not decentralized), or release it when there is none; let a claimed world with no holder be adopted by the country carrying its `_held`, or released; then sync (the first run's old-save migration and its 45-day grace went with #1429). Then every player's Solar System snapshot.
- **Released worlds** (no heir, the console, or an old save's vanished colonies): claim, holder and records removed; each stage's draw also lists every earlier stage's worlds behind their own claim test, and `gen_launch_windows`'s `sr_window_body_in_play_<body>` reads `sr_current_colonization_stage >=` its stage, so a released world departs in its own window at the current stage's goal. The entry's colonizing branch asks `sr_any_site_unclaimed` instead of `NOT sr_all_colonies_complete`, which stays set as history; every holder still completes with Interplanetary Trade Networks through the entry's pulse.
- **Revolutions.** `sr_repair_after_civil_war` runs `sr_sites_repoint_loser`: when the rebels won, every world held by `scope:te_cw_loser` is ROOT's at once.
- **A holder without the milestones** (a colony given by treaty or inherited): `is_shown_when_inactive` and `possible` have a colony branch (`sr_has_space_colony`), the colonizing branch carries the Moon Landing, and `sr_solar_colonization_qualified` joins `sr_solar_colonization_running` and the pulse's progress gate, so such a holder keeps its colony's modifiers, reads Idle, is billed nothing and founds nothing. A transfer opens its entry: a receiver holding the inactive copy every country starts with gets it at the engine's next check (the colony branch), and `add_journal_entry` opens it for one holding no record, whose entry failed or that the engine left out; over an inactive copy the add would be refused with an error (#1416). The failure outcome reports a programme loss only for a qualified country.
- **Notices** (players): `space_race_events.90` for a colony gained or lost by treaty, `.91` for an heir's; the first world each way is named through `sr_site_notice_<side>_name` (generated custom loc on an index variable), any more noted.

### Colony population

Design: the same, § 10 (slice 2), and since #1954 Part B the colony people design (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-22-colony-people-limit-modules-design.md`): the colony's people are its modules' limit. Simple and full colonies only.

- **The record:** `sr_site_pop_<key>` (global), so a cession or an heir takes the people along; cleared when the world is released. A founded colony starts at 0, an automated outpost. Room per world from the table (`room`, 10,000 to 50,000, 945,000 in all), times the world's base room factor `sr_site_room_mult_value_<key>`: 1 plus the holder's `country_sr_colony_room_add` (`gen_solar_sites.ROOM_MODIFIER`, script-only, `space_race_modifier_types.txt`), which Space Colonization (era 12) and Terraforming (era 13) carry at +0.5 each (the owner, 2026-11-22), so double with both, and never below the best factor the world has had (`sr_site_room_mult_<key>`, global, set in its monthly step beside the Habitat share and cleared on release, the same rule as § 4.4's share, so a cession or a break-away keeps the room). That product is `sr_site_base_room_<key>`, and a Habitat's and Terraforming's shares are of it, so they grow with it; the pick's and Solar System's people at the cap (`sr_site_people_at_cap_<key>`) read our own factor, `sr_our_room_mult`. Read through the holder, so it needs no self-check of its own beyond `habitat_marker`'s; growth 0.2% a month up to the room (the per-world monthly step), at `sr_site_growth_<key>`: 1.002 plus the holder's `country_sr_colony_growth_add` (`gen_solar_sites.GROWTH_MODIFIER`, script-only, `space_race_modifier_types.txt`), which the Clade Citizenship law (Bodily Autonomy, from Self-Directed Evolution, era 13; era 13 spec § 7.3) carries at +0.001, people born adapted to low gravity and radiation, so 0.3% a month (the technology itself carried it until 2026-11-24). Its name in tooltips is Space Colony Growth a Month, and `country_sr_colony_room_add`'s is Space Colony Room (owner, the same day: beside vanilla's Colony Growth Speed, "Colony Growth" read as Earth's colonization). ### Orbital debris
Design: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-16-global-commons-design.md` § 6. Follows the space race rule; with it off the pools never exist, the strike action and the decision are hidden, and `space_race_disabled` disables the three removal methods (`extra_game_rules.txt`, `disable_pm_*` flags).
- **Two pools, one index.** Global variables `orbital_debris_low` (1.5% decay a month, half-life ≈ 4 years) and `orbital_debris_high` (0.05%, ≈ 115 years); the index `te_debris_index` is their sum, capped at 100. `te_debris_clamp_pools` holds the pools to the cap too, the **low pool giving way first**: if the high pool gave way, a strike at the ceiling would swap a century of debris for a few years of it and striking would clean orbit. Tiers, for text only (`te_debris_tier` custom loc): Clear < 25, Cluttered 25, Hazardous 50, Kessler Cascade 75.
- **The world's step** (`te_debris_global_monthly`, global `on_monthly_pulse`, the 1st): the Kessler term (+0.2 high a month while last month's index ≥ 75), background traffic (+0.01 low per running Space Program, `te_debris_programs_running`; six programs settle near 4), decay, then removal: `te_debris_removal_world`, every country's `te_debris_removal_country` summed, taken from the high pool first; then, under Full Colonies, the ground lasers' sweep from the low pool alone (`sr_battery_sweep_step`, *Fleets* above). It also writes the panel's globals (`te_debris_removal_month`, `te_debris_cleaners`, `te_debris_top_cleaner`) and runs the once-a-game `TE_SELF_CHECK debris_removal`.
- **What it does** (`te_debris_country_monthly`, the monthly country pulse, the one refresh site of both modifiers): `te_orbital_debris` at `multiplier` = the index on every country that uses orbit (`te_debris_uses_orbit`: a Space Program, a Space Elevator, an Orbital Solar Collector, an Orbital Battlestation or an Extraplanetary Base). Per point: setback risk +1% (`country_space_race_risk_mult`), pace −0.3% (`country_space_race_progress_mult`), and −0.2% throughput on Extraplanetary Bases, Space Elevators, Orbital Battlestations and **Solar Receivers**. The Solar Collector itself is spared on purpose: its only output, `country_solar_receiver_max_level_add`, is workforce-scaled, so a throughput cut there would shrink the receiver slots and `overbuild_protection_on_action` would demolish receivers. The receivers make its electricity, so they take the cut. Nothing halts: pace keeps its 0.5 floor and risk its 50% ceiling. From Hazardous a Space Elevator owner rolls index × 0.02% a month for `te_orbital_debris.1`, the ribbon strike: `te_ribbon_strike_damage` (−50% throughput on the elevator building) for 6 months, or 2 after paying `te_debris_ribbon_repair_cost` (2 × vanilla's very large event sum); the event's trigger keeps a second strike off a ribbon under repair.
- **The anti-satellite strike** (`te_asat_strike_action`, a one-shot hostile diplomatic action on the `nuke.txt` pattern). Potential: the rule, Guided Missiles. Possible: at war with the target, our own Space Program, the target has a Space Program or an orbital megaproject (`te_asat_has_orbital_assets`), and nobody struck it in the last 12 months (`te_asat_struck_recently`, from the target's `te_asat_struck_month`; per target, not per striker: a second strike while the network is down would only stack debris). The disruption is a timed modifier, so the strike also records its end month and strength on the target (`te_asat_disrupted_until`, `te_asat_disrupted_factor`), and the monthly country step puts the rest of it back on a revolution's winner (`te_asat_rebuild_disruption`). The target gets `te_orbital_network_disrupted` (−10% unit offense and defense, −20% pace, −25% throughput on the elevator, battlestation and receivers) for 365 days, 182 with a Space Program on the Mars Mission or higher, at multiplier 0.5 with an Orbital Battlestation. The striker pays `te_asat_infamy` (5, the action's own visible effect), the dossier ground `un_dos_asat` (15, "Destructive anti-satellite strike"), −30 relations with every other country that uses orbit, and +6 low / +6 high debris. A party to the Moratorium (`te_asat_moratorium_party`) pays both twice. The strike records `te_asat_last_strike_month` and `te_asat_strikes_total` and then calls `te_asat_un_on_strike` with `scope:te_asat_target`. The AI: `te_asat_ai_would_strike` (at war, not in default, the enemy's `te_asat_orbital_investment`, Space Program tier plus orbital megaproject levels, above ours) in `will_propose`; `evaluation_chance` 0.03, ×0.05 from Hazardous unless it is losing the war (`is_losing_war_against`). Communications Disruption, the covert operation, is unchanged.
- **Cleanup.** `pmg_space_program_debris_removal`, a secondary group on `building_space_program`: None (default), Active Debris Removal (Reusable Rocketry; 300 Launch Capacity a week, 0.05 a month), Laser Broom (Directed Energy Weapons; 300 + 500 electricity, 0.15), Fusion Sweepers (Compact Fusion Reactors; 150, 0.5), each `country_orbital_debris_removal_add` (workforce-scaled, so funding's +25% throughput a level raises removal and bill together) and a little prestige (50 / 100 / 200). `pm_space_elevator` adds `country_orbital_debris_removal_mult` +0.1 a fully staffed level; `te_debris_removal_country` counts it up to +100% (`te_debris_removal_mult_cap`). Fusion Sweepers with ten elevator levels clear 30 points in about 2.5 years; one Active Debris Removal programme can't stop a cascade. Cleaner prestige: `te_debris_cleaner_prestige` (+1% prestige) at multiplier = removal × 10. The AI's lever is the decision `te_decision_debris_removal` (switches to the best method it has; ai_chance from Cluttered with orbit use and an affordable bill, `te_debris_best_removal_bill`, more at Hazardous), with the group at `ai_selection = most_productive` like the mission group; whether the AI's building manager then holds the method is a watch item.
- **A revolution's winner** inherits the pools (globals), the cooldown (a variable), and gets both refreshed modifiers back from the next monthly pulse; the timed `te_orbital_network_disrupted` is lost with the loser's modifiers, as the space race's other timed penalties are.
- **Event `.31`** (Orbital Debris Concerns) is also offered, at Cluttered and above, to any programme with a milestone running, names the tier, and then moves every running milestone instead of Orbital Flight.

### Important Notes
- **`building_space_program` is a monument** (`expandable = no`, single level). DO NOT check `level >= 3` etc. on it — use `building_aerospace_industry` for level-scaled bonuses instead.
- **Colony modifiers are declared on the entry.** All 80 specializations, the 40 discovery outcomes, the six facilities, the six population and autonomy modifiers and `sr_solar_system_trade` are `scaling_modifiers_while_active` declarations on `je_space_race_solar_colonization` (1.15, #1404; the per-world ones generated into `common/journal_entries/sr_sites_generated.txt`), each reading a country variable or value. The colonization JE stays alive indefinitely while colonies exist (passive mode with hidden buttons when all 40 colonies are claimed), so the modifiers apply to the country while it holds one, and display in the JE panel. Nothing adds, removes or tests them: `has_modifier` cannot see a declared modifier, and an `add_modifier` beside the declaration would double it. Change the record instead (`<modifier>_held`, through the world's sync; an option grants through `sr_site_choose_<modifier>`). A revolution's winner inherits the variables, so it keeps them.
- **Localization is auto-organized** by `organize_loc.py` into category-specific files. Space race loc keys spread across `te_events_l_english.yml`, `te_journal_entries_l_english.yml`, `te_modifiers_l_english.yml`, `te_miscellaneous_l_english.yml`, and `te_concepts_l_english.yml`.
