# Space Race

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Space Race (`je_space_race_*`)

Multi-stage competition system simulating a space race between great and major powers. Nine journal entries: seven milestones with a progress bar, the repeatable Solar System Colonization entry (34 globally-claimed colonies across 5 stages), and a passive entry that waits out the interstellar probe's transit. Semi-parallel progression, "The First" bonuses, Safe/Ambitious approach choices, and failure mechanics. Cross-system connections to the UN, private companies, buildings, and tourism. The per-entry table of techs and prerequisites is in `docs/systems/journal_entry_systems.md` → Space Race.

### Milestone Progression (Semi-Parallel)
```
Suborbital Flight (rocketry; great or major power)
    └→ Orbital Flight (guided_missiles)
        └→ Moon Landing (space_exploration)
               ├→ Outer Solar System Probe (space_exploration) [shown once Orbital is done, but `possible` also needs the Moon Landing]
               ├→ Moon Base [retired 2026-10-29: the first lunar colony takes its place]
               ├→ Solar System Colonization, the Moon (cislunar_infrastructure)
               └→ Mars Landing (knowledge_economy) [shown once Orbital is done, but `possible` also needs the Moon Landing]
                      ├→ Interstellar Probe (compact_fusion_reactors; also needs the Probe)
                      │      └→ Interstellar Results (passive 132-month wait, fires after probe launch)
                      └→ Solar System Colonization beyond the Moon (stages 1 to 5)
                             [Repeatable: 6 stages from the Moon's, 40 colonies in all]
```

Every entry also needs the matching programme bool from the Space Programme building's production method (`building_space_program`, itself unlocked by `guided_missiles`): `pm_earth_orbit` for Suborbital and Orbital, `pm_moon_mission` (space_exploration) for the Moon Landing and the Probe, `pm_mars_mission` (knowledge_economy) for Mars, `pm_solar_colonization` (cislunar_infrastructure) for colonization, plus `pm_deep_space_exploration` (space_colonization) from colonization stage 3, and `pm_interstellar_mission` (compact_fusion_reactors) for the Interstellar Probe. Each PM also carries all the lower tiers' bools. Losing the bool fails the entry (see *Programme loss* below).

**Technologies of the lunar colonization design** (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-lunar-colonization-design.md` § 5, build 1, 2026-10-29): `cislunar_infrastructure` (era 10, military, after `reusable_rocketry`; +2 funding cap) opens `pm_solar_colonization` (10,000 Launch Capacity a week, sized for Reusable Rockets; it was 25,000 on Directed Energy Weapons) and Orbital Assembly. `in_situ_resource_utilization` (era 10, military, after `reusable_rocketry`; +1; the design's production category and Additive Manufacturing prerequisite would be cross-category under Asteroid Mining, § Tech Tree Authoring) opens `building_space_mine` on its default method, `pm_regolith_processing` (half the Asteroid Mine's inputs; £1,600 a level and 2.0 a worker at base prices). `asteroid_mining` now needs `in_situ_resource_utilization` and `directed_energy_weapons`, and gives `pm_space_mine`, the near-Earth room and propellant depots. `nuclear_thermal_propulsion` (era 11, after `cislunar_infrastructure`; +1) gives the smaller off-window premium (design § 9, *Launch windows* below) through `country_sr_nuclear_thermal_launches_bool`, which `sr_launch_has_ntp` reads (so the tooltip line and the effect share one source). Directed Energy Weapons keeps its funding level and its other unlocks. From the design's second build colonization needs the Moon Landing (the Mars Landing for the worlds beyond the Moon), and the method, like every mission method, is gated by its technology alone (a PM has no `possible`): a country with Cislunar Infrastructure can switch to it in the building panel before the milestones and pay the bill for no colonization, as a Directed Energy Weapons country could before. Old saves: `te_repair_lunar_technologies` (`te_old_save_repairs.txt`) gives Cislunar Infrastructure to every country with Directed Energy Weapons and a Space Program, and In-Situ Resource Utilization to every Asteroid Mining holder, once.

### The Space Program: founding and missions

Two kinds of decision (`common/decisions/extra_decisions.txt`) get a country into the race and up the mission ladder, for players and the AI alike. A player can still build the Space Program and switch its production method by hand.

**Why.** In the owner's 2073 player game every AI had every technology, yet only GBR, GER and SPA completed Suborbital Flight, only GBR and SPA Orbital Flight, none went further, and the only Space Program still standing was the player's. Every milestone after Orbital Flight needs a costlier mission, and the space programme's production method group used the default AI selection, `most_profitable`: every mission's goods are a pure cost, so the AI always kept the least-loss one, Earth Orbit.

- **Found a Space Program** (`te_decision_found_space_program`). Shown with the Space Race rule, `rocketry` (when Suborbital Flight appears), for a great or major power that is not a revolutionary or decentralized and has no programme; possible with `guided_missiles` and a market capital the country owns (`market_capital ?= { owner = ROOT }`: the building's `possible` is `owner.market_capital ?= THIS`, so it stands only in the capital of a market the country leads, and a customs-union member's market capital is a foreign state). It places the building there at once, free, on `pm_earth_orbit` (`create_building`'s `activate_production_methods`). The owner's ruling: the programme's expense is its use, not its construction.
- **Begin the next mission** (`te_decision_space_mission_<mission>`), one decision per mission, each `activate_production_method` on `building_space_program`. Each is shown once its production method's technology is researched and while the programme runs the mission it builds on, and is possible once the milestones before it are complete:

| Decision | Production method | Shown while the programme runs | Possible once complete |
|---|---|---|---|
| Begin the Moon Mission | `pm_moon_mission` (`space_exploration`) | Earth Orbit | Orbital Flight |
| Begin the Mars Mission | `pm_mars_mission` (`knowledge_economy`) | Moon Mission | Moon Landing, Deep-Space Probe |
| Begin Solar Colonization | `pm_solar_colonization` (`cislunar_infrastructure`) | Moon Mission or Mars Mission; while a lunar world is open, or after the Mars Landing while stage ≤ 2 or a colony beyond the Moon held | Moon Landing |
| Begin Deep Space Exploration | `pm_deep_space_exploration` (`space_colonization`) | Mars Mission or Solar Colonization; stage ≥ 3, no colony beyond the Moon held, worlds left | Mars Landing |
| Begin the Interstellar Mission | `pm_interstellar_mission` (`compact_fusion_reactors`) | Mars Mission or above; Interstellar Probe not done | Deep-Space Probe, Mars Landing |

The first three follow the owner's rule, "the milestones the current mission serves are complete". The last two depart from it, because the ladder is not straight past Mars: `country_sr_deep_space_exploration_program_bool` is read only by Solar System Colonization's active branch at stage 3 or later, which a holder of a colony beyond the Moon bypasses, so Deep Space Exploration helps only a country holding none once the inner worlds are claimed (a lunar colony alone doesn't open the outer system); and the Interstellar Probe needs only the Deep-Space Probe and the Mars Landing, while colonization never "completes" for one country while worlds are left. The Interstellar Mission carries every lower bool, so switching to it never stops colonization. One more decision, Resume Solar System Colonization, switches no method: it runs a finished colonization programme again for the Moon (*The Moon Base retired* above).

- **AI weights.** Every decision weighs 0 unless `sr_ai_should_participate` (the top three by ranking: any other AI makes no progress but would pay the bill), `in_default = no`, and `net_fixed_income` (the affordability reader `cultural_hegemony_buttons.txt` uses) exceeds the weekly bill: the whole Earth Orbit bill to found (`sr_mission_bill_earth_orbit`), the increase over the current mission to advance (`sr_mission_bill_increase_<mission>`). A bill is the mission's Launch Capacity at full staff (`sr_mission_units_*`, held to the production methods by `test_space_program_decisions.py`) times our market's price, never below base. Whether a government building's goods count as fixed expenses is not verified. Then 100. `ai_chance` is AI-only by nature, so nothing here is an `is_ai` rule.
- **`ai_selection = most_productive`** on `pmg_base_building_space_program`, as vanilla gives every building whose methods cost goods and output only country effects (university, government administration, every monument). Not yet watched in game: whether the AI under it holds its mission until a decision advances it, or climbs on its own (the decisions are then a convenience), or climbs straight to a mission it cannot pay for, which would be a new risk.
- **Why AI programmes may still vanish.** Two existing mechanics fit the 2073 game's lone surviving programme. `remove_invalid_buildings` (`extra_on_actions.txt`, monthly) removes a Space Program that is not in its owner's market capital, so an AI that joins another country's market loses it. And `building_space_program` is downsizeable (the default; the hub and every vanilla monument set `downsizeable = no`), so the AI may demolish a building that only costs money.
- **Stays demolishable (owner, 2026-10-05).** Making it permanent would trap a player paying for a mission, since the group has no idle method. Watch item instead: in the next observer run, look for an AI that founds, demolishes and founds again.

### Key Mechanics
- **"The First" Bonus:** Global variables (`sr_global_first_*`) track first achiever. First nation gets ~2× rewards (prestige, innovation max, tech speed) permanently. Subsequent nations get smaller permanent modifiers.
- **Approach Choice:** Standard, Safe or Ambitious, per entry. Standard is neither `sr_safe_<m>` nor `sr_ambitious_<m>`: every milestone starts on it, it has no modifier and no approach cost, and the player can return to it (scripted GUI op 4, `sr_effect_standard`; no AI button, as the AI never had a way back from an approach). It replaced, 2026-09-29, a no-approach state that drifted at a flat 0.5 a month with no roll (owner's play-test). Safe and Ambitious print their weekly innovation cost (`sr_approach_cost_<kind>_<m>`, from `sr_disp_cost_<kind>_<m>`) in their tooltips and the overview. The base monthly setback risk is a per-milestone script value — `sr_base_risk_<m>`, 5 for suborbital rising to 10 for solar colonization — and `sr_risk_pct_<m>` applies `country_space_race_risk_mult` to it (Safe contributes -0.5, Ambitious nothing). **The monthly roll and the widget's "Setback risk" line read the same script value**, so they cannot disagree; `sr_risk_shown_<m>` is the same figure under the panel's name (it read zero during the failure cooldown, retired 2026-10-31). There is no longer a `sr_base_risk` proxy variable or a `RISK =` parameter — a number copied into a variable one statement before it is rolled is readable by nothing else, which is why the risk could not be shown at all before.
- **Funding Levels:** 0 to `sr_max_funding_level` (base 3, increased by `country_space_race_max_funding_add` modifier from techs like `reusable_rocketry` +1, `space_colonization` +2; with every one a country has 13 by the end of era 10, 18 by era 11 and 26 by era 12, and tier V of Advanced Research adds 1). Each level costs innovation (-15/level via `sr_space_program_cost` consolidated modifier; `sr_innovation_per_cost_level` mirrors the 15 for the display, held equal by `test_space_race_approaches.py`). Solar colonization is billed only while it runs (`sr_milestone_billed_<m>`, 2026-09-29). Funding and approach are **per-JE** — you can fund moon landing heavily with safe approach while running a cheap ambitious probe.
- **Funding past the cap (2026-10-29, lunar colonization design § 5.1).** The cap is soft: `sr_possible_fund_up` tests `sr_max_funding_level_soft` (the cap plus `sr_funding_past_cap_steps`, 6). Each level past the cap adds less pace than the one before, 1/2 of a level for the first down to 1/7 for the sixth (`sr_funding_past_cap_step_<n>`), so six levels past it are worth 1.59. Every level still costs in full (`sr_<m>_cost` reads the variable) and adds its full +25% throughput, so the funding modifier is two: `sr_funding_progress` (`country_space_race_progress_add = 0.5`) at `multiplier = sr_funding_<m>_effective` and `sr_funding_throughput` (`building_space_program_throughput_add = 0.25`) at `multiplier = sr_funding_<m>_level`. `_effective` is entry-scoped and reads the country through `owner` (`_within_cap` + `_past_cap_effective`, country-scoped, with `_past_cap` the raw excess). Nothing clamps a level to the cap: one that falls below it (a lost principle) leaves the level standing and the excess counts past the cap, and a level past the sixth step adds nothing. The AI stays within the cap (owner, § 15.6): its fund-up buttons ask `sr_possible_fund_up_within_cap`, and its fund-down buttons get +100 while `sr_funding_<m>_past_cap > 0`. Because the pace multiplier reads the cap, which technologies and Advanced Research V move with no button press, `sr_recalculate_cost` records the cap it computed with (`sr_cost_funding_cap`, beside `sr_cost_participation`) and the monthly pulse recalculates when it is missing or differs from `sr_max_funding_level`: the pace follows a moved cap within a month, while the stepper and the pace hover's Funding line read it at once. Old saves pick the split up through the monthly `sr_cost_modifiers_missing` repair (an entry with no `sr_funding_throughput`). Panel: past the cap the stepper's figure is `je_space_race_widget_funding_past_cap_<m>` ("9 / 7 (+0.8)", chosen by a `SelectLocalization` on `_past_cap`), the plus button's tooltip adds `SR_FUND_UP_PAST_CAP_<n>` from `sr_effect_fund_up`, and the pace hover gives `sr_disp_funding_within_cap` / `sr_disp_funding_past_cap`. `test_space_race_soft_cap.py` evaluates the values.
- **Per-JE Cost:** each active milestone's journal entry carries `sr_space_program_cost` with `multiplier = sr_<m>_cost` (that milestone's funding level + approach overhead: standard=0, safe=1, ambitious=2, times an era factor `sr_cost_factor_<m>` from 1x for suborbital to 12x for the interstellar probe; solar colonization's is 5 until the country's own Mars Landing, then 8, read through `owner`, and the Mars Landing's completion re-posts it). Re-posted by `sr_recalculate_cost` whenever funding or approach changes. A single consolidated `sr_total_space_cost` script value used to exist for this and is gone — nothing read it after the per-JE split.
- **Failure:** Costs the failed milestone months of progress (`sr_setback_lose_months`, see *Progress in months* below): a minor setback (Safe, Standard) 1 to 4 months, the default answer 2 or 3; an Ambitious failure 4 months with the review, 5 or 6 for the answers that press on without it. Ambitious failures add decaying negative modifiers (`sr_mission_failure_effect`), and most answers the temporary safety review (−10% progress, −25% risk, decaying). All of it is applied by the failure event's chosen option, not in the pulse. Does NOT permanently block — just wastes time.
- **No post-setback cooldown (owner, 2026-10-31).** Until then an Ambitious failure set a six-month country-wide `sr_failure_cooldown` that skipped every milestone's roll while progress ran on, shown as "Shielded" (status codes 3 to 5). Switching every running milestone to Ambitious during it was free progress, so it went: every running milestone rolls every month. The monthly country pulse removes an old save's counter and re-derives its statuses (remove after 2026-12-31).
- **Progress in months (2026-10-25).** Every event that moves a milestone's progress moves it by whole months of the programme's pace, `sr_progress`: setbacks through `sr_setback_lose_months = { MONTHS = N }` (the failed milestone), the in-progress events through `sr_boost_active_milestones = { MONTHS = N }` (every running milestone) or `sr_gain_progress_months` / `sr_lose_progress_months = { MILESTONE = m MONTHS = N }`. Each such event's `immediate` calls `sr_record_event_pace`, so a month is the pace when it fired (`sr_event_pace_value`) and changing funding while it waits changes nothing; the tooltips (`SR_SETBACK_LOSE_<N>_MONTHS_TT`, `SR_PROGRESS_ADD_<N>_MONTHS_TT`, `SR_PROGRESS_LOSE_<N>_MONTHS_TT`) print the months and the amount (`sr_event_months_<N>`). A loss never takes more than the milestone has, nor more than one goal. Why: until then a setback kept ×0.75 (Ambitious) or ×0.85 (Safe, Standard) of the progress, most options took a flat 3 to 10 on top, and the in-progress events gave a flat 2 to 30. The risk is a monthly roll, so a milestone sees setbacks in proportion to the months it takes, and each took a share of a progress that grows with the goal (50 to 650): a slow programme settled where its pace equalled its losses. In a simulation of the setback pools (no windfalls), Standard on the Mars Landing took 880 months on average at a pace of 4 (113 without setbacks) and at 2 never finished in 100 years; the flat amounts meant nothing past the first milestones. In months, the same simulation gives every milestone the same overhead at a given risk, whatever its goal or pace: about ×1.15 (5% risk) to ×1.4 (10%) on Standard, ×1.06 to ×1.18 on Safe, ×1.2 to ×1.37 of its faster base on Ambitious. The in-progress events' months follow their old points at the pace of their era (about 2 a month for Suborbital and Orbital Flight, 4 for the Moon Landing and the probe, 5 to 6 for the Moon Base and Mars, 10 for the Interstellar Probe and colonization), keeping each event's order between its options: 1 to 3 months, 4 for a gamble's best outcome. `test_space_race_progress_months.py`.
- **Every running milestone moves and rolls.** Whatever its approach, the pulse adds `sr_progress` and rolls `sr_risk_pct_<m>`; the setback kind follows the approach: Ambitious fails big (`sr_fire_ambitious_failure_event`), Safe and Standard suffer the minor setbacks (`sr_fire_safe_setback_event`). The flat `sr_progress_drift` for a milestone with no approach is gone (2026-09-29).
- **Failure events know which milestone failed.** The pulse calls `sr_fire_ambitious_failure_event = { MILESTONE = <m> }` / `sr_fire_safe_setback_event = { MILESTONE = <m> }`, which first `save_scope_as = sr_setback_<m>`. Saved scopes travel with `trigger_event`, so that marker is how the pool picks a theme (`.13` only for a probe, `.14/.17/.71` only for Moon base / Mars / colonization, `.15` only for the Moon landing, …; shared triggers `sr_setback_is_deep_space_probe` / `sr_setback_is_crewed_outpost`) and how every option's helper — progress loss (`sr_setback_lose_months`), `sr_temporary_safety_review_effect`, `sr_clear_failed_milestone_flags` — finds its one milestone. Two journal entries failing in the same month cannot mix: each entry's pulse is its own script run. **Never dispatch twice in one script run** (the second event would carry both markers); `te_debug_sr_fire_setback` picks one milestone for that reason. A failure event with no marker at all (open in a save from before the marker existed, or fired from the console) falls back to the old behaviour: the first helper an option runs calls `sr_resolve_setback_markers`, which marks every milestone whose `sr_failed_<m>` is raised.
- **Failure Flags:** `sr_failed_<milestone>` is still raised by the pulse — it is the "setback not yet answered" bookkeeping — and each option clears the flag of its own milestone. Nothing reads it to decide *which* milestone an effect applies to any more.
- **"A more cautious approach"** (`.10.b`, `.15.c`) switches the failed milestone to the safe approach through `sr_switch_setback_milestone_to_safe` → `sr_effect_safe`, the journal-entry button's own body, so the approach flags, the approach modifiers (risk and speed), the cost and the widget status follow (`sr_mission_failure_effect` re-derives the status via `sr_refresh_milestone_statuses`). For solar colonization only while it is running, as the panel's own controls are.
- **Completion texts** (`.1/.3/.6/.7/.8`, and the flavour of `.3/.7`) claim a world first only under `has_variable = sr_was_first_<m>`; otherwise a `_later` variant ("…joins the nations that have…"). The rewards already split first from later (`sr_apply_milestone_reward_base`).
- **Achievement notifications (`.20`).** `sr_notify_milestone_achievement` adds the achiever's capital to a per-milestone *list* on every receiver (`sr_notify_achievers_<m>`) and queues one `.20`; each popup takes exactly one entry (`sr_take_milestone_notice_base`, an `else_if` chain) — the world first's entry before any other, since the list has no reliable order — and reads "world first" from the achiever's own `sr_was_first_<m>`. Two completions inside the 14-day delay — different milestones, or the same milestone by two countries — give two popups, each naming its own achiever, and option b's relations go to that achiever.
- **Programme loss (`.76`).** A lowered space-programme production method, or a lost / demolished building, fails every milestone its tier was carrying, and the journal entries notice on their own 4-day update schedule. Each `on_fail` calls `sr_report_program_loss = { MILESTONE = <m> }`: it records the milestone and only the first queues `.76`, five days out, so one change gives one report whose option tooltip lists the closed programmes. The text is read at fire time: building still there → a refit (a choice, the original "Program Discontinued" text); building gone → "The Silent Gantry", an involuntary loss.
- **Solar colonization "running".** Its journal entry stays open, frozen, while `pm_solar_colonization` is switched off (and after the last colony). `sr_solar_colonization_running` (active + production method on + not completed; also the gate of its four controls) is what events `.35/.44/.48/.49/.50/.53/.55` and `sr_boost_active_milestones` read, so no event narrates or moves a frozen programme. `.35` ("keep the settlements supplied") also needs a real colony (`sr_has_space_colony`).
- **Foreign powers in the text are real.** `.26` ("When Politics Reaches Orbit") names a space power our relations with have really soured (`sr_is_estranged_space_power`: rivalry either way, or relations at cold or worse) and is only in the safe pool while one exists. `.52` (joint orbital station) needs another UN member that has reached orbit (`sr_is_station_partner`), names it, and is worded as our own space agency's proposal rather than a claim about what that country did.
- **Rivals' estimated progress (display only).** The panel's Rivals rows show each rival's progress as an estimate inside a band: a yearly random offset per rival and milestone (`sr_fuzz_<m>`), a band of ±25% of the goal that the viewer's covert network narrows (±10% / ±5% at intelligence tier 1 / 2). Filled monthly for players only by `sr_rivals_estimate_refresh`; the design is in `docs/systems/journal_entry_systems.md` → Space Race → Milestone Panels, item 3.
- **Moon Landing Site:** Special event (space_race_events.5) offers Shackleton Crater (high risk, science windfall) vs Equatorial Plain (low risk, modest rewards).
- **Interstellar Probe:** Launches a probe to Alpha Centauri. On completion, spawns a passive `je_space_race_interstellar_results` JE that ticks 1/month for 132 months (~11 years) using a separate `sr_interstellar_transit_progress` variable (not shared `sr_milestone_progress`). When complete, fires event 60 (Interstellar Probe Data Received) which randomly selects one of 30 possible discoveries across 4 categories:
  - **Category I: Dead Worlds & Data** (40% chance, 8 results) — Barren worlds, asteroid maps, stellar remnants. Grants `sr_probe_dead_worlds_data` (modest prestige/research/innovation).
  - **Category II: Astrophysical Wonders** (30%, 7 results) — Ringed terrestrials, global oceans, runaway greenhouses. Grants `sr_probe_astro_wonders_data` (good prestige/research/innovation/cultural pull).
  - **Category III: Biological Discovery** (20%, 9 results) — Atmospheric biosignatures, alien vegetation, exotic biochemistry. Grants `sr_probe_biological_data` (major prestige/research/innovation/cultural pull).
  - **Category IV: Intelligence & Tech-signatures** (10%, 6 results) — Orbital debris, technogenic gases, artificial light. Grants `sr_probe_intelligence_data` (exceptional prestige/research/innovation/cultural pull).
  - Discovery selection uses two-stage `random_list`: first picks category by weight, then picks specific result uniformly within category. Each result event (601–630) records itself globally on first discovery (`sr_record_probe_result`: `sr_probe_found_<id>` = the discoverer). A later nation drawing the same result gets opening/closing lines that confirm the first finder's discovery, named, instead of "unprecedented data"; the rewards are the same (none is a first-discovery reward). Result events use triggered_desc blocks for the confirmation variant and the result-specific description.
- **Solar System Colonization (Repeatable):** Each launch establishes one colony at a random unclaimed location in the current stage that a fleet can reach that month (*Launch windows* below): one a month at most, when staged progress covers goal × (1 + premium) for the stage's cheapest such site. Progress past the goal is kept (staged in orbit), across a stage change too. Colonies are tracked via global variables (`sr_colony_*`), making them first-come-first-served across all nations. Stage advances when all locations in a stage are claimed. The goal per colony rises with the stage (`sr_solar_colonization_goal`: 300 / 450 / 525 / 600 / 700 / 800, `gen_solar_sites.STAGE_GOALS`). Once all 40 colonies across stages 0 to 5 are claimed, the entry's monthly pulse sets `sr_completed_solar_colonization` (and grants `sr_solar_system_trade`) for **every** colony holder within a month, whether or not its programme is running. Until 2026-10-03 that completion sat inside the bar-fill check, so only the country that claimed the last site finished at once; every other holder kept paying funding and approach for a bar that could found nothing until it next filled, years later, and a holder with the production method off never finished.
  - **Stage 0:** the Moon (6) = 6 colonies (lunar colonization, 2026-10-29; *The Moon* below)
  - **Stage 1:** Mars (5) + Asteroids (5) = 10 colonies
  - **Stage 2:** Jupiter system (6) + Venus clouds (1) = 7 colonies
  - **Stage 3:** Mercury (1) + Saturn moons (5) = 6 colonies
  - **Stage 4:** Uranus moons (4) + Neptune moons (2) = 6 colonies
  - **Stage 5:** Kuiper Belt/Oort Cloud (5) = 5 colonies
- **Launch windows (Global Commons § 5, 2026-10-16; not yet seen in game).** Every colony site but the Moon's and Mercury's belongs to a window body (`gen_launch_windows.py`'s `BODIES`): Mars, the five asteroids (each its own), the Jupiter system, Venus, the Saturn, Uranus and Neptune systems, and the Kuiper Belt (one body on Pluto's orbit for all five stage-5 sites, Sedna included: each has a synodic period within days of a year). The Moon, Mercury, the Mars Landing and the probes are not gated.
  - **The sky.** A body's optimum is the Hohmann-phase departure (its true longitude at arrival is Earth's at departure + 180°, T half the transfer ellipse's period), from Standish's Table 1 (planets) and JPL SBDB osculating elements (asteroids, Pluto), 1900 to `END_DATE`; the window is the optimum's month ±1. The generated `sr_window_next_<body>` is one branch per cycle on `te_history_month_index` (optimum month, plus the months since the previous optimum as two decimals), from which `sr_window_months_to_next_<body>`, `sr_launch_offset_<body>` (signed months from the nearest optimum), the trigger `sr_window_open_<body>` and `sr_launch_premium_<body>` (a binary search on the offset) follow. The premium is `exp(extra Δv ÷ 3.7 km/s) − 1` for the month's best departure against the cycle's best window month, from a Lambert solver over all transfer times, averaged over 16 cycles, capped at 1000 (+100,000%). **Nuclear thermal** (lunar colonization design § 9): `sr_launch_premium_ntp_<body>` is the same extra Δv at 9 km/s, `exp(extra Δv ÷ 9) − 1` = `(1 + p)^(3.7/9) − 1` of the chemical p, rounded once from the Δv (on Mars +16% and +46% the first two months after a window, against +43% and +150%). Transit, drives, fleets, yards and refits don't change: nuclear thermal vehicles fly the same near-Hohmann transfers. `docs/engine/launch_windows_report.md` has every curve, both premiums with their cycle spreads, the off-window rule on Mars for each, the historical check and the Mars windows of era 11.
  - **The cache.** The global monthly pulse (`sr_launch_windows_on_action`, with the space race rule) stores per body `sr_window_is_open_<body>`, `sr_window_wait_<body>`, `sr_window_cost_<body>` and `sr_window_cost_ntp_<body>` (the two premium curves), and `sr_window_cache_month`; the entry's pulse refreshes it first if that month's is missing (a country pulse can precede the global one on the 1st). Nothing but the cache refresh and the self-check reads the generated chains, except that `sr_window_premium_now_ntp_<body>` reads its curve until a refresh has cached it (an old save loaded mid-month). The cached readers `sr_window_premium_now_<body>` and `sr_window_premium_now_ntp_<body>` are any scope; the country reader `sr_window_premium_country_<body>` picks the nuclear thermal one with `sr_launch_has_ntp` (`country_sr_nuclear_thermal_launches_bool`, which `nuclear_thermal_propulsion` carries), and is the only reader of the chemical cache (`test_launch_windows.py` holds it): `sr_launch_cost_<body>`, `sr_launch_target_cost_<body>` and so every colony fleet's cost and score, `sr_launch_months_cost_<body>` (the approach's test, for players and the AI alike) and Next Windows' premium all read it. The design named the country reader `sr_launch_premium_<body>`, but that is the chemical curve the global refresh reads.
  - **The rule** (`sr_launch_window_triggers.txt`). A site departs free in its window, or any month for a country with the fusion torch (`compact_fusion_reactors`' `country_sr_drive_torch_bool`) or the antimatter drive (`sr_launch_has_fusion`; or with the rule off). With `asteroid_mining`, a closed body departs at its premium (the nuclear thermal one with `nuclear_thermal_propulsion`, which makes off-window launches cheaper, never possible without depots) when the approach's test passes: not Safe, and goal × premium ÷ pace (`sr_launch_months_cost_<body>`, pace `sr_pace_rate_solar_colonization`) ≤ k × m (`sr_launch_months_allowed`; k 1 Standard, 2 Ambitious; m = `sr_window_stage_wait`, the stage's soonest window among its unclaimed sites, 0 while one is open or a world of the Moon or Mercury is left). `sr_launch_best_cost` is the cheapest over the stage; each site's `trigger` in `sr_establish_colony_effect` passes only when its body's `sr_launch_cost_<body>` equals it, so the draw can never found a site whose body is closed to that country. The pulse (`sr_launch_month`) founds at most one colony a month and spends goal × (1 + premium) only on a real claim. **A colony-less programme's staged fleets** (review L2): such a programme still restarts its bar when it stops (on_fail's cleanup when its method goes, the monthly cleanup when it deactivates, `immediate` when it re-activates or a revolution's winner inherits it), because progress carried into an activation would become the journal bar's baseline. `sr_launch_bank_staged_fleets` first keeps the whole goals apart (`sr_launch_banked_fleets`, additive, not once every world is claimed), and `sr_launch_restore_banked_fleets` adds them back, at the current stage's goal, at the start of the next `sr_launch_month`; only the remainder below a goal is lost. A colony holder never stops (no `fail`, no deactivation, `immediate` keeps its progress).
  - **Setbacks** never take more than one goal (`sr_lose_progress_months` caps every loss at min(progress, goal)), so fleets staged in orbit are safe.
  - **Panel:** the overview's Staged in Orbit (progress in goals while a full bar waits; hover: each destination's window, how fleets depart, and after Asteroid Mining today's premium, the country's own (`sr_window_premium_country_<body>`), and the approach's verdict, months saved against months cost; printed by `sr_launch_windows_sgui`) and Mission Control's Next Windows (colony management design § 6): a table, one generated `te_sr_window_row` per destination (`gen_solar_sites.WINDOW_ORDER`): its window (Any month, Open now, In N months, or with depots the premium to go now, `sr_disp_win_premium_<body>` capped at +999%) and its transit by our drive (`sr_transit_months_<body>`). A row shows while `sr_window_reach_<body>` is 1: a world there we may target (`sr_cf_targetable_<key>`) within the fleets' 15-year horizon (`sr_cf_horizon_months`); 2, beyond it, is counted under the table. The Staged in Orbit lines read the same value.
  - **Self-check `window_gate`** (`sr_launch_window_gate_check`, from each gated site's draw branch): BROKEN when a country with neither depots nor fusion founds a colony while the date's own `sr_window_open_<body>` is closed; OK once a closed window has held a full bar (`sr_launch_held_months`) and the colony leaves in its window; `window_gate_paid` logs one off-window launch's cached and computed premiums. Play-test list: `docs/testing/global-commons-playtest.md`.
- **Late-Game Economic Rewards:** Interstellar Probe grants one of 4 category-specific modifiers (see Interstellar Probe above), all colonies complete grants `sr_solar_system_trade`.
- **Commercial Space Industry (after the race, 2026-10-03).** Once a country has nothing left to spend progress on, `sr_space_race_concluded` (all seven single-goal milestones complete, `sr_all_colonies_complete`, `sr_has_running_milestone` false), the flat progress its sources still give is paid as Extraplanetary Base throughput: `sr_commercial_space_dividend` (`building_space_mine_throughput_add = 0.05`, a unit value) at `multiplier = sr_space_dividend_points`, re-applied by `sr_refresh_space_dividend` from the monthly country pulse (its one refresh site). The points are `modifier:country_space_race_progress_add` capped at `sr_space_dividend_max_points` (10, so +50%). Three choices pinned by `test_space_race_dividend.py`: the flat sum, not `sr_progress` (its 0.5 floor would pay every finished country) nor the multiplier (Antimatter Engines alone reach +250%); funding is out without special handling, since `sr_funding_progress` sits only on a billed entry; and the cap, because the Space Elevator alone gives up to 20 points against 9.25 for all 33 progress-granting colony specialisations together. At 5% a point a progress specialisation (0.28 on average) is worth about a quarter of a mining one (+6% throughput on average), so mining stays the long-term pick. A country that reaches the end after the last site is gone qualifies once its own milestones are done. The first time it applies, a player gets `space_race_events.77` ("The Commercial Space Age"), recorded in `sr_space_dividend_announced`. A revolution's winner gets the modifier back from the next monthly pulse: the test reads only inherited variables.
- **Milestone goals** (`space_race_values.txt`): Suborbital 50, Orbital 100, Moon Landing 200, Probe 150, Moon Base 300 (retired), Mars Landing 450, Interstellar Probe 600; colonization 300–800 per colony (above).
- **Progress Sources:** each month every running entry adds `sr_progress` = `modifier:country_space_race_progress_add` × (1 + `modifier:country_space_race_progress_mult`), at least 0.5. Everything reaches it through those two modifiers, so the tooltip breakdown lists every source (the overview's hover on the headline's "+N/mo" is that breakdown, `je_space_race_widget_pace_tt`):
  - `country_space_race_progress_add`: the Space Programme PM (Earth Orbit +0.5, every later tier +1, workforce-scaled), funding (`sr_funding_progress` on each running entry, +0.5 per effective funding level, stacking across entries; past the cap a level counts for 1/2 down to 1/7 of one, and the +25% space-programme throughput per level is `sr_funding_throughput`'s, every level in full), the Space Elevator (+1 per level), the SpaceX, Lockheed Martin and Roscosmos company prosperity modifiers (+0.3 each), UN space partnership (`un_space_partnership_modifier`, +0.1), the `reusable_rocketry` and `space_colonization` techs (+0.2 each), and several choice-event and reward modifiers (probe target +0.3 to +0.5, `sr_mars_resource_extraction` +0.5, …).
  - `country_space_race_progress_mult`: the Ambitious approach (+50% per entry), the temporary safety review (−10%), the UN outer-space regime (leader −5%, laggard +10%), covert space espionage (+10%), antimatter engines (+5% each, workforce-scaled) and `principle_advanced_research_5` (+10%).
  - There is no Aerospace Industry or Space Mine term: the space mine only gates event `.53`, and `building_space_program` is a single-level monument. After the race the flow runs the other way: the leftover `country_space_race_progress_add` pays Extraplanetary Base throughput (Commercial Space Industry, above).
- **A revolution's winner continues the programme** (`docs/audits/civil_war_inheritance_audit.md` F5, #464). The winner inherits the loser's variables but none of its modifiers, and every entry it inherits active runs `immediate` again. So:
  - **`immediate` only creates what is missing.** Progress and funding are guarded with `has_variable`, so a milestone at 90 % stays at 90 %. That is safe for the native progress bar because an inherited record keeps the loser's bar: start date, baseline and goal are copied, not evaluated again (the German-revolution saves' `je_global_warming`: baseline 0.1 and goal 4.0 on the winner with the anomaly at 1.18). For a same-record re-activation inside a month (before the monthly cleanup clears the progress), `goal_add_value` subtracts the progress so the goal stays at `sr_<m>_goal`. Solar colonization's bar restarts at every colony, so it keeps progress only while it holds a colony (it cannot deactivate then); a colony-less programme still restarts, and a finished one is not re-opened. `je_space_race_interstellar_results` keeps its transit months the same way.
  - **Choice events are asked once per milestone** (`sr_<m>_choice_made`, checked in `immediate` and in the event's `trigger`). Before, a re-activation or an inherited entry asked again and could leave two answers and two modifiers.
  - **Every reward is rebuilt from a variable.** Milestone rewards (`sr_first_<m>` / `sr_<m>`) from `sr_completed_<m>` / `sr_was_first_<m>`, and the probe result (`sr_probe_*_data`, recorded as `<modifier>_held` by `sr_grant_probe_data`), come back in `sr_repair_after_civil_war`, which the shared hook `te_civil_war_on_won` runs for the winner. It is stateless: it adds a reward only where the record is held and the modifier missing, reads none of the shared layer's side variables, and removes nothing. Entry modifiers come back from each entry's `immediate` (`sr_sync_<m>_entry`) and, as a backstop, the monthly country pulse (`sr_sync_space_race_entries`, only for a country that has run a milestone: `sr_space_race_participant`): the approach modifier from `sr_safe_<m>` / `sr_ambitious_<m>` (and a stale one with no approach selected is removed), the choice modifier from the choice variable, the 68 colony specialisations from `<modifier>_held` (`sr_grant_colony_modifier`), and `sr_solar_system_trade` from `sr_completed_solar_colonization`. The monthly pulse also records a probe result or colony modifier granted before the record existed, so old saves migrate themselves. A loyalist winner still holds every modifier, so all of it is a no-op there. Timed modifiers (the completion events' themed rewards, failure penalties, the safety review) are not restored.
  - **The rebels start no programme of their own mid-war.** Suborbital flight's `is_shown_when_inactive` requires `is_revolutionary = no` (every later entry already needs a completion the rebels lack). Their own run would otherwise beat the loser's progress in the variable merge.

### The colony record

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-solar-colonization-holders-and-cession-design.md` (slices 0 and 1 of the scope in `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-25-solar-colonization-contest-scope.md`). Not yet seen in game; the self-checks and the play-test list are in `docs/testing/solar-colonies-playtest.md`.

- **The record is the truth, per world, in global variables:** `sr_colony_<key>` (claimed, as before), `sr_site_holder_<key>` (the holder, a country), `sr_site_spec_<key>` (1 or 2, the founding event's option), `sr_site_region_<key>` (the holder's capital state region, recorded monthly while it lives), `sr_site_mirror_<key>` (the country that shows the world), and per discovery `sr_site_disc_<d>` (1 or 2) and `sr_site_disc_<d>_pending`. Until 2026-10-25 nothing named a holder: a world was a claim flag and a modifier on its founder's journal entry, so annexing the founder removed the colony from the game.
- **The projection.** `sr_site_sync_<key>` makes the holder show the world and nobody else: the old mirror loses both specializations' `<modifier>_held`, the world's discovery outcomes and its discovery `_pending` / `_done`, each with its entry modifier behind `has_modifier`; the holder gains the chosen ones; both recount `sr_colony_count` from the record (`sr_sites_held_count`). So `<modifier>_held`, the Our Colonies panel, `sr_has_space_colony` and the revolution repair keep reading what they always read. The sync runs at every write and from the global monthly step for all 40 worlds.
- **Founding and choosing.** The draw sets the holder after the claim and syncs (it no longer adds to the count: the sync counts). `sr_grant_colony_modifier` runs `hidden_effect = { sr_site_choose_$MODIFIER$ = yes }`, a dispatcher with one generated effect per specialization and discovery outcome (108), which writes the world's choice. `sr_seed_colony_discovery` also sets the world's pending flag; `sr_finish_colony_discovery` clears it.
- **The monthly step** (`sr_sites_global_monthly`, global `on_monthly_pulse`, under `sr_space_race_on`): per world, record the holder's capital region; pass a dead holder's world (fails `exists` or `is_country_alive`) to the owner of the most populous state in that region that can hold one (`sr_site_can_hold`: alive, not decentralized), or release it when there is none; let a claimed world with no holder be adopted by the country carrying its `_held`, or released once the 45-day `sr_sites_grace` of the first run is over; then sync. The first run also migrates a save from before the record (`sr_sites_migrate_country` on every colony holder). Then every player's Solar System snapshot.
- **Released worlds** (no heir, the console, or an old save's vanished colonies): claim, holder and records removed; each stage's draw also lists every earlier stage's worlds behind their own claim test, and `gen_launch_windows`'s `sr_window_body_in_play_<body>` reads `sr_current_colonization_stage >=` its stage, so a released world departs in its own window at the current stage's goal. The entry's colonizing branch asks `sr_any_site_unclaimed` instead of `NOT sr_all_colonies_complete`, which stays set as history; every holder still completes with Interplanetary Trade Networks through the entry's pulse.
- **Revolutions.** `sr_repair_after_civil_war` runs `sr_sites_repoint_loser`: when the rebels won, every world held by `scope:te_cw_loser` is ROOT's at once.
- **A holder without the milestones** (a colony given by treaty or inherited): `is_shown_when_inactive` and `possible` have a colony branch (`sr_has_space_colony`), the colonizing branch carries the Moon Landing, and `sr_solar_colonization_qualified` joins `sr_solar_colonization_running` and the pulse's progress gate, so such a holder keeps its colony's modifiers, reads Idle, is billed nothing and founds nothing. A transfer opens its entry with `add_journal_entry`. `on_fail` reports a programme loss only for a qualified country.
- **Notices** (players): `space_race_events.90` for a colony gained or lost by treaty, `.91` for an heir's; the first world each way is named through `sr_site_notice_<side>_name` (generated custom loc on an index variable), any more noted.
- **The panel**: Solar System (`te_sr_sec_solar_system`, closed by default, `sr_panel_solar_system_open`; below Space Fleets, just above How Solar Colonization Works, since the owner moved it, 2026-10-31), every world under eight locations, a `te_sr_site_row` each on `sr_disp_site_<key>` (0 unclaimed, 1 ours, 2 another's, 3 another's without a capital); the holder's flag reads its capital, stored on the viewer as `sr_ss_cap_<key>` by `sr_sites_refresh_snapshot` (gotcha #15). Only the flag: a holder's name on hover, since a 48-character name would not fit the row (`test_space_race_labels.py`).
- **The rule.** `space_race_rule` has four settings: `space_race_colonies_full`, `space_race_colonies_simple` (the default in slice 5; Full Colonies is the default from slice 6d), `space_race_enabled` (races only, its name kept for saves, so a save from before the change keeps it) and `space_race_disabled`, one flag each named after it. Script reads it only through `sr_space_race_on`, `sr_colonies_contested_enabled` and `sr_colonies_full_enabled` (`sr_site_triggers.txt`); `test_solar_sites.py` fails on a bare `has_game_rule = space_race_enabled` anywhere else. Not a second flag on each setting: no rule in the mod reads one, and if `has_game_rule` failed to read it the space race would turn off under simple and full with nothing in the log.
- **Cede Colony** (`te_cede_colony`, simple and full): a directed, enforceable, giftable article with `required_inputs = { building_type }`; each world has a hidden building type (`building_sr_site_<key>`, `potential = { always = no }`) that names it in the designer, `building_type_valid_trigger` offers only the source's worlds (`sr_cede_site_input_valid`), `usage_limit = once_per_side_with_same_inputs` lets one treaty cede several or swap, and `on_entry_into_force` moves the world for good (`sr_cede_colony_apply`). AI: asks while a space power in the race, offers only a colony it holds with no programme, accepts by score (giver −30 and minus the world's worth, `sr_cede_ai_input_value`: 30 plus 10 a stage, 8 a module level past the first and 1 a 1,000 people, at most 170, so −60 for an empty lunar outpost to −200; receiver plus the worth, half without a programme; colony management design § 3, the owner's −50 to −200 range of 2026-10-30). War goal: the seabed lease's contestion, maneuvers 5, infamy 10.

### Colony fleets

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-solar-colonization-population-and-transit-design.md` (slice 3 of the scope). Not yet seen in game; the self-checks and the play-test list are in `docs/testing/solar-colonies-playtest.md`.

- **Targets, under every setting but off** (the owner's D19). `sr_cf_target` on the country is a world's index in `gen_solar_sites.SITES`, set by the Solar System row's Target button (`sr_colony_target_sgui`, `scope:site`), cleared by its Clear, and dropped at the next launch month once the world is no longer targetable. Targetable (`sr_cf_targetable_<key>`): unclaimed, and under races only of a stage reached (the draw's own candidates), under simple and full of stage 1 or 2, or with Deep Space Exploration or a colony beyond the Moon held (`sr_has_colony_beyond_moon`; the outer-system rule, per world). Under races only a ready target is founded at once (`sr_cf_found_target_now`: spend, `sr_site_found_<key>`, the window gate check, the draw's charter and stage check); without one, the random draw as before.
- **Drives** (`sr_drive_level`, `sr_launch_window_values.txt`): 0 chemical, 1 fusion (`fusion_power`), 2 the torch (`compact_fusion_reactors`), 3 antimatter (`antimatter_production` and an owned `building_antimatter_facility`). The torch and antimatter leave any month. Each technology carries its drive's marker (`country_sr_drive_<fusion|torch|antimatter>_bool`, script-only booleans in `space_race_modifier_types.txt`), so its tooltip names the drive, and `sr_drive_level`, `sr_launch_has_fusion` and `sr_launch_has_antimatter` read the markers, not the technologies (2026-10-30).
- **Transit** (`gen_launch_windows.py`, `DRIVES`): chemical is the body's median Hohmann time (4 months to Mercury, 9 to Mars, 33 to Jupiter, 192 to Uranus, 611 to the Kuiper Belt); fusion the shortest Lambert transfer within 30 km/s (about 3 to Mars, 8 to Jupiter, 37 to Uranus in a window, slower outside); the torch a brachistochrone at 0.01 g capped at 400 km/s (1 to 13 months); antimatter 0.03 g and 2,000 km/s (1 to 4). By signed months from the optimum, cached monthly as `sr_window_transit_<drive>_<body>`; `sr_transit_months_<body>` reads the country's drive. The report has every table.
- **Launching under simple and full** (`sr_cf_launch_month`, from `sr_launch_month`): a free slot of six (`sr_cf_<n>_site`, `_depart`, `_arrive`, `sr_cf_in_flight`; tested per world in `sr_cf_eligible_<key>` and `sr_cf_target_ready`, so a lunar world needs none), then the target if ready (`sr_cf_target_ready`), else the automatic pick: the eligible world (`sr_cf_eligible_<key>`: targetable, departs, its cost covered, transit within 180 months) with the best `sr_cf_score_<key>` = 100 − 10 × stage − transit ÷ 2 − 40 × premium − 10 a fleet inbound − 60 if another lands first. A world's cost is its own stage's goal (`sr_colonization_goal_stage_<n>`, 300 to 800) × (1 + its body's premium); a targeted off-window departure weighs the body's own wait (`sr_launch_target_departs_paid`). The dispatch spends the cost, runs the window gate check and fills the slot.
- **Landing** (the global monthly step, before the per-world step): a fleet whose month has come founds its world (`sr_site_found_<key>`: claim, holder `this`, sync, discovery seeds, the entry opened, the founding event; then the charter and the stage); a world taken first sends it to the first free world of the same launch-window body, a player told by `space_race_events.92`; with none it is lost (`.92`).
- **The inbound record** (global, per world): `sr_site_inbound_<key>`, `_first_<key>` (earliest landing month), `_by_<key>` (its country), added at dispatch and rebuilt monthly from every country's slots. The Solar System rows show "count·months" (highlighted when the first is ours); the automatic pick avoids a world another fleet reaches first.
- **The stages under simple and full** stop being locks: `sr_window_body_in_play_<body>` ignores the stage, `sr_solar_colonization_goal` takes the target's stage, the entry's `possible` and the two mission decisions read `sr_colonization_inner_open` (an unclaimed world of stages 0 to 2) in place of their stage tests, and `sr_sites_recompute_stage` sets the stage to the lowest with an unclaimed world, and `sr_all_colonies_complete` once none is left. Races only keeps the step-by-one stage check.
- **Panel:** Colony Fleets (`te_sr_sec_colony_fleets`): the destination (our target, or the programme's pick), the drive, and under simple and full the fleets in flight, one row per slot with its world and landing.

Design: the same, § 10 (slice 2). Simple and full colonies only.

### Colony modules and Extraplanetary Base room

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-25-solar-colonization-modules-and-bases-design.md` (slice 4). Simple and full colonies only; races only keeps one specialization at level 1 and unlimited bases.

- **The record** (global, so a cession or an heir takes it; cleared on release): `sr_site_lvl_<a|b>_<key>`, the specializations' levels (absent: the founding choice reads 1, the other 0, so old saves need no migration), sharing `sr_mod_spec_budget` (4, the owner's D18); `sr_site_<type>_<key>`, the generic modules (Habitat, Defences, Spaceport and the ten facilities below), sharing the world's slots.

The stock's cap is 500, the dearest price.
- **Facilities** (design § 7; `gen_solar_sites.FACILITIES`, codes 6 to 15 after the five old ones, so saves keep their queues): ten generic modules, crewed, each giving its effect per staffed level (`sr_site_<type>_staffed_<key>`) summed over the colonies that serve THIS (`sr_mod_units_<type>`: held and never occupied; not blockaded for those that ship, Mining Complex, Orbital Yard, Off-World Resort and Materials Foundry). Four are hooks (`sr_colony_module_values.txt`): Mining Complex, +10 base room at its world (`sr_site_ebase_room_<key>`, read by `sr_ebase_room_total` and the fill); Orbital Yard, +4 fabrication points (`sr_mod_fab_rate` = the method's `sr_mod_fab_rate_method` + the yards) and under Full Colonies +0.5 Space Command strength (`sr_sc_rate` = `sr_sc_rate_buildings` + the yards); Nuclear Command Center, +4 `nd_reliability_target` a unit, at most +12 (`sr_mod_bunker_reliability`); Administration, −0.15 autonomy a unit at its world (`sr_aut_per_admin_level`, in the pressure). The two self-checks read the method's and the buildings' parts alone.

Defences fire on fleets closing in on the world and Spaceport docks and repairs fleets (slice 6, *Fleets, Space Command and combat* below); AI review still never queues them. The entry's backstop puts specializations back at their multiplier (`sr_sync_colony_spec_base`, generated list `sr_sites_sync_entry_specs`).

### Orbital debris
Design: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-16-global-commons-design.md` § 6. Follows the space race rule; with it off the pools never exist, the strike action and the decision are hidden, and `space_race_disabled` disables the three removal methods (`extra_game_rules.txt`, `disable_pm_*` flags).
- **Two pools, one index.** Global variables `orbital_debris_low` (1.5% decay a month, half-life ≈ 4 years) and `orbital_debris_high` (0.05%, ≈ 115 years); the index `te_debris_index` is their sum, capped at 100. `te_debris_clamp_pools` holds the pools to the cap too, the **low pool giving way first**: if the high pool gave way, a strike at the ceiling would swap a century of debris for a few years of it and striking would clean orbit. Tiers, for text only (`te_debris_tier` custom loc): Clear < 25, Cluttered 25, Hazardous 50, Kessler Cascade 75.
- **The world's step** (`te_debris_global_monthly`, global `on_monthly_pulse`, the 1st): the Kessler term (+0.2 high a month while last month's index ≥ 75), background traffic (+0.01 low per running Space Program, `te_debris_programs_running`; six programs settle near 4), decay, then removal: `te_debris_removal_world`, every country's `te_debris_removal_country` summed, taken from the high pool first; then, under Full Colonies, the ground lasers' sweep from the low pool alone (`sr_battery_sweep_step`, *Fleets* above). It also writes the panel's globals (`te_debris_removal_month`, `te_debris_cleaners`, `te_debris_top_cleaner`) and runs the once-a-game `TE_SELF_CHECK debris_removal`.
- **What it does** (`te_debris_country_monthly`, the monthly country pulse, the one refresh site of both modifiers): `te_orbital_debris` at `multiplier` = the index on every country that uses orbit (`te_debris_uses_orbit`: a Space Program, a Space Elevator, an Orbital Solar Collector, an Orbital Battlestation or an Extraplanetary Base). Per point: setback risk +1% (`country_space_race_risk_mult`), pace −0.3% (`country_space_race_progress_mult`), and −0.2% throughput on Extraplanetary Bases, Space Elevators, Orbital Battlestations and **Solar Receivers**. The Solar Collector itself is spared on purpose: its only output, `country_solar_receiver_max_level_add`, is workforce-scaled, so a throughput cut there would shrink the receiver slots and `overbuild_protection_on_action` would demolish receivers. The receivers make its electricity, so they take the cut. Nothing halts: pace keeps its 0.5 floor and risk its 50% ceiling. From Hazardous a Space Elevator owner rolls index × 0.02% a month for `te_orbital_debris.1`, the ribbon strike: `te_ribbon_strike_damage` (−50% throughput on the elevator building) for 6 months, or 2 after paying `te_debris_ribbon_repair_cost` (2 × vanilla's very large event sum); the event's trigger keeps a second strike off a ribbon under repair.
- **The anti-satellite strike** (`te_asat_strike_action`, a one-shot hostile diplomatic action on the `nuke.txt` pattern). Potential: the rule, Guided Missiles. Possible: at war with the target, our own Space Program, the target has a Space Program or an orbital megaproject (`te_asat_has_orbital_assets`), and nobody struck it in the last 12 months (`te_asat_struck_recently`, from the target's `te_asat_struck_month`; per target, not per striker: a second strike while the network is down would only stack debris). The disruption is a timed modifier, so the strike also records its end month and strength on the target (`te_asat_disrupted_until`, `te_asat_disrupted_factor`), and the monthly country step puts the rest of it back on a revolution's winner (`te_asat_rebuild_disruption`). The target gets `te_orbital_network_disrupted` (−10% unit offense and defense, −20% pace, −25% throughput on the elevator, battlestation and receivers) for 365 days, 182 with a Space Program on the Mars Mission or higher, at multiplier 0.5 with an Orbital Battlestation. The striker pays `te_asat_infamy` (5, the action's own visible effect), the dossier ground `un_dos_asat` (15, "Destructive anti-satellite strike"), −30 relations with every other country that uses orbit, and +6 low / +6 high debris. A party to the Moratorium (`te_asat_moratorium_party`) pays both twice. The strike records `te_asat_last_strike_month` and `te_asat_strikes_total` and then calls `te_asat_un_on_strike` with `scope:te_asat_target`. The AI: `te_asat_ai_would_strike` (at war, not in default, the enemy's `te_asat_orbital_investment`, Space Program tier plus orbital megaproject levels, above ours) in `will_propose`; `evaluation_chance` 0.03, ×0.05 from Hazardous unless it is losing the war (`is_losing_war_against`). Communications Disruption, the covert operation, is unchanged.
- **Cleanup.** `pmg_space_program_debris_removal`, a secondary group on `building_space_program`: None (default), Active Debris Removal (Reusable Rocketry; 300 Launch Capacity a week, 0.05 a month), Laser Broom (Directed Energy Weapons; 300 + 500 electricity, 0.15), Fusion Sweepers (Muon-Catalyzed Fusion Reactors; 150, 0.5), each `country_orbital_debris_removal_add` (workforce-scaled, so funding's +25% throughput a level raises removal and bill together) and a little prestige (50 / 100 / 200). `pm_space_elevator` adds `country_orbital_debris_removal_mult` +0.1 a fully staffed level; `te_debris_removal_country` counts it up to +100% (`te_debris_removal_mult_cap`). Fusion Sweepers with ten elevator levels clear 30 points in about 2.5 years; one Active Debris Removal programme can't stop a cascade. Cleaner prestige: `te_debris_cleaner_prestige` (+1% prestige) at multiplier = removal × 10. The AI's lever is the decision `te_decision_debris_removal` (switches to the best method it has; ai_chance from Cluttered with orbit use and an affordable bill, `te_debris_best_removal_bill`, more at Hazardous), with the group at `ai_selection = most_productive` like the mission group; whether the AI's building manager then holds the method is a watch item.
- **A revolution's winner** inherits the pools (globals), the cooldown (a variable), and gets both refreshed modifiers back from the next monthly pulse; the timed `te_orbital_network_disrupted` is lost with the loser's modifiers, as the space race's other timed penalties are.
- **Event `.31`** (Orbital Debris Concerns) is also offered, at Cluttered and above, to any programme with a milestone running, names the tier, and then moves every running milestone instead of Orbital Flight.

### Important Notes
- **`building_space_program` is a monument** (`expandable = no`, single level). DO NOT check `level >= 3` etc. on it — use `building_aerospace_industry` for level-scaled bonuses instead.
- **Colony modifiers are JE-scoped.** All 80 colony modifiers and `sr_solar_system_trade` are applied to `je:je_space_race_solar_colonization`, not to the country directly. The colonization JE stays alive indefinitely while colonies exist (passive mode with hidden buttons when all 40 colonies are claimed).
