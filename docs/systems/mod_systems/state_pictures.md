# State Pictures

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-state-pictures-design.md` (the axes, the technology ladders and the whole planned set of about 360 pictures); play-test list: `docs/testing/state-pictures-playtest.md`.

## State Pictures

- **What:** the banner at the top of the state panel. Vanilla shows one of 35 nineteenth-century paintings by the state's terrain and region; the mod adds later pictures that follow the owner's technology and the size of the state's largest city. The same picture shows in the state tooltip, pop details, the invasion and battle panels and the strategic region panel. Cosmetic: nothing reads it.
- **How the engine picks** (read in game, 2026-11-10; `docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md` § "State pictures"): the state's prevailing terrain (`common/terrain`), then the highest-weight entry of that terrain's `textures` list. The mod doesn't change which terrain prevails. An `INJECT:` adds entries to vanilla's list. Every entry needs an `effect` (the animated overlay): one without it is a load error that leaves its state with the plains picture. The picture can't be logged (`[THIS.GetState.GetBestTexture]` prints empty in `debug_log`).
- **Files:**
  - `scripts/image_pipeline/state_picture_registry.py` — the one source: each picture's terrain lists, technology, rung, region, size, overlay, prompt and picked seed. An entry with `seed: None` is planned art and wires nothing. `DRAFTS` holds the prompts of the waves whose conditions aren't designed yet (45: wave 6's character, function and condition pictures of cities, wave 7's natural terrain), each with its technology and a plain-words note of when it should show; a draft moves to `PICTURES` when its wave is built, as wave 3's did through `WAVE3_WIRING` (what decides between a picture and its neighbors) and `WAVE3_PICKS` (the picked render).
  - `scripts/generators/gen_state_pictures.py` → `common/terrain/te_state_pictures_generated.txt` (one `INJECT:` a terrain; `--check` exits 1 when stale).
  - `common/scripted_effects/te_state_picture_effects.txt`, `common/script_values/te_state_picture_values.txt`, `common/on_actions/te_state_picture_on_actions.txt` — the yearly pass.
  - `scripts/image_pipeline/convert_state_picture.py` — a picked render to `gfx/state_pictures/te_<key>.dds` (1020 × 500, BC7 with mips): the entry's `smudge` boxes (a lettered sign blurred to a dab) and `crop` box, then a Kuwahara filter blended four parts to one with the unfiltered picture, which turns FLUX's near-photograph into flat dabs of color beside vanilla's paintings.
  - `test_state_pictures.py`.
- **The weight** is a number in place value, so the most specific picture that applies wins and none tie: a takeover 10,000,000, the rung on the family's technology ladder × 100,000, the size or scale × 10, a named region its family's digit (so two families that hold the same state can't tie: Japanese outranks East Asian). A **scale** is a worked-land picture's size: a major port (a Port of level 20, read live) over the harbor, a colliery (the pass's `te_sp_coal`) over the open pit. A **takeover** is a picture with no terrain of its own that joins other terrains' lists and outranks every rung there: the oil field (the pass's `te_sp_oil`) on plains, desert, savanna, tundra, hills, the five farmlands and pasture. The spec reserves the places between for prosperity and character, and the place above the takeover for conditions. Vanilla's weights are 1 and 2, so a mod picture that applies always wins; before its technology none applies and vanilla's shows.
- **What a weight may read:** the owner's technology (live), vanilla's region triggers (live), whether the state has a building or one of a given level (live) and the yearly pass's state variables. The engine may evaluate a weight every frame, so nothing dearer goes in one.
- **The yearly pass** (`te_state_picture_refresh`, on `on_yearly_pulse_state`, ROOT the state) stores `te_sp_size`: 1 town, 2 city (100,000 people in the largest city), 3 metropolis (1,000,000), 4 megacity (10,000,000). The largest city's people are the Demographics walk's urban people (`te_dg_urban`, written every year whatever the Demographics rule) times the metropolitan share (`te_dg_mt` ÷ 100 while the census runs, 0.4 otherwise). A size is left only a tenth below its floor. Before a state's first walk it has no size, and only pictures for any size apply. The floors are provisional until the spec's wave 2 sets them against an observer game. A size picture can lag the city by up to a year. The pass also walks the state's buildings once (subsistence buildings left out) and tags what its worked land is known for, a share and not a count (owner, 2026-11-10: many rigs beside a great city don't make an oil state): `te_sp_oil` while Oil Rigs hold 5% or more of the state's building levels and at least 10, `te_sp_coal` while Coal Mines hold half or more of its mine levels (`bg_mining`) and at least 10. A tag is present or absent, and is kept until its figure falls a tenth under the floor. Script can't read a building's share of GDP, so levels stand in; the figures are provisional (`te_state_picture_values.txt`; from a 1940 save, 24 of 109 oil states and 44 of 139 coal states pass).
- **Self-check:** `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN state_picture_size`, once a game, from the first state stored at metropolis or larger, with the people of its largest city and of the state. OK when the city fits inside the state; BROKEN means `te_dg_urban` isn't a head count on `state_population`'s scale and the floors are in the wrong unit. `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN state_picture_oil`, once a game, from the first state tagged oil country, with its Oil Rig levels and all its levels: BROKEN means the building walk's sums don't hold together. The banner itself is on the play-test list.
- **Pictures wired (89):** the city picture of each of the eight region families on each of the five rungs of the city ladder (40: the European family's also show in the states of no family); the pilot's European metropolis of rung 1; and the worked land (48): a harbor and a major port on each of the port ladder's four rungs, an open pit and a colliery on each of the mine ladder's three, an oil field on each of its three, the four farmland families (vanilla's own triggers; the generic one is every state of no other) on four rungs and one designed-crop picture for all on the fifth, a plantation and a ranch on four, conifer and tropical forestry and cleared land on two. Each reuses the vanilla overlay of its kind; the oil field uses the arid plain's. The port pictures also ask for a Port (`where` in the registry): vanilla's `docks` terrain prevails in every one-province state, port or not, and shows its dry plain where there is none. A plain and a special picture of one rung (harbor and major port, pit and colliery, conifer and tropical forestry, the two families of a city overlap) are wired together or not at all, because a rung outranks a scale or a region.
- **Adding a picture:** add the registry entry, render and pick, convert, set its `seed`, run the generator, commit the `.dds` with the generated file. A new region, size or scale is a line in the registry's `REGIONS` (cities), `FARM_REGIONS`, `FOREST_REGIONS`, `SIZES` or `SCALES` (a region family is vanilla's triggers, any one of them, and a digit; `FALLBACK_REGION`, the European family, also takes the states of no family, as vanilla's European picture does); a new axis is a place in the weight and, if it isn't live, a variable in the yearly pass.
