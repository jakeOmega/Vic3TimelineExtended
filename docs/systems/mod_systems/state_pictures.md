# State Pictures

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-state-pictures-design.md` (the axes, the technology ladders and the whole planned set of about 360 pictures); play-test list: `docs/testing/state-pictures-playtest.md`.

## State Pictures

- **What:** the banner at the top of the state panel. Vanilla shows one of 35 nineteenth-century paintings by the state's terrain and region; the mod adds later pictures that follow the owner's technology and the size of the state's largest city. The same picture shows in the state tooltip, pop details, the invasion and battle panels and the strategic region panel. Cosmetic: nothing reads it. Hovering it in the state panel says which picture shows and why (§ "The banner's hover" below).
- **How the engine picks** (read in game, 2026-11-10; `docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md` § "State pictures"): the state's prevailing terrain (`common/terrain`), then the highest-weight entry of that terrain's `textures` list. The mod doesn't change which terrain prevails. An `INJECT:` adds entries to vanilla's list. Every entry needs an `effect` (the animated overlay): one without it is a load error that leaves its state with the plains picture. Each of the mod's entries plays its own copy of a vanilla overlay (below). The picture file can't be logged (`[THIS.GetState.GetBestTexture]` prints empty in `debug_log`), but its overlay's name can (`[THIS.GetState.GetBestTextureEffect]`, read in game 2026-11-13), and that names the kind of picture.
- **Files:**
  - `scripts/image_pipeline/state_picture_registry.py` — the one source: each picture's terrain lists, technology, rung, region, size, overlay, prompt and picked seed. An entry with `seed: None` is planned art and wires nothing. `DRAFTS` holds the prompts not yet wired (the flooded coast's two, which need the warming system; the 223 size and prosperity variants of the regional streets are written there too, and `VARIANT_PICKS` moves each picked one into `PICTURES`; all but the pilot's twelve are written by `variant_prompt()` from a table a region and a table a rung, with `VARIANT_LEAD` putting the time first on the last two rungs and `VARIANT_REWORD` giving one key its own words for one field, so that rewording a failed key never changes the prompt of a picked one), each with its technology and a plain-words note of when it should show; a draft moves to `PICTURES` when it is wired, as waves 3, 6 and 7 did through `WAVE3_WIRING`, `WAVE6_WIRING` and `WAVE7_WIRING` (what decides between a picture and its neighbors) and the matching `_PICKS` (the picked render).
  - `scripts/generators/gen_state_pictures.py` → `common/terrain/te_state_pictures_generated.txt` (one `INJECT:` a terrain), `gfx/models/particles/te_state_picture_overlays_generated.asset` (the overlay copies) and `localization/english/te_picture_hover_generated_l_english.yml` (the hover's text for each copy and each technology's line; `organize_loc.py`'s category `PICTURE_HOVER_GENERATED`); `--check` exits 1 when one is stale. CI's main job checks out `gfx/models/` for it; a sparse worktree needs `git sparse-checkout add /gfx/models/`.
  - `gui/states_panel.gui` (the banner's `tooltip`) and the hand-written `te_sp_tt_*` lines in `te_miscellaneous_l_english.yml`: the hover.
  - `common/scripted_effects/te_state_picture_effects.txt`, `common/script_values/te_state_picture_values.txt`, `common/on_actions/te_state_picture_on_actions.txt` — the yearly pass.
  - `scripts/image_pipeline/convert_state_picture.py` — a picked render to `gfx/state_pictures/te_<key>.dds` (1020 × 500, BC7 with mips): the entry's `smudge` boxes (a lettered sign blurred to a dab) and `crop` box, then a Kuwahara filter blended four parts to one with the unfiltered picture, which turns FLUX's near-photograph into flat dabs of color beside vanilla's paintings.
  - `test_state_pictures.py`.
- **The weight** is a number in place value, so the most specific picture that applies wins and none tie: a takeover 10,000,000 and a condition its digit × 1,000,000 on top of that, the rung on the family's technology ladder × 100,000, a character its digit × 10,000, the size or scale × 10, a named region its family's digit (so two families that hold the same state can't tie: Japanese outranks East Asian). A **scale** is a worked-land picture's size: a major port (a Port of level 20, read live) over the harbor, a colliery (the pass's `te_sp_coal`) over the open pit. A **takeover** is a picture with no terrain of its own that joins other terrains' lists and outranks every rung there: the oil field (the pass's `te_sp_oil`) on plains, desert, savanna, tundra, hills, the five farmlands and pasture. A **character** is a city picture for any region that outranks size and region but not the rung, so it is painted a rung: housing blocks (the owner has Command Economy and Prefabricated Construction) and smog (the state region's `pollution_amount` at 150 of 255 or more), and the five that read the pass's tags: boomtown, declining, divided, heavy industry, resort. Where two hold, the higher digit shows: housing blocks 7, boomtown 6, declining 5, divided 4, smog 3, heavy industry 2, resort 1. A **condition** is a city picture that outranks every rung: a nuclear strike (`nuclear_strike_aftermath` or `tactical_nuke_aftermath` on the state) over ruins (`devastation >= 0.5`). A **great city takes over** every other land terrain (the owner, 2026-11-11): each city picture is also in the list of every vanilla terrain but `urban`, `ocean` and `lakes` (`CITY_TAKEOVER_TERRAINS`), where it asks for a metropolis or larger (`te_sp_size >= 3`) and weighs 11,000,000 more than in the city's own list. So a metropolis shows its city picture whatever terrain the engine holds its state to be, over the land's own pictures and over an oil field, and a smaller city's state keeps its land. The ruins and the strike are in those lists too at their own weights, which already stand above. These entries are written one line each; they are most of the generated file. A test keeps every weight under 16,777,216, the last whole number a 32-bit float holds exactly. Between character and size sit **upkeep** (1,000: run-down, the pass's `te_sp_rundown`) and **means** (its digit × 100: poor 1, affluent 3, the pass's `te_sp_means`), so a run-down street outranks a poor or an affluent one, which outranks a town or a metropolis. Vanilla's weights are 1 and 2, so a mod picture that applies always wins; before its technology none applies and vanilla's shows.
- **What a weight may read:** the owner's technology (live), vanilla's region triggers (live), whether the state has a building or one of a given level (live), the owner's law, a state modifier, the state's devastation and its region's pollution (all live), and the yearly pass's state variables. The engine may evaluate a weight every frame, so nothing dearer goes in one.
- **The yearly pass** (`te_state_picture_refresh`, on `on_yearly_pulse_state`, ROOT the state) stores `te_sp_size`: 1 town, 2 city (100,000 people in the largest city), 3 metropolis (1,000,000), 4 megacity (10,000,000). The largest city's people are the Demographics walk's urban people (`te_dg_urban`, written every year whatever the Demographics rule) times the metropolitan share (`te_dg_mt` ÷ 100 while the census runs, 0.4 otherwise). A size is left only a tenth below its floor. Before a state's first walk it has no size, and only pictures for any size apply. The floors are provisional until the spec's wave 2 sets them against an observer game. A size picture can lag the city by up to a year. The pass also walks the state's buildings once (subsistence buildings left out) and tags what its worked land is known for, a share and not a count (owner, 2026-11-10: many rigs beside a great city don't make an oil state): `te_sp_oil` while Oil Rigs hold 5% or more of the state's building levels and at least 10, `te_sp_coal` while Coal Mines hold half or more of its mine levels (`bg_mining`) and at least 10. A tag is present or absent, and is kept until its figure falls a tenth under the floor. Script can't read a building's share of GDP, so levels stand in; the figures are provisional (`te_state_picture_values.txt`; from a 1940 save, 24 of 109 oil states and 44 of 139 coal states pass). The same walk and the Demographics walk's figures give five city tags (`te_state_picture_refresh_city`): `te_sp_industry` (heavy industry a quarter of the levels and at least 30), `te_sp_resort` (the Tourism Industry 2% and at least 10), `te_sp_boom` and `te_sp_decline` (a city whose smoothed yearly growth `te_sp_growth` is 3% or more, or −1% or less; the rate is `te_dg_walk_pop` over `te_dg_walk_pop_prev`, held within a tenth either way, a fifth of it taken each year, and a year in which `te_dg_provinces` changed from the stored `te_sp_prov` is left out) and `te_sp_divided` (a metropolis with `te_dg_gini` at 0.85 or more). Each is kept a little past its floor. Prosperity (`te_state_picture_refresh_prosperity`): `te_sp_means` is 1 (poor) while the state's `average_sol` is under the poor floor of its owner's city rung and 3 (affluent) from the affluent floor, absent between; the floors are the owner's first-pass figures (rung 1: 12 and 20; 2: 14 and 22; 3: 18 and 28; 4: 26 and 36; 5: 35 and 45), and a level is left only half a point past its floor. `te_sp_rundown` is present while `average_expected_sol` is 2 or more above `average_sol`, until it is within 1.5 (provisional). Before the owner's first city technology a state has neither. Beside the tags the pass stores the figures they were judged on, for the hover: `te_sp_people` (the largest city's people), `te_sp_sol` and `te_sp_esol` (the standard of living and what the state's people expect), and while a tag stands its share (`te_sp_oil_share`, `te_sp_coal_share`, `te_sp_industry_share`, `te_sp_resort_share`).
- **Self-check:** `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN state_picture_size`, once a game, from the first state stored at metropolis or larger, with the people of its largest city and of the state. OK when the city fits inside the state; BROKEN means `te_dg_urban` isn't a head count on `state_population`'s scale and the floors are in the wrong unit. `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN state_picture_oil`, once a game, from the first state tagged oil country, with its Oil Rig levels and all its levels: BROKEN means the building walk's sums don't hold together. The banner itself is on the play-test list.
- **Pictures wired (356):** the size and prosperity variants of the regional streets (223, `VARIANT_PICKS`, 2026-11-12): for each of the eight region families on each of the five rungs a town, a metropolis (the European one of rung 1 is the pilot's) and from rung 3 a megacity, and a poor, an affluent and a run-down street, each picked from four candidates, 21 of them from a later set with words of their own (`VARIANT_REWORD`). The city picture of each of the eight region families on each of the five rungs of the city ladder (40: the European family's also show in the states of no family); the pilot's European metropolis of rung 1; and the worked land (48): a harbor and a major port on each of the port ladder's four rungs, an open pit and a colliery on each of the mine ladder's three, an oil field on each of its three, the four farmland families (vanilla's own triggers; the generic one is every state of no other) on four rungs and one designed-crop picture for all on the fifth, a plantation and a ranch on four, conifer and tropical forestry and cleared land on two. Each reuses the vanilla overlay of its kind; the oil field uses the arid plain's. The port pictures also ask for a Port (`where` in the registry): vanilla's `docks` weight is a flat 1 in a one-province state, port or not, and its picture is the dry plain where there is none. What a one-province state shows is not settled: Singapore (the port-hub province of Malaya, a Port of 50, an Urban Center of 405, no farm) showed rice farmland on 2026-11-11, and its owner's one-province slices of Guangdong and North Sumatra the harbor (`scripting_best_practices.md` § "State pictures", last bullet). That is why a great city takes over every terrain. A plain and a special picture of one rung (harbor and major port, pit and colliery, conifer and tropical forestry, the two families of a city overlap) are wired together or not at all, because a rung outranks a scale or a region. Wave 6 (29, city pictures for any region): housing blocks on city rungs 2 to 4, smog on rungs 1 to 4, ruins and a nuclear strike on two spans each, boomtown and declining on rungs 1 to 4, divided on 2 to 4, heavy industry on 1 to 3, resort on 1 to 4. Wave 7 (15): natural terrain with a road, pylons or a pipeline, after Modern Urban Planning (the tundra after Integrated Circuits), in vanilla's own families: plains and mountains generic, American and arid; desert generic and American; hills generic, subtropic and jungle; tundra, forest, jungle and wetland one each; the arid plain also in every savanna state (`any_region`), as vanilla's is. The city pictures of the European, North American, East Asian and Japanese families, and every wave 6 picture, use the port's birds-and-mist overlay (`NEUTRAL_OVERLAY`): vanilla's European and Asian city overlays are placed for vanilla's paintings.
- **The banner's hover** (owner, 2026-11-12: "make it transparent to the player" why one state shows a run-down street and its neighbor an ordinary one; the region family is left out). The engine can't say which picture file a banner shows, only the name of its overlay. So each kind of picture of each technology plays its own copy of the vanilla overlay it would use (`te_sp_fx_<kind>_<technology>_<vanilla overlay>`, 223 of them; the registry's `hover_kind()` and `overlay_name()`), and the banner's `tooltip` is the text of that name: `Localize( Concatenate( 'te_sp_tt_', State.GetBestTextureEffect ) )`. Each vanilla overlay's text is empty (generated from `vanilla_parsed/`), so a vanilla picture has no hover; another mod's picture would show its key, since a missing key comes back as itself. Each text strings together hand-written lines: the kind and its decade (`te_sp_tt_k_<kind>`, `te_sp_tt_e_<decade>`), why it shows with the state's figure and the floor it crossed (`te_sp_tt_why_<kind>`), under the rule the technology it follows (`te_sp_tt_era_<technology>`), and for a city picture below a metropolis "Terrain: urban, set by the game", since then a city shows only on urban terrain (owner, 2026-11-12). A land picture's reason is its terrain and a city smaller than a metropolis, or, on a metropolis whose owner has the land's technology but not Art Deco Architecture (a takeover entry asks for the city picture's own technology), that city pictures start with it. The figures are the pass's stored ones, "at the yearly count", so they agree with the picture; where a tag stands only because it is kept past its floor, the line gives the figure that keeps it instead (`te_state_picture_hover_kept_<kind>`). Seen in game 2026-11-13 with stand-in files: the copied overlay is accepted and its picture shows, the name reads in the GUI, and the lookup renders the state's own figures and a `SelectLocalization` inside it (`scripting_best_practices.md` § "State pictures"). The hover in the state panel itself is on the play-test list. A new kind needs its `te_sp_tt_k_` and `te_sp_tt_why_` lines, and a new technology its decade; `test_state_pictures.py` fails until they exist.
- **Console events** (`events/te_debug_state_picture_events.txt`, hidden, fired by nothing): `event te_debug_state_picture.1` runs the whole yearly pass at once in every state and logs each of your states' size, largest-city figure, oil and coal tags and the banner's overlay (`overlay=`, which names the kind of picture); `event te_debug_state_picture.2` makes your capital read as a metropolis until the next pass.
- **Adding a picture:** add the registry entry, render and pick, convert, set its `seed`, run the generator, commit the `.dds` with the generated file. A new region, size, scale, character or condition is a line in the registry's `REGIONS` (cities), `FARM_REGIONS`, `FOREST_REGIONS`, `LAND_REGIONS`, `DESERT_REGIONS`, `HILL_REGIONS`, `SIZES`, `SCALES`, `CHARACTERS` or `CONDITIONS` (a region family is vanilla's triggers, any one of them, and a digit; `FALLBACK_REGION`, the European family, also takes the states of no family, as vanilla's European picture does); a new axis is a place in the weight and, if it isn't live, a variable in the yearly pass.
