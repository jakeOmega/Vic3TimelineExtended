# Decolonization

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Decolonization System (`je_colonial_empire`)

### Overview

The decolonization system models the decline of colonial empires through a journal entry with a stability progress bar, scripted buttons for colonial policies, and a series of events. The system is designed so that **most colonial powers except the top 2-3 GPs will lose most of their colonies** after the `decolonization` tech is researched (era 6).

### Architecture

Three layers:
1. **Gate & Progress Bar:** `je_colonial_empire` activates when a country has colonial states and `decolonization` tech. The `colonial_stability_bar` (0-100, starts at 50) tracks how stable the empire is. At 0 the empire collapses; at 100 it solidifies.
2. **Player Interaction:** the **Colonial Stability widget** (`gui/journal_entry_widgets/colonial_empire_widget.gui`) is the human surface, laid out to `docs/guides/gui_style_guide.md`: an overview at the top (the stability bar with where it is heading in 12 months at this month's rate, green while rising and red while falling, and a thin line at the next band's edge; four of Banking's cells: the band and where the next begins, the great powers' escalation and net weight, what holds the entry (the colonies and colonial subjects, defined once as concepts), the programmes running; in the top band the months held at 100 and the months Solidified against their targets; the condemners' and supporters' shares of great-power prestige), then **Colonial Holdings** (every territory, largest GDP first, with each one's primary-culture share, standard of living and GDP against the colony tests and a Release button; every colonial subject with its kind, liberty desire, GDP and Release and Dominion buttons; Planned Full Decolonization), why stability is moving (each term a pillar bar over its range), international pressure (a row per great power with what it does to stability a month), the three programmes (each a level, 0-3, with a minus and a plus), two history charts and a collapsed How the Colonial Empire Works. The 3 decision buttons behind it carry `is_ai = yes` and are the **AI's** path to the decisions; the AI steps the programmes in a monthly review (§ Colonial Programmes below). Every surface calls the same `colonial_empire_possible_*` / `colonial_empire_effect_*` helpers; the per-target buttons are the player's alone (§ Colonial Holdings below). Full reference: `journal_entry_systems.md` § Colonial Stability Widget.
   **Timeline Extended window (2026-09-30; not yet seen in game).** The same panels also show on the *Colonial Empire* tab of the mod's own window (`gui/te_systems_window.gui`), under `datacontext = "[GetPlayerJournalEntry('je_colonial_empire')]"`, then a link back to the entry. The tab is greyed until the entry runs; its tooltip lists `ce_entry_unlocked` (the entry's technology and `possible`).
3. **Events:** 21 events fire from `decolonization_events_on_action` (monthly pulse), covering colonial negotiations, crackdowns, releases, GP stance choices, post-independence transitions, and the Suez Crisis model. `.70`-`.80` are the wars of independence (§ Wars of Independence below). `decolonization_events.400` / `.401` are the confirmation popups the three decisions open — each previews candidates and offers a "Reconsider" option, so nothing is released by pressing a button. `.402` / `.403` / `.404` are the per-target ones: one territory's release (grant or Round Table), one subject's independence, one colony's dominion status, each with Reconsider as its default.

### What a Colony Is (the colony test, 2026-10-24)

The test follows UN General Assembly Resolution 1541 (XV) of 1960: a territory "geographically separate and ... distinct ethnically and/or culturally" from the country ruling it (Principle IV), which other elements place "in a position or status of subordination" (Principle V). Design and the case table: `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-10-24-colonial-criteria-and-independence-wars-design.md` § 1. A state is a **colony** (`is_overseas_colonial_state`) when all hold, cheapest first:

1. not a homeland of our primary cultures;
2. at least `colonial_territory_min_population` (100,000) people;
3. **separated** (`is_separated_from_capital`): not joined to our capital over land through states we own;
4. **distinct** (`colonial_state_distinct_people`): a culture sharing **no heritage trait** with any of our primary cultures is more than `colonial_state_indigenous_min_share` (a tenth) of it. Traits are narrow (Irish and Australian share `heritage_british`, Icelandic `heritage_nordic`, Ryukyuan `heritage_japanese`), so shared-heritage lands and multi-island countries' own islands are not colonies; Korean, Maghrebi, Berber and Maltese are each distinct from their rulers;
5. **subordinated**, any of: unincorporated (`colonial_state_unincorporated_signal`, new), the distinct culture's average acceptance below 60 (`colonial_state_indigenous_signal`, now aimed at the distinct culture), or average SoL below 80% of the national average (`colonial_state_disparity_signal`, unchanged);
6. not settled land (`colonial_state_settler_majority = no`, last: it walks the pops).

A territory is **eligible for release** (`is_decolonization_eligible_state`: the Colonial Holdings list, the decisions, the collapse) under 1-4 and 6, without the signals. So a directly held settler land of shared heritage (Australia, New Zealand) is no longer offered; it leaves by vanilla's release or as a dominion. A **Protectorate** qualifies as a colonial subject (`is_qualifying_colonial_subject`) when none of its states borders a state of ours joined to the capital and none of its primary cultures shares a heritage trait with ours (it was heritage *group*); Colonies and Chartered Companies qualify as before. So Tunisia and Morocco under France and Egypt under Britain now qualify, as the comments always promised.

**Why the geography changed.** The old test (`is_overseas_from_capital`) called a state overseas when it lay outside the capital's macro-region and every neighbouring one. Western Europe's neighbours include North Africa, so Algeria, Tunisia, Morocco, Libya and Egypt were never colonies of Paris, Rome, Madrid or London; Japan's Korea and Taiwan, the USA's Puerto Rico, Portugal's Cape Verde and Britain's Malta sat in the capital's own macro-region. The "salt-water" reading is the UN's: contiguous land empires (Russia's Central Asia, China's Tibet, the Ottoman Levant) were never on its decolonization agenda and left by secession, which vanilla models. `is_overseas_from_capital` stays only for the Continental Union HQ (`common/buildings/wonders.txt`), whose "on the capital's continent" the macro-regions answer well; `is_overseas_from_overlord_capital` is gone.

**The separation cache** (`ce_refresh_separation`, `colonial_geography_effects.txt`). No trigger answers "joined over land through our states", and an effect cannot run inside a trigger, so a ring fill (`form_decolonized_country`'s `while` shape) works it out monthly from `on_monthly_pulse_country` for every country with a non-homeland state of 100,000 people or a protectorate (`ce_needs_separation`), and from `on_game_started` for every country, whatever its technology (the naval laws' AI weights, the anti-colonialist ideology and the UN votes read the test at any date). It marks our states joined to the capital (`ce_linked`), then writes `ce_separated` on the rest and `ce_subject_separated` on our separated protectorates. Each value names the country, so a state that changes hands reads as not separated until its new owner's pass. `ce_separation_cached` on the country says its first pass has run: the entry's voluntary end (`fail`, "no colonies left") waits for it, so a save from before the cache does not lose the entry before the first pass.

**Straits.** Vanilla's map has 26 sea crossings (`scripts/analysis/demographics_state_adjacency.json` → `strait`; `ce_is_strait_endpoint` and `ce_strait_pair_with_other` in `colonial_geography_triggers.txt`, pinned to the JSON by `test_colonial_geography.py`). A crossing joins two states only when both are homelands of our primary cultures: Constantinople reaches Anatolia, Tokyo Kyushu and Hokkaido, Copenhagen Jutland, Rome Sicily; Spanish Morocco, Ireland, Ryukyu and Sakhalin are separated. Whether `any_neighbouring_state` crosses a strait is unverified (the demographics self-check `neighbouring_state_reads` logs `strait=0|1`), so the fill does both halves: it refuses a crossing the iterator returns unless both ends are our homelands (`ce_fill_land_link`) and adds a homeland-to-homeland crossing itself (`ce_fill_homeland_strait`). The answer is the same either way; the test models the fill in Python under both readings.

**Panel.** The Colonial Holdings row's colony icon counts the third signal (`colonial_bloc_disp_unincorporated`), and its hovers state the test in the player's terms. The census `te_debug_colonial_empire.4` logs every country's states and each part of the test (`TE_CE_CENSUS`).

### Colonial Holdings (why the entry runs, and per-target decisions)

The owner's request (2026-10-24): the panel should show why the entry runs for us, the states and the subjects, and an action that targets one of them should sit beside it rather than behind a button that asks which. The overview's line counts the colonies (`is_overseas_colonial_state`) and the colonial subjects (`is_qualifying_colonial_subject`), the two halves of the entry's `possible`. The Colonial Holdings section lists the eligible territories and the colonial subjects, eight of each by GDP from the monthly snapshot (subjects stored as their capitals, gotcha #11), each row with its buttons. A territory row is a release bloc: the territory and the states one release would take with it, so Henan and Southern Anhui (neighbours, both homelands of the Han alone) share a row as "Henan +1", with the bloc's GDP, primary share and standard of living, the people it would form with and its states on the name's hover (`colonial_empire_mark_release_blocs`; a state whose own release would form with other people keeps its own row too):

- **Release** on a territory opens `.402` for the bloc's largest state, whose release takes the whole bloc: grant it independence (`.400`'s unilateral release: +1 stability a month for 8 months, relations -10) or a Round Table (no pop below `acceptance_status_2`, authority above `colonial_round_table_authority`; 12 months, the IG reactions, relations +25), or Reconsider. The Round Table option is shown greyed with its reasons when closed, and the button's hover says which.
- **Release** on a subject opens `.403`: `.1.c`'s grant of independence (former colony, relations +30, Landowners very negative, +1 stability for 8 months), under Neocolonialism `.1.d_neo`'s, or Reconsider.
- **Dominion** on a subject opens `.404`: vanilla's own step up in autonomy, for a Colony only (a chartered company or a protectorate has no looser kind; vanilla's dominion takes only a colonial country): `increase_autonomy`, liberty desire -30, the autonomy cooldown catalyst and a 60-month truce, as `da_increase_autonomy_of_subject`. A dominion no longer qualifies, so it stops holding the entry. It gives no stability bonus of its own, on purpose: the same change through vanilla's diplomatic action gives none.

**Which territories, and which people leave** (owner ruling, 2026-10-24, after a 75%-English British Guatemala was offered for release and left as its 7.7% Irish). A territory is eligible (`is_decolonization_eligible_state`) when it is ours, separated from our capital, a tenth or more of its people share no heritage trait with ours (§ What a Colony Is), not a homeland of our primary cultures, has at least `colonial_territory_min_population` people, and is not settled land: `colonial_state_settler_majority` (our primary cultures more than `colonial_settler_majority_share`, half, of its people, read through `colonial_territory_primary_share_value`) keeps such a state out of the list, every decision, the colony test and the collapse releases (`.201`, `.204`). Every release forms with `decol_choose_release_culture`, which `.402`'s preview and `form_decolonized_country` share: the homeland culture with the most people in the territory; if none lives there, the largest culture that is not ours; failing that, the largest. Each culture is counted whole, its pops summed into a local variable; the old pick ordered single pops, so a culture split over several pops lost to a smaller one held in one. The territory leaves with every connected state of ours that is a homeland of that culture. `.402` says "it goes alone, as a nation of its X people" when the region is the territory alone (`.402.d`), and names the count otherwise (`.402.d_region`).

Peaceful Decolonization from a row release is added only if not held (`decol_chosen_release_rewards`). A subject that stops qualifying loses the band's liberty-desire modifiers at once (`decol_clear_subject_pressure`), because the pulse only reaches qualifying subjects. One confirmation at a time (`decol_offer_pending`, cleared by every option of `.401`-`.404`). The AI keeps its own paths (the `.400` buttons, the subject events). Reference, handlers and gates: `journal_entry_systems.md` § Colonial Stability Widget → Per-target decisions. Open questions and ideas: `docs/systems/decolonization_ui_followups.md`.

### Wars of Independence (`.70`-`.80`, 2026-10-24)

The owner's request (2026-10-24): the colonizer should be able to fight to keep a colony, "hard to win", as Portugal did. History asks that winning be possible mostly by outlasting, buy years rather than a solution, break governments at home (autocracies included), turn on foreign backing as much as battles, be fought hardest for settler colonies, and end better negotiated mid-war than lost. Design: the spec's § 2. Records, settle and endings: `common/scripted_effects/decolonization_war_effects.txt`.

**When a colony declares** (`.70` "Declaration in [territory]"): from the pool when the bar is below 40 (weight 20, 40 below 20), for the colony with the most severely unaccepted share of people, then the largest (`decol_iw_rise_order`), among colonies with a pop below acceptance 20 that have not declared or been reconquered in ten years (`decol_iw_can_declare`, `decol_declared` on the state); two years between declarations outside a collapse (`decol_iw_next_declaration`). Below 40, Blood in the Colonies (`.13`) offers Fight (`.13.e`) in place of its police action (`.13.b`, which stays at 40 and above). At bar 0, `.201` / `.204` no longer release three random territories at once: they start **the wave** (`decol_wave_start` → `.77`, hidden), up to three declarations, worst first, 60 days apart, each with the same three choices; the programme-month counters the successors' legacy reads stay until the wave's last answer (`decol_wave_end`). The preview and lock are `.402`'s (region 1, `decol_offer_pending`).

**The choice.** Concede: `.13.a`'s release. Negotiate: `.402.b`'s Round Table with the declaration as the partner (its acceptance gate waived), or `.13.c`'s terms under Neocolonialism; no authority gate, it costs `decol_iw_negotiation_authority` (100) through `decol_iw_negotiation_modifier`, easing over a year. Fight: needs a free record (three wars at once) and nothing else.

**The war.** `decol_iw_fight` forms the rebels through `form_decolonized_country` with `decol_iw_forming` on us, so `apply_decolonization_path` gives them only the rebels' marks (`decol_iw_rebels_formed`: `decol_iw_metropole`, `decol_collapse_grace`, the settlers flag) and the liberation contagion, and defers the rest (`decol_path_legacy`, `.15`) to the war's end. The next day (`.71`, hidden) the rebels open `dp_te_independence_war` against us from their own scope at escalation 80 (`dp_sepoy_mutiny`'s), with `add_war_goal = { holder = <us> type = te_colonial_reconquest target_country = <rebels> primary_demand = yes }` (vanilla's Ezo republic play), so the war breaks out some three weeks later and the monthly settle sees it; the record waits 60 days (`decol_iw_pending_N`), and longer while the play runs (`decol_iw_slot_has_play`). Their goal, `te_colonial_independence`, is `kind = custom` (no engine effect: they are a country already) with `control_all_own_states` as its objective; ours, `te_colonial_reconquest`, is `annex_country` with `te_reunify_country`'s settings, valid while the rebels name us and talks have not conceded. Both have zero infamy and **no `mirrored_wargoal`**, so occupation never enforces either: only a capitulation or a peace ends the war, and a side that backs down from the play should concede the other's demand (unverified; the `decol_iw_war_started` self-check reads it). The play is script-only and admits no extra goals. `on_enforced` on ours marks the record won and settles it the next day (`.78`). The first build opened `dp_te_colonial_war` from our scope with `war = yes` at escalation 100, and an AI empire's never became a war (2026-10-24, `docs/testing/colonial-wars-playtest.md`); that play stays defined for saves that hold one.

**Records** (three slots on us; full list in the effects file's header): `decol_iw_rebels_N`, `decol_iw_state_N` (their capital, the panel row's datacontext), `decol_iw_kind_N` (1 a territory's rebels, 2 a colonial subject's independence war, 3 a colony's secession), months, the year counter, war-seen, pending, won, red months, talks, backers. A colonial subject at war with us takes a record from the settle (`decol_iw_track_subject_wars`); a secession whose capital our last pass had marked separated (`ce_separated` still names us) and whose people share no heritage trait with ours takes one at `on_secession_start` (`decol_iw_on_secession_start`, `te_civil_war_on_actions.txt`). `decol_fighting_colonial_war` (`.51`) reads the records for the secession case.

**The monthly settle** (`decol_iw_monthly`, from `decolonization_events_on_action` before its pool, whatever the entry's state): for each record, the rebels gone and their capital ours (or the goal enforced) → won (`.72`); at war → a month (counts, the fighters' package re-applied, the yearly IG reactions, red months, talks, the AI's talks, the panel row); a subject still ours after its war → won; no war yet within 30 days → the `decol_iw_war_started` self-check's BROKEN case, and the rebels leave as a release would; war over after accords → `.76`; otherwise lost (`.73`). Then the home front (`decol_iw_home_front_modifier`, −2 legitimacy a war-year summed over every war, up to −10, remove-then-add at `decol_iw_home_front_mult`, two months long), the condemners' share for the war-support line, and the regime-crisis check. `decol_repair_after_civil_war` rebuilds the home front at once after a revolution.

**Making it hard.** The fighters' package `decol_iw_fighters_modifier` (morale loss −50%, defence in their own provinces +50%, provinces lost −65%, conscription ×2, training +50%) on whoever fights us, re-applied monthly; foreign arms (`decol_iw_foreign_arms_modifier`, offence and defence +10% × √(backers' weights summed), no cap; each backer weighs its GDP over ours, clamped to 0.25–4, so equal backers give +10%, +14%, +17%, +20% for one to four; `decol_iw_refresh_fighters` sums `decol_iw_arms_weight_sum` over the countries whose `decol_iw_arming` names the rebels each month and takes `te_sqrt` of it into `decol_iw_arms_mult`, the multiplier; owner, 2026-10-28); the war-support lines (§ War Support Feeds); the bar's `_independence_war` term (§ Stability Bar Formula); the home front; yearly, the Intelligentsia and Trade Unions disapprove, and the Armed Forces approve for two years and then disapprove.

**Talks** (kind 1 only: in a subject's or secession's war the goal is theirs). Opened from the panel's Open Talks (`colonial_empire_open_talks_sgui` → `.80`), by the Captains' Revolt, or by an AI after six red months or four years. Six months later the accords set `decol_iw_conceded` on the rebels, which makes `te_colonial_reconquest` invalid, so the engine drops it and the war can end in a peace, white or conceding the rebels' `te_colonial_independence`, which does nothing (the AI rebels should take one; a player offers one). Whether an AI metropole concedes that goal, a significant demand, is unverified. A conceded war drains the bar at the talks' figure. The `decol_iw_talks_peace` self-check logs OK when such a peace comes, BROKEN if the war still runs six months after the accords.

**The world takes sides.** When a war starts, each other great power hears of it (`decol_iw_war_begins_notice`). One with `gp_anti_colonial_stance` gets `.74`: condemn (relations −20, base 5), arm the rebels (base 2, +3 a rival, +2 with an army half again theirs; `decol_iw_begin_arming`: their foreign arms and a monthly payment, `decol_iw_arms_monthly`, from its treasury to theirs while the war lasts, `decol_iw_backer_monthly`; relations −30), intervene (`join_war` on their side; relations −50), or stay out. Intervene is open only to a great power at peace that is our rival with the larger army, and never to our ally, defensive-pact partner, bloc partner, subject or overlord, nor one under a truce with us or already beside us (`decol_iw_can_intervene`, the owner's guard of 2026-10-24); shown greyed with its reason otherwise. One with `gp_pro_colonial_stance` gets `.79` (not while it already arms a colonial power): arm us or stay out; it never joins. Its arms (`decol_iw_allied_arms_modifier`, offence and defence +5% a unit) are on the same curve as the rebels' foreign arms, each backer weighed the same way and fading over 24 months (`decol_iw_allied_arms_left` on the backer, counted down by `decol_iw_allied_backer_monthly`; the sum takes each weight × fade², so one backer's fade is linear, as the old two-year decaying modifier's was); `decol_iw_refresh_allied_arms` is the one refresh site, from `.79.a` and each backer's month. Both options' tooltips give the arms with ours and now, and our weight, from `decol_iw_value_arms_offer` in the event's `immediate` (on the great power: `decol_iw_offer_with`, `_now`, `_weight`, shown only while `decol_iw_offer_for` names this event's side).

**The home front breaks** (`.75`). When a war has run `decol_iw_crisis_war_months` (48) with our war support in it at `decol_iw_red_war_support` (25) or below for `decol_iw_crisis_red_months` (12) running (`decol_iw_check_crisis`; once a year at most). Without a voting law (`decol_iw_has_voting_law`), the Captains' Revolt: step aside (talks on every war, every eligible territory freed, `law_universal_suffrage`, and `law_parliamentary_republic` unless a monarchy or already one) or crush the officers (legitimacy −20 for two years, large radicals among officers and soldiers, and it can return a year later). With one, the Republic in Danger: call the general (`law_presidential_republic` from a parliamentary republic, +100 authority for three years, and an accords peace costs no legitimacy) or hold the line (legitimacy −15, the Armed Forces disapprove). Owner ruling D11: the law changes are the history.

**The AI at `.70`** (the spec's § 2.8): Concede base 4, +4 not a great power, +3 per condemning great power up to +9, +4 at war, +3 in the Crumbling band or the wave; Negotiate base 3, +3 a voting law, +2 Neocolonialism; Fight base 1, +4 a great power, +3 powerful Armed Forces, +3 settlers (our people a tenth of the territory), +2 no voting law, +2 Garrison level 2 or more, −2 per war already running, 0 in default or at war with a great power.

### Colonial Programmes (levels, costs, AI)

Three programmes: Development Investment (`colonial_development_investment_modifier`), Military Garrison (`colonial_military_garrison_modifier`) and Cultural Assimilation (`colonial_cultural_assimilation_modifier`). Each runs at a **level from 0 to 3** (`colonial_invest_level` / `colonial_garrison_level` / `colonial_assim_level`; read through the guarded `colonial_*_level_value`). **Level 3 is the full programme**, the on/off programme of before 2026-10, and each level is a third of it, linearly: the bar term (`colonial_stability_term_*` = full-strength effectiveness × level ÷ 3), the static modifier (applied at multiplier 0.333 / 0.667 / 1 by `colonial_programme_apply_modifier`) and the upkeep. Base full-strength terms: Investment +0.5, Garrison +1.0, Assimilation +0.7 a month (laws move them), so all three at the top add 2.2, as before.

**One refresh site.** `colonial_programmes_refresh_modifiers` re-applies the three modifiers at their levels and Investment's expense line, once per effect block: from a player's step (`colonial_programme_after_step`), from the entry's monthly pulse after the AI review, and from the civil-war sync. Each step is a core (`colonial_programme_<p>_up` / `_down`: the payments and the level change) plus, for the widget, the refresh (`colonial_empire_effect_<p>_up` / `_down`); the AI review calls the cores, so no block refreshes twice (re-removing a modifier added earlier in the same block is unproven).

**Costs. `country_expenses_add` is weekly and `gdp` yearly**, so every money value divides a yearly figure by 52. Until 2026-10 Investment charged `gdp × 0.005` a week, commented as monthly: 26% of GDP a year at any size (a play-test AI with GDP £2.314B and two small colonies paid £11.57M a week, over half its spending).

| Cost | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Investment upkeep, per level | 5% a year of the colonies' yearly GDP (Σ `gdp` of our overseas colonial states, cached monthly in `colonial_dev_colonial_gdp`), the base never under 1% of our GDP; ÷ 52 a week | `colonial_invest_level_cost`; the expense line's multiplier is `var:colonial_invest_cost_week` |
| Investment one-off | 26 weeks of one level's upkeep, every step up, on the cost base the row shows (cached monthly and when the entry starts) | `colonial_invest_step_cost` |
| Assimilation one-off | 13 weeks of one Investment level's upkeep, every step up | `colonial_assim_step_cost` |
| Garrison | 100 authority a level (the modifier's 300 × level ÷ 3); a step up needs authority > 100 | `colonial_garrison_authority_per_level` |
| Starting a programme (0 → 1 only) | Investment: Industrialists' and Petite Bourgeoisie's disapproval; Garrison: +5 infamy | the up cores |

As a share of GDP a year per Investment level: 0.5% when the colonies make a tenth of GDP (the target for a typical empire; 1.5% at level 3), 1.25% at a quarter, 0.05% on the floor (colonies under 1%). The play-test case is on the floor: £22.3k a week a level, £66.8k at level 3. A Cultural Hegemony funding level, for scale, is about 0.26%.

**The AI review** (`colonial_ai_review_programmes`, monthly from the pulse, `is_ai = yes`; the six AI-only enable/disable buttons are gone). The ceiling `colonial_ai_programme_ceiling` is the monthly cap (1.667) while the bar is below 100 and `colonial_ai_full_bar_margin` (+0.5, the war term) at 100: at a full bar a level only matters while it keeps the change safely positive, because a dip below 100 restarts the 60-month completion count, and a change trimmed to 0 would dip at the first war. The change it compares is the widget's `colonial_stability_drift_uncapped_display`.
1. **Lower** (Investment, Assimilation, Garrison, one level each at most, re-reading the change between them): when the change without the top level still reaches the ceiling (`colonial_ai_drift_without_<p>_step`), a level adds nothing (laws), the colonies are gone, or the programme is unaffordable (Investment: in default or `net_fixed_income < 0`; Garrison: authority < 0; Assimilation: authority or bureaucracy < 0). A cut for cost holds that programme off for 12 months (`colonial_ai_hold_<p>`).
2. **Raise** (only if nothing was lowered): while the change is under the ceiling, one programme up a level, by weight among those it can carry (`colonial_ai_can_raise_<p>`: the up gate, a positive term, out of default and `net_fixed_income` above one level's upkeep for Investment, authority > 34 and a bureaucracy surplus above one level's cut (5% of produced bureaucracy) for Assimilation). The weights are the old buttons' `ai_chance` (`colonial_ai_<p>_raise_weight`), read with `value =` in the `random_list` (vanilla's form; `add = <named script value>` there logs "Malformed token").
The raise and lower rules leave a dead band one level wide, so a programme does not flip back and forth.

**Endings.** The programme-month counters (`colonial_invest_months` etc.) add level ÷ 3 a month, so a month at level 3 counts 1 as before and a cheap low level cannot steer the ending as fast.

**Old saves.** A save holding a programme modifier but no level gets level 3 (`decol_migrate_programmes`, top of the pulse) and pays the new upkeep. Until that pulse, `colonial_<p>_level_value` reads the modifier as level 3 and each step core starts a missing level from it, so a click in the first month neither zeroes the programme nor strips the other two. Leftover `<modifier>_held` records are inert.

### Stability Bar Formula (`colonial_stability_bar.monthly_progress`)

**This table is the formula.** Every term is a named leaf script value in `common/script_values/colonial_empire_values.txt` (country scope), and the bar's `monthly_progress` is nothing but 22 unconditional `add = { desc = "<tooltip key>" value = owner.<leaf> }` lines, plus a 23rd, last line for the monthly cap (below). Each leaf returns 0 when its gate is false. The widget and the history charts read the **same** leaves through the nine `colonial_stability_drift_*` group sums, so there is exactly one place each number lives.

To retune a term, change only its leaf (or the named constant it multiplies by). To add one: add a leaf, add it to its group, add one `add` line to the bar with a `desc` key, and add a row here.

| Leaf (`colonial_stability_term_…`) | Group | Gate | Value | Tooltip key |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `_base` | base | — | **-0.5** | `colonial_base_drift_tt` |
| `_laws` | laws | — | `modifier:country_colonial_stability_drift_add` | `colonial_law_aggregate_tt` |
| `_ig_landowners` | igs | Landowners powerful | **+0.3** | `colonial_ig_landowners_strong_tt` |
| `_ig_armed_forces` | igs | Armed Forces powerful | **+0.2** | `colonial_ig_armed_forces_strong_tt` |
| `_ig_intelligentsia` | igs | Intelligentsia powerful | **-0.4** | `colonial_ig_intelligentsia_strong_tt` |
| `_ig_unions` | igs | Trade Unions powerful | **-0.3** | `colonial_ig_unions_strong_tt` |
| `_overreach` | overreach | `colonial_overreach_ratio > 0` (colonies ÷ other states − `colonial_overreach_free_ratio`, **1.5**) | ratio × `colonial_overreach_per_ratio` (**-0.4**) | `colonial_overreach_tt` |
| `_gp_rank` | rank | great power | **+0.3** | `colonial_gp_rank_bonus_tt` |
| `_non_gp` | rank | not a great power | **-0.5** | `colonial_non_gp_penalty_tt` |
| `_gp_condemnation` | gp | — (0 with no condemners) | `colonial_gp_condemnation_weight` × **-0.6** | `colonial_gp_condemnation_tt` |
| `_gp_high_pressure` | gp | condemners' prestige share ≥ 1/3 | **-1.0** | `colonial_gp_high_pressure_tt` |
| `_gp_extreme_pressure` | gp | condemners' prestige share ≥ 2/3 | **-2.0** | `colonial_gp_extreme_pressure_tt` |
| `_gp_support` | gp | — (0 with no supporters) | `colonial_gp_support_weight` × **+0.3** | `colonial_gp_support_tt` |
| `_garrison` | policies | Garrison level ≥ 1 | `modifier:country_colonial_garrison_effectiveness_add` × level ÷ 3 | `colonial_garrison_aggregate_tt` |
| `_assim` | policies | Assimilation level ≥ 1 | `modifier:country_colonial_assim_effectiveness_add` × level ÷ 3 | `colonial_assim_aggregate_tt` |
| `_invest` | policies | Investment level ≥ 1 | `modifier:country_colonial_invest_effectiveness_add` × level ÷ 3 | `colonial_invest_aggregate_tt` |
| `_low_acceptance` | acceptance | low-acceptance colonies > 0 (`colonial_state_low_acceptance_colony`) | count × `colonial_low_acceptance_per_colony` (**-0.4**) | `colonial_low_acceptance_tt` |
| `_high_acceptance` | acceptance | high-acceptance colonies > 0 (`colonial_state_high_acceptance`) | count × `colonial_high_acceptance_per_state` (**+0.5**) | `colonial_high_acceptance_tt` |
| `_war` | domestic | at war | **-0.5** | `colonial_war_penalty_tt` |
| `_independence_war` | domestic | a war of independence on record (§ Wars of Independence) | wars × `decol_iw_bar_drain_per_war` (**-0.5**), each conceded one at `decol_iw_bar_drain_per_war_in_talks` (**-0.25**) instead | `colonial_independence_war_penalty_tt` |
| `_revolution` | domestic | revolution | **-1.0** | `colonial_revolution_penalty_tt` |
| `_turmoil` | domestic | `country_turmoil > 0.05` | turmoil × **-2.0** | `colonial_turmoil_penalty_tt` |
| `_cap` (**last line**) | — (not in a group) | sum outside ±`colonial_stability_drift_cap` | clamp(sum, ±1.667) − sum | `colonial_drift_cap_tt` |

**Per-territory weight.** The four named constants in the rows above (the acceptance weights, the overreach scale and its free ratio) are also read by the Colonial Holdings list's hover, `colonial_territory_disp_weight`: a territory's own acceptance term plus the overreach term now less the term without it (from the monthly `colonial_empire_colony_count` / `_metropole_count`), so the hover says what releasing it would take off the bar. Retune the constant, not the leaf.

**Great-power terms scale with relative prestige.** Each condemning or supporting great power is weighted by its prestige divided by the empire's (floored at 1), clamped to **[0.25, 2.0]** — `colonial_gp_condemnation_weight` / `colonial_gp_support_weight` sum those weights. A peer great power therefore contributes the table's figure (-0.6 / +0.3), one with double the empire's prestige or more contributes twice that, and one with a quarter or less contributes a quarter. Net effect: the most prestigious empires shrug off condemnation from lesser powers, while small colonial holders (Portugal, Belgium, the Netherlands) feel superpower condemnation at up to -1.2/month each.

**The two escalations fire on prestige share, not head count.** `colonial_gp_condemner_prestige_share` is the condemners' combined prestige divided by `colonial_gp_prestige_pool` — the prestige of every great power *plus the empire itself*, counted even when it is not a great power. At **≥ 1/3** `_gp_high_pressure` applies a flat -1.0; at **≥ 2/3** `_gp_extreme_pressure` adds a further -2.0 (both apply, -3.0 total). Because the empire sits in the pool, a dominant empire is hard to isolate, while a minor one reaches the thresholds as soon as a few big powers condemn it. The pool is great powers rather than world prestige on purpose: condemners are a subset of the great powers that excludes the empire, and great powers together hold well under all of world prestige, so a two-thirds world share would practically never fire. `colonial_empire_refresh_display` snapshots the share into `colonial_empire_condemner_share` for the widget's overview pie and International Pressure section, and the supporters' share of the same pool (`colonial_gp_supporter_prestige_share`, display only: no escalation reads it) into `colonial_empire_supporter_share`, the pie's second slice. The event and button AI weights keyed on `colonial_gp_condemners_count >= N` are unchanged — they count powers, not pressure.

**Monthly cap, applied last.** However large the sum of the 22 terms, the bar moves at most **±`colonial_stability_drift_cap` = 1.667** a month (100 ÷ 60), so crossing the whole bar takes at least five years either way. The cap acts on the *total*, after everything is summed — not on any one term — so a sudden spike (a superpower condemning a tiny colony) cannot empty the bar in months, but a big enough sum still outweighs the empire's programmes and it has to change the inputs (win the great power over, integrate or release colonies) to turn the trend. The bar applies it as the `_cap` row: `colonial_stability_drift_total` (capped) minus `colonial_stability_drift_uncapped`, which is 0 inside the cap, so the hover shows how much the cap absorbed. `colonial_stability_drift_total` is now the capped figure everywhere it is read (the `colonial_empire_d_total` snapshot and the signed history chart, rescaled to ±2); the widget's headline is `colonial_stability_drift_total_display` and its breakdown ends with `colonial_stability_drift_cap_display`. The cap leaf evaluates the uncapped sum twice, i.e. every leaf about three times a month — acceptable at monthly cadence.

**Contributions that are not rows above** reach the bar through `country_colonial_stability_drift_add` (the `_laws` leaf) and surface inside that line's own `GetValueWithBreakdownFor` breakdown: every contributing law (Colonial Affairs, Minority Rights, Citizenship, Distribution of Power, Free Speech, Internal Security), the era techs (`globalization` **-1.5**/mo, `knowledge_economy` **-0.75**/mo), and the timed `colonial_stability_positive_event` / `_negative_event` modifiers. Programme effectiveness likewise aggregates a `base_values` baseline plus per-law contributions: the programme's full-strength figure, of which each level runs a third (§ Colonial Programmes).

**Reading it in game:** hovering the bar shows all 22 terms and the cap with their current values — `GetPeriodicProgressBreakdown`, generated by the engine from the `desc` keys above. The widget shows the nine group sums, the cap row and the projected (capped) total.

**Design intent:** A typical GP with 5 colonies nets roughly -0.5 to -1.0/month even with programmes active. Only great powers with few, well-integrated colonies and little GP condemnation can stabilize. The era techs make holding colonies nearly impossible late.

### Events (1-21)

Events 1-15 handle the core colonial cycle: negotiations, crackdowns, releases, GP stances, cultural identity, post-independence economics, nationalization.

**Post-Independence Events (16-21):**
- **Event 16 "The Partition Question"** — New nations near neighbors with shared heritage can demand unification (claims+tension), propose federation (truces+goodwill), or accept borders (stability).
- **Event 17 "Whose Country Is This?"** — Border disputes between two recently-formed nations. Options: press claims, seek mediation, accept borders.
- **Event 18 "The Strongman's Promise"** — Political instability in new nations. Options: military coup (authority+SoL loss), democratic transition (legitimacy), one-party state (authority+research).
- **Event 19 "The Crisis" (Suez model)** — GP reacts when a former colony nationalizes assets. Options: military intervention (infamy 15, **triggers Event 20** for all other GPs), economic sanctions (infamy 5), accept outcome. This is the flagship "interactive" GP event.
- **Event 20 "Gunboats in the Harbor"** — Other GPs respond to military intervention. Options: condemn (+moral authority, relations penalties to intervener), support intervention, stay neutral. **Not in on_actions** — triggered directly by Event 19 option A.
- **Event 21 "The Non-Aligned Path"** — New nations choose between competing superpowers or non-alignment.

**Choices made by the other party (2026-09, event-agency work, #427):**
- **Former colonies choose first.** The overlord's pool slots that used to fire `.5` (closer ties) and `.6` (reparations) now fire precursors on the former colony: **`.60`** (propose ties) and **`.61`** (demand reparations). The overlord hears only if the colony asked.
  - Lockout variables on the overlord replace the old cooldowns: `decol_ties_overture_cd` and `decol_reparations_demand_cd`.
  - Reparations are a real transfer. The claim is fixed at demand time: 10 % of the payer's yearly revenue, capped at 10 % of the claimant's GDP (`decol_reparations_value`).
- **Truces need consent.** `.16.b` (federation) and `.17.c` (border) ask the neighbour first, through **`.62`** / **`.63`**, instead of imposing a 60-month truce on it.
- **Pressure comes from real stances.** `.2`'s pressuring power is picked only from great powers carrying `gp_anti_colonial_stance`. The General Assembly flavour appears only once the decolonization UN regime exists.
- **`.20` names the real intervener and victim,** saved by `.19.A`, instead of re-picking them.
- **Only colonial subjects count.** `.1`/`.2`/`.3`/`.8`/`.11` iterate `is_qualifying_colonial_subject` only.
- **`.206`'s "by decision" epilogue** needs a chosen release. `decol_record_chosen_release` counts them (`decol_chosen_releases`); otherwise a neutral variant shows.

**What a revolution's winner keeps (PR #508):** the three running programmes, the permanent ending rewards (`colonial_empire_solidified_modifier` and the three path modifiers), `imperial_federation_modifier` and `mandate_system_modifier` are country modifiers, which a civil war's winner does not inherit. Each has a variable record, and `decol_repair_after_civil_war` (from `te_civil_war_on_won`) puts back each modifier whose record the winner holds and whose modifier it lacks. The permanent modifiers' records are `<modifier>_held`, written by `decol_grant_permanent_modifier`, plus the decisions' own `imperial_federation_taken` / `mandate_system_taken`. A programme's record is its level variable (`colonial_invest_level`, `colonial_garrison_level`, `colonial_assim_level`), and `decol_sync_programmes` rebuilds the modifiers from the levels. The programmes need the entry, and whether `on_civil_war_won` runs before or after the winner inherits it is unknown, so the entry's monthly pulse refreshes them too; its programme tests read the levels, so a programme it restores counts that month. The permanent modifiers' records are country variables, merged before `on_civil_war_won`, so the repair restores them at once. A yearly country pulse (`decol_permanent_modifier_sync_on_action`) backfills the records on saves from before they existed. It backfills the Solidified record only while no entry runs, because that key is also the entry's top band modifier. Timed modifiers and the entry's band modifiers are not rebuilt.

**Former colonies, colonial wars and notices (2026-09, #430):**
- **Every freed colony is a former colony.** `decol_mark_former_colony` (`decolonization.txt`) sets `var:former_overlord` (untimed) and `recently_decolonized` (7300 days), the shapes the vanilla release actions set in `te_construction_market_on_released_*`. `apply_decolonization_path` calls it for every country `form_decolonized_country` creates, and so do the `make_independent` options `.1.c`, `.1.d_neo`, `.3.a` and `.3.c`. That opens the precursors `.60` / `.61` (and, through them, `.5` / `.6`) and the post-independence events `.7`, `.16`, `.17`, `.18` and `.21`, which read `recently_decolonized`.
- **Events for countries that never hold the entry gate on the rule.** `.4`, `.7`, `.15`, `.16`, `.17`, `.18`, `.21` (a former colony) and `.14`, `.20` (any great power) test `has_game_rule = decolonization_enabled` instead of `has_journal_entry = je_colonial_empire`. #44 had gated every event in the file on the entry, so until 2026-09 these could not fire: a freed colony never holds the entry, and `.14`'s AI weights and `.20`'s audience are aimed at great powers without colonies. `.14` / `.20` also reach a great power whose own entry has ended, on purpose: they stand for the powers that disapprove of colonial empires and press them (owner ruling, 2026-09-26). `.19` (the former overlord) keeps the entry gate, as do the metropole-side events.
- **The former-colony events need only the world's decolonization era.** `.4`, `.7`, `.15`, `.16`–`.19`, `.21` and the outer gate of `decolonization_events_on_action` test `decol_era_begun` (some country has researched `decolonization`, `colonial_empire_triggers.txt`) instead of the recipient's own research (owner ruling, 2026-09-26). The metropole-side events still need the recipient's own technology through their own triggers (the entry's `is_shown_when_inactive`, `.14`, the `.60` / `.61` target checks).
- **`.51` (Conscription Crisis) fires only in a colonial war.** The trigger is `decol_fighting_colonial_war` (`colonial_empire_triggers.txt`), used by both the event and its pool entry. It is true when the country is at war and one of these holds: the colonial crackdown is running, an enemy is its own qualifying colonial subject, or an enemy fights it in a war of independence on record (`var:decol_iw_metropole`: a territory's rebels, or a colony's secession, § Wars of Independence). Other wars still drain the bar through `colonial_stability_term_war`.
- **`.50`'s City-and-sterling text is Britain's** (`c:GBR ?= this`). Every other empire gets a generic treasury text, since the mod models no per-country currency.
- **Notices name the colonial power.** `.1`, `.2`, `.3` and `.11` save it as `scope:decol_colonial_power` for their `notification_*` loc. A message reads only `notification_<msg>_name/_desc/_tooltip`, never a bare key. `.2.c` frees no one, so it posts its own `colonial_neocolonial_reforms_notice`; `colonial_neocolonial_terms_notice` is for the options that grant independence (`.1.d_neo`, `.3.c`).
- **The tech drops our claims on decentralized states.** `decolonization` carries `country_remove_decentralized_claims_bool` (a marker with no engine effect, defined in `mod_entity_modifier_types.txt`). `tech_marker_grants_on_action` (monthly, `extra_on_actions.txt`) reads it: a country that has the marker and any claim on a decentralized country's state removes each such claim (`state_region = { remove_claim = ROOT }`) and gets the `decentralized_claims_renounced` notice. It is a standing rule, not a one-shot: a claim that arrives after the first sweep goes within the month. The stake action itself is closed to a country with the marker: `te_stake_colonial_claim.txt` `REPLACE`s vanilla `da_stake_colonial_claim` with a `custom_tooltip` requirement (`da_stake_colonial_claim_renounced_tt`) added to its `possible`, so neither the player nor the AI pays for a claim the sweep would remove (the action's `accept_effect` also creates an incident against the target). It is a REPLACE, copied from the installed 1.14.5 game, because an INJECT of a second `possible` block is rejected at load (`scripting_best_practices.md` § "A second trigger block is rejected at load"); re-diff it on every vanilla patch. The sweep is the backstop for claims that arrive another way. Claims on regions with only recognized or unrecognized owners are untouched. A split region with any decentralized portion (such as Equateur shared with Sudan) loses the entire region claim, because claims belong to state regions. There is no claim iterator, so the sweep uses `any_decentralized_country` / `every_decentralized_country` and walks those countries' states. Both the pre-check and removal filter enter `state_region`, then ask the claimer via `ROOT = { has_claim = PREV }`; `PREV` there is the region, not a state's individual owner. `number_of_claims > 0` and the pre-check keep countries with nothing to drop off the removal pass.
- **Liberation contagion: one Colonial Crisis per empire at a time (2026-10-10).** `apply_decolonization_path` gives every other country running `je_colonial_empire` a 12-month `colonial_stability_negative_event` (Colonial Crisis, −1 stability a month) when it forms a country, unless that empire already carries `decol_liberation_contagion`, a 12-month country variable set alongside the modifier. It runs once per country formed, and Planned Full Decolonization (`.401.a`, which the AI reaches through `ce_planned_decolonization`) forms one per eligible state in a single effect, so without the guard one AI liquidation stacked about 20 copies (−20 a month) on every other empire. The guard is a variable, not `has_modifier`, because the loop's next pass must see it within the same effect.

### AI Behavior Tuning

AI weights across events are tuned to favor decolonization:
- Release/negotiate options have base weights of 2-4 with bonuses for non-GPs and high GP condemnation
- Crackdown/suppress options have base weights of 1
- The `ce_planned_decolonization` button fires when 3+ GPs condemn or 3+ states have low acceptance; non-GPs get an extra +30 weight bonus
- GP stance event (14): condemn colonialism base 5 with knowledge_economy bonus; support base 0

## Independence Nationalization Event (`decolonization_events.15`)

- **Purpose:** When a country gains independence, presents a choice about foreign-owned assets.
- **Trigger:** `on_become_independent` on_action (any subject that becomes independent), and `apply_decolonization_path` a week after it forms a country (release decisions and collapses; the new country was never a subject, so `on_become_independent` may not reach it).
- **Condition:** the decolonization game rule, the world's decolonization era (`decol_era_begun`), not a subject, no nationalization in the last 20 years (`nationalization_cooldown_var`, which also stops a double firing), and at least one foreign minor power or above owns >5% of the new country's GDP.
- **Branching description:** Uses `first_valid` + `triggered_desc` — violent version if `is_at_war = yes`, peaceful version otherwise.
- **Options:**
  - **(A) Full nationalization:** Seize all foreign assets. +10 infamy, -60 relations with all foreign owners, prestige boost, throughput penalty. Loyalists among lower strata, trade unions approve, industrialists oppose.
  - **(B) Selective nationalization:** Strategic sectors only. +5 infamy, -30 relations, moderate prestige boost.
  - **(C) Protect foreign property:** No infamy, +20 relations, foreign investment continues. Radicals among lower strata, industrialists approve.
- **Modifiers:** `independence_nationalization_modifier`, `selective_nationalization_modifier`, `foreign_property_protected_modifier` (all timed, decaying).
- **Notification:** `nationalization_notice` sent to affected foreign countries.
- **Files:** `events/decolonization_events.txt` (event 15), `common/on_actions/extra_on_actions.txt` (hook), `common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt` (3 modifiers), `common/messages/extra_messages.txt` (notification).

## Colonial Collapse (`colonial_collapse_effect`)

- **Purpose:** Once the decolonization era has begun, any tiny, poor AI country (not only a former colony: owner ruling 2026-09-26) is absorbed by an AI neighbour sharing its heritage or, with none, reverted to uncolonized land.
- **Location:** `common/scripted_effects/colonial_collapse_effects.txt`, triggered by `colonial_collapse_on_action` in `common/on_actions/extra_on_actions.txt` (wired to `on_yearly_pulse_country`). Each country tests itself on its own yearly pulse.
- **Gates:** the `decolonization_enabled` game rule, and some country in the world has researched `decolonization` (`decol_era_begun`; the collapsing country need not have it).
- **Criteria for collapse:** non-player, alive, not decentralized, not a subject, holding no subjects, in no power bloc and bound by no treaty in force (`colonial_collapse_unattached`, both branches: owner ruling 2026-09-26), no civil war at `civil_war_progress >= 0.75`, not in a diplomatic play or a war, and tiny and poor on a sliding scale: population under 100k with average SoL under 6, 200k and 5, 300k and 4, 500k and 3, or 1M and 2.
- **Twenty years' grace after independence**, from either of two 20-year variables:
  - `recently_decolonized`, set by `decol_mark_former_colony` (every country the mod's releases form, and the `make_independent` options of `.1` / `.3`) and by `te_construction_market_on_released_independent` (vanilla's release as independent). The post-independence events key on it.
  - `decol_collapse_grace`, set by `on_become_independent` (a subject made independent: an independence war won, an overlord's release), by a won secession (`te_civil_war_on_secession_end`) and on a war of independence's rebels when they form (`decol_iw_rebels_formed`). It exempts from collapse only and opens no event (owner ruling 2026-09-26).
- **Resolution order:**
  1. A neighbour of its most populous state whose owner shares a heritage trait group with it and is an AI country at peace and not a subject → that owner **annexes** it (never a player: owner ruling 2026-09-26) (the notice is posted first, while the collapsing country still exists).
  2. Otherwise, if it holds no company and no warheads → **`set_country_type = decentralized`** (revert to uncolonized). Vanilla never does this, so the branch is limited to the plain case.
  3. Otherwise nothing happens; it is tested again next year.
- **Notifications:** Countries in same strategic region AND great powers receive alerts.
- **History:** from db166830 (2026-03-23) until 2026-09 the limit held `any_civil_war = { always = no }`, which is always false, so the system never ran. The owner confirmed on 2026-09-26 that collapse should be live, the decentralize branch included.
