# Power Grids

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Power Grids (`building_grid_transfer_station`)

Electricity stays local (vanilla's `local = yes`), and power plants are unchanged. A **Grid Transfer Station** (one per state, from Electrical Capacitors) joins its state to a grid, and a monthly balancing moves Electricity between the grid's states through a market good, **Grid Power** (`grid_power`, base 30 like Electricity, `tradeable = no`, `fixed_price = yes` like gold, not local), which since 2026-10-30 settles the grid's accounts rather than counting its power (**Settlement**, below). Phase 1 built the station and the balancing within one country's market area; phase 2 added transmission efficiency, HVDC Undersea Interconnectors (a station's method until 2026-10-25, now a building of its own), the Grid Interconnection Agreement treaty article and the state panel tile; cross-market interconnection (2026-10-25) let the agreement join countries in different markets.

**Files.** `common/buildings/power_grid.txt`, `common/production_method_groups/power_grid_pmgs.txt`, `common/production_methods/power_grid_pms.txt`, `common/static_modifiers/power_grid_modifiers.txt`, `common/script_values/power_grid_values.txt`, `common/scripted_triggers/power_grid_triggers.txt`, `common/scripted_effects/power_grid_effects.txt`, `common/on_actions/power_grid_on_actions.txt`, `events/te_grid_events.txt`, `common/treaty_articles/123_grid_interconnection.txt`; the good in `timeline_extended_extra_goods.txt`; `base_values` lines, modifier registrations, `concept_power_grid`, the tile in `gui/states_panel.gui`; `test_power_grid.py`. Play-test list: `docs/testing/power-grid-playtest.md`.

**What a grid is.** A grid grows from one country's online stations in one market area (a *group*: `te_grid_add_group_effect`, `is_in_same_market_area`; France and French Algeria are two groups although they share a market) and joins further groups through two links: a **cable link** (`te_grid_sea_link`, `te_grid_cable_in_range`), two stations with undersea cables where either one's range reaches the other, within one country or between two with an agreement (below, **Undersea cables**); and a **treaty link** (`te_grid_land_link`), bordering stations of two countries whose owners have a Grid Interconnection Agreement. The article needs a land border, a shared sea node, or undersea cables that reach each other (`possible`), not a shared market (§ Between markets, below). A trigger can't walk the sea further than one `any_sea_node_adjacent_state`, so the last is read from a country list the monthly walk keeps (below, **Undersea cables**). The building's `possible` keeps a group contiguous: a country's first station in a market area may go in any of its states there, and each later one must border a state of the same country that has one (`any_neighbouring_state`). Every branch counts a queued station (`te_grid_state_has_station`, `any_scope_building`): `has_building` misses one until it is built.

**The station.** `bg_private_infrastructure` with `ignore_stateregion_max_level` (that group counts levels per state region, which would let only one owner of a split region build one), capped at one level by `state_building_grid_transfer_station_max_level_add = 1` in `base_values`, 100 construction (`construction_cost_very_low`, Barracks' tier; 400 until 2026-10-24), always subsidized by `country_building_grid_transfer_station_require_subsidies_bool = yes` there (Interventionism's highway and airport pattern). It buys and sells both goods at market prices like any private building, so no script books its trade. `pm_grid_transfer_base` carries a 1-unit input and output of both goods, 500 engineers and 500 clerks. The goods lines are `unscaled` (`workforce_scaled` until 2026-10-25, when a station that lost staff to migration or better jobs moved less and, below a quarter staffed, dropped its state off the grid). Whatever the block, a station moves its occupancy's share of its order: `level_scaled` and then `unscaled` lines each ran about 20% of their setting at 20% staffed (Arkansas, 2026-10-25). So the station is a very attractive job (`building_job_attractiveness_mult = 5` on the method), which brings engineers and clerks in and keeps them, and the factor (below) makes up for what staffing still cuts. Hiring is still capped at a tenth of the positions a week, and a building can't hire one profession more than 10 points ahead of the other (`EMPLOYMENT_PROPORTIONALITY_LIMIT`), so a state short of engineers or clerks still holds its station back. The subsidy covers its wages whatever it moves. A station with no staff stays on its grid with its order and modifiers, so it runs again from its first hire rather than from the next balancing; an empty building is expected to run no lines, so until then its grid is short its share (owner, 2026-10-25: better than waiting a month); `pmg_maintenance_heavy`. Four flow modifiers, one line each, scale the base lines: `modifier_grid_station_upload` (`goods_output_grid_power_mult`), `_upload_electricity` (`goods_input_electricity_mult`), `modifier_grid_station_download` (`goods_input_grid_power_mult`) and `_download_electricity` (`goods_output_electricity_mult`), each at 1. With multiplier Q − 1 a line moves Q a week, with −1 it is off. Other modifiers on a line add to its multiplier, so a state trait such as Niagara Falls (`goods_output_electricity_mult` +0.2) would make 0.2 Electricity at an uploading station; the Electricity modifiers are therefore applied net of everything else on their axis (`te_grid_read_other_electricity_effect`: the building's `modifier:` read less the station's own multiplier, stored as `te_grid_upload_electricity_mult` / `te_grid_download_electricity_mult`, the Uploaded Minds Nexus's netting). Nothing else touches Grid Power's lines. This is the Strategic Reserve Hub's mechanism (`goods_*_add` move goods only from a method block, never from a runtime `add_modifier`).

**The connection** (2026-10-30). The station's method gives its state a **Power Grid Connection** (`state_power_grid_connection_bool`, a script-only state boolean registered in `mod_entity_modifier_types.txt`, in the method's `state_modifiers`, `unscaled`, as vanilla's naval fortifications grant `state_control_strait_bool`), named with `[concept_power_grid]`, so the method's tooltip and the state's modifiers say the station joins the state to a grid. The balancing reads the same line: a station takes part when its state is connected (`te_grid_state_connected`, a `modifier:` read in the state) and the station is built (`te_grid_station_online`). Anything else that grants the boolean (a law, a technology, another building) counts the same way, but the station still moves the power, so it connects only a state with a station: the run's loops start from `has_building`. Unscaled, so an empty station keeps its connection as it keeps its order. A **Power Grid Disruption** (`state_power_grid_disrupted_bool`, script-only, `color = bad`) cuts the connection while it lasts: a boolean takes only `yes`, so an outage (a cyberattack, sabotage) can't take the connection away, and grants this line instead, which `te_grid_state_connected` reads as `= no`. A disrupted station is set idle like any station off its grid, keeping its factor, and rejoins at the first run after the disruption ends; its tile reads offline. Nothing grants it yet. Two self-checks read whether the connection's line reaches the read (below, `grid_connection`).

**The interconnector** (2026-10-25). A state's undersea cable is its **HVDC Undersea Interconnector** (`building_grid_hvdc_interconnector`, in `power_grid.txt`), a building of its own since 2026-10-25, when it replaced the station's `pmg_grid_interconnector` method group (`pm_grid_no_interconnector` or the HVDC method): a building shows among the state's buildings, with its range on its own modifiers, where a method hid in the station's panel (owner, 2026-10-25: more intuitive for players). `bg_public_infrastructure`, so government funded like the Strategic Reserve's buildings, since it earns nothing; one per state (`state_building_grid_hvdc_interconnector_max_level_add = 1` in `base_values`, `ignore_stateregion_max_level` as the station); Clean Energy Technologies; 400 construction (`construction_cost_medium`, a Port's tier); `potential = { is_coastal = yes }`, so a state with no coast isn't offered one; `possible` asks for a station in the state, built or queued (`te_grid_state_has_station`, under `te_grid_hvdc_needs_station_tt`); `ai_value = 0`, since a government building without one gets the engine's 1000 and the AI would raise cables on every coast (the AI's own pass is below). Its one method, `pm_grid_hvdc_interconnector` (HVDC Cable, in `pmg_grid_interconnector`), gives Undersea Cable Range +1 unscaled, +5 Electronic Components workforce-scaled and +250 engineers; `pmg_maintenance_heavy`. An interconnector left without a station joins nothing: the balancing reads cables only on states whose station is online.

**Undersea cables** (2026-10-25). A state lays a cable when its interconnector is built (`te_grid_lays_cable`, `level >= 1`: one queued exists at level 0). Its reach is a script-only building modifier registered in `mod_entity_modifier_types.txt`, the **Undersea Cable Range** (`building_grid_undersea_cable_range_add`), counted in Sea Nodes: the method gives 1 (in an `unscaled` block), and Smart Grids and Quantum Materials add 1 each from the country, since a building-scope type flows down, so a late cable crosses 3. A technology only lengthens a cable: the range is read on the interconnector alone (`te_grid_cable_range_value`, `modifier:`), and a state has a cable when its interconnector is built, its range is 1 or more, and it is on a coast (`te_grid_has_interconnector`). Until the interconnector became a building, the method also gave a boolean **Undersea Cable** (`building_grid_undersea_cable_bool`) marking which station laid one; the building does that now, and the boolean is gone. Two stations join when either one's range reaches the other, so a station with a short cable still joins one whose cable reaches it. Script has no Sea Node scope and no iterator over a Sea Node's neighbours, only `every_sea_node_adjacent_state` (the states sharing a Sea Node with a state), so distance is hops between coastal states: at 1 the two share a Sea Node, as before ranges; at 2 a coastal state shares one with each, and so on. A cable can't cross open ocean that no coast touches. `te_grid_sea_reach_effect` walks it breadth-first, marking each state with its hops (`te_grid_sea_hops`) and listing it once (global lists `te_grid_sea_reach`, `te_grid_sea_layer`, `te_grid_sea_next`), so a radius of 1 is one `every_sea_node_adjacent_state`, as before. The build (`te_grid_cable_links_effect`, from `te_grid_expand_from_effect`) walks out to the longest range of any online station with a cable this run (global `te_grid_cable_range_max`), and joins every station in reach that has a cable, passes `te_grid_sea_link` and is within either range: its group if waiting, and, when the two are in different groups (not one owner in one market area), each on the other's state variable list `te_grid_cable_links` for the tile. A station already on the grid is listed too, so the tile names every cable link, not only the one that joined it first. The same walk puts every other country whose station has a cable within either range, partner or not, on the owner's country list `te_grid_cable_reach` and the owner on theirs (`te_grid_add_cable_reach_effect`); the agreement's `possible` and its AI acceptance read it, so cables that reach farther than a shared Sea Node can be agreed, up to a month after both are laid. The lists are cleared at the start of each run and rebuilt, so they survive a revolution's loss of variable lists.

**Transmission efficiency.** `country_grid_transmission_efficiency_add` (script-only, registered in `mod_entity_modifier_types.txt`): 0.75 in `base_values`, +0.1 Rural Electrification (era 6), +0.05 Transistors (era 7), +0.02 Microprocessor (era 8), +0.03 Clean Energy Technologies (era 9), +0.03 Smart Grids (era 11), +0.02 Quantum Materials (era 12), so 0.95 in the present day and 1.0 only in the far future. A downloading station's Electricity line is the efficiency's share of its order (`te_grid_download_supply_mult_value`); an upload buys its whole order, so the flow is conserved and the losses fall on the receiving state. Each state uses its own owner's efficiency (`te_grid_state_efficiency_value`, clamped 0.1–1).

**The balancing.** One world-wide run on the 1st of each month, since a grid can span countries: the global `on_monthly_pulse` fires the hidden `te_grid.1` on one country with a station (the global pulse has no ROOT, and the modifiers' local-variable multipliers want one), and its `immediate` runs `te_grid_world_update_effect`:

1. Clear the run's global lists and marks (at the start as well as the end, so nothing left behind can join two grids). Per country with a station: clear a gone station's figures, refresh its agreement partners into the country list `te_grid_partners` (so the link triggers read a list, never walk treaties per neighbour), switch AI interconnectors (below), and list online stations (built and connected, whatever their staffing: `te_grid_station_online`) in `te_grid_pending_states`; set the rest idle.
2. Build each grid: a random waiting station's group, then follow links breadth-first from a frontier list (`te_grid_frontier`) into `te_grid_region`.
3. Balance it. For each member, Electricity without its station: `LP = production − downloaded × delivery`, `LC = consumption − uploaded`, where the station's own flows are read back from the state's Grid Power over the rate its lines were set at (`sg:grid_power` over `te_grid_power_rate_value`: its production is what the station uploaded last week, its consumption what it downloaded) and `delivery` is the efficiency the download was set to. The grid moves Electricity until it sells at one price `q` (over its base) in the states that send it and at `q / eff` in each state that receives it, what a trader would carry it for. The engine prices a local good on a state's gross orders, `1 + PRICE_RANGE × clamp((buy − sell) / min(buy, sell) / (BUY_SELL_DIFF_AT_MAX_FACTOR − 1), −1, 1)` (0.999 and 4 in `extra_defines.txt`, `te_grid_price_range` / `te_grid_buy_sell_max_factor`), and a sending station's Electricity is a buy order in its state. So with `x(p)` the sell/buy ratio at price `p` (`te_grid_send_ratio_value`, `te_grid_receive_ratio_value`), a sender (`LP > x(q) × LC`) sends `LP / x(q) − LC` and a receiver (`LP < x(q/eff) × LC`) takes `(x(q/eff) × LC − LP) / eff` (`te_grid_surplus_value`; flows are in Electricity sent); a state between the two ratios stays off the grid, as losses would eat what it could move. A short grid evens out above base as a long one does below it: the market is one of orders, not of stock, so a sending station's purchase bids its state's price up with the rest. The net flow rises with `q`, so twelve halvings of the engine's range (0.001–1.999, `te_grid_set_price_midpoint_effect`) find it within 0.0005 of base, with no product past the fixed-point ceiling. Until 2026-10-30 the rule set `LP − U = r × LC` everywhere (`r` the grid's supply over its demand): a large sender's own station was most of its demand, so it stayed at about base whatever the grid's surplus while its receivers got `r` times what they used (the 2013 Byzantium save: Attica at 29.8 with its Fusion Plant grown to 2,062 levels, the rest at the floor, the stations £2.9B a week in subsidy). Each flow moves 70% of the way from last month's to its target, and the larger side is then scaled so the grid's uploads equal its downloads exactly. Each member keeps the price the flow set brings it to (`te_grid_price_target`, the formula forward at the ratio the flow gives, `te_grid_set_price_target_effect`) and the grid's supply over its demand (`te_grid_region_ratio`) for the tile.
4. Throughput and shortfall correction. A station's four lines move its throughput's share of what they are set to. Each run reads the station's `modifier:building_throughput_add` first (`te_grid_read_throughput_effect`, stored as `te_grid_throughput`; `te_grid_station_throughput_value` is 1 plus it, floored at 0.2). The read carries the country's lines, Global Warming's −0.025 a degree among them; in the 2013 Byzantium save every fully staffed station's factor was 1 less that line at 1.81 °C (#1248). `te_grid_factor` is what the station moved last time over what it was asked (`te_grid_command`) and over the read, read back the same way, clamped to 0.2–2, and the order is the flow over the factor and the read. A building takes a new throughput at its next weekly tick, so last week's output ran at the read unless a line landed since; a step earlier in the month is in that output already. For a running station the order is therefore what it was before #1248, the flow times last order over what it moved: the first build of #1253 measured over the read the last order was set for, which counted a mid-month step twice (1.355 of the flow the month after a −0.25 line). What the read changes: the factor holds staffing and shortages alone, so a factor kept while a station is idle or barely ordered carries no old throughput, and a station's first order and its first after idle move its flow at today's throughput. A factor from before #1248 had its throughput in it and is divided by the first read once. A station short of its inputs still moves what the balance needs, and so does one cut by a line the read misses (a strike's `building_group_bg_infrastructure_throughput_add`). It also makes up for staffing, down to a fifth staffed: a station moves its occupancy's share of its order. While a station hires, each month's factor was learned at the week before's staffing, so it moves more than its flow until it is full. A week in which the station moved nothing (no staff) isn't measured: it would clamp the factor to 0.2, and the first hire would move five times the station's share.

The flow modifiers are applied on the building with `local_var:` multipliers (a `var:` or script value would resolve against ROOT, the country: #500).

The station's own flows are read back net (`te_grid_own_uploaded` = Grid Power made less bought, `te_grid_own_downloaded` the reverse, each over the rate and floored at 0), since a pass-through (below) adds the same to both lines and so cancels out of `LP`, `LC`, the factor and the conservation check.

**Settlement** (2026-10-30). A station's Grid Power line is its order times the grid's sending price `q` when it was set (`te_grid_power_rate`, stored in `te_grid_set_station_flow_effect` from `global_var:te_grid_price`; `te_grid_upload_power_mult_value` and `te_grid_download_mult_value` multiply by `te_grid_power_rate_value`), and its Electricity lines are the order as before. Grid Power's 30 is Electricity's base, so a sender buying `U` at `q` is paid `q × U`, and a receiver delivering `eff × D`, which sells at `q / eff`, pays `q × D`: each station covers its goods at the prices the balancing aims for, whoever owns it, and the grid's Grid Power balances because its flows do. Until then one unit of Grid Power was one unit of flow, so a grid below base made its senders' stations a profit of `30 × (1 − q)` a unit and cost its receivers' as much, paid as subsidy (the 2013 Byzantium save on #1242: £890M a week, the senders' margin returned only as dividends, or to private owners where Laissez-Faire, a declared bankruptcy or Enforce Privatization had sold stations). A station's balance is now about minus its wages and upkeep (`pmg_maintenance_heavy`), give or take the gap between its state's price and the one the grid aims for while the grid settles. One case has a loss of its own: on a grid so short that `q` passes the ceiling (1.999) times a receiver's efficiency (above 1.5 at 0.75, an early grid), the receiver's price stops at the ceiling (`te_grid_receive_price_value`), so it pays `D × (q − 1.999 × eff)` more than its power sells for, at most a quarter of what it pays. Power at the cap is worth less than the senders were paid, so some station carries that; on one unit a unit the senders carried more, `(q − 1) × U`. The rate's 1 default reads a station set by an earlier build correctly, so a save needs no migration. Pricing each station at its own state's price now would cover even that gap, but its Grid Power would no longer balance: an unmatched surplus at a fixed price is paid for anyway, and a grid whose receivers' prices run far above `q / eff` could sell under a quarter of the Grid Power it buys and read as short.

**Between markets** (2026-10-25). An agreement can join countries in different markets, and so can their interconnectors. Such a grid still uploads what it downloads, but market by market it doesn't: one market's stations send more than they receive and another's receive more, and the difference crosses the border. Grid Power has a fixed price (`fixed_price = yes`, gold's flag), so nothing settles that by script: a market's sellers are paid for all they sell and its buyers pay for all they buy, the engine paying sellers for sell orders nobody matches as it pays gold mines. The sending market is paid for its exports and the receiving market pays for its imports, at 30 a unit of Grid Power, which since 2026-10-31 is `q × 30` a unit of power sent, and no price moves. `te_grid_cross_market_effect` runs once a grid's flows are set, before the orders: on a grid with members in more than one market, each market's net flow on the grid (`te_grid_market_net`, + sent, − received) goes on every member there, for the tile. A market that buys more than it sells is still counted short by the engine once its sales fall under a quarter of its purchases (`NEconomy.GOODS_SHORTAGE_PENALTY_THRESHOLD = 0.25`), and a shortage cuts its buyers' output, which would cut the receiving stations' Electricity. So one station in each receiving market **passes the import through** (`te_grid_import`, a flow, carried at the rate): it buys and sells that much more Grid Power, which nets to nothing at a fixed price and keeps the market's sales at half its purchases or more. The pass-through rides the two Grid Power lines only (`te_grid_upload_power_mult_value`, `te_grid_download_mult_value`, over the station's factor like its own order); the Electricity lines carry the station's own order. The station is one with staff (an empty station stays on its grid but passes nothing through): last month's if it is still a border station there (one linked to a member in another market this run: `te_grid_border`, set by `te_grid_mark_border_effect`), else a border station, else any member of the market; with none staffed, any member. A grid within one market, an idle station and a gone one keep no pass-through and no market flow (`te_grid_clear_cross_market_effect`). The design note before this build had border stations as a pure sink and source with a treasury transfer settling the money. That keeps every market balanced without relying on the fixed price, but the receiving market's source station then keeps what its buyers pay, and its government pays the bill; it is the fallback if either self-check below fails.

**AI.** The investment pool won't fund a station (it earns about what it pays), so the AI's grid comes from government construction, by two routes (2026-10-24; before, the value was only the price gap, 0 at base price and at most 2500, and the AI built few stations). `ai_value = te_grid_station_ai_value`: 0 unless the owner makes Electricity in some state of the market area (`te_grid_ai_market_area_has_power`, a downloading station counting), else 4000 + 20 per Electricity the state makes a week (to 4000) + 1 per 1,000 people (to 2000) + 10 000 × the price gap either way (to 2000), at most 10 000. The state's own use is deliberately not read: a state without power can't run the methods that use it, so it reads as needing none (owner, 2026-10-24). Producers rank first because a grid's power starts there; population stands in for the demand a grid would find once it brings power. Since the construction AI doesn't reliably build what `ai_value` asks for (the Strategic Reserve Hub), every AI country also queues one station a month itself (`te_grid_ai_build_effect` from `on_monthly_pulse_country`, `start_building_construction`): with Electrical Capacitors, not in default and under a year of government construction queued, in the state that value ranks highest among those with no station, power in the market area and the building's placement rule (`te_grid_station_placeable`, a scope-saving copy of `possible`, which reads ROOT as the state). So a grid starts at the largest producer and grows one bordering state a month. The balancing still moves power only to demand that exists, so a newly joined state receives Electricity once something there uses it. The interconnector's `ai_value` is 0, so script builds the AI's cables (`te_grid_ai_interconnectors_effect`): an AI country with Clean Energy Technologies, not in default and under a year of government construction queued, queues an interconnector (`start_building_construction`) at a coastal online station with none when a station within the reach one there would have (`te_grid_ai_cable_reach_value`: the method's range, `te_grid_hvdc_method_range`, plus the technologies', read from the station, `te_grid_tech_cable_range_value`; walked by `te_grid_sea_reach_effect`) would join it to another grid (its own in another market area, or an agreement partner's: `te_grid_ai_interconnector_wanted`). One a month at most (`local_var:te_grid_ai_hvdc_queued`), and none while one of its own is still being built (`level < 1`): once the far end has a cable too, the next run joins the two grids. It never demolishes one, since a cable that joins nothing may again when an agreement returns and rebuilding costs 400. Until 2026-10-25 the AI switched the station's method and back, at most once a year a station. The walk grows with range (three Sea Nodes with both technologies, a few hundred states against about 30 at one), so each station is judged at most every six months (`te_grid_ai_hvdc_checked`, 180 days), not monthly. The agreement's AI proposes it more when it has stations (`evaluation_chance`, root-only) and accepts it more when both sides have stations across the border or the sea.

**State panel tile** (§ State Panel GUI Enhancements): mode (sending, receiving, idle, offline), the grid's hub, the Electricity moved a week, grid size, on a grid that spans markets the market's net Exports or Imports on it (`te_grid_market_dir_disp`, `te_grid_market_flow_disp`), the pass-through on the station that carries one (`te_grid_import_disp`), on a station with a cable the Sea cables row (how many stations of other groups its cable joins, `te_grid_cable_count_disp`; the hover gives the range and names them through `te_grid_cable_links_sgui`'s `ExecuteTooltip`, `common/scripted_guis/te_grid_sguis.txt`), and efficiency; the hover gives the grid's supply against its demand and the cable's range. The hub is the grid's largest producer (the member with the most Electricity without its station), stored on every member as a state reference (`te_grid_hub`) and named with `State.MakeScope.Var('te_grid_hub').GetState.GetName`, the form Demographics uses for its largest city from a country. A state reference read from a state is new in the mod, so it is on the play-test list.

**Self-checks.** `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN grid_conservation` once a game (marker `_v2` since phase 2), at the first grid with an uploader and a downloader each ordered 100 or more and the same members as last month: the grid's stations' uploads against their downloads last week, in flow (Grid Power over the rate), within 10%. `BROKEN grid_readback` if both read 0 (`sg:grid_power` reports nothing). `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN grid_station_cascade` once a game, at the first station whose state reads a non-zero Electricity output bonus: whether the building's read, less its own modifier, carries it (if not, the netting misses state and country lines). `TE_GRID staffing` (info, once, at the thirteenth balancing after the station became an attractive job): how many stations on grids sit under a quarter staffed, under half, under 90%, and at 90% or more. It replaced `grid_unscaled` and `grid_level_scaled`, which found that both blocks' goods lines run at occupancy. `TE_GRID empty_station` (info, once; marker `_v2`): what the first station with no staff at last month's run and none now moved of its order; 0 confirms an empty building runs no lines. `grid_conservation` judges only stations that had staff at last month's run and have staff now (`te_grid_staffed`, recorded per member at each run; `te_grid_station_staffed_throughout`), so a grid of new, empty stations can't read as BROKEN `grid_readback`. `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN grid_cable_range` once a game, at the first online station whose state has had a built HVDC Undersea Interconnector since the last run (`te_grid_chk_cable_seen`, so its modifiers have settled): whether the interconnector reads the method's range back, net of the technologies' (`te_grid_chk_method_range_value`: its `te_grid_cable_range_value` less the station's `te_grid_tech_cable_range_value`, ≥ `te_grid_hvdc_method_range`; the line prints both reads); BROKEN means no cable joins any grid without technology. `TE_GRID dispatch` (info, once) prints the first run's date and month index. `TE_SELF_CHECK OK|BROKEN grid_price` once a game: a grid member's Electricity price now against the formula on its production and consumption now (station flows included), within 0.05 of base, at a sending state whose station bought at least half as much as the rest of the state uses and whose ratio prices it at 0.1–0.9 of base (`te_grid_chk_price_sender`), so a formula that left the station's purchase out would read far off; `grid_price_above_base` does the same at a member the formula prices at 1.36–1.86 (`te_grid_chk_price_above_base`), where the branch above base and the obvious alternative are 0.19 or more apart. A price read that returns nothing reads 1.000 and fails both.
