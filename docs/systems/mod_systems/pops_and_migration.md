# Pops, Migration and Resettlement

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## Migration Crowding

- **Purpose:** Reduces migration pull to heavily populated states. Prevents unrealistic population concentrations.
- **Modifier:** `migration_crowding` — `state_migration_pull_mult = -0.1` (base, scaled by multiplier).
- **Script values:** `common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt` — search for `MIGRATION CROWDING`.
- **On action:** `migration_crowding_on_action`, wired to `on_yearly_pulse_state`.
- **Density-based:** Uses `state_population / migration_crowding_effective_land` as a proxy. The effective land is `arable_land_base` (min 1) plus Urban Engineering's land equivalent.
- **Urban Engineering:** vanilla's `state_building_construction_sector_max_level_add`, relocalized (`replace/timeline_extended_override_l_english.yml`, `concept_urban_engineering`). The mod's `REPLACE:building_construction_sector` drops `has_max_level`, so the modifier is free; vanilla grants it from `urbanization` (+10), `urban_planning`, `modern_sewerage`, `steel_frame_buildings` and `elevator` (+5 each), and the mod's Public Housing building adds it in one state (below). Each point adds `migration_crowding_urban_engineering_pop` (1,000) people to the threshold before tolerance: `migration_crowding_effective_land` adds points × 1,000 / the floor density as arable land, so threshold = (10,000 × arable + 1,000 × points) × (1 + tolerance), and the 10x knee moves with it. It does not touch the resettlement frontier densities (`resettlement_crowding_scale`), which are per km² over the region.
- **Density modifier:** `state_migration_crowding_density_mult` — custom modifier that divides effective density (density / (1 + mult)). Applied by `institution_ministry_of_urban_planning` (+10% per level) and five technologies: `modern_skyscrapers` (+15%), `modern_urban_planning` (+20%), `advanced_structural_engineering` (+20%), `autonomous_vehicles` (+15%), `post-scarcity_economy` (+15%).
- **Scaling:** Quadratic up to the 10x density knee, then linear beyond it. `migration_crowding_ratio` is 0 at the floor, 1.0 at the 10x knee, and can exceed 1.0 in the linear tail. `migration_crowding_mult` uses `4.5 * r^2` below the knee and `9 * r - 4.5` above it, giving a 45% pull penalty at 10x density and 145% at 20x density.
- **Demographics:** the census raises a state's infection deaths by its migration penalty from crowding (0.1 × the multiplier, with no ceiling since 2026-10-20; +50% at most before). The refresh stores the multiplier it applies in `migration_crowding_mult_applied` for it: the census then reads the year's figure (the one the save holds) at fast mode's steps between refreshes too, and whatever ROOT is, since `migration_crowding_mult` reads `ROOT.owner` and the console's benchmark (`te_debug_demog.1` d) steps every state with ROOT the player's country. A state carrying the modifier without the variable (a save from before it) reads no term until its next refresh.
- **Tuning:** `migration_crowding_density_reference` (10000), `migration_crowding_floor_ratio` (1x), `migration_crowding_ceiling_ratio` (10x knee).
- **Split states:** Arable-land-derived threshold and tooltip breakdown values subtract the same-owner regional `arable_land_added` cache, so fully owned split states do not undercount geographic base land.

## Public Housing

- **What (2026-10-31, owner's request):** `building_public_housing` (`common/buildings/extra_buildings.txt`), a government building (`bg_government`, paid from the budget) that raises its own state's crowding threshold. Its methods grant the state Urban Engineering (`state_building_construction_sector_max_level_add`, `state_modifiers` `workforce_scaled`), the modifier the threshold already reads (§ Migration Crowding), so there is no new modifier, script value or pulse: the tile's Urban Engineering breakdown lists the building, and an unstaffed level houses no one. Like the technologies' points, a level's points are multiplied by the state's tolerance (`state_migration_crowding_density_mult`). Crowding is refreshed yearly, so a new level shows in the pull penalty within a year.
- **Unlock and gate:** `urban_planning` (era 1). No eligibility gate (§ Design defaults: soft caps): it can be built anywhere, and in a state below its threshold it does nothing.
- **Methods** (`pmg_base_building_public_housing`, `common/production_methods/extra_pms.txt`; inputs at base prices, about £30–45 a week per 1,000 people housed on every method; ~1,000 staff a level):

  | Method | Technology | Urban Engineering a level | People housed a level (before tolerance) | Inputs a level |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Model Dwellings | none | 10 | 10,000 | wood 15 |
  | Sanitary Tenements | `modern_sewerage` | 15 | 15,000 | wood 10, iron 5, glass 5 |
  | Steel-Frame Apartments | `steel_frame_buildings` | 20 | 20,000 | steel 10, glass 10 |
  | High-Rise Apartments | `elevator` | 30 | 30,000 | steel 10, glass 10, electricity 10 |
  | Prefabricated Tower Blocks | `prefabricated_construction` | 45 | 45,000 | steel 15, glass 10, electricity 15 |
  | Arcologies | `advanced_structural_engineering` | 70 | 70,000 | advanced materials 0.5, electricity 25, robotics 3 |

- **Maintenance:** `pmg_maintenance` (0.1 construction a level): housing is all plant (`construction.md` § Maintenance tiers).
- **AI:** the building's `ai_value` (state scope) is 0 until `migration_crowding_ratio` passes 0.1, then 300 + 2,000 × the ratio, at most 4,300; each level lowers the ratio, so the value falls as it works. The group is `most_productive` and each method's `ai_value` doubles the one before (1,000 to 32,000), so the AI climbs the ladder. Whether the construction AI builds it at these values is unverified (`scripting_best_practices.md` § AI and Cost-Only Buildings); if it doesn't, the fallback is a monthly AI pass that queues a level in its most crowded state with `start_building_construction`, the Grid Transfer Station's shape.
- **Icon:** borrows vanilla's `urban_center.dds` until the icon pipeline renders its own (`scripts/image_pipeline/icon_prompts.py`, `ICONS["building"]`, seed `None`).

## Unemployment as a Share of the Workforce

- **Why:** the engine's `state_unemployment_rate` is the idle workforce over all the state's people, dependents included (Demographics' self-check `unemployment_rate_read`, 2026-10-17: 0.048 where the pops held 0.195 of their workforce with no workplace). A threshold written as a share of the workforce bit at about four times that.
- **The conversion:** `te_state_unemployment_share` (state scope, `common/script_values/te_unemployment_values.txt`) = the engine's figure, read live, ÷ `te_state_workforce_share`, the workforce's share of the people from the state's last walk (`te_unemp_wf_share`; 0.25 before the first, at least 0.05), clamped 0–1. Unemployment moves in months and the workforce share in years, so only the slow part is cached.
- **The walk:** `te_unemp_share_refresh` (`common/scripted_effects/te_unemployment_effects.txt`) sums each pop's `total_size`, its `workforce`, and the workforce of pops with no workplace, and stores `te_unemp_wf_share` and `te_unemp_share_exact` (the walk's own idle share, the self-check's fallback).
  - **Construction Cost Scaling's counts:** it also stores `te_unemp_wf_count`, `te_unemp_slack_count` and `te_unemp_peasant_count` (people). Slack is every `peasants` pop's workforce plus every other pop's with no workplace, the dataset's rule (`econ_save_series.pops_by_state`).
  - **One rule:** each pop is counted by `te_unemp_count_pop`, which Construction Cost Scaling's self-check reuses.
  - **Timing:** yearly from `on_yearly_pulse_state`. Monthly only for a state with no walk yet, or none since the counts were added (`common/on_actions/te_unemployment_on_actions.txt`).
  - **Rules:** it runs whatever the rules, beside the Demographics walk, which counts the workforce only under the census.
- **Readers:** the Settlement Authority's `ai_value` (10%; 5% as first written, raised by the owner on 2026-10-17), the Greenest Grass Campaign's and Recruitment Drive's `ai_weight` (10%), and `migration_attraction_script_value` (10%, the mass-migration destination). The Demographics census stores it at its walk (`te_dg_unemp`), which its youth bulge, event wave, pension AI and panel read (owner, 2026-10-17: one source); a state the census walks before the conversion's own walk has reached it takes the census walk's workforce share as `te_unemp_wf_share`, the same sum. Vanilla's own readers (Greener Grass, the Spanish flu, cultural communities) still read the engine's figure. Not for a scripted GUI or loc, which read the trigger as none (`scripting_best_practices.md` § The op slots).
- **Self-check:** `unemployment_share_read` (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md` § Unemployment as a share of the workforce).

## Dynamic Homeland Progress

- **Parallel per-culture tracks:** each eligible culture gets its own script container and progress bar. All eligible homelands can form or decay simultaneously. A new candidate begins at zero and cannot inherit another culture's progress. Containers are parented to the state, kept in separate creation/removal lists, and removed from the list before destruction.
- **Timing:** the monthly state pulse adds te_homeland_annual_progress units toward 1200; display divides stored units by 12. Base 10 units/month completes in exactly 120 months (10 years). Promote National Values alone makes this 30 months. Existing additive speed modifiers, turmoil/legitimacy multipliers and the 1–95 annual-rate clamp remain.
- **Gates:** homeland changes enabled on the owner (`country_homelands_can_change_bool`, granted by the laws `law_ethnostate`, `law_minority_rights_violent_hostility` and `law_linguistic_purity`, Cultural Unity principle tiers II–V, and the `mass_media` tech; the tile names them via `TE_HOMELAND_UNLOCK_SOURCES`, which `test_homeland_unlock_sources.py` keeps in sync), unified regional ownership, and culture meeting existing primary/homeland/population checks. Creation is inclusive at the formation threshold; removal is strictly below its threshold.
- **Removal rate:** the removal track runs at the shared annual rate × (1 + `state_homeland_removal_speed_mult`), floored at a tenth of it (`te_homeland_removal_annual_progress`, `homeland_values.txt`). The modifier is script-only; the UN Convention on Cultural Diversity gives its parties −30% × UN enforcement (UN redesign §0.12). The creation track keeps the shared rate.
- **Pausing/resetting:** losing the unlock or unified ownership pauses valid progress. A different owner or invalid target (population, primary status, homeland status) clears that track. Progress cannot pass to another owner/culture. A zero removal threshold cannot be met.
- **UI:** always-visible Homeland Dynamics tile, including states with no existing homelands; its contents are conditional. A Creation or Removal section appears only while it has projects or can run here (`te_homeland_<track>_shown`; removal also needs a removal threshold above the base 0%, `te_homeland_removal_threshold_open`), so a state where no primary culture lacks a homeland and no non-primary culture has one shows a single "no culture here can gain or lose homeland status" line (`te_homeland_relevant = 0`) instead of empty sections. Bars exist only for real per-culture projects (percentage, culture, and a warning once a project stops qualifying). An unlock or split-region pause is one tile-level line (`te_homeland_pause_status`), shown only when a track applies; while paused, only tracks with kept progress are listed. A visible section's own line is its population threshold, monthly points, or "starts next month". Threshold numbers (in the blocker lines and the tile tooltip) are hoverable: each shows the clamped threshold the gate tests, a note when the clamp binds, and the modifier's per-source list (`GetDescFor`). Tooltip explains thresholds, rate and pause/reset rules.
- **Files:** homeland_triggers.txt, homeland_effects.txt, homeland_values.txt and homeland_sguis.txt under their respective common/ directories. te_homeland_monthly is wired in extra_on_actions.txt; GUI content lives in te_state_panel_widgets.gui.
- **Save compatibility:** variables initialize on the first eligible monthly pulse; UI reads missing variables as zero. No migration or new game is required.

## Internal Resettlement (Settlement Authority)

- **Design:** `docs/superpowers/archive/specs/2026-09-26-internal-resettlement-design.md` (Decisions table is settled). International transfers stay with the Population Transfer treaty article below.
- **Building:** `building_resettlement_colony`, displayed as Settlement Authority, in the destination state only. Frontier gate: a new one needs fewer than 2 people per km² over the whole state region, and it closes at 10 per km²; both scale with `1 + state_migration_crowding_density_mult` and, since 2026-10-25 (owner), in proportion to world population (`resettlement_world_population_scale` = `global_population` ÷ `resettlement_frontier_reference_population`, a billion, about the 1836 start's, so the start's calibration holds and two billion people double both). The world's land (about 109 million km² in the area table) averages about 9 people per km² at a billion, so a frontier opens below about a fifth of the world's average density and closes near it, and natural growth alone no longer closes every frontier late in the game. The AI's bonus for a frontier emptier than 0.5 per km² scales the same way (`resettlement_frontier_empty_margin`). Areas come from `te_region_area_generated.txt` (`scripts/generators/gen_region_area.py`). Level cap is 5 from `base_values`, plus 5 each from nationalism, civilizing_mission, mass_propaganda, keynesian_economics and civil_rights_movement.
- **Programs** (`pmg_resettlement_programme`): Land Grants, Military Colonies, Penal Transportation, Organized Colonization, Special Settlements, Development Program, Rustication, Managed Retreat. Each sets who is recruited (`resettlement_pop_eligible`; never by culture or religion), capacity (`state_resettlement_transfer_add`, half level-scaled, half workforce-scaled), staff, goods and additive destination effects. Settlement plans (`pmg_resettlement_settlement`): Homesteads, Work Settlements, Planned Towns. Transport (`pmg_resettlement_transportation`) adds capacity.
- **Destination bonuses:** no PM carries a multiplicative or unscaled effect. Each program and settlement plan has a state modifier, `resettlement_<key>_destination` (incorporation and colony growth for every program, plus turmoil for Military Colonies and mortality for Penal Transportation and Special Settlements; virgin soil for Homesteads, mining and logging for Work Settlements, construction for Planned Towns). After the walk, the country pulse stores `rs_bonus_scale` = min(1, the month's arrivals ÷ `resettlement_bonus_full_arrivals`, 2,000); the state pulse's `resettlement_refresh_destination_bonus` removes all eleven and re-adds the active pair with `multiplier = var:rs_bonus_scale`. So a level-1 Authority or an idle program buys little, and a vast region (Yakutsk) does not keep the bonus for good. `resettlement_clear_destination_bonus` removes them, then `rs_bonus_scale`, at closure and (from the state pulse) on any state that has lost its Authority.
- **Monthly pulse** (`resettlement_country_monthly`, `on_monthly_pulse_country`):
  - Each Authority walks its owner's states in order of eligible population (Recruitment Drive decree states first), skipping takes under 100. No pop gives more than 2% of itself to one Authority, and each source gives at most 2% of its eligible population a month (4% under a drive), across all Authorities: a walk's budget at a source is that share of the people its program may recruit there, less `rs_recruits` (what earlier destinations took this month).
  - It moves the survivors with `move_partial_pop`; for coercive programs, deaths in transit are removed at the source in steps of 100, and the source carries the fraction (`rs_death_carry`) until it adds up to 100, so a small program's dead are neither rounded away nor missing from *Died in transit*.
  - It stores the month's figures as variables (`rs_arrived`, `rs_died`, `rs_bonus_scale` on the destination, `rs_recruits` on each source) and applies only country modifiers (politics, the Declaration).
  - **The census** (the Demographics rule's Display only or Full, 2026-10-19): each move and its transit dead also go to the census's counters at both ends, by the programme's own age and sex profile (`te_demog_rs_note_move`, `te_demog_rs_note_dead`). The transit dead are known kills at the source (`te_demog_note_kills`). Each state's yearly census step books them and takes them out of its migration residual (§ Demographics, Internal Resettlement in the census, and the US frontier).
- **State pulse** (`resettlement_state_monthly`, `on_monthly_pulse_state`, gated to states with an Authority, `rs_recruits` or leftover `rs_bonus_scale`): applies every state- and building-scoped `add_modifier { multiplier = var:… }`, because the multiplier resolves against ROOT (`scripting_best_practices.md` § "`add_modifier { multiplier = var:X }` Resolves Against ROOT") and ROOT in the country pulse is the country.
  - Readouts: *Settlers arrived* and *Died in transit* on the building (read from the state's `rs_arrived` / `rs_died` as `root.var:`, since a building has no variables), *Recruited for resettlement* on each source (removed with `rs_recruits` after a month with none). If the state pulse runs first in a month they show the month before, as their "last month" labels say.
  - Destination bonuses (above), and their clean-up once the Authority is gone.
- **Consequences:**
  - Source unrest (Special Settlements, Rustication).
  - Land pressure on pops below second-class acceptance at the destination: only ever a cost.
  - One political modifier per program, `resettlement_<program>_politics`, scaled by a volume counter that decays 11/12 a month, never per building.
  - `resettlement_declaration_violation` for parties to the UN Declaration running coercive programs, announced by `resettlement.20`.
- **Events:** `resettlement.1`–`.8` rolled at most every 18 months; `.9` on closure; `.20` the Declaration warning.
- **Game rule:** `internal_resettlement_rule` (enabled / AI voluntary only / disabled).
- **Homelands:** left emergent. Settlers shift culture shares at the destination, which may eventually create or remove a homeland under the homeland system's own gates and timer. Nothing here special-cases it.
- **Adding a program:** add a row to `PROGRAMMES` in `test_resettlement_programme_registry.py` and follow the failures: the PM and its loc, the group list, the code switch, the eligibility branch, the month counter, the voluntary or coercive trigger, the politics modifier, the destination modifier (`DESTINATION_EFFECTS`) and this section.
- **Debug:** `event te_debug_resettlement.1`; `TE_RESETTLEMENT:` lines in `debug.log`. The census's counters: `event te_debug_demog.120` (`TE_DEMOG_RESETTLE` lines).

## Population Transfer (Treaty Article)

- **Purpose:** Moves minority pops of the target country's primary culture(s) from the source country to the target country. Models forced population exchanges (e.g., Treaty of Lausanne).
- **Article type:** Directed, one-time effect on entry into force.
- **Gating (`possible`, ROOT = the conceding source):** the source hosts pops of a primary culture of the target that is not also primary to the source, at least one of those cultures is below Acceptance Status 4 in some source state (the effect still moves every pop of those cultures, however well accepted), and the demanding target does not have Universal Citizenship (`law_multicultural`).
- **Mechanism:**
  1. `on_entry_into_force` saves scopes via `scope:article_options.source_country` / `.target_country` (critical scoping pattern — see `docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md`).
  2. Calls `population_transfer_effect` scripted effect.
  3. The effect iterates the target country's primary cultures and moves **every** matching pop in every source-country state (pops whose culture is also primary in the source stay). Each pop goes to a random target-country state, weighted by population (tiered `weight` modifiers, since `state_population` is a trigger, not a value).
  4. Uses `move_pop` to transfer pops, adding each pop's size to `var:pop_transfer_count` on both countries.
  5. Applies `population_transfer_disruption` to **both countries** as a 5-year decaying country modifier. Its multiplier is the share of that country's population moved (`te_pop_transfer_disruption_strength` = `pop_transfer_count / total_population`, clamped to 0.25–2, so even a small transfer costs at least a quarter of the full effect), precomputed into `var:pop_transfer_disruption_strength` because an inline multiplier would be re-evaluated on every decay tick.
- **AI floor:** the AI demands the article only when it would move at least `population_transfer_ai_min_pops` (100,000) people, as counted by `population_transfer_number` (the same pops the effect moves). `proposal_weight` is 0 below it, which keeps the AI from asking for it in a treaty, and `wargoal_score_multiplier` is 0, which keeps it from pressing it as an enforce war goal in a play. Both bind `scope:source_country` / `scope:target_country`; `evaluation_chance` has root only, so the check can't sit there. Players are not bound, and the acceptance scores are unchanged.
- **Key scripted effect:** `population_transfer_effect` in `common/scripted_effects/extra_effects.txt`.
- **Key modifier:** `population_transfer_disruption` (country scope) — at multiplier 1: `country_bureaucracy_mult` −50%, `country_legitimacy_base_add` −10, `country_radicals_from_conquest_mult` +50%. There is no per-state throughput or mortality effect.
- **Follow-up event:** while the disruption lasts, `treaty_article_events.10` (weight 10 in `treaty_article_events_on_actions.txt`) offers resettlement support (`population_transfer_aftermath_modifier`) or neglect (`population_transfer_neglect_modifier`).
- **Files:** `common/treaty_articles/extra_treaty_articles.txt`, `common/scripted_effects/extra_effects.txt`, `common/script_values/extra_script_values.txt` (`te_pop_transfer_disruption_strength`), `common/static_modifiers/extra_modifiers.txt`, `events/treaty_article_events.txt`, localization.
