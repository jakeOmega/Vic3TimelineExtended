# AI Weights and Company AI

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## AI Weights Guidelines

- **Journal entries:** `weight` determines priority in the JE list. Global issues use 1000+, personal/national issues use 10.
- **Scripted buttons:** `ai_chance = { value = 0 ... }` — base value with conditional `add` modifiers.
- **Diplomatic actions:** Key fields: `evaluation_chance`, `will_propose`, `propose_score`, `will_break`.
- **Treaty articles:** Key fields: `article_ai_usage = { offer request none }`, `evaluation_chance`, `inherent_accept_score`.
- Always look at **vanilla files** for AI pattern reference.

## Company AI

The engine picks one company at a time for the AI to work toward: among the types whose `ai_will_do` holds (and at least `attainable`), weighted by `ai_weight` (engine default 1) times its `NAI` `COMPANY_TYPE_*` factors (stickiness ×3 for 60 months, a formable bonus, predicted productivity, competition with companies already held). While planning it builds toward the type's `ai_construction_targets`. Numbers live in `common/script_values/te_company_ai_values.txt`.

- **Weights.** Vanilla gives its flavored companies 3 and generic ones nothing (1). The mod's 84 flavored companies get `te_company_ai_weight_flavored` (10), plus `te_company_ai_weight_home` (10) while the country holds one of the company's `preferred_headquarters` states; its 11 new generic companies get `te_company_ai_weight_generic` (3). `REPLACE:`/`INJECT:` entries are vanilla's companies and keep vanilla's weight.
- **Construction targets need a `state_trigger`.** Vanilla's `companies.md`: a target with an empty state trigger does nothing. Every mod target now names the company's preferred HQ states, the capital, or a state that already has the building (that last one covers mines and rigs whose resource the capital lacks). `test_company_ai.py` fails on a target without one, a mod flavored company without `ai_weight`, or a buildable flagship without `ai_value`.
- **Flagships** (`common/buildings/company_buildings.txt`) carry `ai_value = te_company_flagship_ai_value` (50000, vanilla's top tier). Their only max level comes from the company's prosperity bonus (on at 100, off below 75), so only a prosperous company's flagship can be built at all. Only the state's owner builds one (`te_company_flagship_builder_owns_state` in `possible` and `can_build_government`), so investment rights abroad never reach another country's flagship. Before the `ai_value`, the AI built 9% (2029 observer save) to 14% (2073 save) of its prosperous companies' flagships. If `ai_value` doesn't move that, the fallback is a decision that places the flagship (the Strategic Reserve hub and Space Program precedent).
- **Failing companies.** The engine's AI never disbands a company by choice (observer run 1972–2029: no surviving country dropped one company for another), so its slots fill before the mod's late-era companies unlock. `te_company_failing_monthly_effect` (`te_company_effects.txt`, on `on_monthly_pulse_country`) counts each company's consecutive monthly checks that fail either test (`te_company_fails_this_month`): prosperity below `te_company_failing_prosperity` (10), or owned levels (`company_owned_levels`) below `te_company_small_share` (0.1%) of the country's `building_levels`, saved as `scope:te_company_min_levels` before the iteration. The count lives in the company variable `te_company_months_failing`; an AI country removes one company a check once a count reaches `te_company_failing_months` (60, about 4.9 years), then runs the flagship cleanup. The size test catches a prosperous company too small to matter to a large economy; since the engine dissolves companies under 5 levels anyway, it only bites above 5,000 country levels. Together they stand in for "profit below 0.1% of GDP for five years", which script cannot read (no company profit trigger or link): on the 2073 save, of 406 AI companies with five years of history, 102 fell under the profit rule; prosperity alone flags 50 (41 of the 102), both tests 83 (52 of the 102, the rest earning a median 0.34% of GDP). Players are exempt; it models the AI choosing what a player chooses in the companies panel.
- **Flagship subsidies.** The engine's AI almost never changes a subsidy itself. It keeps the ones a building gets by default (ports, railways, power plants) and adds none: on the 2073 save it subsidized 0 of its 23 company buildings (the player 20 of 20), 0 of 140 shipyards, and 0 of 250 airports and highways outside the laws that force them. 12 of the 23 were under half staffed, some losing workers to subsidized buildings nearby, since a building that is not subsidized stops hiring below `BUILDING_DEFAULT_MIN_EARNINGS_TO_HIRE_EMPLOYEES`. `te_company_ai_subsidy_monthly_effect` (`te_company_effects.txt`, same monthly on-action) subsidizes every `bg_company_buildings` building of an AI country that is not in default, and removes those subsidies in default, because the AI never drops one on its own (in default it still paid for 60 of 61 railways). A subsidy only tops a building up to `SUBSIDIES_MIN_GAIN` (0), so a profitable flagship costs nothing. Players are exempt, as for dissolution. The mod's `country_building_*_require_subsidies_bool` forced-subsidy modifiers (airports and highways under Interventionism and Command Economy) held on only 35 of 52 such AI buildings in that save, so this uses `set_subsidized` on each building instead.
- **Firms as actors.** Behind its default-off rule, Private Actors makes every company a firm with a goal, currencies and a yearly turn, AI countries' and players' alike; an AI country's posture toward each firm is derived each turn (spec § 12.1). It reads `te_company_months_failing` for a firm's condition and Survival, and changes nothing here. See [Private Actors](private_actors.md).
- **Verifying in an observer run:** company counts per country and companies lost while the country survived, from saves (the 2026-10-06 study's decoders: company records key `company_type` 0x5c80, prosperity 14193, `prosperous` 24210), and flagship counts with `scripts/analysis/save_world_report.py buildings`. Flagship subsidies: in a plain-text save each building record under `building_manager` carries `subsidized=yes` when subsidized; an AI flagship should have it unless its country is `in_default`.

## Law-Change Cadence

AI countries start law changes about as often as a player would: an AI with a law it wants seldom waits more than a few months after finishing one enactment before starting the next (2026-11-15). Before, a conservative AI waited about four years, which left most governments enacting little of what their movements asked for; a Workshop player reported that the reformist side could hardly win vanilla's Reform or Revolution entry. The owner ruled that frequent AI law changes are fine ("even if the AI is enacting laws 100% of the time it is probably fine", 2026-11-15), so the rate is sized for rarely waiting. The aversion to civil-war laws rises with it, so that more law changes don't bring more civil wars. The raised rate is in each political agenda's own `change_law_chance` since 2026-11-16. From 2026-11-15 it was in the default strategy's field, and an unattended run read no rise there (below, "The first read"); a run with it in the agendas' fields read the rise ("The second read").

**Files.** `common/ai_strategies/edited_political_strategies.txt`: every political agenda's `change_law_chance` is vanilla's plus 39, and seven agendas' `revolution_aversion` is raised. `common/ai_strategies/edited_default_strategy.txt`: `ai_strategy_default`'s `change_law_chance` is vanilla's 1, times 10 in the mod's crisis case (a movement with over 20% support at over 0.75 radicalism), as before 2026-11-15. `test_ai_law_cadence.py` pins both, holds their restated vanilla lines to the game's files, and holds the outcome table below to the script. In-game checks: `docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md` § AI law cadence.

**How the engine runs it.**
- **One roll every 50 days.** Each country has a law update every 1,200 hours (50 days) at a phase of its own. At it the AI starts changing a law with chance `change_law_chance` (1 = 1%). This is measured, not documented: across the saves of one game, every pair of one country's enactment starts is a whole number of 50-day periods apart (99% of the pairs among 155 starts of 46 countries in eleven saves, 1895–1958; 94% among 76 starts of 24 countries in six saves of a second game), and the greatest common divisor of one country's start-to-start gaps is 1,200 hours for most of them. A 100-day period fits only 44–46% of the pairs. `scripting_best_practices.md` § The AI Rolls `change_law_chance` Every 50 Days.
- **The chance is the political strategy's own, plus the default strategy's if vanilla's comment holds (unread in game).** Vanilla's comment on the default's field: "Using this value in other strategies will function additively". Every political agenda declares its own field. Vanilla 1.15.1's agendas: 1 (Mandate of Heaven), 2.5 (conservative; +7.5 with an insurrectionary interest group), 3.5 (reactionary; +1.5), 10 (progressive, nationalist, consolidation; +10, +10, +5), 15 (egalitarian; +5), 20 (confrontation; +5), 50 (Meiji Restoration) and 100 (Tanzimat, Great Reforms), plus 25 under revolutionary socialist laws and 10–25 under a law commitment. The agendas' own values do count: the saves of the mod before read progressives mid-enactment 42% of the time and conservatives 19%, which a 1% rate shared by all could not give (at most 16%). Whether the default's 1 adds to them is what the first read could not tell (cause (b), below), so the rise is now in the agenda's field, which counts either way.
- **Which law.** A successful roll weighs the laws the country could start: +100 for each ideology of an interest group the strategy favours that wants it, −25 for each of one it opposes, 1,000 × support × radicalism for each movement that wants it, plus the law's `ai_enact_weight_modifier`, with randomness (`NAI|CHANGE_LAW_*`). `min_law_chance_to_pass` drops a law whose base chance to pass is below it (the agenda's value replaces the default's 50: 10 to 20, often halved when the AI has reasons not to oppose the law), and `max_progressiveness` and `max_regressiveness` cap how far it moves. A country enacts one law at a time.
- **Civil-war laws.** A movement that would defend the current law at 0.70 radicalism or more makes the change a possible civil war (`CHANGE_LAW_POTENTIAL_CIVIL_WAR_THRESHOLD`). The defines name two gates: at the start the AI goes ahead with chance 100 − `revolution_aversion` and remembers a refusal for 24 months; during the enactment it cancels once its aversion, times 1 + 0.005 × the revolutionary movement's support (× 5 for a subject), beats a boldness drawn from 0–100 for that law and kept 24 months. `revolution_aversion` is per agenda (its value replaces the default's 5). It has a third use: `CHANGE_LAW_CIVIL_WAR_AVERSION_LAW_DIRECTION_FLEXIBILITY` (2) adds twice the aversion to the `max_progressiveness` or `max_regressiveness` the AI accepts "to avoid a revolution", so a conservative AI facing one takes a law up to 200 progressiveness away under vanilla's 75. Vanilla's comment on the default's field gives a fourth reading, "Chance each day that the AI will stop enacting a law that is going to spark a civil war (1 aversion should translate into ~25% chance of avoiding a civil war)"; the defines' two gates are the more specific account, and the comment's daily chance is not used here.

**The table.** Expected wait from the end of one enactment to the start of the next, for an AI with a law it can start: half a period to the next roll, then 50 days per failed roll, so 25 + 50 × (1 − p) / p days. Start to start adds the enactment itself, about 15 months (below). "Vanilla and mod before" counts the default's 1 as adding, as vanilla's comment says; "mod before" is the mod before 2026-11-15, whose default field was vanilla's 1 in normal times and 10 in its crisis case. "New" is the agenda's vanilla value plus 39; if the default's 1 adds, each chance is one point higher and no wait moves by more than 0.1 month. "New, crisis" holds only if the default's field adds: its crisis branch makes that 1 a 10.

The new rate stands on the normal-times column alone. From 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-16, with the 39 in the default's field, the crisis branch multiplied 40 and made a start certain; with the 39 in the agendas it adds 9 points at most, and nothing if the default's field doesn't add. It was left in place, not ported into the agendas: at a 41–60% chance a certain start only shortens a two-to-three-month wait to under a month. It will fire less often if #1824 merges, which puts `MOVEMENT_RADICALISM_FROM_ACTIVE_LAW_STANCE` back from the mod's −0.1 to vanilla's −0.05: under −0.1, a 1907 observer save had 17 of 70 socialist and labor movements at 0.75 radicalism or more. The saves below were read under −0.1. Vanilla's own insurrection case (an insurrectionary interest group) takes a conservative AI to 11% (14 months) and a progressive or nationalist one to 21% (7 months); under the new rate it adds the same points to a chance already near half.

**What the saves show for the mod before.** In eleven saves of four games (1864–2020, `scripts/analysis/ai_law_cadence_report.py`), 22–31% of the AI countries that change laws at all were in the middle of an enactment; decentralized nations never do (121 of the 123 AI countries in an 1864 save that had changed none). By agenda, pooled: conservative 19% (of 1,247), reactionary 21% (347), egalitarian 38% (226), progressive 42% (106), nationalist 43% (450). Conservatives are most AI countries (163 of 210 in 1864, 96 of 250 in 1958), so they set the world's pace. Enactments that passed between two saves took a median of 17 months (13 months to 5 years); that sample favours long ones, so the table uses 15. With 15 months, the wait formula predicts 24% for conservatives and 51% for progressives and nationalists if every AI always had a law to start, against 19% and 42–43% read: the model fits, and roughly a fifth of the time an AI has nothing it would start. Counted over every AI country that is not decentralized, as the self-check counts (countries that never changed a law stay in; `ai_law_cadence_report.py`'s "self-check count" line), 20 saves of six games read conservatives at 0.00–0.26 and the rest at 0.21–0.47; early saves read lowest (all AI countries 0.11 in 1849 and 0.12 in 1867, when few have a law to start), and from 1890 conservatives read 0.06–0.26 and the rest 0.26–0.43.

**The rate: +39 in each agenda's own field.** A roll at 41–60% puts the wait at two to three months, a sixth of an enactment's length, so an AI with something to enact is enacting about five sixths of the time. Doubling it to certain would shorten the wait to under a month and add about 15% more starts, at the cost of the agendas' spread and of the crisis case meaning anything. Each of the eleven agendas' fields restates vanilla's whole block and adds 39 on its own line; the outcome table below gives what that reads under each thing the engine may do with it. It lifts Mandate of Heaven, which vanilla holds at 1 on purpose, to 40% (fine, by the owner's ruling as the wave's coordinator relayed it, 2026-11-15); that strategy still starts only laws with at least a 50% chance to pass (10% when it has reasons not to oppose one), moves at most 25 progressiveness and 2 regressiveness, and its aversion stays 100. The Tanzimat and the Great Reforms are certain at vanilla's 100 already: their 39 changes nothing and keeps one rule for all eleven.

**Where the 39 goes, and the default's field.** In each agenda's own field the rise counts whether or not the default's adds, so the next run tells (b) from the rest and carries the fix if it was (b). The default's `add = 39` was deleted rather than kept beside the agendas': if the default's field does add, the two would make the chance vanilla's + 79, certain for most agendas, where without it the chance is vanilla's + 39, or + 40 if the default adds, the 41–60% the rate is sized for either way. Keeping it would help only if the engine read the default's field alone and ignored the agendas', which the agendas' spread above rules out. The field is now what it was before 2026-11-15: vanilla's `value = 1`, restated because the crisis branch multiplies it, and that branch.

**The second read (2026-11-16): in force.** An unattended game from 1836 to 1850 on a test build with the 39 in the agendas' fields (`bdc33ca56`) read conservatives at 0.0668 law starts per idle month among the 20 largest AI economies (the 20 largest at January 1850, each by its last report) and 0.0501 over every AI country, against 0.0105 and 0.0135 in the first read's game and 0.0198 and 0.0115 in a game on `main` at `81b947368`, over the same months; every agenda over every AI country, 0.0569 against 0.0155 and 0.0138. Only this change touched the AI strategies between the last two builds, so the agendas' INJECT reaches their field, and the first read was most likely cause (b). The rate is still a fifth to a quarter of what an AI with a law always to start would show (0.28–0.29 for conservatives). It was highest in the first years (among each decade's 20 largest, 0.082 before 1843 and 0.0475 from it) and fell after, most likely as countries passed the laws they wanted: cause (c), not the rate, now binds. Of the 1,395 enactments that ended, 248 were cancelled (18%), Primogeniture 116 of them. The self-check did not read on this build, since the run ended in 1850; it read OK on `main` at `2d26c6b5a`, in run 1's world played on from its 1925 save: `cons=55/75=0.73 rest=77/113=0.68` on January 1, 1928, 36 months after the load, against at most 0.26 from 1890 on the build before. **What the fields read, by what the engine does with an INJECT.** The engine may replace vanilla's field with the INJECTed one (an interest group's script-value field, read 2026-10-10), merge in only the keys vanilla's lacks (an AI strategy's `subsidies` block, read 2026-10-16), sum the two (as it sums modifier blocks), or ignore the INJECT. Each INJECT restates vanilla's block and adds the rise on its own `add` line, and `add` is the one top-level key vanilla's fields lack, so a replace and a merge both read vanilla's plus the rise. Appending the INJECT's lines after vanilla's, as a script value's steps would be, reads the same, since the restated `value` line resets the total. A sum counts vanilla's lines twice; an ignored INJECT leaves vanilla's. A sum overshoots the chance by vanilla's own value, at most 20 points on a common agenda (confrontation 79), which costs little: one law at a time caps the starts, and even a certain roll adds only about 15% more of them. A sum's risk is the aversion: at 110 to 160 four agendas never pass a law a radical movement defends (below). The rate and the aversion are now INJECTed into the same strategy, so whatever the engine does with one it most likely does with the other.

**The pass-chance floor: kept.** Progressive and nationalist agendas roll at the same 11% and differ in floor, 10 against 20, and the saves read 42% and 43% of each mid-enactment: no sign the floor binds. With the rate no longer the bottleneck, a lower floor would add attempts at laws below a 20% chance, most of which fail and lock that law for two years (`LAW_ENACTMENT_COOLDOWN_DAYS`). If conservatives stay well under their predicted share after the change (below), the floor or `max_progressiveness` is what binds, and the play-test list says so.

**Revolution aversion: raised in seven agendas.** A faster AI meets each civil-war law more often within the 24 months its boldness and its refusal last, so its yearly exposure rises by the rise in enactment starts per 24 months. That rise is not the chance's ratio, because one law at a time caps the starts: a conservative AI goes from 0.39 starts per 24 months to 1.35. The factor lies between the start ratio (when the risky law is a rare pick) and the ratio of the chances of at least one start in 24 months (when it is the first pick). With both gates counted, the chance to go ahead and not cancel is (1 − aversion / 100)², so the new aversion is 100 − (100 − vanilla) / √factor, at the band's geometric middle (the "middle" column, unrounded), rounded to 5. With one gate the chance is 1 − aversion / 100 and the aversion that holds exposure at vanilla's is 100 − (100 − vanilla) / factor, the last column:

With the rise in the agendas' fields that is the chance if the default's field adds; if it doesn't, the chance is a point lower, and the factors fall by under half a percent (conservative 2.775 against 2.786). The aversions still match: every two-gate value rounds to the same 5 except the reactionary one, which sits on the edge (67.5 at + 40, 67.4 at + 39) and stays 70, on the side of fewer civil wars (×0.85). The one-gate values at + 39 are 91.0, 78.7, 66.4, 56.4, 42.7, 83.2 and 38.4, so the play-test's one-gate fallback (91, 79, 67, 57, 43, 83, 39) still holds exposure at or below vanilla's.

Mandate of Heaven (aversion 100: it never goes ahead), the Meiji Restoration (its starts rise by 6%), the Tanzimat and the Great Reforms (already certain) keep vanilla's. Each INJECT restates vanilla's whole block, conditionals and `min` included, and adds the rise as one `add` line, for the same two readings as the rate. If the engine instead summed the two fields, aversion would come out 160 for conservatives, 155 for consolidation, 120 for reactionaries and 110 for progressives: those AIs would never pass a law that a radical movement (0.70 or more) defends, which blocks contested reforms outright, against what the change is for, and the self-check would still read OK. It is unlikely (an interest group's field is replaced, not summed); the play-test's civil-war row watches for it as "far fewer, near none". The flexibility define above widens the concessions an AI accepts to avoid a revolution by twice each rise, 10 to 40 points (a reactionary facing one goes from 125 to 165 progressiveness, a conservative from 200 to 220, against laws that span −100 to 200): small, and in the direction the change wants, so it changes no value here.

**Systems that wait for a gap between enactments.** Four of the mod's systems act only while a country is not enacting a law: the cultural-hegemony law-pressure event (`cultural_hegemony.16`), the judiciary's events 35 and 36, and its monthly court challenge (`te_jud_challenge_monthly`, which takes a forced law to the court once the enactment is over). An AI enacting most of the time gives them fewer windows; a player's are unchanged. One edge, read from the script and not run: `te_jud_open_challenge` keeps one pending case in `te_jud_challenge_law`, so if a second forced law starts before an idle month comes, it overwrites the first, and the first escapes review. That needs two forced laws inside the capacity gate, so it is rare and AI-only; nothing is changed for it.

**What is unread.**
- Whether an INJECT reaches an AI strategy's script-value field at all: the second read (above) says it reaches a political agenda's `change_law_chance`, by its effect; whether by replacing vanilla's field or by merging is unread. The first read can't say, and the one read on a strategy, `subsidies`, kept vanilla's value (`scripting_best_practices.md`). No trigger or script value reads a strategy's value (`has_strategy` says only which strategy a country holds), so the self-check `TE_SELF_CHECK ai_law_cadence` reads the effect: in 1890 or later, the share of AI conservatives mid-enactment, and of the rest. Its OK, not yet read on this build, would confirm the second read. The build before read conservatives at 0.06–0.26 from 1890 (20 saves of six games, counted as the check counts), and OK is 0.40. But a working change can read as low as 0.23 if, after each enactment, an AI goes a long stretch with no law it would start. With the rise in the agendas' own fields a second BROKEN has two causes: the agendas' INJECT is ignored, or nothing to start. The faster read is the probe's law starts per idle month by agenda over ten game years; the play-test row gives the figures and says how to tell the causes apart.
- Whether the default's field adds to an agenda's own. With the rise in the agendas it moves the chance by a point, so it no longer matters to the rate; only the crisis branch rests on it.
- The 50-day period is read from saves, not from a define or the documentation.
- Whether the two civil-war gates compound or share one boldness draw. If they share one, exposure is the "one gate" column: up to two thirds above vanilla for conservatives.
- The enactment length, which moves the factors little: at 10 or 20 months the conservative factor's middle is 3.2 or 2.5.

**With the third-party AI mod that a Workshop player runs alongside** (its files: `kai_political_strategies.txt`, a whole-file copy of `00_default_strategy.txt`; from the 2026-11-15 study of it, not from its files). It INJECTs `change_law_chance`, `min_law_chance_to_pass` and `revolution_aversion` into the five common agendas, the Tanzimat and the Great Reforms, and REPLACEs the Meiji Restoration. Its copy of the default strategy keeps vanilla's `change_law_chance = { value = 1 }`, and this mod's INJECT lands on that copy, which since 2026-11-16 adds nothing to it outside the crisis branch. On `change_law_chance` and `revolution_aversion` both mods now INJECT the same fields. INJECTs apply in file order and, by the interest-group read, the later one wins; `edited_political_strategies.txt` sorts before `kai_political_strategies.txt`, so in those seven agendas that mod's rates and aversions stand together, as it sized them, and this mod's 39 and aversions land only on Mandate of Heaven and the consolidation and confrontation agendas it doesn't touch. The Meiji Restoration is that mod's REPLACE, which drops this mod's INJECT there. Its floors stand, since this mod sets none. That two INJECTs of one strategy field resolve this way is unread; if the earlier one wins instead, this mod's rates and aversions apply in place of that mod's, the pair this mod sized together. From 2026-11-15 to 2026-11-16 this mod's 39 sat in the default and landed on that mod's agenda rates as well.

## AI Coverage of the Mod's Content

The engine's AI learns about content from fields on that content: a goods stance in a strategy, an `ai_value` on a building, an `ai_enact_weight_modifier` on a law, a movement list in a political strategy, an `ai_weight` on a mobilization option. Content with none of them is not broken; the AI treats it as it treats a plain factory or a law nobody asked for. #1822 listed where the mod's own content had none while vanilla's equivalent has one (2026-11-15). Each part below says what was added, what vanilla value it stands on, and what was left alone and why. A review the same day set a rule over all of it: AI governments are mostly insolvent and queue one government construction at a time (the 2026-11-15 study of the owner's saves found 209 of 239 AI countries with no university in a 1958 save, AI power plants 43% staffed and infrastructure 39%, and Electricity selling under its base price), so a value that pulls that one slot toward something that does not serve growth or technology is a step back even where it matches vanilla. Sizes are named script values in `common/script_values/te_ai_coverage_values.txt`, for tuning after an observer run; the in-game checks are in `docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md` § AI coverage of the mod's content.

**Goods stances** (`common/ai_strategies/edited_default_strategy.txt`; `test_ai_goods_stances.py`). A stance tells the AI it wants a good in high supply, wants to export it or does not want it. A wanted or exported good is "favored": the AI adds to the score of a building or a production method that makes it (`NAI` `PRODUCTION_BUILDING_FAVORED_GOODS_FACTOR` 0.5 and `PRODUCTION_METHOD_FAVORED_GOODS_FACTOR` 0.25 per pound of it), and a high-supply good also counts for more when its price is high and when the AI weighs a subsidy. Five of the mod's goods had no stance, and one input of the later construction tiers had none. Five entries were added, all `wants_high_supply` in the default strategy:

| Good | From (technology) | Why that gate |
|---|---|---|
| `tech_metals` | `personal_appliances` | the Consumer Appliances Industry's base method is their first user |
| `consumer_appliances` | `integrated_circuits` | Integrated Circuits is their first heavy industrial user (20 a level; 37 methods use them in all); before, they are a household good |
| `motor_ships` (countries with a port at major power or above) | `advanced_submarine_technology`, until `modern_material_science` | vanilla's `steamers` stance one generation on, retired as vanilla retires clippers |
| `magnetic_drive_ships` (the same countries) | `modern_material_science` | the next generation |
| `automobiles` | `prefabricated_construction` | Advanced Construction uses 10 a level |

- **`grid_power` and `tourism` have no stance on purpose** (the test's `EXEMPT` list holds both with the reason). Grid Power is a fixed-price settlement good that can't be traded between markets, so nothing a stance does could move it. Tourism is a household luxury no building uses, and vanilla's default strategy names no household luxury; a stance would favor the Tourism Industry, a building of the arts group, where investors already queue more than they staff (491 art academies queued at 37.5% staffing in the 1958 save).
- **No export stances.** The first version gave the industrial strategy an export stance for Consumer Appliances and the agricultural and plantation strategies one for tourism, in vanilla's style. Both were taken out in review: a strategy's stance replaces the default's, so the industrial one would have taken the high-supply stance from the countries that use appliances most as an input, and the tourism one has the fault above. The mod now gives no vanilla administrative strategy a goods stance (the test holds that), which also means nothing here is lost when another AI mod REPLACEs those strategies.
- **The other inputs of the later construction tiers already had stances.** Reinforced Concrete, Advanced Construction, Nanomaterial and Self-Replicating Construction add engines, automobiles, digital assets, advanced materials and robotics to vanilla's tiers and use more copper and electricity. Vanilla's default strategy wants copper and electricity from `electrical_generation` and engines from `railways`; the mod's wanted digital assets, robotics and advanced materials already, each gated on a technology the tier's own technology needs first (the test walks the prerequisites). Steel, glass, explosives, tools and transportation have no default stance in vanilla either, though vanilla's own construction tiers use them.
- **Vanilla's `steamers` stance can't be retired.** Motor ships replace steamers in ports and fishing fleets, and vanilla's stance (major powers with a port, from `ironclad_tech` and `gantry_cranes`) never ends. An INJECT into the default strategy can't change a key vanilla's block already holds (read for `subsidies`, 2026-10-16), and the executable holds four stance values (`wants_high_supply`, `wants_export`, `wants_import`, `does_not_want`), none of which means "no stance", so a strategy-level override could only turn steamers into a good the AI avoids. What the new stances do is end the tilt: before, a shipyard's steamer methods scored as favored and its motor-ship methods did not.
- **Unread in game:** that an INJECT adds a new key to a strategy's `goods_stances` block. It does for `subsidies`, the same shape, and the default strategy's seven older mod stances rest on the same fact. No trigger reads a stance, so there is no self-check; the play-test rows read the effect.

**Building `ai_value`** (`common/script_values/te_ai_coverage_values.txt`, `common/scripted_triggers/te_ai_coverage_triggers.txt`; `test_ai_building_values.py`). A building with no `ai_value` scores the engine's base, 1000, so a missing one is parity with a plain factory, not a fault. Vanilla gives some roles more (a power plant +4000 in a state with none, a railway 2000, a buildable monument 10000), and the first version copied those onto the mod's buildings of the same role. The review took them back to the base, for the reason in the opening paragraph and because the buildings are not short: a 2058 save (the owner's game, almost all AI) holds 495 Renewable Energy Plants, 451 Fusion Plants, 149 Hydro Plants and 129 Nuclear Plants beside 423 coal and oil plants, and 528 Highways, 464 Airports and 522 Network Infrastructure beside 580 Railways. What is left:

| Buildings | `ai_value` | Why |
|---|---|---|
| the 37 wonders, the UN Headquarters, a Power Bloc Headquarters | `te_wonder_ai_value`: the base (`te_ai_wonder_base_value`, 1000), and 0 for a country in default or under a declared bankruptcy | a wonder is 5,000 construction points and 10,000 government employees; vanilla's 10000 is for a 2,500-point monument employing 100, and would put a wonder ahead of a University (2000) and a Government Administration (1000) in the one government queue |
| the seven megaprojects' construction sites, the Antimatter Warhead Plant | `te_megaproject_ai_value`: the same shape | a site's inputs run to hundreds of millions a week, paid by the government |
| Highway, Airport, Network Infrastructure | `te_infrastructure_ai_value`: the base, under a name so it can be tuned after a run | not under-built, and AI infrastructure runs 39% staffed |
| vanilla's Power Plant (INJECT, the whole field restated) | vanilla's own 1000 + 4000 for a state's first plant, with the "first" test widened to `te_state_has_a_power_plant` | so a state that already has power draws no coal plant for the bonus |
| Hydro, Nuclear, Renewable Energy and Fusion Plants | none (the engine's base, as before) | they cost 2,000 to 10,000 points against the coal plant's 400, and power is over-built |

- **So for a solvent country nothing scores higher than it did.** The two changes in behavior are that a country in default or under a declared bankruptcy starts no wonder and no megaproject site, and that vanilla's plant loses its first-plant bonus in a state that already has power (if the INJECT takes).
- **`te_state_has_a_power_plant`** counts every building with a production method that outputs Electricity, built or under construction: vanilla's plant, the mod's four, the Solar Receiver, the Grid Transfer Station (it delivers its grid's power as Electricity) and the nine company power stations, sixteen in all. The test derives the set from the production methods and fails when a new maker is missing. One consequence to watch: an AI country queues a Grid Transfer Station wherever it makes Electricity in the market area, so in such a country few states keep the bonus; a state whose grid delivers little would then wait for the engine's own new-goods term to draw its first plant.
- **Skipped, with the reason.**
  - *The four megaprojects #1822 named* (Antimatter Containment Facility, Consciousness Network, Orbital Battlestation, Orbital Solar Collector) are never started from the queue: `potential` asks for the building itself and its only max level is the one its own method grants per level, so script places and raises them when a construction site completes (`generic_wonder_construction_base`). What the queue starts is the site. The Space Program is placed by its decision. The test's `PLACED_BY_SCRIPT` holds all five.
  - *The 13 discoverable mines.* Vanilla's iron, coal, lead and sulfur mines carry `ai_value = 1000`, which is the engine's base, and its copper mine none; only the gold mine has more (5000). The mod's mines are at parity already. What they lacked was a stance for what they dig: `tech_metals` has one now.
  - *Nothing in the UN's or the power blocs' design asks that the host or the bloc leader be pulled toward its headquarters*, so those two take the wonders' value.
- **Unread in game:** that an INJECTed `ai_value` replaces a vanilla building's (a script-value field does on an interest group and a party; a plain field does on a building). If the engine keeps vanilla's field, the coal plant still earns the bonus in a state with only another maker, as before. If it summed the two fields, the coal plant's base and bonus would double; the play-test row watches for that. And whether `ai_value = 0` keeps the construction AI from queuing a building at all is the checklist's reading (`scripting_best_practices.md` § Checklist for New Buildings/Goods), not a recorded one.

**Institution laws and the AI's bureaucracy** (`te_ai_coverage_triggers.txt`, `te_ai_coverage_values.txt`, `common/laws/extra_laws.txt`; `test_ai_institution_laws.py`). 26 of the mod's laws carry an institution: 16 ministry laws, National Bank, Central Bank Independence, Neocolonialism, Private Policing, Civic Monolingualism, Universal Basic Income, Post-Scarcity and three augmentation laws. Each level of an institution costs bureaucracy for as long as it exists (1 for each 100,000 people in incorporated states, at least 10), and a country short of bureaucracy loses tax revenue. None of the 26 weights looked at bureaucracy; the National Bank's standing +600 applied to a country already short. 22 of them now do. The other four (the three augmentation laws and Central Bank Independence) can only be enacted by a country that already has the institution, because every law that unlocks them carries it, so a check there could never fire.

- **One shared trigger.** `te_ai_can_staff_new_institution` holds while the country has the spare bureaucracy that level 1 of a new institution takes (`te_ai_new_institution_bureaucracy`: incorporated population ÷ 100,000, at least 10, read from `cached_ai_incorporated_population`, the engine's own figure for an AI country and the one vanilla's AI strategy reads) and its existing institutions are not heading for a shortage (`approaching_bureaucracy_shortage`, the engine's forecast). Vanilla's own test for a bureaucracy in order is the pair `bureaucracy >= 0`, `approaching_bureaucracy_shortage = no`; this adds the new institution's cost to the first.
- **One branch per law.** Each of the 22 `ai_enact_weight_modifier` blocks adds `te_ai_unstaffed_institution_law_weight` (−1000) while `te_ai_law_brings_unstaffed_institution = { INSTITUTION = … }` holds: the country does not yet have that institution and can't staff a new one. A country that already has the institution is untouched, so moving from Old Age Pension to Universal Basic Income, or from one Ministry of Labor to the other, weighs as before: the institution's bureaucracy is a running cost it pays already.
- **The size.** Vanilla's readme calls +1000 "very significant" beside a base weight in the low hundreds. −1000 puts every one of the 22 laws below zero on its standing terms (a ministry's +10 to +100, the National Bank's +600, the Ministry of the Environment's +500) and below a movement's push. Two laws can still come out ahead: the National Bank, whose rank, debt, economic-system and bloc terms add up to +2450, and the Ministry of the Environment with its +750 branch (+1250). Every other law's terms sum to 800 at most (Neocolonialism and Private Policing 760), so for those the check is in effect a ban while it holds.
- **The Ministry of the Environment is one of the 22.** A country short of bureaucracy sees its standing +500 become −500. The check stays on it, since an unstaffed ministry does nothing, but the climate AI's will, both fossil laws and the Environmental Movement's activism all read that ministry (`global_warming.md`), so fewer AI ministries would move the climate system. The play-test row counts them against warming at 2015; taking the branch off `law_ministry_of_the_environment` is the owner's call, as for the National Bank.
- **Unread:** that `cached_ai_incorporated_population` is filled when a law's weight is weighed (vanilla reads it in its AI strategy's values, not in a law). If it read 0 there, the check would ask only for the minimum of 10 spare bureaucracy, which fails open: the forecast test still applies.
- **How much headroom that is.** A country's people cost 1 bureaucracy for each 25,000 in incorporated states, four times what one institution level costs, so a country with L institution levels uses at least (4 + L) times the new level's cost. It passes with spare bureaucracy of about 1 ÷ (4 + L) of its usage: a fifth for a country with one level, 4% for one with twenty. The engine's AI builds administrations until produced bureaucracy is 1.2 times usage and stops at 1.3 (`MONEY_SPENDING_BUREAUCRACY_DESIRED_THRESHOLD`, `…_EXCESSIVE_THRESHOLD`), so a country that keeps up with its own target passes and one that can't afford to does not, hardest of all for its first institutions. Whether that delays the National Bank across the world is a play-test row.
- **It does not slow law changes.** The AI picks among the laws it could start; a law weighed down leaves the others. The owner's ruling that frequent AI law changes are fine stands (§ Law-Change Cadence).
- **Left out:** vanilla's Migration Controls, which the mod gives an institution by INJECT. That institution costs a tenth of a normal one, and the check would mean restating a vanilla law's weight by INJECT (unread, and another AI mod INJECTs vanilla laws' weights too).

**The AI's institution-spending defines: left at vanilla's, with the arithmetic.** Vanilla's `NAI` block caps what the AI puts into institutions: `MAX_INSTITUTION_SPENDING_BASE` 0.015 ("AI will not spend more BUR than this on institutions (scaled by weekly GDP)") plus `MAX_INSTITUTION_SPENDING_PER_INSTITUTION` 0.005 "for each institution a country has". The AI raises an institution while spending stays at or under the ceiling (`INSTITUTION_SPENDING_INCREASE_SPENDING_RATIO` 1.0) and lowers one only at twice the ceiling (`…_DECREASE_SPENDING_RATIO` 2.0). The mod has 24 institutions to vanilla's 7 and raised `MAX_INSTITUTION_INVESTMENT` from 5 to 9, and set none of these.

| `…_PER_INSTITUTION` | Ceiling at 7 institutions | at 12 | at 17 | at 24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.005 (vanilla, in force) | 5.0% | 7.5% | 10.0% | 13.5% |
| 0.003 | 3.6% | 5.1% | 6.6% | 8.7% |
| 0.002 | 2.9% | 3.9% | 4.9% | 6.3% |
| 0.0015 | 2.6% | 3.3% | 4.1% | 5.1% |

The figures are shares of weekly GDP; the AI starts cutting at twice each (10% at vanilla's 7, 27% at 24). So the ceiling already grows with the number of institutions: it was not sized for seven in a way that breaks at 24. What changes is the total: a country holding all 24 may put 2.7 times as much into institutions as a vanilla country holding all 7, and cuts only at 27% of GDP. Holding a 24-institution country to vanilla's 5.0% would take 0.00146 per institution.

- **What the ceiling is a share of is not in any file.** Read as bureaucracy points per pound of weekly GDP it never binds, in vanilla or here (a country of 14 million people with £12M of GDP a year, roughly Prussia at the start, would be allowed about 11,500 points against about 4,900 for every vanilla institution at its top level), so the define would do nothing. Read as money (the cost of the bureaucracy the institutions use, as a share of weekly GDP) it binds where vanilla's AI is seen to hold back: the institution levels a country can afford come to 1,923 × ceiling × GDP per head ÷ pounds per bureaucracy point, about 6 of 35 levels for that country at £15 a point (the example's figures are round numbers, not read from a save). The money reading is the one that fits, and is inferred.
- **In money, on that reading:** an AI country with all 24 institutions may spend up to 13.5% of its GDP on the bureaucracy behind them, out of a government income that is itself only a part of GDP (not measured here). With weekly GDP of £50M that is £6.75M a week against £2.5M under vanilla's seven, and no cut before £13.5M.
- **In bureaucracy:** for a poor late-game country (£5 of GDP a head) the ceiling buys about 32 institution levels at £40 a point and 87 at £15, of up to 216 (24 × 9); at vanilla's seven institutions it would buy 12 and 32 of 35. For a rich one (£50 a head) it buys more levels than exist in both cases, and only produced bureaucracy limits it. A point's cost is the unknown: a Government Administration level on Internet Communications makes 220 points for £2,640 of goods a week (£12 a point) plus the wages of 500 to 750 staff, which no file gives.
- **Why nothing was changed.** The case rests on the money reading and on what AI countries actually spend, and neither has been measured. The October observer run had `declared_bankruptcy` on 126 of 179 AI countries by 2029 with institution levels falling alongside, and nobody has shown the cause. A lower ceiling also cuts the levels of the institutions the mod wants the AI to fund (the National Bank and the Ministry of the Environment score 100). The next observer run should read, for each AI country, its institutions and their levels, bureaucracy produced and used, and its Government Administrations' cost against GDP (the play-test row). If countries sit near the ceiling and those near it are the bankrupt ones, 0.003 is the first step (8.7% at 24 institutions, vanilla's figure for about fourteen).

**Political agendas and the mod's movements** (`common/ai_strategies/te_ai_movement_strategies.txt`; `test_ai_movement_lists.py`). Each vanilla political agenda lists the movements its government sides with (`pro_movements`) and against (`anti_movements`). None named `movement_environmental`, `movement_civil_rights`, `movement_anti_war` or `movement_transhumanist`, so every AI government was indifferent to all four. What a list does is not documented anywhere; a government can bolster and suppress movements, and the lists are the only place a strategy names one.

- **Placement is by what each movement wants, not by its name.** The movements have no description of their own, so the reading is each movement's ideology (`extra_ideologies.txt`): the laws it approves and disapproves, set against the stances of the interest groups each agenda favors and opposes. For every law the movement's ideology has a view on, multiply its stance (−2 to +2) by each base ideology's stance of the agenda's favored groups, sum, and subtract the same sum over the groups the agenda opposes. `scripts/analysis/ai_agenda_movement_alignment.py` computes it and prints the table below; with `--calibrate` it shows that the same sum puts vanilla's own movements on the side vanilla lists them in 90 cases of 98 (the misses are the three national movements under the nationalist agenda, anarchists and nihilists under the progressive one, religious minorities twice, and utilitarians under the conservative one). The test holds each shipped entry to the score's sign.

| Movement, and what its ideology wants | Conservative | Reactionary | Progressive | Egalitarian | Nationalist |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `movement_civil_rights`: multicultural citizenship, minority protection and affirmative action, devolution, strong courts; against ethnostates, segregation, religious courts | held (−196) | anti (−189) | pro (+125) | pro (+48) | anti (−64) |
| `movement_anti_war`: limited war, an aid ministry, no war ministry, a militia, no colonies | held (−103) | — (−39) | — (−13) | pro (+62) | anti (−122) |
| `movement_transhumanist`: unrestricted augmentation, open-source patents, algorithmic government, automation, a basic income | held (−187) | anti (−260) | pro (+206) | — (−10) | — (−15) |
| `movement_environmental`: an environment ministry, a fossil phase-out; against laissez-faire, extraction economies and a commerce ministry | — (−49) | — (−22) | — (−7) | pro (+57) | — (−49) |

- **A score within about 60 of zero is left off both lists**, as vanilla leaves liberals off the conservative agenda's, with one exception decided by reading: Civil Rights is pro for the egalitarian agenda (+48), where vanilla lists the feminist and anti-slavery movements. The Environmental Movement stays off the conservative and nationalist anti lists (−49 each): the climate system's AI and its calibration rest on how strong that movement grows (`global_warming.md`).
- **The conservative agenda's three anti entries are held** ("held" in the table; see the proposals below). Seven entries ship: the progressive and egalitarian pro entries and the reactionary and nationalist anti entries.
- **Legal modernization.** An anti listing works against the laws a movement asks for, and a suppressed movement can fall below what it needs to last and disband, which the mod's own systems then read (`je_colonial_empire.txt`, `civil_rights_effects.txt`, `decolonization.txt`, the movement events). The reactionary and nationalist entries follow vanilla, which lists the cultural and religious minority movements as anti for those two agendas only. If the next observer run shows minority-rights or war-law reforms stalling under those governments, drop their entries.
- **Unread in game:** what an INJECTed list does on an AI strategy (append to vanilla's list, as on production method groups and laws; replace it; or nothing), and what the lists drive. `TE_SELF_CHECK ai_movement_lists` reads the effect each year once the world holds 20 listed movements, with three of vanilla's own entries on each of the same lists as its control, and ends on one of three verdicts:
  - **OK:** three or more of the mod's listed movements bolstered or suppressed as listed, under a tenth treated the other way round, listed ones acted on more often than unlisted ones, and vanilla's entries on the same lists still acted on. The INJECT appended.
  - **REPLACED:** the same, but none of 20 or more of vanilla's entries is treated as listed. The INJECT replaced vanilla's lists, and each INJECT must then restate vanilla's list in full.
  - **BROKEN:** a tenth or more of the listed movements (and at least three) treated against their list, or none treated as listed while vanilla's entries are. The INJECT is ignored.
  - Otherwise it tries again the next year, up to 30 tries, and logs NO_VERDICT at the first and the last.
- **With the third-party AI mod**, which INJECTs other fields into the same agendas, the lists merge; it REPLACEs none of them.

**Proposals left for the owner (not built).**

- **The conservative agenda's anti entries.** Civil Rights (−196), Anti-War (−103) and Transhumanist (−187) score against the conservative agenda's interest groups as strongly as against the reactionary one's. They are not shipped, for three reasons: vanilla lists the nearest vanilla movements (the cultural and religious minorities) as anti for the reactionary and nationalist agendas only; the conservative agenda is the most common among AI countries (163 of 210 in an 1864 save, 96 of 250 in 1958), so these three entries would be most of the change's weight; and suppression is how a movement falls below what it needs to last. Before they go in, the self-check should read OK (so the lists are known to append and to drive suppression), and an observer run with the seven shipped entries should show the reactionary and nationalist countries' Civil Rights and Anti-War movements still standing and their minority-rights and war laws still moving (the play-test list's "Reforms don't stall" row). The entries are three lines in `te_ai_movement_strategies.txt`, a row in the test's `PLACEMENT` and a branch in `te_aml_on_anti_list`.
- **Agenda choice by the ruler's ideology.** #1822 said vanilla's leader-ideology triggers omit the mod's ideologies. Vanilla's agendas use no such trigger: each agenda's `weight` names ruler ideologies one by one (the progressive agenda adds 20 for a radical, nihilist, feminist, abolitionist or market-liberal ruler and 40 for a republican; the conservative agenda names none). The mod has 33 new ideologies, six of them leader ideologies. Giving them a branch means restating five vanilla `weight` blocks by INJECT, special cases included (Metternich, the regime-change +1000s), and the third-party AI mod INJECTs the same field, so the later file would win outright. By the same sum as above, the buckets would be:

|---|---|---|
| `ideology_optimist_transhumanist` | progressive | +206 |
| `ideology_corporate` | progressive | +230 (nationalist +63, egalitarian −231) |

- **Agenda scores for the mod's institutions.** The default strategy scores all 24 institutions; each agenda adds 50 to 200 for each of vanilla's seven and nothing for the mod's seventeen. So under any agenda a vanilla institution scores 60 to 210, the National Bank and the Ministry of the Environment 100 (their default score, set level with each other on purpose), science, war, foreign affairs and thought control 50, and the minor ministries 5 to 20. An agenda score for a mod institution moves levels out of schools, health and police, because scores only decide which institution gets the next level, and the first two priorities for the AI (growth, technology) lean on those. Two options were sized and neither built: every agenda's floor (50 or 75) for all seventeen, which would take the mod's share of the scores from about 40% to about 65% for a country holding them all; or 50 for the three or four ministries an agenda's own interest groups approve most (war, intelligence and propaganda for conservatives; propaganda, religion and thought control for reactionaries; labor, consumer protection and urban planning for egalitarians), about 40% to 45%. The second is the one to try if an observer run shows the ministries stuck at level 1.

**First Aid: left as it is.** #1822 said the mod's `REPLACE` of First Aid has no peace-time switch-off for the AI. Neither does vanilla's: its `ai_weight` is `value = 1`, the same as the mod's, and so is every other vanilla option's except Forced March. Four things argue against adding one. A mobilization option costs upkeep only while the army is mobilized, so an army at peace usually pays nothing. The medic group is exclusive, so switching off First Aid alone would move an AI with the technology to Field Hospitals, which cost more. Turning an option on for a mobilized army costs a quarter of its organization and turning one off half its morale (the options' own script, vanilla's). And vanilla's nursing journal entry and the mod's war-wounded system count armies with medic support (`war.md`). The other AI mod's switch-off is an INJECT, which the mod's `REPLACE` outlasts.

## AI Telemetry Probe

## AI Government Construction

In a market game the engine's AI chooses which government building to queue and where, and script decides how much construction the government buys for the queue each week. Both halves have to fit an AI's budget for an AI government to build anything. The owner's order of importance for AI behavior (2026-11-15) is economic growth, technological advancement, legal modernization, military modernization, expansion; the purchase rule serves the first, by keeping governments out of debts that end in default.

**The purchase** (`te_construction_market_ai_purchase_target`; the full account, the evidence and the tables are in [Construction](construction.md) § The AI's purchase). Each week an AI government with a level queued, and not paused by the engine, buys the larger of:
- **its surplus**: net fixed income over the smoothed price, times 1 + reserves − debt (never under 0.1), moved by the interest rate (*The reserve and the credit by interest rate*, below): at a high rate it holds part of the surplus back until it keeps a reserve, at a low one it borrows a little;
- **a floor**: a twentieth of fixed income, never more than 10 points, paid from the reserves above the one the rate asks for (whole at a tenth or more above it, nothing with none to spare) or, up to a tenth of it, from the surplus. No part of it is on credit. While the country is short of bureaucracy the floor is a fifth of fixed income higher, so it buys at least a fifth; where its surplus already buys more, the shortage adds nothing.

A government with no surplus, no reserves and no shortage of bureaucracy buys nothing, and its queued level waits, whatever the rate: the credit is sized on the surplus that pays its interest.

**Through the engine's pause: tried and removed** (2026-11-16). A paused country buys nothing, whatever its bureaucracy. #1854 let one that was short of bureaucracy with a Government Administration level in its queue go on buying that fifth (owner, 2026-11-16: "bureaucracy shortages limit income"), with a self-check on whether the engine builds the level while it reports the pause. An observer game on that build (`main` at 81b947368, 1836 to October 1913) read `TE_SELF_CHECK BROKEN construction_admin_while_paused` in 1877, for East India after 14 weeks and 420 points: in none of the game's 1,056 probe rows of a paused country had the government queue used any construction. What was bought went to the private queue, at the investment pool's expense, or went unused; East India's treasury paid nothing, and one row shows a treasury paying (Uruguay in 1909, 2,370 in the week, its investment pool empty). The clause and its self-check were removed the same day (owner, 2026-11-16: "Let's do this."). The line, what its numbers meant and the counts are in [Construction](construction.md) § The AI's purchase, *Through the engine's pause*.

Until 2026-11-15 the floor was a flat 10 points a week, about 12,500 to 20,000, which was several times the whole income of most AI countries: they borrowed to the engine's pause within months and stayed there.

It is the same market, price and purchase variable a player has; there is no AI-only modifier and no subsidy. The rule only sets the number a player sets by hand.

- **Why 0.02.** It lets through half again what 0.05 does (25 years against 16), and 0 adds five more (1% of TOP20 years) while asking for nothing past the building's cost, so the next week of deficit would be debt. 0.02 of the limit is about 1.3 weeks of a TOP20 government's fixed income (0.1 is 6.7; the limit is about 0.28 of GDP in the run) and about 4.8 weeks of its surplus to save. - **What the gate does not hold.** The run shows the engine queueing what it counts critical with no reserves at all: of 33 AI countries in debt at two yearly rows, short of bureaucracy, with an empty government queue at the first row, 27 had a queue a year later, 20 of them a Government Administration (61 of the 220 not short did too). Vanilla has a second gate of the same 0.1: the budget AI adds no spending to a should-have below a tenth of reserves (`MONEY_SPENDING_MIN_GOLD_RESERVE_FRACTION_TO_ADD_SHOULD_HAVE`; a fifth for a wants-to-have, three tenths for a nice-to-have, none for a must-have). If a university is a should-have, that line holds it at 0.1 whatever the reserve gate says, and the run cannot tell the two apart. The second is left at vanilla's here, since it reaches all should-have spending, not only construction. `TE_SELF_CHECK … ai_reserve_gate` reads which holds universities (the play-test list).
- **With the purchase rule.** A government that queues at thin reserves buys its surplus term, (1 + reserves) × the surplus: at 0.02 it spends 2% more than its surplus, drawing reserves down in proportion to what is left and never past nothing. The floor keeps vanilla's tenth (`TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_FULL_RESERVES`), so at reserves of 0.02 to 0.1 it pays a fifth to all of the twentieth from them, no faster than before at any level. The queue's cap (queued levels × the weekly maximum) is a ceiling and only lowers the purchase, so a longer queue raises spending only where the uncapped purchase is above one level's maximum: a country with a large surplus (China bought 80 points in 1934 with 31 levels queued; Germany's 140 in 1958 was one level's maximum), or one short of bureaucracy, whose floor is a fifth of fixed income. A country in deficit buys only the reserves' floor, under 10 points, below any cap, so a longer queue buys it nothing more; and a country short of bureaucracy already queues its administration with no reserves (above). Credit is drawn only at a low rate and only in proportion to the surplus (below), so a longer queue adds none.
- **The risk** is upkeep, and shocks met with less buffer: more universities and administrations staffed and supplied from treasuries that kept 0.02 where they kept 0.1. The budget AI's ratio test still applies to each addition (income 1.2 times expenses after it, or a surplus of 15,000 whatever the ratio, `MONEY_SPENDING_MIN_RATIO_TO_ADD_SHOULD_HAVE`), and the purchase spends only the surplus, so the new bill is the buildings' upkeep. In a direct game (Free Market Construction set to Disabled, not the default) the define applies too, and there the treasury pays construction as in vanilla, so the tenth's buffer mattered more; no run covers it. What would show the change has gone wrong, against the run's 1930–1947 figures, and the fallbacks: `docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md` § The AI's reserve gate.

If the solvency rows go wrong: delete the reserve-gate line (vanilla's again). **The reserve and the credit by interest rate** (#1877; not yet run in game). The owner, on the gate above (2026-11-16): "Hmm, I would lean towards having it depend on interest rates. If interest rates are high, requiring a reserve makes sense. If low, no need, and you could even allow spending in (small amounts of) debt." The engine's gates are one figure for every country, so the rate acts in what the mod controls: the purchase, and a monthly pass that queues for a government the engine leaves with nothing queued (*The AI construction pass*, below). Every number, with its reason, is in `common/script_values/te_construction_market_ai_values.txt`, THE RATE and THE PASS; `test_construction_market_ai_purchase.py` (`TheRate`, `TheRateLongRun`) and `test_ai_build_pass.py` pin them.
- **The rate it reads.** `var:te_rate_paid_pts`, the rate the monetary update writes for every country every month under every banking setting (`monetary_policy.md`), which is what the mod's interest modifier applies, less the world reference rate (`var:te_mon_era_base_now`: 3, 2.5, then 2 by era). So the rule reads the spread lenders charge the country over the world's safe rate: its risk premium (the banking cycle's among it) and its central bank's stance. Sure as a read: it is a variable the mod writes. What it leaves out of the engine's figure: vanilla's interest multipliers the mod does not cancel (`declared_bankruptcy`'s +50%, a few event, trait and company modifiers) and the engine's per-loan bucket, which script cannot see (`monetary_policy_design.md` § 2). `modifier:country_loan_interest_rate_add` and `_mult` would read the engine's sums, but neither read has been seen in game and the monetary modifier sits on the banking journal entry for a country that holds it, so they are not used. The debt ceiling the credit is sized on is `credit`, which the probe logs: `principal` over `credit` is `scaled_debt` (median 1.002 over 2,440 rows of the 1925–1947 run, 5th to 95th percentile 1.000 to 1.017). The probe's `interests` is the number of strategic interests (`num_active_interests`), not interest paid; no run logged a rate, so the rates below are from the January 1925 save the 1925–1947 run started from (each country's variable, and the history store's monthly `mon_rate_paid` samples for the 18 countries with a banking cycle running), and the probe logs them from now (`rate`, version 5). **It is a real rate**: the update subtracts headline inflation from the lenders' yield (the reference rate plus expected inflation), so inflation above what lenders expected reads as cheaper credit, and only hyperinflation (inflation band 6) counts as a crisis. That is what the treasury pays, since the mod's interest modifier applies this rate. Reading the nominal rate instead (adding `var:te_inflation` back) would make an inflation surprise tighten the rule; listed for the owner, not built.
- **The rates in the runs** (the run of 1836–1925 that made the save, `dab9335f1`; the reference rate was 2.5 in 1925):

- **The curve**, a straight line between its points, so it has no cliff a country can flicker across:

  | Spread over the reference rate | Reserve the rate asks for | Debt it allows |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2 or less | none | a tenth of the ceiling |
  | 2 to 4 | none | falling to none |
  | 4 | none | none: the rule as it was before |
  | 4 to 7 | rising to a tenth of the limit | none |
  | 7 or more | a tenth of the limit (vanilla's gate) | none |

  The surplus term's factor is 1 + reserves − debt + 2 × the credit not yet used − (the part of the reserves kept, which is not drawn down) − 2 × what it lacks of the reserve, then the war and banking brakes, never under 0.1. A country the monetary update has not reached reads the middle, the rule as it was. The floor draws only the reserves above the one asked for.
- **In a crisis it tightens by itself.** In a financial crisis (`te_mon_in_financial_crisis`: the cycle's panic or downturn, a gold peg losing confidence, hyperinflation) the spread is read as 7 at least: no credit, and the full reserve asked for. The rate alone would not always do it: in the history store, of 33 panics the rate rose above the year before at some month of the following year in 25, but over that year it averaged lower than the year before in 16, the central bank cutting through the panic. No credit at war either, nor while the banking cycle's bubble pressure is 50 or more (the reviewer, 2026-11-16): there the war brake and the banking brake hold the purchase back, and the credit would offset them (at high bubble pressure with no reserves the old factor was 0.7, and the credit would have made it 0.9 at a low rate). So nothing here eases a downturn or a bubble (the owner's rule of 2026-11-14).
- **The debt, and what it costs.** Never more than a tenth of the ceiling: the debt at which the engine's own AI begins to call debt significant and raise taxes (`CHANGE_TAX_SIGNIFICANT_DEBT_THRESHOLD`), half the pause's resume line (20%), a quarter of the pause (40%), and under the monetary system's debt-load premium, which starts at 25%. And never more than the debt whose interest, at the rate 7 points over the reference, is a third of the country's surplus, so a government with a small surplus borrows only a little and none with no surplus. With a surplus and no reserves it borrows a fifth of its surplus a week at first and settles at two-thirds of the allowance, 6.7% of the ceiling at most, where it spends its surplus exactly. A large economy's ceiling is about 320 weeks of its fixed income (the 20 largest, 1925–1947), so that is about 22 weeks of fixed income, about a tenth of its GDP. In the 1925–1947 run's rows where the rate allowed credit, interest on that balance at the rate paid came to a median 4.7% of the surplus (at most 11.7%) and 1.3% of fixed income for the 20 largest; at the rate 7 over the reference, at most 22% of the surplus.
- **The credit's switch** is `TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_CREDIT_MAX`: at 0 there is no credit, with nothing else to change (the purchase then spends no more than its surplus at any rate, a low rate asks no reserve, a high one still does, and the pass still reaches a low-rate debtor on its own number, `DEBT_MAX`; `TheCreditSwitch` pins it). The owner keeps the credit (2026-11-16, below).
- **With a level always queued the debt stands.** At the balance the factor is 1, so nothing pays the debt down while a level is queued: in the week-by-week model it climbs to 0.043 of the ceiling in ten years, 0.064 in thirty and two-thirds of the allowance by fifty, and stays there (the reviewer, 2026-11-16; `TheRateLongRun` pins it). While a government holds any debt it holds no reserves, so the engine's own reserve gate queues nothing for it that the engine does not count critical, and only the pass (below) queues: the owner's second decision makes the pass the intended route for that money. Whether that keeps out what the engine would have built (infrastructure, power, military) is what a test run must show (the play-test list).
- **When the rate rises on that debt** the allowance shrinks, the factor falls under 1 and the surplus pays the debt down while the queue goes on being bought, never below a tenth of the surplus and never below the floor: at a high rate a country at the balance keeps 0.73 of the factor it had at the middle. It never pauses half-built construction: the rule borrows nothing past a tenth of the ceiling, far from the engine's pause. In the week-by-week model (`TheRateLongRun`), a rate that climbs from 1 to 9 over the reference after ten years has the debt more than halved by year thirty, with points bought every year.
- **What it changes while a level is queued** (the surplus term's factor, the new rule's over the old, in the run's yearly rows of a country building and not paused; for 1925–1947 each country's January 1925 rate, moved by the debt-load premium as its debt moved, since the run logged no rate; for 1905–1924 the 18 countries' own yearly rates):

  |---|---|---|---|
  | Median | 1.04 | 0.80 | 1.00 |
  | GDP-weighted median | 1.10 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
  | Lower, higher | 27%, 53% | 79%, 12% | 13%, 47% |
  | 10th to 90th percentile | 0.89 to 1.18 | 0.72 to 1.05 | 0.96 to 1.18 |

  These are the factor on the rows as the old rule left them, not the purchase (the queue's cap is not applied), and they are a first week's: once reserves and debt settle under the new rule, a low-rate country with a level queued spends its surplus exactly (the factor is 1 at the balance) and a high-rate one too once it holds its reserve (the reviewer, 2026-11-16), so what lasts is the debt front-loaded at a low rate and the reserve held at a high one. - **The long run, with and without a return** (`long_run` in `test_construction_market_ai_purchase.py`, a level always queued, fixed income 3,000 a week, the rate paid 3.5% against 6.5% at the middle). **The model has no growth unless `returns` is set**: what is built earns nothing, so building sooner is worth only the points. `returns` is what a point adds to fixed income each year, as a share of its cost, all of it surplus; the debt ceiling and the reserve limit follow fixed income. The credit buys 16.7 weeks of income (40 points at 1,250) sooner and pays interest on them from then on. Points bought at the low rate less the middle's:

  | Surplus 17% of income, return on a point | Year 10 | Year 30 | Year 50 | Year 100 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | None (the model as it was) | +20.5 | +9.1 | −16.7 | −86.3 (−4.1%) |
  | 2.5% a year, under the rate paid | +26.6 | +32.6 | +20.8 | −79.5 (−0.8%) |
  | 3.5%, the rate paid | +29.6 | +50.0 | +69.2 | +246.9 (+1.3%) |
  | 4.5%, a point above it | +32.8 | +75.1 | +162.2 | +1,629 (+3.9%) |
  | 6% | +38.3 | +134.6 | +472.2 | +12,534 (+8.8%) |

  A surplus of 25% reads the same way (+24.5, +6.6, −20.8, −90.8 with no return; ahead at every year from 3.5%). So with no return the credit is ahead at first and behind from between years 30 and 50, and once a point returns about the rate paid or more it stays ahead for good; the break-even is a little under the rate, since the ceiling grows with income and the credit's pounds with it. `TheRateLongRun` pins both, so nobody reads the model as proof either way (owner, 2026-11-16: "the whole reason we are building this is to get the AI to grow faster"). **What return the pass's buildings earn**: an administration in a shortage, far more than the rate. A shortage's tax waste falls on every tax the country collects (vanilla: up to all of it at a full deficit), so a level that closes a shortage of 2% of usage saves about 2% of taxes: for a large economy with taxes of 50,000 a week, about 1,000 a week, against a level costing 100 points (125,000 at 1,250), about 40% a year. A university earns innovation, not money; the mod has no figure that turns innovation into income, so its return is unknown.

**The AI construction pass** (`common/scripted_effects/te_ai_build_pass_effects.txt`, `te_ai_build_pass_triggers.txt`, `te_ai_build_pass_values.txt`, `common/on_actions/te_ai_build_pass_on_actions.txt`; `test_ai_build_pass.py`; not yet run in game). The engine queues nothing new for a government in debt, which holds no reserves: in the 1925–1947 run, 80 of the 112 years in which one of the 20 largest had nothing queued. Once a month, for an AI government in a market game, not decentralized, at peace, not paused and not in default, whose government queue already stood empty a month before (so the engine has gone first), the pass queues one level of government construction with `start_building_construction`, as `te_grid_ai_build_effect` queues a power station and `gm_com_queue_levels` a monument's levels, if:
- **the rate allows it the means**: its reserves above the reserve asked for, plus the debt it may hold at its rate (`te_ai_build_pass_debt_tolerance`: `TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_DEBT_MAX`, a tenth of the ceiling, on the curve's low side, capped as the credit is), less its debt, are not negative. So at a high rate it needs the reserve, at the middle no debt, and at a low rate a debt within the tolerance. The tolerance is the credit's number for the same reasons but apart from it, so that the credit's switch at 0 leaves the pass reaching a low-rate debtor;
- **it has the surplus the engine's budget AI asks for before it adds spending**: income 1.2 times expenses, a sixth of fixed income (`MONEY_SPENDING_MIN_RATIO_TO_ADD_SHOULD_HAVE`), or a surplus of 15,000 a week (`MONEY_SPENDING_MIN_SURPLUS_TO_IGNORE_RATIO_FOR_SHOULD_HAVE`), which leaves room above the 1.05 where that AI cuts should-have spending;
- **something on its list does most good there**, and the surplus pays for it within two years (`TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_MAX_WEEKS`, 104: the pass queues into an empty queue, so its level is first in line, and the engine fills the government queue first come, first served, `vanilla_economy_reference.md` § 9.3; at a surplus of a sixth and 1,250 a point a university or a power plant fits from a fixed income of 28,800 a week, an administration from 7,200, a railway from 57,700, a highway or an airport from 144,200).

**What the pass queues: THE ORDER** (owner, 2026-11-16: "bureaucracy over the requirement is very rarely worth it ... This then allows the AI to spend in debt with low interest rates to get the highest leverage government buildings"; on the power plant, "we should exclude countries that have any building that produces power (e.g. hydro, nuclear, etc.) so the AI isn't forced to have fossil fuel power plants"; and "railroads when infra is below zero, airports and highways in major industrial states below cap"). The list is written once, one line per entry (`te_ai_build_pass_try = { KIND = … }` in `te_ai_build_pass_effect`), so reordering those lines reorders it; each entry's conditions and the reason for its place are in `te_ai_build_pass_triggers.txt`. The first entry that applies is queued; if none does, nothing is:
1. **A Government Administration, while net bureaucracy is under 2% of its usage** (`TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_BUREAUCRACY_MARGIN`), or below nothing, in the incorporated state whose taxes come closest to its tax capacity (which the level also raises). A shortage puts tax waste on every tax the country collects, so a level that ends or forestalls one repays its cost many times over, and at 100 points it is the cheapest entry; above the margin it would be bureaucracy over the requirement. 2% is two years (the longest a level may take) of the median growth of bureaucracy usage among the 20 largest, 0.8% and 0.9% a year in the 1900–1924 and 1925–1947 runs, and about what one of their administration levels adds (medians of 2.3% and 1.8% of usage); among them net bureaucracy was under 2% of usage in a little over a quarter of their years and below nothing in 10% and 16%. The engine itself queues an administration it counts critical while bureaucracy is short, with no reserves (27 of 33 such countries in the 1925–1947 run); the pass covers the rest and the countries close to a shortage.
2. **The country's first power plant**, with Electrical Generation, while nothing of the country's makes Electricity, built or under construction, and the grid's pass would not bring it a partner's power: one level of vanilla's coal and oil plant (`building_power_plant`, 400 points), in the incorporated state with an urban centre whose urban-centre and manufacturing levels are most (`te_ai_build_pass_power_users`). *What makes Electricity* is `te_state_has_a_power_plant` in any state: every building with a production method that outputs Electricity, derived from the methods (`test_ai_building_values.py` holds the trigger to that list, `test_ai_build_pass.py` pins its 16 names): vanilla's plant, the mod's hydro, nuclear, renewable and fusion plants, the Solar Receiver, the Grid Transfer Station, the Generic Power Hub and eight company power stations. So once any of them stands or is queued anywhere in the country the entry never fires again, and the pass never adds a fossil plant beside other power. Where the grid's pass would queue it a station to bring a partner's power instead, the country is left to it (`te_ai_build_pass_grid_serves`: Electrical Capacitors, a partner, and a state where `te_grid_ai_station_site` allows a station, which for a country with no power of its own is a state beside a partner's station); a partner with no station near, such as a bloc member across the world, leaves the plant to this pass. That exclusion is the author's, not the owner's, whose words exclude only a country with power of its own. Vanilla's `possible` (no banned-industry law) and the mod's Resource Transition rule (`rt_fossil_construction_allowed_here`: no fossil plant under a moratorium or a phaseout) are copied in. Second, before the entries that repeat: it fires once in a game, and behind a railway or a university it would wait as long as those keep firing (a country under its innovation cap would never get it). Unsubsidized and with few buyers at first, it is kept as built (owner, 2026-11-17: "That's fine, it allows them to build buildings and select PMs that use electricity, and that will in turn spur electricity demand. We'll see if that's good enough in practice with playtesting").
3. **A railway, where a state's infrastructure is short of its usage by more than 2% of that usage** (`TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_INFRASTRUCTURE_MARGIN`), with Railways, in the incorporated state short by most, in points of infrastructure. A short state's market access is its infrastructure over its usage, so it loses access in proportion across its whole output; the absolute shortfall ranks the loss, since output goes with usage. The margin is a departure from "below zero" (at 0 it reads the owner's words exactly): it keeps a state at 99% from taking an 800-point level, twice a university, and from flipping in and out as usage moves with staffing. Per point a railway is the cheapest infrastructure in every era: 20 (early trains) to 60 (Vactrain Freight) a level for 800 points, against 25–50 for a highway's 2,000 and 50–100 for an airport's. It has no level cap. *The mod's later infrastructure* is the railway's own methods (steam, electric, diesel and autonomous trains, Vactrain Freight, in the group the mod INJECTs into), so `building_railway` is right in every era and a new level runs what the building runs; no building of the mod's replaces it, and highways and airports are entries of their own. An unincorporated state is left to investors: it pays no pop taxes, so a government level earns less there. *No staffing test*: every base method of the mod's railway gives its infrastructure per level (`level_scaled`, since #1050; `test_ai_build_pass.py` checks all twelve), so a level nobody staffs still closes the shortfall it was queued for, and a state that cannot staff its railways stops being short as levels are added. The 2026-11-17 review's case of a shortfall that persists holds for vanilla's methods, whose infrastructure is per worker, not the mod's. Nor do railways keep a subsidy by default: since #1050 every vanilla administrative strategy says `building_railway = should_remove` (`te_ai_subsidy_strategies.txt`), and the 60 of 61 subsidized railways in default (*Flagship subsidies*, above) were read before it.
4. **A university, while innovation is under nine-tenths of its cap** and bureaucracy is not short, in the most populous incorporated state whose universities are staffed to 80% (`te_ai_build_pass_staffed`): research, the owner's second priority after growth; past the cap a level is wasted. A university's innovation is per worker (`workforce_scaled`), so a level its state cannot staff, for want of academics where literacy is low, adds none. Vanilla's Polish closure is copied in, since whether `start_building_construction` honours `can_build_government` is unread.
5. **A highway**, then 6. **an airport**, **in a major industrial state under the building's level cap, beside no level of the building under 80% staffed**, with Paved Roads (era 5) or Commercial Aviation (era 6), 2,000 points each, the most industrial such state first. *The cap* is a level limit. Both buildings carry `has_max_level = yes` and no number of their own (`common/buildings/11_zz_te_infrastructure.txt`), so the limit is the state modifier `state_building_highway_max_level_add` or `state_building_airport_max_level_add` (registered in `mod_entity_modifier_types.txt`): 1 from the unlocking technology and from each later one that adds it (eras 6 to 13), and more from power bloc principles, a company and production methods. Where the building stands or is queued the pass asks the engine (`can_queue_building_levels = 1`, which counts what is queued and a state region split between owners, as `bg_infrastructure`'s `stateregion_max_level` asks); where it does not, for a cap of at least 1. *A major industrial state* holds at least a tenth of the country's manufacturing levels (`TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_INDUSTRIAL_SHARE`, light, heavy and military industry): at most ten states of a country, its industrial core; the pass counts the country's levels once, before the list. Two years of the surplus for 2,000 points keeps these to economies with about 144,000 a week of fixed income. The highway comes first: at base prices its method sells more than it buys (`pm_civil_highway`, 5,505 of transportation a week for 4,400 of inputs) and it comes an era sooner. The airport's buys more than it sells (`pm_airport`, 4,400 of aeroplanes and oil for 2,700 of merchant marine and transportation, before wages), so at base prices an airport the government builds runs at a loss. Kept (owner, 2026-11-17: "I think this is fine, it provides state-level modifiers that make it worth it"); what that means in each economic system is below. Last, since the university comes first while research is under its cap. *Staffing*: neither goes beside a level of its kind under 80% staffed (`te_ai_build_pass_staffed`), the buildings' own `should_auto_expand = { occupancy >= 0.8 }`, so the pass does not add a 2,000-point level beside one that stands empty. A state with none of the building still takes a first level, so under a law that forces no subsidy each major industrial state can still hold one empty airport. *A split state region* (accepted): where this country's part of a region split between owners has none of the building, the cap can read room the region has used; should the engine refuse the level, the follow reads it lost the next month and the kind backs off for a year, which costs a month a year and a lost level in the self-check.

**What the order means for highways and airports.** Each comes only where every entry above it declines: bureaucracy above its margin, the country with power (or no site for a plant), no incorporated state short of infrastructure by more than 2% (or railways backed off or too dear), and innovation at nine-tenths of its cap (or no university site staffed). So "in major industrial states below cap" is reached only in a country whose infrastructure is not short anywhere, and the infrastructure they add is not what it lacks; their state modifiers are. To bring them sooner, move their two lines up the order.

**Airports and highways: what the laws force, what they give, what they cost.**
- *What the laws force.* The mod's Interventionism and Command Economy carry `country_building_airport_require_subsidies_bool` and `country_building_highway_require_subsidies_bool` (`common/laws/construction_system_law_injections.txt`); no other law or modifier forces either, and the other six economic systems (Traditionalism, Agrarianism, Industry Banned, Extraction Economy, Laissez-Faire, Cooperative Ownership) force nothing on them. A forcing modifier holds on buildings built before it too, live (scripting_best_practices.md § *A Save's `subsidized=yes` Is Only the Chosen Flag*). Elsewhere the AI keeps no subsidy on them: a building group's default subsidy applies only at campaign start, and the engine's AI strips a subsidy nothing requires within days (0 of 250 such airports and highways subsidized in the 2073 save).
- *What they give.* Every state modifier of every airport and highway method is per worker (`workforce_scaled`, `extra_pms.txt`; `test_ai_build_pass.py` checks each): an airport's 50 infrastructure, +1 infrastructure per unit of aeroplanes the state consumes, +5 to the economy-of-scale level cap of the state's buildings, +15% tourism output, with 100 pollution and 1.74 greenhouse gas; a highway's 25 infrastructure, +0.25 per unit of automobiles consumed, +10% transportation output, +5% maximum trade advantage from capacity, +10% migration crowding density, +5% tourism, +5 economy-of-scale cap, with 40 pollution and 8.7 greenhouse gas. So an empty level gives none of them, and the owner's reason for the airport holds where it is staffed.
- *What a level costs the treasury a week*, fully staffed at base prices, with w the state's weekly wage for a laborer (machinists and clerks earn 1.5 times it): an airport buys 4,400 of goods and 150 of construction for upkeep (`pm_maintenance_heavy`, per level whatever its staff) and sells 2,700, and pays 2,250 w in wages, so it loses 1,850 + 2,250 w; a highway buys 4,400 and 150 and sells 5,505, and pays 1,750 w, so it earns 955 − 1,750 w, a loss only above a wage of about 0.55. Under Interventionism and Command Economy the subsidy tops each level up to no loss (`SUBSIDIES_MIN_GAIN` 0), so the treasury pays an airport's 1,850 + 2,250 w a week (2,300 at w = 0.2, 4,100 at w = 1) and a highway's loss if it has one (none up to w ≈ 0.55, 795 at w = 1), and the level stays staffed. Under the other six the treasury pays nothing a week: an airport at a loss stops hiring below `BUILDING_DEFAULT_MIN_EARNINGS_TO_HIRE_EMPLOYEES` and stands empty, buys none of its goods and gives none of its modifiers, and its 150 of upkeep comes from its own cash; who pays once that runs out is unread. Under Laissez-Faire its levels are offered to investors (`country_force_privatization_bool`), and a privately held empty level is downsized by the engine after six months (`AUTO_DOWNSIZE_*`), if any investor buys it. Transportation is a local good priced over the mod's 0.999 range, so where a highway's output floods its state its price, and the highway's earnings, fall; the figures move with prices.

**The grid's pass and this one.** `te_grid_ai_build_effect` (`power_grid_effects.txt`) queues one Grid Transfer Station a month for an AI with Electrical Capacitors, not in default and with under a year of government construction queued, where the country makes Electricity in the market area or a partner's station is near; for a country with no power and no partner station near it queues nothing. This pass's plant is only for that country (`te_ai_build_pass_grid_serves`, which reads the grid's own site rule), so the two never queue for the same need: the plant makes the first Electricity, and the grid's pass then queues the station that carries it to the market area's other states (and a partner's grid to this country). Neither waits on the other: the grid's pass queues whatever the queue holds, and the station, once queued, counts as power for this pass.

**Who owns and staffs what the pass queues.** Government construction gives the state the levels (country-owned, `vanilla_economy_reference.md` § 10.1), so the treasury takes each level's profit, and pays its loss where a subsidy tops it up (*Airports and highways*, above). No economic law bars government construction: the engine's modifier list has none that does, and the grid's station, a private-infrastructure building, is queued the same way under every law. The laws decide what becomes of the state's levels afterwards: Laissez-Faire forces privatization (`country_force_privatization_bool`: the levels are offered to the investment pool, which buys them as it has the money), Command Economy allows privatization to companies only (`country_disable_non_company_privatization_bool`), the middle laws keep both ways open at their costs, and Cooperative Ownership lets power and infrastructure be collectivized (`building_group_bg_power_allowed_collectivization_add`, `…_private_infrastructure_…`). Whether a government-queued level starts worker-owned under Cooperative Ownership, and whether `start_building_construction` is refused for a building a law would not let the government own, are unread; the self-check's lost count by kind would show a refusal. **A plant with no subsidy and few buyers** (kept by the owner, 2026-11-17, quoted under entry 2): the #933 subsidy setting (`building_power_plant = should_remove` in every vanilla administrative strategy) is unchanged. Electricity is a local good and the mod's price range is 0.999 (`common/defines/extra_defines.txt`), so a good with no buyers sells for almost nothing: a plant whose state buys no Electricity earns next to nothing for its inputs (an early-plant level buys 470 a week of engines, coal and copper for 25 Electricity, 750 at base price), its wages fall to the minimum and its workers leave for any job that pays more. An unsubsidized plant in a state that buys none would not stay staffed. That is why the site is the state with the most urban-centre and manufacturing levels: electric street lights, unlocked by the plant's own technology and allowed only where Electricity is supplied (`required_input_goods`), take 3 a level for 10 services against gas lights' 2 coal for 5, so nine urban-centre levels take a level's 25. Whether they switch fast enough to keep the plant staffed is the play-test list's row.

The pass **follows each level it queued** (`te_ai_build_pass_follow`), in the state it queued it in (`te_ai_build_pass_pending_state`), from that state's built levels of the kind: built once the state holds more; lost once, unbuilt, none of that kind is queued there or the government queue is empty (dropped, or the state lost); a level of the kind taken down there lowers the base, so it reads as neither. In the state, not the country: investors build railways, plants, highways and airports too, and a level of theirs elsewhere is not the pass's. *Investors' levels in the same state.* The pass queues only into an empty government queue and adds nothing while it follows, so an empty government queue with no new level means its level is gone, even where an investor's level of the kind is queued there; before the 2026-11-17 review that case read as waiting, and held the pass, until the investor's level was built and read as the pass's. What is left: an investor's level of the kind finished there before the pass's reads as built (the pass's own level may still be queued, and is then unwatched), and an investor's level queued there while the government queue also holds another government level hides a dropped one until either clears. Neither touches universities and administrations, which only governments build; `rail`, `power`, `road` and `air` can carry them, the railway most, since its site is where investors build railways. While it follows a level the pass queues nothing more, and after a lost one it queues no level of that kind for the country for a year (`TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_BACKOFF_DAYS`, 365), since what was bought for it is lost with it (`vanilla_economy_reference.md` § 9.3). *Built, then gone.* A level it built is watched for two years (`te_ai_build_pass_watch`, `TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_WATCH_DAYS` 730) in its state at the levels of the kind that state then held; if they fall, by the engine's budget AI downsizing what thin reserves bought or a private level there downsized empty, the kind backs off for a year as for a lost one, so the pass does not rebuild what the budget will not keep (a university downsized while innovation is still under the cap, a first plant downsized away and then wanted again). One watch per kind; a second level of a kind built within two years moves the watch to it. The weekly purchase pays for the level on the rule above. The probe counts what it queued (`cm_pass`), the states short of infrastructure (`inf_short_states`, `inf_short`, which count unincorporated states and any shortfall) and the states the railway entry would act on (`rail_sites`, its own site test).

The budget AI's cuts (`MONEY_SPENDING_MAX_RATIO_TO_REMOVE_*`, `00_ai.txt`) are by spending category: bureaucracy (administrations), innovation (universities), infrastructure, military, construction and the like, at income under 1.05 times expenses with under 20 weeks of reserves for a should-have; the categories' buildings are engine-side, so whether "infrastructure" takes railways, highways and airports is unread, and power plants have no category. In the two observer runs the probe read (all owners' levels, so losses of territory too), the share of year pairs in which a country's levels of the kind fell: universities 3% and 9% of the 20 largest's (1905–1924, 1925–1947), administrations 5% and 6%, railways 6% and 10%, power plants 0 of 10 and 1 of 91, highways 0 of 1 and 1 of 119; no AI airport stood by 1947.

## Space Program Mission Budget

An AI country's Space Program runs the dearest mission its government can pay for, as a player would choose (2026-11-15). Before, the mission group's `ai_selection = most_productive` climbed every AI program to the dearest mission its technology allowed, whatever it cost: in the owner's 1958 save (the Singapore game) nine of the eleven AI programs ran Solar Colonization, the eight fully staffed among them at 94% to 791% of their government's own revenue. Together the eleven paid 31.0M a week, 62% of all AI government goods spending in that save (the 2026-11-15 save study), and six of the eleven were in default or under the bankruptcy modifier. Seven of the nine on Solar Colonization had never run a milestone, so the bill bought them only the method's innovation and prestige. Players are exempt; the pass models the AI choosing what a player chooses, as the company AI's subsidy pass does.

**Files.** The monthly pass `sr_ai_mission_budget_monthly` (`common/scripted_effects/sr_ai_mission_budget_effects.txt`, from `space_race_on_action`, whatever the Space Race rule, since the program buys its Launch Capacity either way), its values (`common/script_values/sr_ai_mission_budget_values.txt`, every number below) and triggers (`common/scripted_triggers/sr_ai_mission_budget_triggers.txt`); the Space Program's fifth group, Mission Limit (`sr_mission_ceiling_pmgs.txt`, `sr_mission_ceiling_pms.txt`); the missions' gates (`extra_pms.txt`), the funding buttons' AI weights (`space_race_buttons.txt`), the space decisions' (`extra_decisions.txt`), the building's `can_build_government` (`extra_buildings.txt`), the founding means test's tooltip (`sr_ai_program_means_tt`, `te_miscellaneous_l_english.yml`) and the four panels' method-group filter. `test_ai_space_mission_budget.py` runs the pass as written in a small evaluator; in-game checks: `docs/testing/space-race-playtest.md` § The AI's Space Program budget.

**The rule.**
- **The bill** of a mission is its Launch Capacity a week at full staff (`sr_mission_units_*`), times the program's throughput without its funding (never below 1), times our market's Launch Capacity price (never below base, `sr_launch_capacity_unit_price`). Full staff, because a program that fills its jobs pays the full bill: Sindh's ran Solar Colonization at 11% staff in 1958 for 18% of its revenue, and fully staffed it would pay 164%.
- **The income** is `fixed_income`, the government's own weekly revenue. The investment pool's transfer only repays private construction and is not the government's to spend; vanilla's AI construction target reads the two apart (`fixed_income` and `investment_pool_income`, `00_default_strategy.txt`). Not read in game: that `fixed_income` leaves the transfer out (the self-check's line prints it, to set against the budget panel).
- **Climb** to the dearest mission whose bill is at most **10%** of fixed income, as far as the country's technology allows.
- **Hold** while the mission's bill is between 10% and 20%.
- **Step down** once the mission's bill has been over **20%** at **six** monthly reviews in a row, straight to the dearest mission within 10%, and then wait **five years** (1,825 days, as long as the mod's retooling) before climbing again. One review under the line restarts the count.
- **In default or under the bankruptcy modifier**, Earth Orbit at once, and the funding buttons shed funding.
- **A mission past the ceiling** the pass set (the engine's AI ignoring the gate) is stepped back to the ceiling at the next review, with no five years' wait: every hold is at `sr_ai_mission_held_tier`, the running mission but never past last month's ceiling. A climb within the ceiling, after a new technology, is held.
- **Funding first.** Each funding level adds 25% to the program's throughput and so to its bill, which the funding buttons' innovation weights never saw: Germany's seven levels in 1958 took its program's throughput, and with it the bill, to 2.56 times the mission's own. An AI's increase-funding buttons weigh 0 while one more level would take the bill past 10%, and its decrease-funding buttons get +100 while the bill is over 20% (`sr_ai_space_fund_up_blocked`, `sr_ai_space_fund_down_due`). The mission rule reads the bill without funding, so funding is shed before a mission is stepped down.
- **The milestone guard** (owner, 2026-11-16). A running milestone past **75%** of its goal (`sr_ai_mission_guard_share`; `var:sr_progress_<m>` against `sr_<m>_goal`, the goal its entry pins) holds the six-month step down at the cheapest mission that still carries it (`sr_ai_mission_guard_tier`). The Moon Mission carries the Moon Landing and the Deep-Space Probe, the Mars Mission the Mars Landing, the Interstellar Mission the Interstellar Probe. Solar Colonization carries Solar System Colonization for a country with no colony (a holder's only pauses, below) while a lunar world is free or an inner world open. Where only outer worlds are left, only Deep Space Exploration can found one, so the guard holds a program already on Deep Space Exploration or above and none on Solar Colonization. Default and bankruptcy still drop the program at once: a government that can't pay its debts doesn't finish a Mars Landing.
  - **Only while the milestone can move.** A milestone counts only while its progress moves this month, read by the same triggers its monthly tick reads (`sr_ai_milestone_guarded`, `sr_ai_colonization_guarded`; `test_ai_space_mission_budget.py` holds the two to each other). For the six single-goal milestones that is the rule on and a place in the global top three (`sr_space_race_on`, `sr_ai_should_participate`: `sr_monthly_progress_update_effect`, `space_race_effects.txt`), while their entries stay open for any great or major power. For Solar System Colonization it is the programme running (`sr_solar_colonization_running`: the mission on, the Moon Landing done, colonization not finished unless resumed, and with the race over and a lunar world free only by the country's choice, `sr_colonization_by_choice`), the top three again, and a world left to found (`sr_any_site_unclaimed`, the entry's `possible` line).
  - **A three-year backstop** for a freeze no gate shows (`sr_ai_mission_guard_months_limit` = 36, `sr_ai_mission_guard_in_time`). While the guard holds part of the step, the six-month count is kept and goes on rising by one at each review, so it is the clock. The rest of the step comes at the first review after the milestone completes, fails or stops moving, and at the latest at the 42nd review in a row over the line (six plus 36). Three years is longer than the 26 months between Mars launch windows, so a real wait for a window is never cut. The freezes only the backstop sees: Solar System Colonization's staged progress waiting on a world this country can't reach (only worlds beyond the Moon left in its stage and no Mars Landing of its own, which no aggregate trigger reads; `sr_cf_targetable_<world>` is per world), and a launch barred for years (Earth blockaded or the launch site lost, `sr_launches_barred`), while the progress itself goes on moving. Setting the constant very high removes the cap. Still open for the owner (below).
- **A program even Earth Orbit is too dear for** (owner, 2026-11-16). An AI country whose Earth Orbit bill has been over the step-down share at **twelve** monthly reviews in a row (`sr_ai_program_unaffordable_months_limit`; one review under ends the count) pulls its Space Program down (`sr_ai_program_demolish`). A player never counts, and a country that becomes player-run loses its count at the next pulse. In the 1958 save that is Italy, Mexico, Indonesia, Scandinavia and Oyo.
- **Founding** is the same test in reverse (`sr_ai_may_found_space_program`): Earth Orbit's bill within the climbing share, out of default and bankruptcy, and not within **five years** of pulling a program down (`sr_ai_program_demolished`, 1,825 days). Found a Space Program's AI weight asks it, and so does the building's `can_build_government` for an AI (`sr_ai_may_build_space_program`; a player's government always may). The engine's own construction AI can build the program (`can_build_government` was `always = yes`), which is the likeliest way countries never great or major powers came to run one: Oyo had one in 1958. It is a means test, not a rank test, by the owner's soft-caps default. The founding weight's existing top-three test stays.
- **Module fabrication** (owner, 2026-11-20) is paid beside the mission, not counted in its bill. A colony holder's yearly module review (`sr_mod_ai_review`, `space_race.md` § Colony modules and Extraplanetary Base room) runs the dearest fabrication method its technology allows whose bill fits in **5%** of fixed income (`sr_mod_ai_fab_share`, half the climbing share, since the two are paid together), priced as a mission's is: the programme's throughput with its funding, times the Launch Capacity premium (`sr_mod_ai_fab_room_<method>`, `sr_colony_module_values.txt`). Otherwise it runs the cheapest method it has whatever the budget, as every method ran before, so a holder's modules stop only on the review's own gates (a positive weekly surplus, out of default, something queued, and since 2026-11-23 the stock below its cap: a module waiting for its launch window now stays queued, so a full stock would bill the method's goods for points thrown away, for up to a Mars cycle; owner, 2026-11-23). At full staff and base prices Fusion Shipyards (1,368,000 a week) needs fixed income of about 27M and Orbital Foundries (732,000) about 15M. Each step down costs about 11% more a module and takes about twice as long (4, 8 and 20 months a module), since the methods' bills were set about 10% apart a point (`sr_colony_module_pms.txt`).

**Why these numbers.** The share is the government's room for the program. In the 1958 save the 76 AI governments in surplus, none with a program, ran a surplus of 16% of their own revenue (1.8% of GDP on revenue of 11.2%; the save study's fiscal table): a program at 10% leaves such a budget in surplus, one at 20% turns it into a deficit. The players' programs cost 1% to 5% of revenue once their economies had grown (Singapore 1958 2.2%, 1947 5.2%; Byzantium 2020 1.0%; New York 1940 2.8%), though Singapore's paid 69% in 1932; the AI programs read alongside a sound budget 2% to 6% (Scandinavia 1940 2%, the USA 1940 3%, Russia 1940 6%, Britain 1958 6%). The study's starting point, 15%, rested on Byzantium 2020's four AI programs at 12% to 26% "without ruin", but three of the four were under the bankruptcy modifier in that save. The gap between the two shares is the hysteresis: a mission chosen at 10% needs its bill doubled (the price can at most double from base, the mod's `PRICE_RANGE`) or the income halved to be stepped down, and the six months and the five years keep a swing around the line from failing the same milestones again and again. No vanilla building costs a comparable share, so there is no vanilla value to anchor to. In the 1958 save the choice of share barely matters: default and bankruptcy decide six of the eleven programs. With the climbing and step-down shares at 5% and 10% every program ends on Earth Orbit; at 10% and 20%, and at 15% and 25%, Germany keeps the Moon Mission; at 20% and 30%, Germany the Mars Mission and the North American Union the Moon Mission.

**The ceiling.** A production method has no trigger, so the only per-building gate the engine checks for its own AI is another group's active method (`scripting_best_practices.md` § Locking Production Methods). Each mission above Earth Orbit lists in its `unlocking_production_methods` the Mission Limit methods that allow it: Any Mission, the default, and the AI's ceilings from that mission up. Every Mission Limit method carries nothing and unlocks only on itself, the Grand Monuments' ratchet: only the active one is ever valid, so the engine's AI can't move the group; script moves it past the lock (`te_debug_monuments` probe, 2026-10-08). No panel shows the group (owner, 2026-11-16). The filter that hides the maintenance groups leaves it out of every strip of a building's method groups: the building panel, the building browser, the construction panel's rows and the goods-by-state cards (`building_details_panel.gui`, `production_methods.gui`, `building_browser_panel.gui`, `goods_state_panel.gui`). That also hides an AI rival's limit from a player inspecting its program; the saves show it (`scripts/analysis/save_world_report.py buildings --type building_space_program`). Its loc stays, for any tooltip or log that names a method. The mission group keeps `most_productive`, which climbs, and is capped by the ceiling; under `most_profitable` the engine would pull every AI program down to Earth Orbit, which no ceiling gates. The pass records its ceiling (`var:sr_ai_mission_ceiling`, tier 1 Earth Orbit to 6 Interstellar) and the mission it sets (`var:sr_ai_mission_target`), and sets both itself: stepping down, the mission first and then the ceiling; climbing, the ceiling first; so neither move ever leaves a mission its ceiling doesn't allow. A mission decision's AI weight asks that the ceiling allow its mission, since script would move the method past the ceiling and the engine would then switch the program off it; for a player the decision puts Any Mission back first (*Players*, below).

**What a step costs.** The Space Program buys no construction, so the retooling surcharge (`pm_retooling`, a construction input multiplier) costs it nothing whatever the Free Market Construction setting. The mod's `on_production_method_changed` hook (`te_on_production_method_changed_retooling`, `te_construction_market_on_actions.txt`) only takes `pm_retooling` back off where a setting waives it (no retooling or no maintenance, or no AI retooling on an AI's building), and does nothing else to this building. A step down fails every milestone the higher mission carried, and their progress is lost (`space_race.md` § Programme loss); the milestones come back from zero once the program can carry them again. That is why the pass waits six months before stepping down and five years before climbing again.

**A colony holder** (traced 2026-11-16, from the script). Stepping a holder off Solar Colonization, or sending it to Earth Orbit in default or bankruptcy, does not fail Solar System Colonization: the entry's failure outcome needs no colony held (`je_space_race.txt`, the outcome after `progressbar`), and its `possible` holds for any holder, so the entry stays open, frozen.
- **What stops** is what `sr_solar_colonization_running` gates (`space_race_triggers.txt`): the bar's progress, which is kept, not reset; founding and the month's launch; the four controls and the target button (`sr_colony_sguis.txt`); the colonization events and setbacks; and the milestone's bill (cost, funding and approach, `sr_milestone_billed_solar_colonization`).
- **What goes on** is the colonies themselves. The entry's monthly pulse runs them outside that gate, for every holder, "programme or not" (`je_space_race.txt`, after `sr_set_milestone_status_base`): settlers (`sr_emig_month`, which needs a Space Program, room and an emigration step; they come from every state), module fabrication and shipments (`sr_mod_month`; the fabrication methods need only their technology), free-colony supply, colony life, and each world's own month. So contact (settlers, a module, a founding or a supplier's month landing) does not lapse because of the step.
- **What a colony costs and returns while frozen** is unchanged. Its upkeep is Space Program throughput (+0.01 a module level past the first, +0.03 a free colony supplied; `space_race_modifiers.txt`), so it is already in the bill of whatever mission runs, Earth Orbit's included. Settlers are billed apart since #1954 Part B (they were +0.05 throughput a level of emigration): 3 Launch Capacity each, `sr_settler_launches` on the Space Program, and an AI's settler step takes what fabrication's 5% leaves (`sr_emig_ai_step`, `space_race.md` § Colony population). Its returns are its specializations, its discoveries and the off-world population bonus.
- **Drift** comes only where contact lapses anyway: a full colony with no module queued, or a country in default. In default the AI's yearly reviews turn emigration off and stop fabrication (`sr_emig_ai_review`, `sr_mod_ai_review`), whatever the mission. The bankruptcy modifier alone keeps emigration on, and stops fabrication only with no weekly surplus. A lapse adds 0.25 a month to the pressure, and autonomy settles at 100 times the pressure, a hundredth of the gap a month (`sr_colony_autonomy_values.txt`).
  - A 50,000-person Mars colony by chemical drive settles at 9, or 34 without contact. At war as well it settles at 59, passing the ask at 50 after about 14 years.
  - Breaking away at 100 needs a pressure over 1, for example 100,000 people at Jupiter, without contact and at war. Even then the ask at 50 comes after about four and a half years, and an AI that still has a program lets the colony go only at weight 1 (`space_race_events.94`).
- **The five years.** During the wait the ceiling stays at the held mission, so neither the pass nor Begin Solar Colonization (whose AI weight asks that the ceiling allow it) puts a holder back on colonization. Afterwards it climbs back only if Solar Colonization's bill is within 10%. A holder therefore founds nothing new for at least five years, while its colonies go on.
- Nothing is stranded: the entry stays open, its progress is kept, and climbing back resumes it. In the 1958 save no AI country held a colony; only the player did.

**What pulling a program down does** (read from the script, 2026-11-16).
- **The removal.** It is the mod's own, a state's `remove_building = building_space_program`, as the capital rule's (`te_on_state_owner_change`, `extra_on_actions.txt`). Every running milestone fails as any lost program's does, with The Silent Gantry for the country (`space_race_events.76`, which the AI doesn't see), and comes back from zero once a program can carry it again (`sr_reentry_monthly`). A `TE_SR_AI_PROGRAM demolished` line in `debug.log` gives the country, its fixed income and the Earth Orbit bill.
- **A colony holder.** Its Solar System Colonization entry stays open (`possible`'s first branch) and its colonies stay held, with their modifiers. Settlers, modules and free-colony supply all need a Space Program (`sr_emig_month`, `sr_mod_ai_review`, `sr_free_can_supply`), so they stop, contact lapses after twelve months, and autonomy climbs as *A colony holder* above describes. At the ask at 50, an AI with no program most often keeps the colony under a charter, and otherwise lets it go as a free colony on good terms (`space_race_events.94`). The weights: the charter 40, plus 40 for any holder (`sr_ai_tends_colonies`), 80; the garrison 5, or 65 at war; let it go 1, plus 60 with no program, 61. At peace that is about 55% charter and 42% let go if the engine draws by weight, and the charter every time if it takes the highest. Letting it go is the existing orderly hand-on; a charter keeps the colony held, without contact. Either way nothing is stranded: a colony under 1,000 people has no pressure and stays held, and a program founded later supplies it again.
- **Other buildings.** A standing Space Command stays, since its `possible` keeps an existing one (`05_zz_te_sr_space_command.txt`), and so do its fleets (`sr_fleet_power` doesn't read the program); a new Space Command needs a program. The launch-site test (`sr_launch_site_lost`) needs a program, so it simply stops applying. The program's share of the debris index goes with it (`te_debris_uses_orbit`).
- **What a player sees.** The rival leaves the Rivals rows of the milestones it was running (`sr_is_rival` reads `sr_active_<m>`) and stays in the Space Powers standings with what it finished (`sr_space_race_participant`). Nothing else.
- **Founding again.** The five years match the mission wait and the retooling. The means test already asks Earth Orbit within 10% where the demolition came at 20%, so founding again needs the income to have doubled; a longer wait would guard only against a boom that large. It answers the owner's watch item of 2026-10-05 (`space_race.md`, "Stays demolishable": an AI that founds, demolishes and founds again). It is consistent with that ruling too: the building stays demolishable, and now an AI that can't pay pulls its own down.

**Players.** Never reviewed. A player's program stays on Any Mission. A country a player takes over from the AI keeps the AI's ceiling until the next monthly pulse, which puts Any Mission back and leaves the mission alone. Until then its panel offers no mission above the ceiling, and with the Mission Limit row hidden nothing on the panel says why; that is accepted, since it lasts a month at most. A Begin decision puts Any Mission back before it switches the mission (`sr_ai_mission_ceiling_lift`): script passes the ceiling's lock, so the decision would otherwise leave the program on a mission its ceiling doesn't allow, and the engine would switch it off, failing its milestones. The funding gates read `is_ai = yes`.

**Decided by the owner, second round (2026-11-16).** He was given a recommendation on each point then open, each of them to keep what was built, and answered "I'm happy with your recommendations":

## AI Conscript Refresh

Once a year an AI country at peace has its conscription centers removed where they hold conscripts far out of date, and the AI rebuilds them in its formations' current unit types (#1877 trap 5; the owner chose it on 2026-11-18: the old conscripts "don't contribute much to their combat forces but still take casualties", and 1960s armies read as musket levies, "worse the later it gets").

- **Why it is needed.** The AI keeps each formation's default unit types at its best unlocked type, so new conscripts come in modern, but it never upgrades a conscript battalion it already has. In the owner's 1964 save, 38% of the 20 largest AI economies' conscript battalions were Irregular Infantry, against 4% of their barracks battalions, and Japan's 100 conscripts were the same as in 1946. No effect changes a battalion's type or disbands one. Removing a state's conscription center deletes its conscripts, though, and an AI at peace rebuilt both centers it lost within a month, in modern types (measured 2026-11-18; `docs/guides/scripting_best_practices.md`).
- **Who and when** (`te_ai_conscript_refresh_may`). AI countries, when not at war, in no diplomatic play (committed or undecided) and with no army mobilized, so that no raised battalion is deleted and no country is short of conscripts as a war starts. A country looks each month and runs the pass the first month its gate holds, then not for a year (`te_ai_conscript_refresh_year`), so a great power often in a play does not miss whole years. Fleets don't count: a fleet's mobilization says nothing about conscripts. Players are exempt: a player upgrades conscripts in the formation panel and may keep cheap ones on purpose.
- **Which centers** (`te_ai_conscript_center_stale`). One holding a battalion three or more eras behind a type of its own unit group that the country has unlocked (`te_ai_conscript_unit_stale`), in a state not cleared in the last five years (`te_ai_conscript_cleared`, a five-year state variable, so that a center the AI rebuilds stale is not cleared every year). A type's era is its earliest unlocking technology's; Irregular Infantry is era 0. So Irregular and Line Infantry are stale once Trench Works is researched, Skirmish Infantry once Squad or Mechanized Infantry is unlocked, Hussars, Dragoons, Cuirassiers and Lancers once Light Tanks are, Cannon and Mobile Artillery once Siege Artillery is, and Shrapnel Artillery once Motorized Artillery is. The trigger has one line per old type, and `test_ai_conscript_refresh.py` derives the lines from the unit-type and technology data, so a new unit type fails it until its line is written.
- **What it costs.** A raised modern conscript buys goods (Armored Infantry about 1,100 a week at base prices) where Irregular Infantry buys none, so an AI's bill in a war rises, and a goods shortage cuts organization. The rebuild follows the formation's defaults in every unit group, so the mix can change: the Dutch state rebuilt in the measurement held no infantry.
- **Reading it.** `TE_SELF_CHECK … ai_conscript_refresh` follows the first center cleared in a game for three months, and `TE_SELF_CHECK … ai_conscript_refresh_rule` reads, once a game and before anything is cleared, whether the stale rule catches a center holding Irregular or Line Infantry in a country with Trench Works, so that a pass that never fires is not silent; the play-test list (`docs/testing/ai-economy-playtest.md` § The AI conscript refresh) has its row and the checks.
