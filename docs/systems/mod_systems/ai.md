# AI Weights and Company AI

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

## AI Weights Guidelines

- **Journal entries:** `weight` determines priority in the JE list. Global issues use 1000+, personal/national issues use 10.
- **Scripted buttons:** `ai_chance = { value = 0 ... }` — base value with conditional `add` modifiers.
- **Diplomatic actions:** Key fields: `evaluation_chance`, `will_propose`, `propose_score`, `will_break`.
- **Treaty articles:** Key fields: `article_ai_usage = { offer request none }`, `evaluation_chance`, `inherent_accept_score`.
- Always look at **vanilla files** for AI pattern reference.

## Company AI

The engine picks one company at a time for the AI to work toward: among the types whose `ai_will_do` holds (and at least `attainable`), weighted by `ai_weight` (engine default 1) times its `NAI` `COMPANY_TYPE_*` factors (stickiness ×3 for 60 months, a formable bonus, predicted productivity, competition with companies already held). While planning it builds toward the type's `ai_construction_targets`. Numbers live in `common/script_values/te_company_ai_values.txt`.

- **Weights.** Vanilla gives its flavored companies 3 and generic ones nothing (1). The mod's 82 flavored companies get `te_company_ai_weight_flavored` (10), plus `te_company_ai_weight_home` (10) while the country holds one of the company's `preferred_headquarters` states; its 11 new generic companies get `te_company_ai_weight_generic` (3). `REPLACE:`/`INJECT:` entries are vanilla's companies and keep vanilla's weight.
- **Construction targets need a `state_trigger`.** Vanilla's `companies.md`: a target with an empty state trigger does nothing. Every mod target now names the company's preferred HQ states, the capital, or a state that already has the building (that last one covers mines and rigs whose resource the capital lacks). `test_company_ai.py` fails on a target without one, a mod flavored company without `ai_weight`, or a buildable flagship without `ai_value`.
- **Flagships** (`common/buildings/company_buildings.txt`) carry `ai_value = te_company_flagship_ai_value` (50000, vanilla's top tier). Their only max level comes from the company's prosperity bonus (on at 100, off below 75), so only a prosperous company's flagship can be built at all. Only the state's owner builds one (`te_company_flagship_builder_owns_state` in `possible` and `can_build_government`), so investment rights abroad never reach another country's flagship. Before the `ai_value`, the AI built 9% (2029 observer save) to 14% (2073 save) of its prosperous companies' flagships. If `ai_value` doesn't move that, the fallback is a decision that places the flagship (the Strategic Reserve hub and Space Program precedent).
- **Failing companies.** The engine's AI never disbands a company by choice (observer run 1972–2029: no surviving country dropped one company for another), so its slots fill before the mod's late-era companies unlock. `te_company_failing_monthly_effect` (`te_company_effects.txt`, on `on_monthly_pulse_country`) counts each company's consecutive monthly checks that fail either test (`te_company_fails_this_month`): prosperity below `te_company_failing_prosperity` (10), or owned levels (`company_owned_levels`) below `te_company_small_share` (0.1%) of the country's `building_levels`, saved as `scope:te_company_min_levels` before the iteration. The count lives in the company variable `te_company_months_failing`; an AI country removes one company a check once a count reaches `te_company_failing_months` (60, about 4.9 years), then runs the flagship cleanup. The size test catches a prosperous company too small to matter to a large economy; since the engine dissolves companies under 5 levels anyway, it only bites above 5,000 country levels. Together they stand in for "profit below 0.1% of GDP for five years", which script cannot read (no company profit trigger or link): on the 2073 save, of 406 AI companies with five years of history, 102 fell under the profit rule; prosperity alone flags 50 (41 of the 102), both tests 83 (52 of the 102, the rest earning a median 0.34% of GDP). Players are exempt; it models the AI choosing what a player chooses in the companies panel.
- **Flagship subsidies.** The engine's AI almost never changes a subsidy itself. It keeps the ones a building gets by default (ports, railways, power plants) and adds none: on the 2073 save it subsidized 0 of its 23 company buildings (the player 20 of 20), 0 of 140 shipyards, and 0 of 250 airports and highways outside the laws that force them. 12 of the 23 were under half staffed, some losing workers to subsidized buildings nearby, since a building that is not subsidized stops hiring below `BUILDING_DEFAULT_MIN_EARNINGS_TO_HIRE_EMPLOYEES`. `te_company_ai_subsidy_monthly_effect` (`te_company_effects.txt`, same monthly on-action) subsidizes every `bg_company_buildings` building of an AI country that is not in default, and removes those subsidies in default, because the AI never drops one on its own (in default it still paid for 60 of 61 railways). A subsidy only tops a building up to `SUBSIDIES_MIN_GAIN` (0), so a profitable flagship costs nothing. Players are exempt, as for dissolution. The mod's `country_building_*_require_subsidies_bool` forced-subsidy modifiers (airports and highways under Interventionism and Command Economy) held on only 35 of 52 such AI buildings in that save, so this uses `set_subsidized` on each building instead.
- **Verifying in an observer run:** company counts per country and companies lost while the country survived, from saves (the 2026-10-06 study's decoders: company records key `company_type` 0x5c80, prosperity 14193, `prosperous` 24210), and flagship counts with `scripts/analysis/save_world_report.py buildings`. Flagship subsidies: in a plain-text save each building record under `building_manager` carries `subsidized=yes` when subsidized; an AI flagship should have it unless its country is `in_default`.
