# Local Climate

Part of the [mod systems reference](../mod_systems.md), which indexes every system.

Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-local-climate-design.md`; plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-01-local-climate.md`.

## The Climate Record

- **What:** each land state region's climate where most of its people live, nine figures per region. Every piece of a split region reads the region's record, since the values are keyed on `state_region`.
- **Fields** (`scripts/generators/data/local_climate.csv`; the generated values in brackets):
  - `summer_mean_c`: the warmest month's mean temperature, °C. CSV only since heat v2: the generator checks the hottest day against it and `--plausibility` reads it, but the formula doesn't.
  - `summer_humidity` (`te_climate_summer_humidity`): that month's dew point as a class, 0 dry (under 10 °C), 1 moderate (10–16), 2 humid (16–20), 3 oppressive (over 20); the tile's words are Dry, Moderate, Humid and Sultry. A description only since heat v2, which carries humidity in the heat index.
  - `winter_mean_c` (`te_climate_winter_mean`): the coldest month's mean, °C.
  - `aridity` (`te_climate_aridity`): water for settlement, 0 ample, 1 limited (Köppen BS), 2 scarce (Köppen BW). A great river whose valley holds the main settled area counts as ample, so the Nile valley is 0; a desert whose settled area a lesser river or a large oasis waters is 1 (Lima, Isfahan); a desert of small oases and wells is 2 (Ghardaia). Ruled 2026-11-01 when the eight agents' rows were merged.
  - `malaria` (`te_climate_malaria`): malaria as of 1836, from history rather than vanilla's traits. 0 none, 1 seasonal or local, 2 endemic. Local ague in a region's lowland counts as 1 (the Sologne, the unregulated Rhine plain, the Mälaren basin), as do the Hungarian plain and the Yangtze lowland's vivax.
  - `latitude` (`te_climate_latitude`): signed degrees of the reference place.
  - `hot_day_c` (`te_climate_hot_day`): the hottest day of a typical year, °C: the mean over years of each year's highest daily maximum. The tile's Hot days row.
  - `heat_index` (`te_climate_heat_index`): heat stress on a typical year's worst day: each day's 0.7 × wet-bulb (Stull's formula, from the day's maximum and dew point) + 0.3 × maximum, and the mean over years of each year's highest. The heat term reads it.
  - `winter_sun_h` (`te_climate_winter_sun`): mean daily hours of bright sunshine in the three months around the winter solstice (December to February north of the equator, June to August south), to 0.1 h. The tile's Sunshine row. From satellite data, the same way for every row (below, *Winter sunshine*).
  - `heat_source`: where the two heat figures come from: a station (`KSVN:1km`, its code and distance), a station with one figure from the fit (`;fit_hot_day`, `;fit_index`), `fit` or `analog:STATE_X` (whose row it must copy). CSV only.
  - `ref_place` and `reason`: the place the figures describe and a short note on the climate. CSV only, never shown in game.
- **Neutral defaults:** a region with no row (one a vanilla patch adds) reads a hottest day of 20, heat index 0, winter 15, London's 2.4 h of winter sun and zero for the rest, which costs no comfort, and `te_climate_has_record` is 0.
- **Generator:** `scripts/generators/gen_local_climate.py` writes `common/script_values/local_climate_generated.txt` (one if-chain on `state_region` per field). `--check` exits 1 when the committed file is stale; `--plausibility` lists rows worth a second look (a warm winter at a high latitude, a cool tropical highland, oppressive humidity with scarce water). Re-run after editing the CSV.
- **Checks** (`test_local_climate.py`): every land region of the mod's `map_data/state_regions/` (not `99_seas.txt`) has exactly one row, in map order; values in range (summer −10…45, winter −50…30, winter ≤ summer; hottest day 10…52 and no cooler than the warmest month; heat index 5…40 and no hotter than the hottest day; winter sun 0…12 h and no more than the quarter's daylight at the row's latitude, `winter_day_hours`); `heat_source` in one of its forms (station, station with one fitted figure, `fit`, an analog copying its source's row); vanilla's `state_trait_malaria` needs malaria ≥ 1 and `state_trait_severe_malaria` needs 2; a `state_trait_*_desert` needs aridity ≥ 1, except the regions in `DESERT_TRAIT_EXCEPTIONS`, whose people live by a great river or outside the desert (Aswan, Perth, the Top End): the trait describes the land, the record where people live. Adelaide, on the semi-arid edge, keeps 1. Where vanilla's trait is plainly wrong, the mod takes it away rather than bend the record: Fiji, Tonga and Namaqualand lose Severe Malaria, and Zambezi and Botswana swap it for the mild trait, through `state_trait_config.json`'s `-state_trait_x` entries (owner, 2026-11-01; it also speeds their colonization, which the owner judges too slow). The winter agrees with vanilla's cold (below).
- **Production:** the 2026-11-01 table was filled by eight subagents from climate normals for each region's main settled area, then validated and spot-checked. Fifteen anchor rows (Georgia, West Bengal, the Home Counties, Île-de-France, Catalonia, Lazio, Lower Andalusia, the Sahara, Arizona, Yakutsk, Massachusetts, Ohio, Florida, Middle Egypt, Moscow) are the calibration's fixed points. Two predate the merge's malaria ruling and stay as calibrated: the Home Counties (London, 0, though Essex and Kent had marsh ague) and Catalonia (Barcelona, 0, though the Ebro delta had malaria).
- **Heat figures** (heat v2, 2026-11-01; spec § 11.2): Wolfram `WeatherData` daily maximum and dew point, 2010–2020, at the first of the six stations nearest the reference place with six years of both (2000–2020 where none had). Better stations replaced the nearest for South Tyrol, Calabria, Piedmont and New York; the Eastern Himalayas take the Himalayas' figures. Where the station is over 150 km away or 300 m above or below the place and misses a least-squares fit on the record's other fields by 2.5 or more, the row takes the fit for that figure: 17 rows for both (`heat_source = fit`) and 5 for one (`KSVN:312km;fit_hot_day`, `;fit_index`; the per-figure rule came with the winter axis, after #1312's review found far stations' hot days such as Kumasi's 39 °C). Mogadishu keeps Wajir's 39.4 °C hot day from 584 km inland, within 2.5 of a fit that can't see the coast. Adelaide and Melbourne keep their stations' high figures, which are real heat waves. The pull is `scripts/analysis/local_climate_heat_pull.wl`, run through the Wolfram tool in batches; a new row takes its station's figures the same way, or the fit.
- **Winter sunshine** (2026-11-06; spec § 13): `scripts/analysis/local_climate_sun_pull.py` (`--report`, `--write`; network: `power.larc.nasa.gov`, which this repo's cloud environment allows). Station sunshine normals cover only some regions and their instruments disagree, so every row is estimated from NASA POWER's 2001–2025 monthly climatology (CERES SYN1deg, about 1°) at the region's point in `scripts/analysis/local_climate_sun_coords.csv`: the heat figures' station where it lies within 60 km, else the reference place. Each winter month's sunny share of its daylight is a line in all-sky over top-of-atmosphere sunlight, all-sky over clear-sky, and a snow term (the record's coldest month, 0 at 0 °C to 1 at −10 °C, times the first ratio: snow under cloud reflects light back down, so a snowy overcast lets more through than its sunshine suggests), fitted to 80 stations' normals (`local_climate_sun_reference.csv`, approximate WMO and national figures). It misses a held-out station by 0.53 h a day (root mean square). The 11 American stations are listed but not fitted: their records, from the National Weather Service's older sunshine recorders, read about 0.9 h a day above every other country's against the same satellite data. London reads 2.4 h, so London is the neutral point by the same measure as every row. The estimate runs high where a thin overcast lets sunlight through without sun (Lima's garúa 2.4 h against a station's 1.4, Chengdu 2.5 against 1.6, Yakutsk 2.9 against 2.0) and low under clear but hazy skies (Delhi). The API's cache is `build/local_climate_power_cache.json` (gitignored). A new row takes its point in the coordinates file, then a `--write` run.
- **Vanilla 1.15's regions** (#1407): Tula and Kostroma are new, and Oryol lost Tula. Oryol's and Kostroma's rows first copied Tula's and Yaroslavl's; they now hold their own stations' records (2026-11-06): Oryol UUOR at 6 km (hottest day 34.2 °C, heat index 25.8; July 19.8 °C, January −6.0, 1991–2020) and Kostroma WMO27242 at 87 km, the nearest with six years of dew points (32.8 °C, 25.7; July 19.0, January −9.6; the city's own station, with eleven Januaries, reads −9.5).
- **Vanilla's cold** (#1407). Vanilla 1.15 gives each state region a cold for the peak of winter (`max_cold_effect`, with a `min_cold_effect` for the rest of the year; 525 of the 676 land regions), moderate from 0.5 and severe from 0.75, which slows fleets and supply ships and cuts the world market access of a state whose hub's sea freezes. It is fixed per region: nothing in script moves it, so Global Warming warms the record's winters but not vanilla's cold. The two agree across the map, a straight line through the record's winter and latitude missing vanilla's figure by 0.12 (root mean square). `cold_band_flags` (`gen_local_climate.py`) names a row that disagrees in kind: a winter above freezing under severe cold, one milder than London's 3 °C under moderate cold, or one of −10 °C or harder where vanilla's cold isn't moderate. Nine do, each accepted in `COLD_BAND_EXCEPTIONS` with its reason: four regions whose people live in a valley or on a coast below the cold vanilla's figure covers (Kathmandu, Punakha, Dushanbe, and Alaska's Sitka), and five hard, dry winters where vanilla's cold runs low (Urumqi, Hohhot, Mukden, Jilin, Kars). `test_local_climate.ColdBand` fails on a new disagreement and on a stale exception; `--plausibility` lists all nine. North Rhine is the one cold-wintered land region vanilla gives no cold pair at all (Cologne, 2 °C, beside neighbors at 0.49 to 0.56); the mod passes vanilla's map fields through unchanged, so the gap stays vanilla's.

## Comfort

- **What:** a cut to a state's migration attraction for its climate, in percent (`state_migration_pull_mult`), or a small bonus where a mild or sunny winter outweighs the rest (spec § 12, § 13). Mass migration attraction is the average of a country's eligible states' attraction, so it reaches mass migration too. Percentages add to the state's others before they multiply its base, and attraction floors at 0.
- **Why percent** (heat v2, owner, 2026-11-01; spec § 11.1): phase 2's points, shared by every state of a hot, poor market, could pin its poorer states at 0 and erase the gaps pops move for. A percentage scales each state's attraction, so the gaps survive in proportion.
- **Formula** (`common/script_values/local_climate_values.txt`, state scope; spec § 11.3), in percent:
  - summer warming = `temperature_anomaly_display` − `te_climate_normals_warming`; winter warming = that × amplification(|latitude|)
  - x = max(0, heat index + 0.8 × summer warming − 26)
  - heat = (4.1 x + 0.36 x²) × (1 − heat relief), so the eighth degree over 26 costs a little over twice the first
  - winter' = winter + winter warming
  - cold (frost) = max(0, 0 − winter') × 0.94 × (1 − cold relief)
  - winter (mildness) = (max(0, 3 − winter') − max(0, min(winter', 15) − 3) × summer share) × 0.6 × (1 − winter relief): a winter like London's in 1836 (3 °C) is neutral, a harsher one costs, a milder one draws, up to 7.2% at 15 °C and over (owner, 2026-11-01)
  - summer share = max(0, 1 − heat before relief / 20): a hot summer cancels a mild winter's draw, so Khartoum or Singapore never out-draw London once air conditioning eases their heat (owner, 2026-11-01, after #1313's review)
  - dry = 16 × water class × (1 − water relief)
  - disease = 5 × malaria class × (1 − disease relief)
  - sun (winter sunshine) = (max(0, 2.4 − sun) − max(0, min(sun, 6) − 2.4) × summer share) × 1 × (1 − sun relief), sun the winter's daily hours: London's 2.4 h is neutral, each hour gloomier costs 1% (at most 2.4 at polar night), each hour brighter draws 1% up to 6 h (at most 3.6), and a hot summer cancels the draw as it cancels winter's (owner, 2026-11-06; spec § 13). Denver's 5.5 h draws 3.1%, Seattle's 2.2 h costs 0.2, Bergen's 1.3 h costs 1.1. Warming leaves it alone.
  - `te_climate_penalty` = the sum, capped at 80, and 0 for a region with no row. Negative only through the winter and sun terms, so never below −10.8; `te_climate_bonus` is its negative part, as a positive percent.
- **Warming:** the record holds today's normals, about 1.1 °C of warming in, so a region reads its record exactly when the world's felt anomaly reaches `te_climate_normals_warming` (1.1). 1836, at anomaly 0, runs 1.1 °C colder on its hot days and 1.1 °C × amplification colder in winter (owner, 2026-11-01).
  - Amplification is 1 up to 30° of latitude, rising in a straight line to 2.5 at 66.5° and beyond. Winters alone take it, since Arctic amplification is a cold-season effect; with it on the hot days Yakutsk's cost rose with warming (spec § 11.3).
  - The heat index rises 0.8 per degree of warming, relative humidity holding about steady.
  - With the Global Warming rule off the anomaly stays 0, and every region keeps its 1836 climate.
  - The felt anomaly is used because an aerosol haze cools the air people live in. It can go negative when carbon removal passes zero.
- **Constants** sit at the head of the values file, and each is a script value: `te_climate_heat_start` (26), `_heat_linear`, `_heat_quadratic`, `_index_per_degree`, `_cold_start`, `_cold_weight`, `_winter_neutral`, `_winter_mild`, `_winter_per_degree`, `_winter_heat_cancel`, `_dry_per_class`, `_malaria_per_class`, `_penalty_max`, `_sun_neutral`, `_sun_full`, `_sun_per_hour`, `_amp_low_lat`, `_amp_high_lat`, `_amp_max`, `_normals_warming`.
- **Terms stay separate** (`te_climate_<term>_raw`, `te_climate_<term>_term`) all the way to the tile, because each has its own technologies. The `_term`s aren't gated on `te_climate_has_record`, but the tile's rows are, so a no-row region shows none.

## Relief

- **Types** (`common/modifier_type_definitions/local_climate_modifier_types.txt`, `script_only`, percent): `state_climate_heat_relief_add`, `_cold_relief_add`, `_winter_relief_add` (Winter Relief: it closes the gap between a raw winter and a mild one, both ways), `_water_relief_add`, `_disease_relief_add`, `_sun_relief_add` (Winter Sun Relief: the gap between a gloomy winter and a sunny one, both ways). `te_climate_relief_<kind>` reads each at state scope and clamps it to 0–1; disease relief also adds the census's `state_malaria_control_add`, which Malaria Prevention carries at 1.

## The Modifier and Its Refresh

- **Modifier:** `local_climate_discomfort` (`common/static_modifiers/local_climate_modifiers.txt`), `state_migration_pull_mult = -0.01` (one percent), applied with `multiplier = var:te_climate_penalty_applied`; and `local_climate_amenity`, `state_migration_pull_mult = 0.01`, applied with `multiplier = var:te_climate_bonus_applied` when the total is a bonus. Two fixed-sign modifiers, not a negative multiplier, which no one has seen work (monetary policy's `te_fx_weak`/`_strong` chose the same). Each is plain "Local Climate" in loc, with the concept link in its `_desc`, because the state panel hides it by comparing `TimedModifier.GetName` with `Localize()`.
- **Refresh** (`te_update_local_climate_modifier`, `common/scripted_effects/local_climate_effects.txt`): remove the old copy, store `te_climate_penalty` in `te_climate_penalty_applied`, its negative part in `te_climate_bonus_applied` (`te_climate_bonus`'s formula, worked from the stored penalty so the penalty isn't computed twice) and the penalty before any relief, `te_climate_penalty_raw` (the six raw terms, capped at 80), in `te_climate_base_penalty`, then add the cost when the penalty is above 0.5, or else the bonus when that is above 0.5, the tile's gate too (a smaller cut would show as −0%). No variable is ever removed. The Map Room's Current and Base Comfort maps read the first and the third (`journal_map_modes.md`), stored because a map paints every state and the live values read nine `state_region` chains.
- **Old saves:** a state from before the sunshine term (2026-11-06) takes it at its next yearly refresh; the tile's Comfort hover shows today's parts, the sunshine among them, at once. A state carries phase 2's points in `te_climate_penalty_applied` until its yearly refresh, read as percent meanwhile. That errs low almost everywhere (Georgia −15%, against −28%) but high where phase 2's heat overcounted a steady climate (Hawaii about −8%, against −1%). No repair: it lasts at most a year. A state from before the Map Room's Base Comfort map (2026-11-06), or from before the winter axis (§ 12), refreshes within a month: the first application keys on `te_climate_base_penalty`, the newest variable every refresh sets.
- **Sites** (`common/on_actions/local_climate_on_actions.txt`): the yearly refresh, `local_climate_yearly_on_action` on `on_yearly_pulse_state`, the one refresh site; the first application, `local_climate_first_on_action` on `on_monthly_pulse_state`, for states without `te_climate_base_penalty` (a new game's first month, a save from before it, a new state object), since `on_game_started` has no ROOT for the multiplier. A state that changes owner keeps its penalty until its yearly pulse.
- **Hidden** from the state panel's modifier rows (both `TimedModifier.GetName` filter lines in `gui/states_panel.gui`, and `gui_state_hidden_modifier_count`), both modifiers, since the tile shows them; `NOT_ON_COUNTRY` in `scripts/generators/gen_nation_tab.py`.
- **The define:** `MASS_MIGRATION_TARGET_MIN_ARABLE_LAND = 1` in `common/defines/extra_defines.txt`'s first `NPops` block. It is 1, not 0, because the engine rejects 0 at load (`defines.cpp:167 … Must be greater than 0`, the first launch with #1308); every state region has at least 2 arable land, so 1 lets them all in, so city-states and small partial states can be mass migration targets; climate now keeps deserts out early instead. Vanilla's few-target-states penalty (`MASS_MIGRATION_NUM_TARGET_STATES_FOR_NO_PENALTY` 4, `MASS_MIGRATION_FEW_TARGET_STATES_PENALTY` 0.2) is kept (owner, 2026-11-01). `concept_migration_target_desc` is overridden in `localization/english/replace/` without the floor's clause.
- **Log line** (`local_climate_log_on_action` on `on_yearly_pulse_country`; informational, not a self-check): each year the first country to pulse samples the mass migration targets, and each decade `TE_LOCAL_CLIMATE yearly_targets_summed=N under_10_arable=M years=Y date=…` prints the sums. `under_10_arable` above 0 shows the define at work.

## The Local Climate Tile

- **Where:** a `state_panel_status_item_small` in `gui/states_panel.gui` after Migration Crowding, always shown; its contents are the type `te_state_climate_tile` in `gui/te_state_panel_widgets.gui`.
- **Rows** (`te_state_climate_row`: 80 px of label, 76 of value, since "Humidity" and "Seasonal" overrun the standard tile rows): Hot days (°C, the hottest day), Humidity (Dry, Moderate, Humid, Sultry), Winter (°C), Sunshine (hours a day around midwinter, `te_climate_winter_sun`), Water (Ample, Limited, Scarce), Malaria (None, Seasonal, Endemic). The words come from nested `SelectLocalization` on the class. A region with no row shows `TE_STATE_CLIMATE_NO_DATA` instead.
- **Comfort row:** what the modifiers apply, from the yearly variables, not the live value: the cost in red (`te_climate_penalty_now`) or a mild or sunny winter's bonus in green (`te_climate_bonus_now`), in percent. None shows when neither is above 0.5, the refresh's threshold, and Pending until the state's first monthly application (`te_climate_penalty_shown`). Its hover splits it into the six terms, today's, against the total of the last yearly refresh; None has a hover of its own.
- **Hovers:** each reading's hover gives its term before relief (`_raw`), the relief (`te_climate_relief_<kind>`, as a figure; no breakdown of the `script_only` types) and the cost left (`_term`). Hot days and Winter show the climate the formula uses now (`te_climate_hot_day_eff`, `_winter_eff`), and their hovers give today's normal and the warming against it (`te_climate_summer_warming`, `_winter_warming`); Hot days' hover also gives the heat index now (`te_climate_heat_index_eff`), and Winter's gives both the frost cost and winter's mildness as what it draws (`te_climate_winter_draw_raw`, `te_climate_winter_draw`: positive a bonus), and Sunshine's gives its term the same way (`te_climate_sun_draw_raw`, `te_climate_sun_draw`) against London's hours (`te_climate_sun_neutral`). Figures the text quotes, the heat threshold and the cap, are spliced from the constants. Humidity's hover gives no cost, since the heat index already holds humidity.
- **Concept:** `concept_local_climate` ("Local Climate"). The name is never bare "Climate", which the global warming panels, the Market panel's Climate tab and the player guide's chapter 15 already use.
- **Icon:** the tile and the concept show `gfx/interface/icons/state_status_icons/local_climate.dds`, a thermometer with a sun at its top and a snowflake at its foot, on vanilla's state-status ring (icon batch 9; the `state_status` category in `scripts/image_pipeline/icon_prompts.py`). They used vanilla's `tropical_climate.dds` until then.
- **Loc:** `TE_STATE_CLIMATE_*` and `te_state_climate_*` in `te_miscellaneous_l_english.yml`; the words are `TE_CLIMATE_HUMIDITY_0`–`3`, `TE_CLIMATE_WATER_0`–`2` and `TE_CLIMATE_MALARIA_0`–`2`.
- **Play-test list:** `docs/testing/local-climate-playtest.md`.

## Calibration

- `scripts/analysis/local_climate_calibration.py [--warming W]` mirrors the formula in Python and prints each anchor region's terms and penalty at eras 1, 4, 7, 10 and 13, and each anchor country's mean over its 1836 heartland. W is the world's anomaly; the default 0 is 1836.
- `test_local_climate_comfort.py` pins spec § 11.4's anchor targets on the mirror. It also runs `ScriptValues`, a small interpreter of `local_climate_values.txt`, over every row, era and a range of warming, against the mirror, so the game's arithmetic can't drift from the calibration unseen. An operation the interpreter doesn't know fails the test.
