## Context

**What exists** (`docs/systems/mod_systems.md` § Demographics):
- The census: one-year cohorts to 150 and a pool, five causes of death, fertility as wealth × natural × (1 − means ×
  (1 − desired)). Under Full it sets each state's births and deaths through its term M (phase 2 step 4).
- The moved lines (#892): every birth and mortality line on a law, technology or institution sits on a script-only
  type, and a fixed-sign pair per field re-applies the families' sum while the census doesn't set the state's births
  and deaths (`te_demog_lines_refresh`, generated by `gen_demographics.render_line_effects`).
- Causes of Death (#916): medicine drifts toward its targets, turmoil raises violence, the state subtab shows why.
- In flight: Late Marriage and Birth Spacing (#906, branch `feat/demog-late-marriage`). It adds two practices that
  multiply children per woman beside natural fertility, outside the means, and edits the step order, the fertility
  terms, the generator and `demographics_params`/`_model`/`_harness`. Phase 2's step 1 lands after it (§12).
- **Nothing in the census moves the workforce, conscription, pensions or health costs yet.** The workforce follows the
  base game's rules plus about twenty working-adult lines (§1.7).

**Rulings this design keeps** (the late-game spec's table, and #894):
- Workforce from participation: men in the working ages work at a bit under all; women from about 0 to about men's
  by the women's-rights laws, higher in the lower strata, entering through each state's class mix, since the engine

- Cash payments cost money; services cost bureaucracy; medical technology moderately raises the health bureaucracy

## 1. Step 1: the interfaces (changing no number)

Late-game §8.1 lists what phase 2 must build so the late game doesn't inherit 1836's assumptions. Step 1 builds them
all and changes no birth, death or workforce number in any game. Items 8 and 9 of late-game §8.1 (the workforce term
and conscription's floor) change numbers, so they wait for steps 2 and 4; step 1 builds their inputs.

### 1.1 Two clocks in the sweep

- **The calendar index** is the sweep's running age: `te_dg_age` (the age after the step) and `te_dg_r` (the rate
  age), with the rate groups entered by `te_demog_enter_group` when `te_dg_r` reaches the next group's start
  (`te_demog_effects.txt:1142`, `gen_demographics.py:173`).
- **The biological index** is a second local, `te_dg_ab`, set from the calendar one at each slot. Until phase 3 it
  equals the calendar age. Phase 3 subtracts the band's deficit (`te_dg_d<j>`, a reserved name) for the treated part.
- **Who reads which** (late-game §0): capacity and health need read the biological index; the working-age start
  (schooling and child labour) and pension eligibility read the calendar one. Deaths keep the running calendar
  pointer until phase 3 gives the treated part its own rates (late-game §1.3), so step 1 leaves the death and birth
  arithmetic untouched.
- **How a biological table is entered.** Before phase 3 biological ages arrive in order with the calendar ones, so a
  second pointer (`te_demog_enter_bio_band`, generated, a tree on the band index like `te_demog_enter_group`) sets the
  capacity and need locals when `te_dg_ab` reaches the next band. Phase 3 replaces the pointer with a per-slot lookup
  (a five-comparison tree on `te_dg_ab`), the form late-game §1.3 describes; the readers don't change.
- **Where the new sums run:** in `te_demog_accumulate_slot` (`te_demog_effects.txt:767`), which already adds each
  slot's people after the step to the bands at the age after the step, in both the seed and the step. So the figures
  describe the people as counted, as the bands do. The step's newborns enter in `te_demog_step_finish` and are added
  there; the pool (150+) is added in `te_demog_close_accumulators`, as the 85+ band takes it.

### 1.2 The pension age

- **One number per country**, `te_dg_pension_age` (country variable). A revolution's winner inherits it (CLAUDE.md,
  "the winner continues the nation").
- **Step 1 sets it to the Standard position's age** (`P.PENSION_AGE["standard"]`, 65) while the country has Old Age
  Pension, and removes it otherwise. It is set by the country's yearly census and by a hook on the law's activation.
  The setting itself (`te_dg_pension_pos`, five positions) and the linked positions' drift come with step 2's buttons,
  so the first choice a player makes has its bill (§2.2).
- **Every reader reads the variable** through one script value, `te_demog_pension_age` (state scope, through
  `owner`, guarded by `has_variable`). Nothing compares with a literal 65.
- **Without Old Age Pension:**
  - with no pension law, there is no pension age: people work until capacity stops them;
  - under Universal Basic Income and Post-Scarcity (`extra_laws.txt:285`, `:344`) there is no pension age either; the
    after-pension share applies from the state's old-age threshold as the stand-in for choosing leisure (late-game
    §2.2).
- **The working ages for the panel** end at the pension age, or at the old-age threshold under UBI and Post-Scarcity.
  With no pension law they have no upper end: late-game §2.1 runs them "to capacity's end", and capacity never reaches
  0 (0.1 from 85). Workers past the end still count (late-game §2.1); the end only splits the figure into its two
  factors.

### 1.3 Participation and the workforce figures

For each cohort and sex, workers = people × participation (late-game §2.1):

    participation = start(calendar age) × [below the end of the working ages (calendar) ? 1 : after-pension share]
                    × capacity(biological age) × the sex's rate

Call 3's participation inputs (§1.7) adjust two of these: the sexes' rates in the working ages and capacity, each
within 0 to 1.

The after-pension share applies only where a pension law ends the working ages (Old Age Pension, UBI,
Post-Scarcity). With none the working ages have no end, and participation at every age past the start is capacity ×
the sex's rate.

**The tables** (in `demographics_params.py`; step 2's harness fits them, step 1 uses the starting values):

| Table | Starting value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Working-age start (calendar), ages 10–14 | Child Labor Allowed 0.3, Restricted Child Labor 0.1, Compulsory Primary School 0; 1 from 15 (since 2026-10-17 a census-only line on the laws, `state_child_labor_participation_add`) | parent §7 |
| Capacity (biological) | 1 to 59, 0.95 at 60–64, 0.85 at 65–69, 0.7 at 70–74, 0.5 at 75–79, 0.3 at 80–84, 0.1 from 85 | late-game §2.1 |
| After-pension share | 0.15, × capacity | late-game §2.1 |
| Men's rate | 0.92 | late-game §2.1 |
| Women's rate | see below | #894 |

**The women's rate** (#894: from about 0 to about men's by the women's-rights laws, higher in the lower strata through
the state's class mix):

    w_law  = W_BASE + (MEN_RATE − W_BASE) × min(1, S_w ÷ W_FULL)
    w      = lower × max(w_law, W_LOWER) + (1 − lower) × w_law

- `S_w` is the state's women's-work family (`modifier:state_demog_womens_work_ratio_add`, §1.7): the women's-rights
  laws, the Pill, second-wave feminism, personal appliances, State-Sponsored Family Planning and Communal
  Child-Rearing, at their base-game sizes. Reading the lines themselves gives #894's "maybe technology" for free and
  keeps the base game's own scale of each law.
- `lower` is the share of the state's people in the lower strata, from the yearly walk (`strata = lower`).
- Starting values: `W_BASE` 0.05 (middle and upper strata under No Women's Rights), `W_LOWER` 0.2 (the lower strata's
  women under any law: fields, mills, domestic service), `W_FULL` 0.30 (Protected Class with the three era-6/7
  technologies: women at the men's rate).
- So 1836 under No Women's Rights in a state of four-fifths lower strata: w ≈ 0.17; today under Protected Class with
  the technologies: w = 0.92 everywhere. Women in the Fields alone (+0.3, the largest single line) also reaches 0.92:
  the base game's own size for farm women's work, kept.

**The figures** (state variables, computed at every seed and step wherever the census runs, applied nowhere in step 1):

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `te_dg_wa_share` | the working-age share: people from the working-age start to the end of the working ages ÷ all people |
| `te_dg_at_work` | the share at work: all workers (past the end included) ÷ the working-age people. It can pass 1 where those past the end far outnumber the working ages (a fixed pension age under immortality); stored uncapped so the product stays the workforce, and the panel shows at most 100% with the rest on hover (§2.6) |
| `te_dg_pensioners` | people at or above the pension age ÷ all people, under Old Age Pension only (0 otherwise) |
| `te_dg_low_share` | the lower strata's share of people, from the walk |
| `te_dg_wbase` | the pops' own base ratios, people-weighted, from the walk: aristocrats 0.2, slaves 0.5, the rest the define's 0.25 (vanilla `pop_types`) |
| `te_dg_wf_engine` | the engine's workforce ÷ people, from the walk (a pop's `workforce` reads as a value: `te_debug_demog_values.txt`'s option f probe) |

`te_dg_wa_share × te_dg_at_work` is the model's workforce share (workers ÷ people); `te_demog_wf_target` (a script
value, §1.8) puts it on the engine's scale. `te_dg_working_share` (15–64, the overview's figure) stays as it is.

### 1.4 Health need

- **A weight table by biological age** (late-game §5.2, after American health spending per person by age): 0–4 1.0,
  5–14 0.5, 15–44 0.8, 45–64 1.4, 65–74 2.4, 75–84 3.4, 85+ 4.5.
- **The figure** `te_dg_need`: the people-weighted weight ÷ the 1836 reference (`P.HEALTH_NEED_1836`, starting 0.95,
  §1.8's sketch of the 1836 structure; step 2's harness sets it from the 1836 save's world mean). 1.0 is 1836's need.
- Step 1 stores it; step 2's health bill reads it.

### 1.5 The old-age threshold

- **The age with 15 years of life left** (Sanderson and Scherbov; late-game §1.5), women's and men's averaged, from the
  census's own life table: `te_dg_old_age`.
- **How:** the life table's forward pass (`te_demog_life_table`, `gen_demographics.py:267`) keeps survivors and
  person-years at each group's start from 40 up as locals; remaining life at a start is (all person-years − those so
  far) ÷ survivors; the first group whose remaining life is 15 or less gives the age by linear interpolation from the
  group before. Every death rate past 140 is near certain, so a threshold always exists before phase 3.
- **Two readers, one variable until phase 3:** `te_demog_old_age_average` (the state's blend, which Linked to Average
  Longevity follows) and `te_demog_old_age_common` (the untreated's, Linked to Common Longevity) both read
  `te_dg_old_age`. Phase 3 adds the untreated's own figure under the second.
- Anchors from the sketch: 63 / 60 in 1836, 77 / 73 today.

### 1.6 Planned and unplanned births

- The census's fertility is already the sum of two parts (late-game §3.1): **planned** = wealth × natural × means ×
  desired, **unplanned** = wealth × natural × (1 − means). With Late Marriage and Birth Spacing (#906) the practices
  multiply beside natural, outside the means, so they scale both parts alike.
- **The model** (`demographics_model.fertility`) returns both parts; their sum is the children per woman it returns now.
- **The script** gets two script values, `te_demog_tfr_planned` and `te_demog_tfr_unplanned`, read from the stored
  `te_dg_means`, `te_dg_desired`, `te_dg_wtfr`, `te_dg_natural` (and #906's practice terms), so no variable is added.
- **Two schedules in the parameters:** `P.ASFR_PLANNED_SHAPE` (by calendar age) and `P.ASFR_UNPLANNED_SHAPE` (by
  biological age), both `ASFR_SHAPE_BY_GROUP` until phase 3. **The generator writes the sweep's birth line as one term
  while the two schedules are the same object**, which is today's script byte for byte; given two different
  schedules it writes the two-term form, which a test runs against the model. So the interface exists and is tested,
  and no game's births move by a rounding step.

### 3.3 Measures: how one works

A measure is a policy choice within a law (parent Decisions), switched on and off on the Demographics tab:
- **A button** per measure in the Family Policy section (open), beside its name and running cost. Greyed with the
  reason when the law doesn't allow it, its five-year term hasn't run (with the year it ends) or the rule isn't Full
  ("Family policy measures act only under the Full Demographics rule").
- **While on:** a country static modifier carrying its census types (script-only, read by the census at state scope;
  a state's read includes its country's modifiers, probe Q5) and its IG approvals, plus a separate `_cost` modifier
  for a money upkeep with a GDP-scaled multiplier (Expense Scaling with GDP) refreshed by the country's yearly census.
  Authority upkeep is a flat `country_authority_add` line (authority doesn't scale with size); bureaucracy upkeep is an
  institution's cost multiplier, so it scales with the country.
- **The term:** five years from switching on (`te_dg_ms_<measure>` holds the year). Crash Campaign switches itself off
  after five years and can't return for ten.
- **The ramp** is the census's own: a measure moves desired fertility or the means, and births follow at the next step;
  the cohort shows fifteen years later.
- **Leaving the law:** a measure the new law doesn't allow switches off at once, with a notice, and its term resets.
- **Revolutions:** the winner keeps the variables but not the modifiers (CLAUDE.md); the country's yearly census
  re-applies every measure whose variable is set and whose law still allows it.
- **Wording:** each description says what the measure aims at, not how hard it pushes (parent §8.1).

## 4. Step 4: conscription, the youth bulge and sex balance

All census mechanics, Full only; Display only shows the figures.

### 4.1 Conscription

- **The pool:** men of calendar age 20 or more and biological age 39 or less (late-game call 21), summed in the sweep
  through step 1's two indexes (`te_dg_pool`). Before phase 3 this is men aged 20–39.
- **The term:** T = clamp(pool ÷ people ÷ `CONSCRIPTION_REF` − 1, −1, +0.25), on `state_conscription_rate_mult`
  (`modifiers.log:1671`) by a fixed-sign pair refreshed at the state's step. `CONSCRIPTION_REF` is the 1836 world's
  share (starting 0.14, the sketch's reference; the harness takes it from the 1836 save). No floor but −1 (call 21);
  parent §7's −0.25 floor goes.
- **The engine's base is the workforce.** Potential conscripts are the conscription rate × the workforce
  (`state_conscription_rate_add`, `modifiers.log:1667`), so under Full, women entering work raise them as they do in
  the base game, and T then scales by the pool. Measuring T against the workforce instead would make conscripts follow
  men alone and cut a modern country's conscripts by about a third against the base game; not proposed.
- **Targets:** 1836 0 ± 0.05; today's rich structure about −0.25; an aged society after the era-10 ladder about −0.4;
  a young 1950s state up to the +0.25 ceiling.

### 4.2 The war dead's window

The war dead come from the same pool (call 21), not from rate ages 18–40 (`P.WAR_DEAD_AGES`, `demographics_params.py:
222`). The ring then loses its dead where the conscription term finds its soldiers. This moves the census's pyramid
under Full and Display only (the census runs under both), not the game outside Full.

### 4.3 The youth bulge

- **Measure:** the share of people of calendar age 20–29 (late-game §0: life stage) above 0.18 (1836's is about 0.17),
  times the state's unemployment (`state_unemployment_rate`): y = clamp((share − 0.18) ÷ 0.06, 0, 1) × clamp(unemployment
  ÷ 0.2, 0, 1).
- **Effect:** one state modifier with multiplier y: radicals from movements +15% and turmoil penalties +10% at full
  (`modifiers.log:1899`, `:1971`). Small on purpose.
- **Early Retirement** helps through the engine, not a special case: fewer old workers leave jobs for the young, and
  unemployment falls.
- **Targets:** 1836 states 0; a 1950s high-fertility state with 20% unemployment at most the full effect; under 5% of
  states in a 2000 save above y = 0.5.

### 4.4 Sex balance

The owner's answer sizes sex-balance effects with phase 2 ("small"); the parent's §12 had them in phase 3. They sit in
step 4 (owner, call 10). From parent §3, on adults 20–59 (`te_dg_sex_balance`, men per 100 women):
- **Fewer men than women:** births × √(men ÷ women), at most 1 (fewer marriages; a census fertility term,
  `te_demog_tfr_sex_balance`); the women's rate (§1.3) + up to 0.1 at 80 men per 100 women; feminist movement support
  + up to 10% (`state_pop_support_movement_feminist_add`, `modifiers.log:8789`).
- **More men than women** (110 or more): radicals from movements + up to 10% at 150 (Hudson and den Boer's *Bare
  Branches*).
- **Targets:** the Soviet Union of 1959 (82 men per 100 women) births −9%, women's rate +0.09; a frontier state at 150
  men the full +10%; under 2% of states at either effect in a peacetime save.
