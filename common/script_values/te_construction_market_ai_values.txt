# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# What an AI government buys a week, in construction points
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# te_construction_market_ai_update_buy writes it to the country's purchase from
# the weekly pulse of a market game. A player sets the purchase in the
# construction panel, and a direct game has no purchase.
#
# Nothing while the government queue is empty, or while the engine has paused
# the country's construction. Otherwise the larger of two amounts, never more
# than the queue can take in a week. Both can be
# nothing: a government with no surplus, no reserves and no shortage of
# bureaucracy cannot pay for anything and buys nothing, and its queued level
# waits (a market game has no base construction) until one of the three
# returns. That is intended (owner, 2026-11-16).
#
#   The surplus. Net fixed income (the budget before construction, which the
#   engine counts as a temporary expense) over the smoothed price, times
#   1 + reserves - debt, each as a share of its limit, moved by what the
#   country's interest rate asks of it (THE RATE, below), less the war and
#   banking brakes below, never under 0.1. At a middling rate, and for a
#   country the monetary update has not yet reached, that is the rule as it
#   was: with no reserves and no debt it spends the surplus exactly; reserves
#   raise it and are drawn down, debt lowers it and is paid off. At a high rate
#   it holds part of the surplus back until it has a reserve; at a low rate it
#   spends a little more than the surplus, on credit, up to a small allowance.
#
#   The floor, te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor, for a government whose
#   surplus buys less. The war and banking brakes do not touch it.
#
# The floor is an amount the country can pay. Until 2026-11-15 it was 10 points
# a week for every country: about 12,500 a week at the smoothed price, and
# 20,000 at the price paid, since the floors together asked for several times
# what the construction sectors made and held the good at its price ceiling. In
# February 1836 that was the purchase of 26 of 28 buying AI countries and more
# than the whole weekly income of 23 of them, so they borrowed to the engine's
# pause (NAI CONSTRUCTION_DEBT_PAUSE, 40% of the debt ceiling) within months. A
# debt ceiling is about 250 weeks of an AI's income, so interest on that debt,
# at the 9 to 11% a year an AI pays, takes about a fifth of its income, and a
# country with less than that to spare never gets back under the resume line
# (20%). Numbers and the long-run comparison:
# docs/systems/mod_systems/construction.md, "The AI's purchase".
#
#   A twentieth of fixed income over the smoothed price. That is the margin the
#   engine's own budget AI defends: it cuts wages and should-have spending when
#   income is under 1.05 times expenses (NAI WAGE_CUT_INCOME_RATIO_THRESHOLD,
#   MONEY_SPENDING_MAX_RATIO_TO_REMOVE_SHOULD_HAVE). Fixed income, not income,
#   which also counts temporary income: what the investment pool repays the
#   treasury for private construction is not the government's to spend.
#
#   Never more than the 10 points it was, so no country's floor rose.
#
#   Paid from reserves, not on credit: whole while the reserves are a tenth of
#   their limit or more above the reserve the rate asks for (none at a low or
#   middling rate, up to a tenth at a high one: THE RATE, below), less in a
#   straight line below that, and nothing with none to spare. So the floor
#   never spends the reserve a high rate asks a country to hold. The tenth is
#   vanilla's reserve gate on a new construction (NAI
#   CONSTRUCTION_RESERVES_NEW_CONSTRUCTIONS), which the mod lowered to 0.02
#   on 2026-11-16 (#1877, common/defines/extra_defines.txt) so that a
#   government with a surplus queues without first saving a tenth. The floor
#   keeps vanilla's tenth: a government that now queues at reserves of 0.02 to
#   0.1 draws its floor from them no faster than before, a fifth to all of
#   the twentieth, and the surplus term (below) pays the rest. A floor on
#   credit, however small, is the old floor again for a country whose surplus
#   is under it: a few points now, interest from then on, less built every
#   year after.
#
#   With no reserves, a country that has a surplus is still held to a tenth of
#   that twentieth (a 200th of fixed income, at most one point), whatever its
#   debt and the brakes take off the surplus term, and never more than the
#   surplus itself. So the surplus pays it and no part of the floor is on
#   credit. Until 2026-11-16 that tenth was owed to every country, and one with
#   no surplus bought it on credit: about 0.6 points a year for a country with
#   3,000 a week, and a fifth of its debt ceiling owed after thirty years.
#
#   Short of bureaucracy, the floor is a fifth of fixed income higher
#   (te_construction_market_ai_bureaucracy_purchase), whatever the reserves and
#   the debt. So a country short of bureaucracy buys at least a fifth of its
#   fixed income's worth; it is a floor, and adds nothing where the surplus
#   already buys more. A shortage costs tax in proportion (vanilla's
#   administrative_overburden) and the building that ends it costs 100 points.
#   The clause is from 2024, when it divided by the price held between 500 and
#   1,500 around a base of 1,000. The bounds became 50 and 150 when the base
#   was halved and stayed there when it went back to 1,000, so it bought a
#   fifth of income at a price of 150: 1.7 times the country's whole income at
#   the price in play.
#
# While the engine reports construction paused (is_construction_paused) the
# purchase is 0 for every country, whatever its bureaucracy. For a day
# (2026-11-16, #1854) a country short of bureaucracy with a Government
# Administration level in its queue went on buying that fifth through the
# pause. An observer game on that build (1836 to 1913) showed that nothing
# bought reaches the government queue while the flag is yes: in the AI probe's
# 1,056 yearly rows of a paused country the queue had used no construction,
# the 76 rows under that clause among them. What was bought went to the
# private queue, at the investment pool's expense, or went unused. So the
# clause and its self-check (TE_SELF_CHECK construction_admin_while_paused,
# which read BROKEN) were removed (owner, 2026-11-16); its queue test
# (te_construction_market_ai_building_administration) stays for the AI
# probe's cm_admin and is not read here. An indebted country
# short of bureaucracy still builds where the engine itself reports no pause:
# that game had 39 such rows at 40% of the debt ceiling or more, the floor
# above bought the fifth in all of them, and in 30 the queue was building. The
# record is in docs/systems/mod_systems/construction.md, "The AI's purchase".
#
# The purchase is not rounded (it was, to whole points, which the 10-point
# floor hid): a small economy buys a fraction of a point a week.
#
# test_construction_market_ai_purchase.py runs these values through an
# evaluator against the same rule restated in Python (which checks the
# operators and their order), tests the rule's properties apart from that, and
# holds the constants to the engine's defines.
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_INCOME_SHARE = 0.05
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_MAX_POINTS = 10
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_FULL_RESERVES = 0.1
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_MIN_FACTOR = 0.1
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_BUREAUCRACY_INCOME_SHARE = 0.2

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# THE RATE: the reserve a government keeps, and the debt it may run, by what
# borrowing costs it (#1877; owner, 2026-11-16: "If interest rates are high,
# requiring a reserve makes sense. If low, no need, and you could even allow
# spending in (small amounts of) debt.")
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
# The engine's own gates are one figure for every country (NAI
# CONSTRUCTION_RESERVES_NEW_CONSTRUCTIONS, 0.02 in this mod), so the rate acts
# here, on the purchase, and in the AI construction pass that queues for a
# government the engine will not queue for (te_ai_build_pass_effects.txt).
#
# The rate is var:te_rate_paid_pts, the rate the monetary update writes every
# month for every country under every banking setting (it is what the mod's
# interest modifier applies: te_monetary_effects.txt, step 7), less the world
# reference rate (var:te_mon_era_base_now: 3, then 2.5, then 2 by era). So it
# reads the spread lenders charge this country above the world's safe rate:
# its risk premium and its central bank's stance. Vanilla's interest
# multipliers the mod leaves alone (declared_bankruptcy's +50% and a few event
# and trait modifiers) are not in it. A country the update has not reached
# reads the middle of the curve, which is the rule as it was.
#
# The curve, a straight line between its points, so a rate that moves by its
# usual 0.2 points a month (the median monthly move in the history store of
# 18 countries, 1836-1924; 0.7 at the 90th percentile) moves it a little:
#
#   spread           required reserve      credit allowance
#   LOW or less      none                  CREDIT_MAX of the debt ceiling
#   LOW to MID       none                  falling to none
#   MID              none                  none: the rule as it was
#   MID to HIGH      rising to RESERVE_MAX none
#   HIGH or more     RESERVE_MAX           none
#
# In a financial crisis (te_mon_in_financial_crisis: the cycle's panic or
# downturn, a gold peg losing confidence, hyperinflation) the spread is read
# as HIGH at least, so a crisis always tightens the rule, whatever the rate
# does. It needs saying: in the history store the rate rose above the year
# before at some month of the year after a panic began in 25 of 33 panics, but
# its mean over that year was lower than the year before's in 16, the central
# bank cutting through the panic. No credit at war, where the war brake below
# already holds the purchase back.
#
# The surplus term's factor gains RATE_GAIN times the credit the country has
# not used, and loses RATE_GAIN times what it lacks of the reserve; only the
# reserves above the one asked for are drawn down:
#
#   1 + reserves - debt + RATE_GAIN x max(0, allowance - debt)
#       - min(reserves, required) - RATE_GAIN x max(0, required - reserves)
#
# At a high rate a country short of the reserve saves RATE_GAIN times the
# shortfall's share of its surplus, and settles at the reserve asked for,
# where it spends its surplus exactly. With a surplus and no reserves, at a
# low rate it borrows a fifth of its surplus a week at first and settles
# where the two pulls on the debt balance, at RATE_GAIN / (1 + RATE_GAIN) of
# the allowance (two-thirds: 6.7% of the ceiling), its debt holding there. It
# never borrows past the allowance; above it the debt is paid off as before.
#
# When the rate rises while it holds that debt, the allowance shrinks, the
# factor falls under 1, and the surplus pays the debt down while the queue
# goes on being bought: never under 0.1 of the surplus, and never less than
# the floor. At a high rate a country with debt 0.067 and no reserves keeps
# 0.73 of the factor it had at the middle (1 - 0.067 - 2 x 0.1). The engine's
# pause (40% of the ceiling, resuming at 20%) is not reached by anything the
# rule borrows.
#
# THE NUMBERS ARE THE OWNER'S TO CHANGE. Each one's reason:
#
#   SPREAD_LOW 2. A borrower lenders trust: the world reference rate plus the
#   0.5-point structural floor and a point or so of cyclical premium. In the
#   January 1925 save of the AI-only run (era base 2.5), 11 of the 20 largest
#   AI economies paid 2 or less above it (Britain -0.3, the United States 0.7,
#   France 1.6, Japan 2.0), 9% of all AI countries.
#   SPREAD_MID 4. Between: the 20 largest's 75th percentile was 4.3 (China).
#   SPREAD_HIGH 7. Past where a large economy pays: in 1925 only Korea (9.7,
#   at 53% of its ceiling and paused) among the 20 largest, and 63% of all AI
#   countries, whose median spread was 9.7. Most small countries borrow dear.
#   RESERVE_MAX 0.1. Vanilla's reserve gate, which this curve gives back at a
#   high rate.
#   CREDIT_MAX 0.1 of the debt ceiling. The debt at which the engine's own AI
#   starts to call debt significant and raise taxes
#   (CHANGE_TAX_SIGNIFICANT_DEBT_THRESHOLD); half the pause's resume line and
#   a quarter of the pause; under the monetary system's debt-load premium,
#   which starts at 0.25. A ceiling is about 320 weeks of a large economy's
#   fixed income (the 20 largest, 1925-1947), so the balance point is about
#   22 weeks of fixed income, about a tenth of GDP.
#   RATE_GAIN 2. How hard the rate pulls: at a high rate with no reserves the
#   surplus term buys 0.8 of what it did, and a large economy with a surplus of
#   a quarter of its fixed income closes about a third of its shortfall a year
#   (the reserve limit is about 67 weeks of its fixed income); at a low rate
#   it buys up to 1.2 of what it did. In the 1925-1947 run's rows (each
#   country's 1925 rate, moved by the debt-load premium as its debt moved),
#   while building, that is a median of 1.04 for the 20 largest (1.10 by
#   GDP) and 0.80 for all AI countries, 79% of which buy less: most small
#   countries borrow at 12% or more and are in debt, so the surplus they no
#   longer spend pays that debt down.
#   CREDIT_SERVICE_SHARE 1/3. The allowance is also never more than the debt
#   whose interest, at the rate HIGH asks for (the reference rate + 7), is a
#   third of the country's surplus: a government with a small surplus may
#   borrow only a little, and cannot borrow itself into a deficit when the
#   rate climbs. At the balance point that interest is at most 22% of the
#   surplus at that rate; at the rate paid, a median 4.7% for the 20 largest
#   (1.3% of fixed income).
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_LOW = 2
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_MID = 4
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_HIGH = 7
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_RESERVE_MAX = 0.1
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_CREDIT_MAX = 0.1
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_RATE_GAIN = 2
TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_CREDIT_SERVICE_SHARE = 0.33333

# THE PASS (common/scripted_effects/te_ai_build_pass_effects.txt). The engine
# queues nothing new for a government in debt: it holds no reserves, and its
# reserve gate asks for some after the building's cost. In the 1925-1947 run
# that was 80 of the 112 years in which one of the 20 largest AI economies had
# nothing queued. So once a month, for an AI government in a market game, at
# peace, not paused and not in default, whose queue was already empty a month
# before, the pass queues one level, where the rate allows it the means: its
# reserves above the reserve asked for, plus the credit allowance not yet
# used, are not negative. A university while innovation is under
# INNOVATION_SHARE of its cap, else a Government Administration in the state
# most short of tax capacity.
#
#   SURPLUS_SHARE 1/6 of fixed income, or SURPLUS_MIN 15,000 a week: the
#   engine's own test before it adds should-have spending (income 1.2 times
#   expenses, MONEY_SPENDING_MIN_RATIO_TO_ADD_SHOULD_HAVE, or that surplus,
#   _MIN_SURPLUS_TO_IGNORE_RATIO_FOR_SHOULD_HAVE), read on fixed income. Not
#   a judgement: it keeps the pass from queueing what the engine's budget AI
#   would call unaffordable, and leaves room above its 1.05 for cutting
#   should-have spending.
#   INNOVATION_SHARE 0.9. The engine's AI wants innovation up to its cap
#   (MONEY_SPENDING_INNOVATION_DESIRED_THRESHOLD 1.0) and a level past the cap
#   is wasted, so the pass stops a level short of it.
TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_SURPLUS_SHARE = 0.16667
TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_SURPLUS_MIN = 15000
TE_AI_BUILD_PASS_INNOVATION_SHARE = 0.9

# The rate the monetary update last wrote, less the world reference rate, in
# points a year; the curve's middle where the update has not run.
te_construction_market_ai_rate_spread = {
	value = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_MID
	if = {
		limit = {
			has_variable = te_rate_paid_pts
			has_variable = te_mon_era_base_now
		}
		value = var:te_rate_paid_pts
		subtract = var:te_mon_era_base_now
	}
	if = {
		limit = {
			te_mon_in_financial_crisis = yes
		}
		min = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_HIGH
	}
}

# The reserve the rate asks for, as a share of the reserve limit.
te_construction_market_ai_required_reserves = {
	value = te_construction_market_ai_rate_spread
	subtract = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_MID
	divide = {
		value = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_HIGH
		subtract = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_MID
	}
	min = 0
	max = 1
	multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_RESERVE_MAX
}

# The rate the allowance is sized against: the reference rate plus HIGH, as a
# fraction a year (the first era's reference rate where the update has not run).
te_construction_market_ai_credit_stress_rate = {
	value = 3
	if = {
		limit = {
			has_variable = te_mon_era_base_now
		}
		value = var:te_mon_era_base_now
	}
	add = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_HIGH
	divide = 100
}

# The debt whose interest at that rate is a third of the surplus, as a share of
# the debt ceiling (`credit`, which scaled_debt is the principal over).
te_construction_market_ai_credit_service_cap = {
	value = net_fixed_income
	min = 0
	multiply = 52
	multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_CREDIT_SERVICE_SHARE
	divide = te_construction_market_ai_credit_stress_rate
	divide = {
		value = credit
		min = 1
	}
}

# The debt the rate allows the purchase to run, as a share of the ceiling.
te_construction_market_ai_credit_allowance = {
	value = 0
	if = {
		limit = {
			is_at_war = no
		}
		value = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_MID
		subtract = te_construction_market_ai_rate_spread
		divide = {
			value = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_MID
			subtract = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_SPREAD_LOW
		}
		min = 0
		max = 1
		multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_CREDIT_MAX
		max = te_construction_market_ai_credit_service_cap
	}
}

# What the factor gains from credit not yet used.
te_construction_market_ai_credit_term = {
	value = te_construction_market_ai_credit_allowance
	subtract = scaled_debt
	min = 0
	multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_RATE_GAIN
}
# What it loses to the reserve the rate asks for: the part of the reserves it
# keeps, which is not drawn down, and RATE_GAIN times what it lacks of it.
te_construction_market_ai_reserve_term = {
	value = te_construction_market_ai_required_reserves
	subtract = scaled_gold_reserves
	min = 0
	multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_RATE_GAIN
	add = {
		value = scaled_gold_reserves
		max = te_construction_market_ai_required_reserves
	}
}

# A fifth of fixed income, in points at the smoothed price.
te_construction_market_ai_bureaucracy_purchase = {
	value = fixed_income
	multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_BUREAUCRACY_INCOME_SHARE
	divide = var:te_construction_market_price_stabilizer
}

# The twentieth of fixed income in points, never more than the floor was.
te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor_whole = {
	value = fixed_income
	multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_INCOME_SHARE
	divide = var:te_construction_market_price_stabilizer
	max = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_MAX_POINTS
}

te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor = {
	# What reserves pay: all of it at a tenth of their limit above the reserve
	# the rate asks for, nothing with none to spare.
	value = te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor_whole
	multiply = {
		value = scaled_gold_reserves
		subtract = te_construction_market_ai_required_reserves
		divide = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_FULL_RESERVES
		max = 1
		min = 0
	}
	# What the surplus pays where that is more: a tenth of it, and never more
	# than the surplus.
	min = {
		value = net_fixed_income
		min = 0
		divide = var:te_construction_market_price_stabilizer
		max = {
			value = te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor_whole
			multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_MARKET_AI_FLOOR_MIN_FACTOR
		}
	}
	if = {
		limit = {
			bureaucracy < 0
		}
		add = te_construction_market_ai_bureaucracy_purchase
	}
}

te_construction_market_ai_purchase_target = {
	if = {
		limit = {
			is_construction_paused = no
			construction_queue_num_queued_government_levels > 0
		}
		value = net_fixed_income
		min = 0
		divide = var:te_construction_market_price_stabilizer
		multiply = {
			add = scaled_gold_reserves
			subtract = scaled_debt
			add = 1
			# THE RATE, above: credit at a low rate, a reserve at a high one.
			add = te_construction_market_ai_credit_term
			subtract = te_construction_market_ai_reserve_term

			# Bankruptcy-avoidance brake: when reserves are thin under shock
			# (war or financial-cycle stress), cut spending PROPORTIONALLY
			# rather than waiting for debt to spike. Linear sliding scale —
			# avoids cliff effects that would cascade economic shocks.
			#
			# scaled_gold_reserves runs ~0 (empty) to ~1 (well-stocked).
			# Subtracting (target - reserves) when reserves < target eats
			# into the multiplier; the higher the target (war/banking stress),
			# the deeper the cut while reserves are thin.

			# Wartime: target adequate reserves of ~0.5 scaled
			if = {
				limit = {
					is_at_war = yes
					scaled_gold_reserves < 0.5
				}
				subtract = {
					value = 0.5
					subtract = scaled_gold_reserves
				}
			}

			# Banking cycle stress (high bubble pressure or active crash):
			# target ~0.3 scaled — modest extra caution
			if = {
				limit = {
					has_variable = bubble_pressure
					var:bubble_pressure >= 50
					scaled_gold_reserves < 0.3
				}
				subtract = {
					value = 0.3
					subtract = scaled_gold_reserves
				}
			}

			# Floor at 0.1× — never fully halt; AI should keep some momentum
			min = 0.1
		}
		min = te_construction_market_ai_purchase_floor
		max = {
			value = construction_queue_num_queued_government_levels
			multiply = modifier:country_max_weekly_construction_progress_add
		}
	}
	else = {
		value = 0
	}
}

# Building scope: its levels still in a construction queue

te_construction_market_building_queued_levels = {

}

# ai_value of the Construction Sector in a market game (building_construction_sector,
# common/buildings/te_construction_market_site.txt). Scope: the state the AI is
# considering building in.
#
# A production building scores its ai_value plus profit terms, and in play the AI
# underbuilt the sector: its scoring doesn't seem to credit how cheap a level is.
# A level costs construction_cost_very_low (100 points) against 600 to 800 for the
# base game's factories and 2,000 to 5,000 for the mod's heavy industry, so while
# its margin is positive it returns far more per point spent than anything else
# the AI could build. TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_BASE makes up for that; an
# ordinary factory scores 1,000 and Tooling Workshops 2,000.
#
# The need for more sectors is read from the construction good's price in the
# state's market, not from the queue. The government's purchase and the
# investors' are both divided by the price, so a queue lengthens as the price
# rises and already follows it, and a count of queued levels also follows the
# country's size. The base at base price; above it, up
# TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_PER_PRICIER per 100% (about 15,000 at the
# ceiling, above the base game's monuments at 10,000); below it, the base times
# price ÷ base price, so about 0 at the floor (0.1% of base), where sell orders
# are four times buy orders and more sectors would only deepen the glut. Floored
# at 0, so the war and banking brakes can't take it negative. Which of
# market_goods_pricier and market_goods_cheaper is clamped at 0 is undocumented,
# so each is floored at 0 (scripting_best_practices.md, "Is it signed below base
# price?").
TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_BASE = 5000
TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_PER_PRICIER = 10000

te_construction_sector_ai_value = {
	value = TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_BASE
	add = {
		value = 0
		market = {
			mg:construction = {
				add = market_goods_pricier
			}
		}
		min = 0
		multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_PER_PRICIER
	}
	subtract = {
		value = 0
		market = {
			mg:construction = {
				add = market_goods_cheaper
			}
		}
		min = 0
		multiply = TE_CONSTRUCTION_SECTOR_AI_VALUE_BASE
	}
	# At war: deprioritize construction expansion (focus existing capacity on war effort)
	if = {
		limit = {
			owner ?= {
				is_at_war = yes
			}
		}
		subtract = 500
	}
	# Active banking-cycle stress: don't add construction during a financial crunch
	if = {
		limit = {
			owner ?= {
				has_variable = bubble_pressure
				var:bubble_pressure >= 60
			}
		}
		subtract = 500
	}
	min = 0
}
